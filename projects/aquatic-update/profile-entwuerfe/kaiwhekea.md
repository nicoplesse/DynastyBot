**Warum tagaktiv statt cathemeral:** Wie beim Para wird die Nacht zur verwundbaren Zeit. Das gibt dem nächtlichen Prognathodon seine Nische.

**Warum nur Health und nicht auch CW:** Mehr Health lässt Kai gegen Apexe länger durchhalten. Die CW bleibt, damit Xiphactinus-Rudel einen vereinzelten Kai weiterhin mit 4 Tieren erlegen können, genau wie Achillobator einen Para.

**Leedsichthys in der Gruppe:** Erlaubt, aber ohne Schutz. Keiner verteidigt den anderen, und es gibt keinen Stufenschutz. Kai sollen ohne Leeds genauso gut zurechtkommen.

**Warum dieselbe Route wie Leeds:** Kai ziehen in die Ozeane, weil es dort die wertvollsten Fischarten gibt, nicht wegen des Leeds. Die Route wechselt zwischen zwei Gefahren. In den Flussengen warten Xiphactinus-Schwärme, Deino und Spino. In den Ozeanen jagen Tylo, nachts Prog, und außer bei Palm Islands Livy. Einen sicheren Ort gibt es nicht, nur bessere und schlechtere Zeiten.

---

Kaiwhekea is an extinct genus of plesiosaur from the Late Cretaceous (Maastrichtian age) of what is now New Zealand. Kaiwhekea belongs to the clade Aristonectinae inside the family Elasmosauridae. With their long necks, small heads and fine interlocking teeth, they fed on small, soft-bodied prey such as fish and squid.

Author: QueenBella, with contributions from Lukesasa. Revised for the Aquatic Update.
Profile inspired by sea turtles and bottlenose dolphins.

## Overview

**Medium Diurnal Aquatic Piscivore**

- Kaiwhekea form harem bonds. After 5 successful clutches together or mutual agreement, these can develop into lifelong pairbonds.
- Kaiwhekea are nomadic and follow the Leedsichthys migration route.
- Kaiwhekea have a passive nature.
- Kaiwhekea feed on fish, shellfish and squid. They do not hunt other players and do not claim or feed from bodies.
- Kaiwhekea may group with Eurhinosaurus and with one Leedsichthys. Kaiwhekea do not defend the species they group with, and those species do not defend them.
- Kaiwhekea tolerate albino and melanistic individuals.

## Genetics

Skins that are considered mutations for Kaiwhekea include: Albino, Melanistic.
Players must roll in genetics-rolls to use these skins in game. This is one roll per species, per character.

## Group Limits (In-Game Group)

- Up to 12 Sub/Adults
- Unlimited Offspring
- Unlimited Adoptees

This group is known as a bale.
- Eurhinosaurus may join a bale and count towards the 12 Sub/Adults.
- One Leedsichthys may join a bale in addition to the 12 Sub/Adults, together with its own offspring.
- A bale that travels with a Leedsichthys is known as a flotilla.
Once offspring reach Sub-Adult, the bale must remain at 12 Sub/Adults or below. Harems and pairbonds may stay together when a bale splits.

## Engagement Limits (Defence & Hunts)

**Predator Reaction: Flighty / Defensive**

- Up to 2 individuals may act as active defenders. In addition, up to 6 may form 1 protective ring around calves.
- Active defenders stay within 3 body lengths of the bale.
- Kaiwhekea never chase predators that flee or have forfeited.
- Kaiwhekea flee from Apex and Giant carnivores on sight, unless defending a nest with hatchlings.

**Abilities in an engagement:**
- Only the active defenders may use Call to Arms.
- Every member may use Hustle and Rejuvenating Roar.
- The protective ring may use Rallying Horn.

## Resource Challenges

- Kaiwhekea only challenge or accept challenges for resources while nesting, or when offspring below Sub-Adult are present.
- At all other times they move away from a contested resource.
- Kaiwhekea do not accept territory challenges and simply move on.

## Preferred Diet

- Kaiwhekea live on fish, shellfish and squid.
- Calves catch small fish on their own from the moment they can swim. Mothers often share their catch.

## Nesting Limits

- 🌧️ Rainy Season: 4 🥚 per 4 hours.
- ☀️ Dry Season: 2 🥚 per 4 hours.

Kaiwhekea nest along the coastline or in shallow pools on coastal islands in their preferred POIs (Palm Islands, Castaway Isle, Volcano Islands). Their nests are built from kelp, branches and rocks.
Kaiwhekea may form same-sex pairs and adopt and raise offspring together.

## Physical Dimorphism

- ♀️ Females, known as cows, are slimmer than males and have duller colors. Suitable subspecies include: tenui.
- ♂️ Males, known as bulls, are bulkier, with contrasting coloration. Suitable subspecies include: katiki, grandis.

Physical dimorphism is not mandatory, but individuals that differ from the typical dimorphism may face difficulties during courtship.

## Growing Up

### Offspring Behavior

Kaiwhekea hatchlings, known as calves, hatch fully developed and able to swim, but they stay in the shallow pools where they were born until they reach Juvenile. From birth they watch their mother leave the pool to rejoin her bale in deeper water, and wait behind in the shallows for safety.
After reaching Juvenile, calves make the stretch into deeper water to meet their maternal bale. From then on they migrate with it, swimming in the middle of the group, close to their mother's flank.
Calves chase small fish and practise catching them. Play-fighting, leaping and chasing games between calves are common and build friendships that often last into adulthood.

### Orphaned Behavior

Calves can survive on their own from the moment they hatch. Orphans without visible adults become skittish, and hide in shallow water, near the surface or among rocks, away from deep-water predators.
Orphans seek out other Kaiwhekea, and also follow Eurhinosaurus shoals or an adult Leedsichthys. A bale readily adopts orphaned calves.
Orphans that never find a bale may grow up quieter and more independent than most of their kind.

## General Behavior

Kaiwhekea are fast and agile swimmers. They swim near the surface and enjoy leaping out of the water as a form of play.
They can be spotted basking in shallow waters, or even on land for a short period of time.
Kaiwhekea swallow pebbles and shells to help digest their food.

### Day and Night

- **Day:** Kaiwhekea are diurnal. During the day they migrate, fish and socialise.
- **Night:** At night a bale settles in calm shallows near islands and rests close together. A Leedsichthys travelling with the bale often sleeps beneath or beside it, but does not keep watch.
- **Night watch:**
  - Up to 2 individuals stay awake and slowly circle the resting bale. These are usually bulls, or cows if no bulls are present.
  - Watch duty is passed on with a gentle nudge.
  - Eurhinosaurus grouped with the bale often take on part of the watch. Their loud calls give the earliest warning.

### Migration

Bales are creatures of habit, and follow the Leedsichthys migration route marked on the profile map.

- **Channels:** The route loops through the warm saltwater channels between the islands: the Volcano Islands, Twisted Forest, Stillwater Bog, the Mudflats and Coastland Swamp.
- **Oceans:** From there, bales regularly head out into the open oceans of Kelp Vale, the Abyssal Depths, Palm Islands and Stonebed Shoal. The oceans hold the fish Kaiwhekea value most, and the richest fishing grounds of the year.
- **Company:** Bales often travel out together with a Leedsichthys, which heads to the cooler ocean water to cool down. A bale does not need one to follow the route.
- **Tradition:** The route is passed down between generations. Bales leave an area once the fish grows scarce.

### Curiosity

Kaiwhekea take a great interest in terrestrial species they encounter near the water. Some attempt to get their attention by fetching objects from the seafloor and presenting them as trinkets.

## Social Interaction

Kaiwhekea are a highly social species. Bales migrate together in mixed-sex groups, guided by an experienced lead cow, who decides where the bale fishes, rests and travels.

- **Bulls:** Bulls form alliances of two, usually lifelong friends. A bull alliance escorts a bale, swims on its outer edge and drives away rival bulls. A bull holds his harem within the bale.
- **Bonding:**
  - Bonded Kaiwhekea swim side by side, breach in sync and rest with their necks gently crossed.
  - Gift-giving is a common sign of affection, for example a shell, a bright stone or a piece of kelp.
  - Close friends of any sex share fish and groom each other by brushing along each other's bodies.
- **Solitary time:** Kaiwhekea also enjoy time alone, sunbathing on beaches or swimming spread out, careful not to crowd one another.
- **Other bales:** When two bales meet, they greet each other warmly and may exchange members. They share a POI for no longer than one day–night cycle before one of them moves on.

### Correcting Over-Aggression

Over-aggression is rare and not tolerated.

1. A warning is a hard slap of the flipper on the water surface.
2. If it is ignored, nearby members block the aggressor with their bodies, followed by firm nips.
3. If the behavior continues, the bale shuns the individual.

## Aggressive Interaction

Kaiwhekea avoid conflict with their own species, except in courtship. Bulls that accidentally drift too close to another bull's harem quickly slip away.
During courtship, rival bulls bite and spin into each other until one backs down. These fights rarely end in death, but leave deep scars on both.

## Cross-Species Interaction

Kaiwhekea are tolerant towards other species and avoid conflict at all times. When they meet a new species they approach it with curiosity, though experienced adults keep a safe distance from carnivores.

- **Eurhinosaurus:** Kaiwhekea welcome them as bale mates. Their speed and loud voices make them excellent sentries. Kaiwhekea do not defend grouped Eurhinosaurus, but call out in grief if a bonded one is lost.
- **Leedsichthys:**
  - A bale may travel with one Leedsichthys, like pilot fish around a whale shark, and may group with it in game. Together they are known as a flotilla.
  - Neither side defends the other. The Leedsichthys defends only itself and its own offspring, and Kaiwhekea never step in for it.
  - Kaiwhekea may use the giant's body as cover and hide behind or beneath it. The Leedsichthys never moves to block a predator on their behalf.
- **Apex carnivores:** Kaiwhekea flee from adult Tylosaurus, Prognathodon, Livyatan, Spinosaurus and Deinosuchus on sight.
- **Xiphactinus:** Single Xiphactinus are watched closely. Packs of 3 or more are treated as an active threat.
- **Tropeognathus:** Tropeognathus follow flotillas from the air and dive in when the flotilla reaches a gathering of fish. Fish are the one thing a bale does not share, so Kaiwhekea may drive them off. They give an aggressive call first, then charge and snap at it until it leaves the water. This is not a hunt: once the Tropeognathus is airborne, they let it go.

## Predator Reaction

At the first sign of danger, Kaiwhekea raise their heads above the surface to look around, and alert the bale with sharp calls. The bale gathers, calves move into the centre, and the bale either forms a ring or flees.

### Hunted by Non-Apex

- **Defenders:** Up to 2 adults, usually the bull alliance, step forward as active defenders. Once the engagement starts, they cannot be swapped out.
- **Reaching the bale:** An attacked individual must swim back to the bale within 30 seconds to receive defenders. Being within 2 body lengths of the bale counts as reaching it.
- **Protective ring:**
  - Up to 6 adults form a ring around the calves. Members stay side by side, facing outwards.
  - They may leave the ring by 1 body length to land a hit, then return to it.
  - Once formed, the ring cannot swap out individuals.
- **Everyone else:** The rest of the bale flees, using Hustle, and pushes stragglers ahead of it.
- **When the hunters forfeit:** Kaiwhekea let them go and do not pursue.

### Hunted by Apex

- Kaiwhekea flee from Apex and Giant carnivores on sight. While fleeing they may defend themselves, but they must prioritise escape. They do not intervene in an Apex's chase of another member.
- A grouped Leedsichthys can offer cover to hide behind, but no protection.
- **Nesting:** Kaiwhekea only stand their ground at a nest with hatchlings, and only against a single Apex. Up to 2 parents may defend one nest and must stay within one body length of it. If an Apex pair approaches, they flee and the calves hide in the shallows.

## Courtship & Parenthood

### Courtship

- **Calls:** During the courting season both sexes become extremely vocal underwater.
  - Long-distance calls are used to find a mate.
  - Repeated local calls signal interest.
- **Display:** Bulls court in pairs, if they are part of an alliance. They breach side by side, slap their tails on the surface and swim tight circles around the cows they court.
- **Choice:** Cows choose by joining the display and swimming alongside the bull. A bull's harem lays its nests first.
- **Lifelong pairbonds:** After 5 successful clutches together, or by mutual agreement, a bull and a cow may become a lifelong pair. A pair always rejoins the same bale.

### Offspring Rearing

1. When preparing to nest, cows head to coastal shallow pools and build simple nests.
2. After all young hatch, mothers return to deeper water nearby and wait for the calves to reach Juvenile. The bale stays close to the nesting area during this time.
3. Once the calves join, the bale travels on, with the bulls on the outside.
4. Bulls protect their bale and their calves until the calves reach Adolescent. Bonded bulls stay with the bale afterwards. Unbonded bulls may leave to find another bale.

## Elderly & End of Life (Optional)

When Kaiwhekea reach old age, they slow down and swim at the back of the bale, with bonded friends or a lifelong partner beside them. Eventually they leave the bale and seek deeper, quieter waters. They become less vocal and may shed into darker or duller coloration. Most die when they no longer have the strength to resurface.

## Rule Exemptions

- **Grouping:** As piscivores, Kaiwhekea may group with Eurhinosaurus and one Leedsichthys (exemption from 8.1, under the proposed piscivore rule). There is no defence between the species.
- **Abilities:** In a mixed group, only active defenders use Call to Arms (8.1.1).

## Profile – Quick View

- Kaiwhekea are Medium Diurnal Aquatic Piscivores.
- They group in bales of up to 12 Sub/Adults, with unlimited offspring and adoptees. Eurhinosaurus may join, and so may one Leedsichthys, without any defence between the species. A group with a Leedsichthys is known as a flotilla.
- They are passive, feed only on fish, shellfish and squid, and never hunt other players.
- 2 active defenders protect the bale, and up to 6 form a ring around the calves. They flee from Apex and Giant carnivores on sight, except at a nest.
- They form harems that can become lifelong pairbonds.
- Bull alliances escort the bale.
- They migrate by day along the Leedsichthys route, through the saltwater channels and out into the oceans, and rest in the shallows at night under watch.
