## Overview (replaces the current overview)

**Medium Cathemeral Aquatic Piscivore**

- Eurhinosaurus form promiscuous bonds.
- Eurhinosaurus are nomadic and follow the Leedsichthys migration route, like Kaiwhekea.
- Eurhinosaurus have a passive nature towards other species. Shoals may become defensive towards other Eurhinosaurus shoals when resources are low.
- Eurhinosaurus feed on fish and crustaceans. They do not hunt other players and do not claim or feed from bodies.
- Eurhinosaurus may group with Kaiwhekea and with one Leedsichthys. Eurhinosaurus do not defend the species they group with, and those species do not defend them.
- Eurhinosaurus tolerate albino and melanistic individuals.

## Group Limits (addition)

Unlimited Sub/Adults, Offspring and Adoptees stay as they are.
- When grouped with a Kaiwhekea bale, Eurhinosaurus count towards the bale's limit of 12 Sub/Adults.
- One Leedsichthys may join in addition. A group with a Leedsichthys is known as a flotilla.

## Engagement Limits (replaces the current section)

**Predator Reaction: Flighty**

- Up to 3 Sub/Adult Eurhinosaurus may defend if attacked.
- Eurhinosaurus rely on their speed. They flee from Apex and Giant carnivores and from Xiphactinus packs, and only turn to fight when cornered or defending their nest.
- Adolescent Eurhinosaurus never take part in a defence.
- When grouped with Kaiwhekea, Eurhinosaurus do not join the protective ring or act as active defenders. They raise the alarm and flee with the calves.

## Preferred Diet (replaces "Preferred Prey/Diet")

- Eurhinosaurus live on fish and various crustaceans.
- They hunt fish as a sport, not out of need.
- Eurhinosaurus no longer hunt Kaiwhekea or Leedsichthys offspring.

## Fishing Strategy (replaces "Hunting Strategy")

Fishing is seen as a sport. Shoals train constantly to refine their coordination.

- **Loud style:** Some members circle a fish school with loud calls and fast turns until it bunches into a tight ball. The others then dart in from below and the sides to snatch fish.
- **Silent style:** Some shoals approach in silence, dive deep and strike the fish school from below.
- **Mixed style:** Shoals switch between loud chaos and silent strikes to keep the fish off balance.
- **Sharing:** A successful shoal often leaves part of the catch to young or to bonded Kaiwhekea nearby.

## Migration (new section)

- Eurhinosaurus follow the Leedsichthys migration route marked on the profile map: a loop through the warm saltwater channels between the islands, with regular trips out into the open oceans of Kelp Vale, the Abyssal Depths, Palm Islands and Stonebed Shoal.
- The oceans hold the fish shoals Eurhinosaurus value most. Shoals often travel out together with a Kaiwhekea bale or a Leedsichthys.
- When driven to nest, Eurhinosaurus leave the route and return to their nesting river, as before.

## Cross-Species Interaction (replaces the Kaiwhekea paragraph; the rest stays)

- **Kaiwhekea:**
  - Eurhinosaurus and Kaiwhekea are frequent companions. Eurhinosaurus enjoy the calm of a bale and the shelter of its larger members.
  - In return they act as its sentries. Their loud voices carry far through the water and warn the bale long before a predator arrives.
  - Shoals often swim on the outer edge of a migrating bale and dart ahead to scout for fish.
  - Eurhinosaurus tease Kaiwhekea with playful nips and races, but never harm them.
- **Leedsichthys:**
  - Eurhinosaurus may travel and group with one Leedsichthys, like pilot fish around a whale shark. Such a group is known as a flotilla.
  - Neither side defends the other.
  - Eurhinosaurus may use the giant's body as cover. The Leedsichthys never moves to block a predator for them.
  - Shoals love to race circles around the slow giant.
- **Tropeognathus:** Tropeognathus follow flotillas from the air and dive in when the flotilla reaches a gathering of fish. Eurhinosaurus take this as an insult to their sport, and drive them off. They give an aggressive call first, then charge and snap at it until it leaves the water. This is not a hunt: once the Tropeognathus is airborne, they let it go.
- **Xiphactinus:** Xiphactinus are the only fish faster than Eurhinosaurus, and its most feared hunter. They live in the saltwater channels, so shoals cross them quickly and scatter at the first sight of a pack.
- **Apex carnivores:** Eurhinosaurus flee from adult Tylosaurus, Sachicasaurus, Livyatan, Spinosaurus and Deinosuchus. They sometimes come across as arrogant and enjoy outrunning these predators in open water.

## Rule Exemptions (replaces the current section)

- **Grouping:** As piscivores, Eurhinosaurus may group with Kaiwhekea and one Leedsichthys (exemption from 8.1, under the proposed piscivore rule). There is no defence between the species.
- The Metriacanthosaurus exemption is no longer needed, because Eurhinosaurus do not feed from bodies.
