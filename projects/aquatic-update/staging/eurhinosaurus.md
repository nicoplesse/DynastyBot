# Eurhinosaurus
"Eurhino"
Eurhinosaurus are sleek, swordfish-billed ichthyosaurs and some of the fastest swimmers in the seas of Riparia. Loud, playful and a little arrogant, they live in leaderless shoals that follow whoever finds the fish, travel the oceans alongside a Leedsichthys as its flotilla, and race upstream like salmon when it is time to nest.

%% banner Overview
**Medium Cathemeral Aquatic Piscivore**
Eurhinosaurus form promiscuous bonds, and close friendships with others of the same sex.
Eurhinosaurus are nomadic and follow the Leedsichthys migration route. To nest, they return to their nesting river.
Eurhinosaurus have a passive nature towards other species. Shoals may become defensive towards other Eurhinosaurus shoals when resources are low.
Eurhinosaurus are not scavengers. They feed on fish and crustaceans, and do not hunt other players.
Eurhinosaurus may group with one Leedsichthys, forming a flotilla. Up to 4 may join a Kaiwhekea bale instead.
Eurhinosaurus may not defend the species they group with, and those species do not defend them.
Eurhinosaurus tolerate albino and melanistic individuals.

## Profile Specific Unique Feature
Eurhinosaurus sleep with one eye open. In a shoal of 3 or more, one member always stays awake.
To nest, Eurhinosaurus make a run upstream to their nesting river as one school, like salmon. See ✦ Migration.
Eurhinosaurus change their skin with their surroundings and the seasons. See Color Dimorphism.

## Group Limits (In-Game Group)
Unlimited Sub/Adults
Unlimited Offspring
Unlimited Adoptees

This group is known as a shoal.
- One Leedsichthys may join in addition. A group with a Leedsichthys is known as a flotilla.
- Instead, up to 4 Eurhinosaurus, usually one friend group, may join a Kaiwhekea bale. They do not count towards the bale's 16 Sub/Adults.
  - A shoal grouped with a bale cannot also group with a Leedsichthys.
- During courtship, shoals mix into one large breeding group, known as a school. See Courtship & Parenthood.

## Engagement Limits (Defence & Hunts)
Predator Reaction: Flighty
Eurhinosaurus do not hunt other players.
Up to 3 Sub/Adult Eurhinosaurus may defend if attacked.
Eurhinosaurus rely on their speed. They flee from Apex and Giant carnivores and from Xiphactinus batteries of 3 or more, and only turn to fight when cornered or defending their nest.
Adolescent Eurhinosaurus never take part in a defence.
When grouped with Kaiwhekea, Eurhinosaurus do not join the protective ring or act as active defenders. They raise the alarm and flee with the bale.
Eurhinosaurus never lead a predator towards another group on purpose.
Eurhinosaurus may show off by swimming past hunters, within strict limits. See ✦ Showing Off.

## Resource Challenges
Eurhinosaurus only challenge other Eurhinosaurus shoals, and only when fish are scarce. See ✦ The Line.
Eurhinosaurus do not accept territory challenges from other species, and simply move on.
Eurhinosaurus never show aggression to other adults within their nesting grounds.

## Preferred Diet
Eurhinosaurus live on fish and various crustaceans. They check rocks and underwater caves for crustaceans.
Eurhinosaurus hunt fish as a sport, not out of need.
Eurhinosaurus do not fish on the run to their nesting river. See ✦ Migration.
Eurhinosaurus do not hunt Kaiwhekea or Leedsichthys offspring.
Eurhinosaurus do not share fish with Tropeognathus, and drive them off. See Cross-Species Interaction.

## Nesting Limits

☀️ Dry Season: 2 🥚 per 3 hours.
Eurhinosaurus nest in the Cedrus Forest rivers, ideally as far upstream as possible. Their nests are built out of kelp, branches and rocks.

## Color Dimorphism
Outside of the breeding season, Eurhinosaurus shed their skin regularly depending on their environment, changing their color, but not patterns, to better fit into their surroundings and hide from predators.
Both males and females shed into bright colors during nesting season, sometimes also changing their skin patterns.
Females prioritise blue skins, while males prioritise green skins.
Color dimorphism for Eurhinosaurus is mandatory. The changing of environmental camouflage is optional, however courting color changes are mandatory.

%% banner Growing Up
### ✦ Offspring Behavior (Nested)
Eurhinosaurus young, known as fry, hatch from their egg completely mobile and talkative.
Often locking sights with their mother, they stay particularly close to her tail, feeding from shells and small fish within their nesting areas.
When shore hunters such as Austroraptor, Halszkaraptor or Sarcosuchus come near, fry fall silent and hide in the shallows, among roots and reeds.
Approaching Juvenile, young fry adjust to the chaos of other young Eurhinosaurus within the nesting areas.
Juveniles from all nests often take up games of tag together, with slight nips and pushing into each other's sides. These games promote key survival instincts, and the friendship bonds they need for their survival in the future.
Mothers monitor these games of tag to make sure they never cause too much injury.
Juveniles practise the fishing styles of the adults on small fish, one loud, one silent, and race each other along the river.
When the oldest fry in a mother's clutch becomes Adolescent, the mother leaves the nesting grounds to return to the ocean. The nesting ground turns into a creche of youngsters looking out for one another while they continue to grow. See ✦ Parenthood.
At Adolescent, siblings or other similarly aged young start to form their own shoals, and form close friendship bonds with those of the same sex. The first friend groups form here.
They swim downstream with their newly formed shoal, or sometimes choose to branch out on their own.
Young shoals often join their first flotilla or Kaiwhekea bale to learn the migration route.
These youngsters only return to the river nesting ground when wishing to nest themselves.

### ✦ Orphaned Behavior (Wild Spawn)
Orphaned Eurhinosaurus are born into the waters alone and defenceless.
With natural survival instincts, these orphans tend to hide within overgrown kelp and coral rocks. They remain hidden, feeding off small fish and crustaceans.
As they mature into Sub-Adult, these orphans explore more bravely, venturing out into open waters in hopes they may find a shoal to join.
Kaiwhekea bales welcome orphaned Eurhinosaurus kindly. An orphan may travel with a bale like any Eurhinosaurus, but it is not adopted, and not protected.
%% banner General Behavior
Eurhinosaurus are usually loud and rambunctious by nature, and are normally heard long before they are seen.
While both sexes enjoy vocalising, males call more loudly and more often, projecting their voices through the water and singing out to their group mates.
Shoals often follow a familiar route around their current POI, travelling between popular fish locations and checking rocks and underwater caves for crustaceans.
Eurhinosaurus are among the fastest swimmers in the ocean, and can hold a sprint longer than almost any other aquatic. Only Xiphactinus sprint faster.
They race each other, leap high out of the water with Hydrofoil, and ride the bow wave of a swimming Leedsichthys. Friend groups often leap in sync, like spinner dolphins.
Despite being confined to the waters, Eurhinosaurus are playful and endlessly curious about land animals. They follow them along the shore, take great pleasure in calling to them, and poke their heads above the water to peer into nearby forests.
For an audience on land they leap high with Hydrofoil, like dolphins leaping beside a boat.
They enjoy their speed and their perceived dominance over the ocean, and like to show off in front of hunters. See ✦ Showing Off.

### ✦ Fishing
Fishing is seen as a sport. Shoals train constantly to refine their coordination.
- **Taking turns:** Like sailfish, the shoal herds a fish school into a tight ball. Members dart into it one at a time with a quick sweep of the bill, then pull away to make room for the next.
- **Loud style:** Some members circle a fish school with loud calls and fast turns until it bunches into a tight ball. The others then dart in from below and the sides to snatch fish.
- **Silent style:** Some shoals approach in silence, dive deep and strike the fish school from below.
- **Mixed style:** Shoals switch between loud chaos and silent strikes to keep the fish off balance.
Friend groups compete for the biggest catch. The winners boast with high leaps and loud calls, and the losers splash them. Contests never turn into fights.
In a bale, Eurhinosaurus join the Kaiwhekea's herding with their speed, and drive fish into the bale's circle.
A successful shoal often leaves part of the catch to young, or to bonded Kaiwhekea nearby.

### ✦ Downtime Behavior
Eurhinosaurus are cathemeral. They are active by day and night in short bursts, and rest in short naps whenever the shoal feels safe.
Like dolphins, Eurhinosaurus sleep lightly with one eye open, and wake at the slightest disturbance.
In a shoal of 3 or more, at least one member always stays awake while the others rest. Members take turns.
In a Kaiwhekea bale, Eurhinosaurus often take on part of the night watch, resting on the bale's outer edge.
When Kaiwhekea nap on high rocks, Eurhinosaurus stay in the water below and keep watch.
Xiphactinus hunt the saltwater channels at dawn and dusk. Shoals cross the channels by day where they can, and stay close together in the half light.
Sachicasaurus hunt sleeping flotillas at night. Eurhinosaurus resting at a flotilla's edge are among the first they reach, and among the first to wake.

### ✦ Time Out of Water
Eurhinosaurus leave the water only to display in courtship. Outside courtship, they stay in the water, apart from leaps.
Their leaps carry them high above the surface in play, in contests, and in their joy at the courtship of others.

### ✦ Migration
Eurhinosaurus follow the Leedsichthys migration route marked on the profile map: a loop through the warm saltwater channels between the islands, with regular trips out into the open oceans of Kelp Vale, the Abyssal Depths, Palm Islands and Stonebed Shoal.
The oceans hold the fish shoals Eurhinosaurus value most. Most shoals travel the route with a Leedsichthys as its flotilla, and go where the giant goes.
Shoals may leave the route for detours into the lakes and fresh waters connected to the channels, where land animals come to drink. Spinosaurus live there, and land carnivores wait on the banks.
**The run:** When driven to nest, Eurhinosaurus leave the route and return to their nesting river, like salmon.
- Shoals gather at the river mouth, and swim upstream together as one school.
- On Riparia, the run leads from the saltwater channels up through Big Tree Overlook into the Cedrus Forest rivers.
- On the run, they do not fish, and do not stop in Big Tree Overlook. Spinosaurus wait there like bears at a salmon run.
- The school swims fast and close together. Loud calls keep stragglers with the school. At the sight of a hunter, the school falls silent and speeds up.
- The run passes the courting grounds of the Leedsichthys. Eurhinosaurus swim past the males' nests without disturbing them.
Once they have finished courting, males swim back downstream to the route. Mothers follow once their oldest offspring become Adolescent.

%% banner Social Interaction
Shoals form strong, lasting bonds with their members, often spending the majority of their lives together. These connections are vital due to their cooperative fishing and their mutual reliance for comfort, protection and survival.

### ✦ General Communication
A shoal is rarely quiet. Constant calls tell everyone where the others are. Sudden silence means danger, or a silent hunt.
Males sing longer sequences of (:broadcast_call:) to their group mates.
Joy and play are shown with high leaps, racing circles and tail slaps.
Eurhinosaurus poke their heads above the water to look at the shore, often with a (:question_emote:), and call out to land species.
Alarm: the chatter stops, and a (:danger_call:) follows. The shoal either bunches together or bursts apart. The call carries far, which is why Kaiwhekea value Eurhinosaurus as sentries.

### ✦ Shoal Life
Shoals have no leader. Like a real fish school, a shoal follows whoever has found the fish.
A member that spots fish or crustaceans gives a (:broadcast_call:) and swims towards them, and the shoal follows.
Disagreements are settled by splitting. Those who want to go elsewhere may leave, and the shoal often meets again later.
**Friend groups:**
- Within a shoal, 2 to 4 close friends, usually of the same sex, form a friend group. They swim in sync, leap together, share fish and rest side by side.
- Friend groups often stay together for life, even when the shoal around them changes.
**Scouts:**
- Up to 2 members may swim ahead of the shoal as scouts, no further than 10 body lengths.
- A scout that finds fish calls the shoal over. A scout that spots a predator falls silent and turns back at once.
- In a Kaiwhekea bale, the lead cow decides whether the bale follows a scout's call.
When a shoal meets a smaller shoal, they often merge, favouring safety in numbers.

### ✦ Other Shoals
When two separate shoals encounter each other, they may briefly interact in a friendly manner before moving on.
Shoals rarely spend more than a few minutes within the same POI as one another, so as not to empty its fish.
Eurhinosaurus never show aggression to other adults within their nesting grounds.

### ✦ Albino & Melanistic Behavior
Both albino and melanistic Eurhinosaurus are accepted by their shoals.
Albino Eurhinosaurus cannot hide with their colors, and stand out to predators. They stay in the middle of the shoal, and rarely scout.
Melanistic individuals are hard to see at night, and are often trusted with the watch.
%% banner Aggressive Interaction
Eurhinosaurus are generally not aggressive towards members of their own species. Typically, shoals strive to maintain harmony among their own kind.
In order to express annoyance towards another Eurhinosaurus, a Eurhinosaurus raises its bill and shakes its head (:shake_emote:).
If this is ignored, a hard tail slap beside the other follows; it may NOT land this hit.
These motions are immediately respected by the offending Eurhinosaurus, who moves away at least 1 body length.
Aggressive encounters are rare, and usually occur only when resources are scarce. In most cases, shoals prefer to relocate to another point of interest where resources are more abundant.

### ✦ The Line
If a shoal occupies a POI with few resources, it may challenge a larger shoal that enters:
1. The resident shoal lines up side by side, facing the newcomers. It calls out with (:threaten_call:), and does not move from its line.
2. The newcomers have 30 seconds to turn away.
3. If they stay, up to 3 members of each shoal may fight. The fight ends when one shoal leaves.

### ✦ Correcting Over-Aggression
Eurhinosaurus are rarely aggressive towards their own kind. Over-aggression is corrected in steps:
1. A raised bill and a (:shake_emote:).
2. A hard tail slap beside the aggressor, while its friend group closes in around it.
3. Firm nips from the shoal.
4. If it continues, the shoal leaves it behind.
%% banner Cross-Species Interaction
Eurhinosaurus are curious about nearly every species they meet, and passive towards them. Their speed lets them watch from close by, and leave whenever they wish.

### ✦ Kaiwhekea
Eurhinosaurus and Kaiwhekea are frequent companions. Up to 4, usually one friend group, may live with a bale.
They stay because they gain more from it than the Kaiwhekea do: safety in numbers, and the fish the bale drives together. Yellowfin tuna travel with spotted dolphins in the same way.
In return they act as the bale's sentries. Their loud voices carry far through the water and warn the bale long before a predator arrives.
When the bale hauls out at a colony, they keep watch in the water below.
Shoals often swim on the outer edge of a migrating bale, and dart ahead to scout for fish.
Eurhinosaurus tease Kaiwhekea with races and close passes, but never bite them.
Eurhinosaurus watch the bulls' courtship dance closely. It excites them, puts them in a happy mood and stirs their own wish to court, and they often breach along with the bulls. Kaiwhekea do not let this distract them.
Neither species defends the other.

### ✦ Leedsichthys & Flotillas
Eurhinosaurus may travel and group with one Leedsichthys, the way tuna travel with whale sharks. Such a group is known as a flotilla. They hunt the fish that gather around the giant.
The Leedsichthys chooses the way and keeps to its route without exception. The shoal goes where it goes.
Neither side defends the other. Eurhinosaurus may use the giant's body as cover, but the Leedsichthys never moves to block a predator for them.
Shoals love to race circles around the slow giant. Its pups soon learn to school alongside Eurhinosaurus young.
When the Leedsichthys stops to watch a courting Kaiwhekea bale, its Eurhinosaurus breach along with the bulls. Afterwards, the shoal travels on with the giant, not with the bale.

### ✦ Tropeognathus
Tropeognathus follow flotillas from the air and dive in when the flotilla reaches a gathering of fish.
Eurhinosaurus take this as an insult to their sport, and drive them off. They give a (:threaten_call:) first, then charge and snap at the Tropeognathus until it leaves the water.
This is not a hunt. Once the Tropeognathus is airborne, they let it go.

### ✦ Xiphactinus
Xiphactinus are the only fish that sprint faster than Eurhinosaurus, and their most feared hunters.
Xiphactinus live in the saltwater channels, so shoals cross them quickly.

### ✦ Sarcosuchus & Shore Hunters
As a Large carnivore, Sarcosuchus may be defended against by up to 3 Eurhinosaurus. Most often, the shoal escapes by speed. Sarcosuchus lurk along the Cedrus Forest rivers, near the nests.
Austroraptor and Halszkaraptor are a danger to fry in the shallows of the nesting river.

### ✦ Land Animals
Eurhinosaurus are fascinated by every land animal they meet at the water.
Herbivores get calls and leaps up close. Carnivores get the same show, but only from a safe distance.

### ✦ Deinocheirus
Eurhinosaurus display a strong fascination and affection for Deinocheirus, admiring these docile semi-aquatics for their ability to spurt water, which creates playful environments the Eurhinosaurus eagerly enjoy.
They often attempt to trade fish or decorative shells with Deinocheirus in exchange for water spouts, turning the encounter into a joyful game.

### ✦ Pycnonemosaurus
Terrestrial species they encounter are sometimes teased. Eurhinosaurus especially enjoy screaming matches with Pycnonemosaurus, who rarely back down from the noisy challenge.
Both Deinocheirus and Pycnonemosaurus live along the Cedrus Forest rivers, so most of these meetings happen during nesting.

### ✦ Apex Carnivores
Eurhinosaurus flee from adult Tylosaurus, Sachicasaurus, Livyatan, Spinosaurus and Deinosuchus. See ✦ Where to Flee.
%% banner Predator Reaction
At the first sign of danger the chatter stops. A sudden silence is the shoal's first warning, followed by a (:danger_call:).

### ✦ Solo Predator Reaction
A lone Eurhinosaurus flees from every hunter it can outrun, and relies on its speed.
It only fights when cornered.

### ✦ Shoal Predator Reaction
- **Fountain:** Against a single hunter, the shoal splits around it, and closes again behind it, like a herring school.
- **Burst:** If a hunter strikes into the shoal, members dart away in all directions, then regroup out of reach, calling softly.
- **Batteries:** Against a Xiphactinus battery, the shoal bunches together and sprints. Batteries try to split it, so it bursts apart only once the battery strikes.
- **Defenders:** Up to 3 Sub/Adults may turn to fight when cornered, at their nest, or against non-Apex hunters such as Sarcosuchus. They strike with Joust and pull away again, rather than holding a drawn-out fight.
- **In a bale:** Eurhinosaurus do not join the protective ring or act as defenders. They raise the alarm and flee with the bale.

### ✦ Where to Flee
- **Tylosaurus:** along the surface at full speed. Eurhinosaurus are faster, but a Tylosaurus does not tire sooner.
- **Livyatan:** into the shallows, where Livyatan do not follow.
- **Sachicasaurus:** out of the kelp and the reef, into open water. A seized Eurhinosaurus is lost, and its shoal does not help it.
- **Deinosuchus and Spinosaurus:** away from banks and shores, into deeper, open water. On the run, the school passes their waters without stopping.
- **Xiphactinus:** as one tight group, holding the sprint for as long as it takes. A battery that is spotted early tires first.

### ✦ Showing Off
Eurhinosaurus enjoy their speed, and sometimes come across as arrogant. Like minnows that swim up to a pike to inspect it, they show off by swimming past hunters they know they can outrun.
- Only healthy Sub/Adults in open water may show off, and no more than 2 at a time.
- They pass a hunter once, no closer than 3 body lengths, then swim away, and do not return in the same encounter.
- They never show off near fry, at a nest, or near healing water, and never lead the hunter towards a group.
- A hunter may chase them, and showing off is a risk the Eurhinosaurus takes. It does not change the hunter's own rules for when it may hunt.
%% banner Courtship & Parenthood
Once ready to court, shoals from all over the world travel to the nesting rivers of Cedrus Forest.
Shoals mix together and group into one larger breeding group, called a school.
Eurhinosaurus are passive to all members of their species within these POIs, and will travel further away to seek food if resources are scarce, before returning to their chosen nesting area.
Both males and females shed into bright courting skins, which signifies their desire to court and nest. Females prioritise blue skins, while males prioritise green skins.

### ✦ Courtship
Males begin the courtship process by beaching themselves on the river edge, vocalising and flapping their fins on the sand or mud.
They move up and down the small section of beach and show off their strength and ability to stay out of water for a short period of time.
Females who are interested watch from the water's edge, approaching, splashing, and calling out softly with approval.
When the courtship display ends, the male returns to the water and approaches a female nearby that has been calling to him.
They circle together and brush their bodies against one another to form a close bond.

### ✦ Nesting
The female leads the male to a section of the riverbed, and builds her nest to lay eggs and allow the male to fertilise them.
Females stay near their nest to protect their eggs once laid, but males are promiscuous and return to the beach to display themselves once more, hoping to attract another female.
Once males have finished courting and fertilising eggs, they leave the nesting grounds. A small group of bonded males will usually leave together.
Males take no responsibility in child rearing, and return to the ocean long before any offspring hatch.

### ✦ Parenthood
Mothers stay within the nesting grounds with their young until their oldest offspring becomes Adolescent.
Then, the mothers leave the rivers and swim back to the open ocean with other adult females. They seek out their previous shoal members, and closely bonded females sometimes wait at the river's mouth for a companion to leave her nest.
Mothers do not wait for their youngest offspring to mature. They leave all the offspring in their nesting ground, which turns into a creche of youngsters looking out for one another while they continue to grow.

### ✦ Offspring Maturity
At Adolescent, young Eurhinosaurus form their own shoals and friend groups, and swim downstream to the migration route.
Young shoals often join their first flotilla or Kaiwhekea bale to learn the route.
They only return to the nesting river when wishing to nest themselves.

### ✦ Adoption
Young Eurhinosaurus in the creche look out for one another regardless of whose clutch they come from.
Orphans that find a shoal are taken in like any other member, as shoals readily merge.
Kaiwhekea bales welcome orphaned Eurhinosaurus kindly, but do not adopt or protect them.
%% banner Elderly
As Eurhinosaurus age, they spend less time in the open ocean and stay close to rivers and coast lines.
They lose the ability to shed their skin to camouflage themselves, and show more white or pale colors in their skin as their patterns and original colors fade.
The pale colors often make elderly Eurhinosaurus stick out and become a target for predators. They no longer scout, and swim in the middle of the shoal.
When the shoal migrates to breeding territory, elderly males will beach themselves to display, but sometimes struggle to return to the water quickly and become a bigger target for predators.
Companions of elderly individuals will sometimes stick close by their side, guiding them to feeding areas and encouraging them to keep up with the shoal. Friend groups often stay with an elderly friend to the end.

### ✦ Death
Eventually, elderly Eurhinosaurus lose their swimming strength and float gently to the bottom of the sea or river bed, in their final resting place.
Closely bonded individuals mourn the loss of their friends for a short period of time, before moving on with their shoal and forming new bonds.

## Profile - Quick View
Eurhinosaurus are Medium Cathemeral Aquatic Piscivores.
Eurhinosaurus group in unlimited numbers, offspring and adoptees in a group known as a shoal. With one Leedsichthys, the shoal becomes a flotilla. Instead, up to 4 may join a Kaiwhekea bale. There is no defence between the species.
Eurhinosaurus have a passive nature. They feed only on fish and crustaceans, and fish as a sport: in turns, loud or silent.
Shoals have no leader. They follow whoever finds the fish, send up to 2 scouts ahead, and hold close friend groups of the same sex.
Eurhinosaurus sleep lightly with one eye open. In a shoal of 3 or more, one always stays awake, and they act as sentries for Kaiwhekea bales.
Eurhinosaurus flee from Apex and Giant carnivores and Xiphactinus batteries of 3 or more. Up to 3 may defend. Within limits, they show off by swimming past hunters.
Eurhinosaurus follow the Leedsichthys route, usually as its flotilla. To nest, they make the run upstream to their nesting river as one school, without fishing or stopping.
Eurhinosaurus court at the nesting river: males beach themselves to display, and females choose. Mothers stay with their young until the oldest reach Adolescent, and leave the rest in a creche.
Eurhinosaurus are endlessly curious about land animals. They are friendly towards Deinocheirus, and enjoy screaming matches with Pycnonemosaurus.
