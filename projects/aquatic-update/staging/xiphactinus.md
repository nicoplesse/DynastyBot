# Xiphactinus
"Xiph"
Xiphactinus are huge, bulldog-jawed predatory fish and the fastest swimmers in the warm, brackish channels of Riparia. From the outside, a Xiphactinus hunt looks like pure chaos. From the inside, it is pure control: every member of a battery has its role, its place and its moment.

Written by Dynasty Realism
Mod: Xiphactinus by Arazoa (ArazoaXiph)
Profile inspired by piranhas, sharks, yellow saddle goatfish, tarpon, Xiphactinus paleobiology and creative liberty.

%% banner Overview
**Medium Cathemeral Aquatic Carnivore**
Xiphactinus form no lasting bonds. Pairs form in the frenzy, and last only until their eggs have hatched.
Xiphactinus are nomadic within the channels, and hold no territories.
Xiphactinus have an aggressive nature towards prey, and a tolerant nature towards their own kind, outside the frenzy.
Xiphactinus may scavenge abandoned bodies, but may not take claimed bodies.
Xiphactinus may not group with other species.
Xiphactinus tolerate albino and melanistic individuals.

## Genetics
Skins that are considered mutations for Xiphactinus include:
Albino, Melanistic
Players must roll in #🎲・genetics-rolls to use these skins in game. This is one roll per species, per character. Only when the character dies can you roll again if using the same character name.

## Profile Specific Unique Feature
Every Sub/Adult Xiphactinus has a hunting role for life: ripper, blocker or biter. Each role has its own attack. See ✦ Hunting Roles.
Xiphactinus live only in warm fresh and brackish water: the channels between the islands and the lakes connected to them. They never enter the open ocean, and never go on land.
Xiphactinus are active by day and by night, and most active at dawn and dusk.

## Group Limits (In-Game Group)
Up to 6 Sub/Adults, of any relation
Unlimited Adolescents

This group is known as a battery.

Up to 12 young below Sub-Adult, of any relation
No Sub/Adults

This group is known as a juvenile school.
- Offspring grow up without their parents, in juvenile schools in Coastland Swamp. They travel with a battery from Adolescent on at the earliest. See Growing Up.
- For the courtship, every Xiphactinus leaves its in-game group. Batteries form anew once the eggs have hatched. See ✦ Courtship.
- Two small batteries may merge, as long as they stay at 6 Sub/Adults or below. A member who no longer wishes to follow the battery may leave at any time, except during a hunt or a flight.
- Members of a battery stay within 60 m of each other, the reach of Agility Mentality, so that all of them share its effect. Only a scattering battery breaks this.

## Engagement Limits (Defence & Hunts)
Predator Reaction: Flighty
Up to 6 Sub/Adults may take part in a hunt or a defence.
Xiphactinus may hunt up to Medium tier in any party size.
With at least 4 in a hunting party, they may hunt up to Large tier, Sarcosuchus included.
Xiphactinus never hunt adult Apex or Giant creatures, with one exception: a lone Deinosuchus, with at least 6 in the hunting party.
Every member hunts in its role. There is no rule on how many members take each role.
Only one battery may hunt a group at a time. The battery that opens first has the hunt. Any other battery pulls back, and does not join.
Xiphactinus may strike, pull away and strike again. The hunt continues as long as the battery keeps the target in sight (3.3.1, 4.8.3).
A battery has up to 3 attempts per group. After the third, it leaves that group alone for 15 minutes. See ✦ Breaking Off.
Xiphactinus flee only from adult Tylosaurus. See ✦ Predator Reaction.
Xiphactinus do not hunt, challenge or defend against Spinosaurus. See ✦ Spinosaurus.
Adolescent Xiphactinus do not join hunts. They may hunt fish, critters and hatchlings on their own.
**Abilities in an engagement:**
- **Flash** opens every hunt, usually from the lead. After the opening, every member may use it.
- **Agility Mentality** and **Sanguine Hunger** may be used freely in hunts, except that blockers do not use Sanguine Hunger. Xiphactinus do not group with other species, so no other species shares the buff.
- **Tearing Bite** is used only by rippers. Blockers and biters never use it, even when they lead.
- **Lunge** deals no damage. It is a burst of speed, used to dart in and out again, and to rise at prey near the surface from below.

## Resource Challenges
Batteries hold no territories. They do not challenge other species, do not accept territory challenges, and simply move on.
Xiphactinus give up a body at once to an adult Tylosaurus. They do not take bodies claimed by others, and do not fight other batteries over bodies.
Males that want the same nesting hollow in Coastland Swamp settle it with a flank fight. This is the only resource Xiphactinus fight over. See ✦ Flank Fights.

## Preferred Prey
Xiphactinus prefer Eurhinosaurus. They are fast and loud, and the hardest test of a battery's skill.
Kaiwhekea cut off from their bale are the next choice, as are Leedsichthys pups and the young of any species.
Between hunts they live on fish. Their fast metabolism keeps them hungry, and they eat often.
Up to 4 Eurhinosaurus may group with a Kaiwhekea bale, and a flotilla is a Leedsichthys with its Eurhinosaurus. Within such a group, a battery may switch its target between Kaiwhekea and Eurhinosaurus during a hunt (4.5). It never targets the adult Leedsichthys.
Xiphactinus may hunt pterosaurs up to Medium tier that sit or swim on the water surface. Pterosaurs in flight are not attacked.
Swimming land animals within the battery's tier may be hunted while they swim, but are never preferred.
Xiphactinus never leave the water, and do not attack creatures on land. A target that leaves the water is let go.

## Nesting Limits
🌧️ Rainy Season: 4 🥚 per 3 hours.
☀️ Dry Season: 2 🥚 per 3 hours.
Xiphactinus court and nest in Coastland Swamp, in mud holes under roots or under thick growth, where the young grow up hidden on their own. See ✦ Nesting.
Xiphactinus form no lasting pairs, do not form same-sex pairs, and do not adopt. A male may mate with several females.

## Physical Dimorphism
This profile does not have high and low T individuals.
♀️ Females are larger and heavier, with broad flanks. They wear dark main colours: black, grey, deep brown or dark red.

%% banner Growing Up
### ✦ Offspring Behavior (Nested)
Xiphactinus young, known as fry, hatch in mud holes under the roots and growth of Coastland Swamp. They are small, silver and quick from the first moment.
Their parents are already gone, and they are on their own.
Fry gather in juvenile schools of up to 12, with their clutchmates and the fry of other nests. Like young tarpon in mangrove pools, which grow up without any parental care, they live in shallow, still and oxygen-poor water, where few large predators follow, and they rise often to gulp air.
Until Adolescent, the young stay in Coastland Swamp, and keep hidden among the roots and the growth.
The school hunts small fish and critters among the roots, and moves as one. At the sight of a large shadow it falls still among the roots.
Fry up to Juvenile are light enough for Austroraptor to seize. No adult guards them. The school hides deep among the roots whenever a shore hunter wades into the swamp.
Juveniles chase small fish in pairs, one driving and one cutting off. This is how they learn the roles of the battery.
Fry also practise the boil on small fish: they circle a fish school, dart in and dart back.
Play never turns into biting each other. A juvenile that bites another is pushed away from the school by the others.
At Adolescent, young Xiphactinus may leave Coastland Swamp, together with their school.
- They travel on as a school until Sub-Adult, or join a battery. In a battery they have no hunting role yet, and swim at its edge.
- They follow the battery's hunts from a distance of at least 10 body lengths, and do not join them. They watch closely the member whose role they want to take on.
- They may hunt fish, critters and hatchlings on their own.
At Sub-Adult, young Xiphactinus are full members of their battery. They choose their hunting role, take turns as lookout, and may court.

### ✦ Orphaned Behavior (Wild Spawn)
Every young Xiphactinus grows up without its parents, so a wild-spawned one lives much like the others.
It makes its way to Coastland Swamp, hides in the shallowest, most overgrown water it can find, and joins any juvenile school it comes across. Schools accept newcomers without hesitation, as long as they stay at 12 or below.
Young that never find a school grow up as loners. They are skittish, flee early and are slow to trust a battery. Many hunt alone for the rest of their lives. See ✦ Solitary Behavior.
%% banner General Behavior
Xiphactinus are the fastest swimmers in the sea, and restless. They patrol the channels in loose formation, always watching for the flash of prey.
Like tarpon, they roll at the surface to gulp air. A battery often rolls together, a silver flash across the water. In the warm, still swamps they roll more often.
Xiphactinus are famous for their leaps. They leap to shake off parasites, to show off, to dodge in the frenzy, and to strike at prey on the surface.
Xiphactinus feel at home only in fresh and brackish water, and they need it warm. The open oceans are too salty and too cool for them, and they never stay there.
Tarpon can die in a single cold snap, and Xiphactinus keep away from cool water in the same way. In the Dry Season, when the channels are warmest, batteries are at their most active.
Like tarpon, which swim far up rivers, Xiphactinus may follow the water into the lakes connected to the channels.
Xiphactinus never go on land. Out of the water they are extremely uneasy.
Xiphactinus are silent, until it comes to a fight or a kill. Then they bark, drum and snap like piranhas.

### ✦ Downtime Behavior
Xiphactinus are cathemeral. They hunt and rest in turns, by day and by night, and are most active in the half light of dawn and dusk.
At rest, the battery lies close together in open water. The Sub/Adults rest in the centre, and the young at the edge. Large piranhas hold the safe centre of their shoal in the same way.
The lead chooses where the battery rests: a sheltered bend in open water, away from the banks. A bend where the battery was attacked is not used again in the same session.
In a battery of 3 or more, one member always keeps watch for Tylosaurus while the others rest. The members take turns, and pass the watch on with a gentle nudge.
Rest time is for rolling, swimming in sync with a friend, play leaps and chasing games. Rippers often chase blockers in play, and blockers practise cutting them off.

### ✦ Range and Seasons
Batteries roam all five channel POIs and the lakes connected to them, following the fish and the groups that pass through on the Leedsichthys route. They do not hold territories, and do not return to the same bend every day.
Where the channels open into the oceans, batteries wait for bales and shoals coming back in. Tarpon gather at passes between the islands in the same way. Batteries never follow their prey out into the ocean. See ✦ The Gates.
In the Rainy Season, the bales spend longer in the channels, where the rivers bring fish. It is the rich season for Xiphactinus, and the season of the largest frenzies.
In the Dry Season, the bales spend longer in the cooler oceans. Batteries live more on fish and on the young, and wait at the gates.

%% banner Social Interaction
Xiphactinus are social hunters. A battery is a tight, strictly ordered group, and its members often stay together for years, even when unrelated.

### ✦ General Communication
Xiphactinus are silent most of the time. Like piranhas, they find their voice in a fight.
- **Bark:** a (:threaten_call:) face to face. It is a warning, and the start of every flank fight and every frenzy.
- **Drum:** fast, repeated (:threaten_call:) while circling. Batteries drum in the boil, and females drum in the frenzy.
- **Snap:** a Bite into open water, without landing it. It warns a battery mate off, or rejects a suitor.
- **Gathering:** A (:broadcast_call:) calls the battery together. In a hunt, the lead's (:broadcast_call:) ends the attempt. In the frenzy, the females call again and again with (:broadcast_call:) as they circle the males.
- **Greeting:** When two Xiphactinus meet, they swim up alongside each other, flank to flank, and roll at the surface together. When initiated, the greeting must be returned. Battery mates greet after time apart, and members of different batteries greet before they pass close.
- **Contentment:** swimming in sync, rolling together, and soft (:friendly_call:).
- **Unease:** restless circling at the edge of the battery, and looking around above the surface (:question_emote:).
- **Alarm:** a (:danger_call:), and the battery scatters.

### ✦ Battery Structure
Batteries have no fixed ranks. Within a battery, it soon shows who usually goes ahead: who chooses where the battery hunts and rests, when it moves on, and who opens and ends the hunts. The others follow this member, the lead.
The lead has a hunting role like every other member, and hunts in it once the hunt is open.
The lead may change. When another member goes ahead more and more often, and the battery follows it, the battery has a new lead. The lead is never fought for.
When the lead is absent, the battery follows whoever goes ahead.
At rest and at a kill, one member keeps watch as the lookout.
Adolescents travel at the edge of the battery, have no hunting role yet, and stay out of its hunts.

### ✦ Hunting Roles
Every Sub/Adult Xiphactinus has a hunting role: ripper, blocker or biter. Like yellow saddle goatfish, which hunt as chasers and blockers, each keeps its role for life. Players choose the role at Sub-Adult.
Every role has its own task and its own attack. There is no rule on how many members of a battery take each role.
- **Rippers** open the wound. They chase the target at full speed, tear at it in passing with Tearing Bite, and dart away at once with a Lunge. They keep the target bleeding, but do not try to kill it. Rippers are the only ones who use Tearing Bite. They usually wear the Streamlined hide.
- **Blockers** swim between the target and its group, cut off its way back, and hold the ring of the boil closed. When the target turns back towards its group, they blind it with Flash. Blockers bite only with Bite, and do not use Sanguine Hunger, because they take the blows of the defenders. They usually wear the Rough hide.
- **Biters** kill. In the boil they strike with Chomp and Bite, and use Sanguine Hunger. They strike in the 10 seconds after a Chomp, while the target's armour is weakened.

### ✦ Battery Behavior
Battery members often stay together for years. Friends swim in sync, and greet each other first after time apart.
Leaping contests, races and chasing games are common at rest. Play never includes biting.

### ✦ Other Batteries
Two batteries that meet greet each other, lead to lead, and pass. They never attack one another.
Only one battery may hunt a group at a time. At the gates, batteries take turns.
Batteries may exchange members, and small batteries may merge, within the group limit. In a merged battery, it soon shows whom it follows.
All batteries share Coastland Swamp and the frenzy peacefully.

### ✦ Solitary Behavior
Solitary Xiphactinus are old individuals, orphans who never found a battery, and young ones between batteries. Like the large barracuda that hunt alone, they are quiet and patient.
Alone, a Xiphactinus turns sharper and accelerates faster (Lone Survivor), but it has no one to close a ring. It hunts by ambush rather than by chase, and prefers the young and the weak.
Solitary Xiphactinus hunt up to Medium tier.
They keep their distance from Kaiwhekea bales.
A solitary Xiphactinus approaches a battery with the greeting. The lead accepts it by returning the greeting. A battery that does not want it snaps at it, and it moves on.

### ✦ Albino & Melanistic Behavior
Both albino and melanistic Xiphactinus are accepted by their batteries.
Albino Xiphactinus stand out in the water, and their prey sees them early. They are sensitive to bright light, rest in the deepest shade, and often take the blocker role. An albino female cannot wear dark colours, and is recognised by her build and her behaviour in the frenzy.
Melanistic Xiphactinus are hard to see in the half light, and are often trusted with the lookout. A melanistic male cannot wear light colours, and is recognised by his place in the frenzy.
%% banner Aggressive Interaction
In order to express frustration towards another Xiphactinus, a Xiphactinus shakes its head (:shake_emote:), then snaps beside the other; it may NOT land this bite.
If this is ignored, a flank bump follows: the Xiphactinus swims hard against the other's flank, with its mouth closed.
These motions are immediately respected by the offending Xiphactinus, who moves away at least 1 body length.
Xiphactinus rarely fight their own kind outside the frenzy. Batteries never fight each other, not over prey, and not over bodies.
A foreign Xiphactinus that joins a battery's hunt uninvited is driven off with snaps and flank bumps.

### ✦ Flank Fights
Flank fights are only fought between males over a nesting hollow in Coastland Swamp. Every flank fight follows the same procedure.
1. **Bark:** The two meet face to face, and each barks once.
2. **Drum:** They circle each other head to tail, drumming. Either may back down here by swimming away.
3. **Flank bites:** They use only Bite. Each makes one bite at the other's flank, then they swim past each other, turn and meet again. Chomp, Tearing Bite, Flash and Sanguine Hunger are not used.
4. **The end:** The fight ends when one drops below 50% health, or gives up with a (:heart_call:). The loser gives up the hollow and finds another.
Flank fights rarely end in death.

### ✦ Correcting Over-Aggression
Over-aggression is rare, and not tolerated.
1. A (:shake_emote:) and a snap.
2. If it is ignored, the nearest members give flank bumps, and the battery closes in around the aggressor.
3. If it continues, the lead gives firm nips.
4. If the behavior continues despite this, the battery drives the aggressor out.
Over-aggression is never defended by battery mates, regardless of relations.
A female who keeps biting a male after he is out of the frenzy counts as over-aggressive.
%% banner Cross-Species Interaction
Xiphactinus treat other species as prey, competitors or danger, and very little in between. Their own channels are the only world they know.

### ✦ Eurhinosaurus
Eurhinosaurus are rivals in speed and the favourite prey. Their loud shoals are easy to find, but hard to surprise, because one of them is always awake.
Xiphactinus sprint faster, but Eurhinosaurus hold the sprint for longer. A battery that is spotted early loses the chase.
When a shoal bursts apart, the rippers stay on the burst and tear at it, and the blockers pick up the one that bursts their way.
Eurhinosaurus like to show off by swimming past hunters. A battery may answer with a hunt.

### ✦ Kaiwhekea
When bales pass through the channels, batteries shadow them from a distance, and wait for a calf or a straggler to drift away.
A Kaiwhekea that reaches its bale within 30 seconds, coming within 2 body lengths of it, is safe for that attempt.
Batteries never strike at a protective ring, or at a bale that has turned to face them.
Even a full battery loses against two Kaiwhekea that stand together. A pair, too, must be split before it can be hunted.
A Kaiwhekea on a rock, a beach or a mudbank is out of reach, and the battery moves on.

### ✦ Leedsichthys
Xiphactinus never strike an adult Leedsichthys, even one that defends its pups. If it turns on them, the battery scatters and tries again from another side.
Leedsichthys pups are a prized catch.
Kaiwhekea and Eurhinosaurus like to hide behind the giant, so the battery splits up and comes from two sides.

### ✦ Tropeognathus & Other Pterosaurs
Tropeognathus follow flotillas from the air. When one lands on the water at a gathering of fish in the channels, it is fair game. It cannot fight back in the water, and its only escape is to take off.
Batteries watch fishing pterosaurs closely. Any that land on the water are a target.

### ✦ Tylosaurus
Batteries fear Tylosaurus the way great white sharks fear orcas. Great whites leave their hunting grounds for months once orcas have passed through.
A battery scatters at the first sight of an adult Tylosaurus, leaves the POI, and does not return for at least 15 minutes.

### ✦ Deinosuchus & Sarcosuchus
Crocodylians hunt Xiphactinus with relish. They lie still at the surface, clamp a passing fish and carry it out of the water, where it is helpless.
Xiphactinus do not flee from them. A battery may hunt them by tier: a Sarcosuchus with 4 or more, a lone Deinosuchus with 6 or more.

### ✦ Spinosaurus
Xiphactinus and Spinosaurus ignore each other completely. Neither hunts, challenges or defends against the other.
Batteries may hunt within a Spinosaurus territory.

### ✦ Sachicasaurus & Livyatan
Livyatan live in the oceans, which batteries never enter. {+}
Sachicasaurus mostly live in the oceans too. Batteries only meet them in the secondary Sachicasaurus territories of Volcano Islands and Twisted Forest, where a Sachicasaurus may hunt them by night. Xiphactinus do not flee from them, and never hunt an adult. {+}

### ✦ Austroraptor, Halszkaraptor & Concavenator
Shore hunters are a danger to the fry in Coastland Swamp, and Austroraptor can seize fry up to Juvenile. No adult guards the fry, so the schools hide deep among the roots when they wade in.
Away from Coastland Swamp, they are competitors for Leedsichthys pups. Batteries avoid conflict with them, and give up a pup rather than fight over it.

### ✦ Predator Reaction
Xiphactinus flee only from adult Tylosaurus. At the first sight of one, the lookout gives a (:danger_call:).
The battery scatters, every member in a different direction at full speed, like a fish school bursting apart. Once out of sight of the Tylosaurus, the members regroup outside the POI with a (:broadcast_call:).
They flee along the channel at full speed, then out of the POI for at least 15 minutes.
A Xiphactinus clamped by a Tylosaurus is lost. The battery does not attack the Tylosaurus, and flees. The seized one may fight back, and flees at once if it is released.
A lone Xiphactinus relies on its speed and its turns. A pair flees together, and once both have turned to face a threat, they stay together until it is over.
The mother and the father may defend a nest with eggs against any hunter. They stay within 1 body length of the nest, and do not chase. Once the eggs have hatched, the parents are gone, and no one defends the fry.
%% banner Hunting Strategy
From the outside, a Xiphactinus hunt looks like pure chaos: six of the fastest fish in the sea, everywhere at once. From the inside, every member knows its place, its role and its moment.
Every hunt on a Sub/Adult follows the same course:
1. **Finding:** The battery spreads out along a channel, within reach of Agility Mentality, and watches for prey near the surface. When it finds a group, it shadows it in silence from a distance, and waits for a chance: a calf that drifts, a straggler, a member that turns away to fish.
2. **The opening:** A Flash marks the target, usually from the lead. Any member may open, and the others follow. No member strikes before the opening. If no Flash is ready, the hunt opens with the first bite instead.
3. **The wound:** The rippers chase the target at full speed, tear at it in passing and dart away again. A bleeding target cannot heal, and while it swims fast, its bleeding closes only slowly.
4. **Cutting off:** At the same time, the blockers swim between the target and its group, and turn it back towards the rippers. A Kaiwhekea has 30 seconds to reach its bale.
5. **The boil:** Once the target is cut off, the battery closes around it, and the biters go to work.
6. **Surface prey:** Prey near the surface is met from below: a Lunge upwards, and a bite.

### ✦ The Boil
Sharks circle their prey before they strike. Piranhas tear at it from every side until the water seems to boil. The boil is both, held together by strict order.
- **The ring:** The battery circles the target at full speed, in a tight ring. Every member has its own place in the ring, beside the same neighbours every time. The blockers keep the ring closed on the side of the target's group.
- **Bite and back:** A member darts in from its place, bites once, and returns to its place at once. No member stays on the target. Like piranhas, which bite out a piece and are gone before the next one comes, the battery strikes from every side, one after another.
- **The drum:** Once the ring has closed, the battery drums: fast, repeated (:threaten_call:). Until then it is silent.
- **The turn:** At a sudden turn of the lead, the whole ring reverses its direction at once, like a fish school turning as one.
- **Who bites:** The biters strike with Chomp and Bite, hardest in the 10 seconds after a Chomp. The rippers keep the target bleeding with Tearing Bite. The blockers hold the ring, and bite only with Bite. The lead strikes in its own role.
- **Blinding:** Flash punishes the sharp turns that Kaiwhekea and Eurhinosaurus use to break out.
- **The first bites** go for the flippers and the tail, the way piranhas bite at fins, to slow the target down.
- **Breaking the ring:** At the lead's (:broadcast_call:), the ring bursts outwards, every member in a different direction, and regroups out of reach.

### ✦ Breaking Off
An attempt ends when:
- the target reaches its group and is safe there;
- its group forms a ring or turns to face the battery;
- the target leaves the water;
- or the lead gives a (:broadcast_call:).
After a failed attempt, the battery pulls back at least 10 body lengths before it tries again.
A battery has up to 3 attempts per group. After the third, it leaves that group alone for 15 minutes, like the timer of rule 4.5.1.
A battery does not wait below a rock or at a beach for prey that has left the water. It moves on.

### ✦ Feeding
The battery feeds together, with no order at the kill.
One member keeps watch, and eats once another takes over.
Xiphactinus leave a body at once when an adult Tylosaurus comes near.

### ✦ Pterosaurs
Pterosaurs up to Medium tier that sit or swim on the water are met from below, with a Lunge upwards and a bite, like giant trevally leaping at young terns.
Tropeognathus that follow a flotilla into the channels and land at a gathering of fish are the most common catch.

### ✦ The Gates
Batteries wait at the mouths of the channels for bales and shoals coming back from the oceans.
They strike only once their prey is back inside the channels.

%% banner Courtship & Parenthood
Xiphactinus form no lasting pairs. A pair forms in the frenzy, and lasts only until its eggs have hatched.
A male may be chosen by several females in the same frenzy, and nest with each of them.
Xiphactinus do not form same-sex pairs, and do not adopt.

### ✦ Courtship
Courtship is the most brutal sight in the channels. To anyone who watches, it looks like a massacre. To the Xiphactinus, it is a ritual with strict rules: the females test the speed and the agility of the males, and only those who endure may nest.
Each season, all Xiphactinus gather in Coastland Swamp. Batteries from all channels come together there, and every Xiphactinus leaves its in-game group for the ritual. This ritual is known as the frenzy.
1. **The gathering:** Xiphactinus ready to court swim to the courting pool, a stretch of open water in Coastland Swamp. No one hunts there, and no flank fights are fought. Xiphactinus who do not court may keep watch at the edge of the pool.
2. **The rush:** The water heats up. The females swim wildly around the males, calling again and again with (:broadcast_call:). The males gather in the middle of the pool. They leap with a Lunge and roll at the surface, flashing their bright bodies.
3. **Last chance:** A male who does not want to nest must leave the middle of the pool now, quickly, before the chase begins.
4. **The bark:** When a female barks, and at least one other female answers, the chase begins. All females turn on the males at once, drumming.
5. **The chase:** The females hunt the males, and test their speed and their agility. They bite whichever males they choose. A male they do not want gets chased harder.
  - Females use only Bite. No Chomp, Tearing Bite, Flash or Sanguine Hunger. After every bite, a female swims past and turns before she bites again.
  - Males may not bite back, and may not use Flash. They may only dodge: turn, roll, dive and leap.
  - A male may not hide among roots, growth or rocks, and may not leave the POI. A male who hides or leaves is out, and his courtship is over for this season.
  - A male who gives a forfeit call (:friendly_call:) is out. He leaves the pool at once, and keeps his distance. No female may bite him again.
  - Males may die in the frenzy. Their bodies are never claimed, and the ritual goes on.
6. **The end:** The females stop as soon as only half of the males who entered the chase are left, rounded up (of 5 males, 3 stay).
  - A single male must endure the chase for 60 seconds.
  - The males still in the pool at the end have passed.
7. **The choice:** Each female chooses one of the males who passed. Most choose the male who was hardest to hunt. She swims to him, and both circle each other head to tail for one full lap, the way piranha pairs swim in circles before they spawn. A male may be chosen by several females.
8. **The nest:** Each new pair nests in Coastland Swamp.
Males who drop out do not nest this season.
If an adult Tylosaurus comes near, the frenzy ends at once, and everyone scatters.
The bites of the frenzy leave scars. Scars are a mark of honour, and females remember the males who passed.

### ✦ Nesting
Xiphactinus nest in Coastland Swamp, in mud holes under the roots or under thick growth.
Like a piranha, the male chooses the nesting hollow. He clears it, and shows it to his female. A male chosen by several females clears a hollow for each of them.
The female inspects the hollow. She accepts it by laying her eggs, or rejects it with a snap, and he must find another.
Nests lie at least 1 body length apart.
Males that want the same hollow settle it with a flank fight. See ✦ Flank Fights.
The parents guard the eggs until they hatch, and take turns to fish. A male with several nests moves between them.

### ✦ Parenthood
Xiphactinus care for their eggs, but not for their young.
Once all eggs of a nest have hatched, the parents leave and do not come back.
The adults gather in batteries again, and go back to hunting the channels.
The fry stay behind in Coastland Swamp, form juvenile schools of up to 12, and hide there until Adolescent.

### ✦ Offspring Maturity
At Adolescent, young Xiphactinus may leave Coastland Swamp with their school, or join a battery and swim at its edge.
At Sub-Adult, they are full members of their battery, choose their hunting role and may court.

### ✦ Adoption
Xiphactinus do not adopt.
Juvenile schools accept any young Xiphactinus without hesitation, as long as they stay at 12 or below.
%% banner Elderly
Old Xiphactinus slow down and can no longer keep up with their battery. An old lead goes ahead less and less, and the battery soon follows another. Many old members take the lookout more often.
Many leave to hunt alone in quiet mangrove backwaters, as Lone Survivors.
Old males often still enter the frenzy. Most drop out early, and some never court again.
Some die trying to swallow prey too large for them, the same fate that may have befallen the famous Gillicus fossil. Others fall to the crocodylians at the banks, because they are no longer quick enough.

### ✦ Death
When a member dies, the battery circles once over the place and rolls together at the surface. Then the battery moves on.
If the lead has died, it soon shows whom the battery follows next.

## Profile - Quick View
Xiphactinus are Medium Cathemeral Aquatic Carnivores, most active at dawn and dusk.
Xiphactinus live in batteries of up to 6 Sub/Adults and unlimited Adolescents, led by the member the others follow, with no fixed ranks. Young grow up in juvenile schools of up to 12.
From the outside, Xiphactinus are pure chaos. From the inside, they are pure control: every member hunts as a ripper, a blocker or a biter, each with its own attack.
Xiphactinus live only in fresh and brackish water: the channels between the islands and the lakes connected to them. They never enter the open ocean, and never go on land.
Xiphactinus may hunt up to Medium tier in any party size, up to Large tier with 4 or more, and never adult Apex or Giant creatures. The one exception is a lone Deinosuchus, hunted by 6 or more.
Every hunt opens with a Flash. The rippers open the wound, the blockers cut off the way back, and the battery closes the boil: a drumming ring at full speed, from which every member darts in, bites once and returns to its place.
Xiphactinus flee only from adult Tylosaurus. A Xiphactinus seized by one is lost. Spinosaurus and Xiphactinus ignore each other completely.
All Xiphactinus court in Coastland Swamp, in the frenzy: the females chase the males until only half of them are left, and choose among those who endured. Colour dimorphism is mandatory: females dark, males light.
The parents guard the eggs, and leave once they have hatched. The young grow up alone in Coastland Swamp until Adolescent.
