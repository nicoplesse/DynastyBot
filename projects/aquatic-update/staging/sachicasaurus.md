# Sachicasaurus
"Sachi"
Sachicasaurus are massive, short-necked pliosaurs that rule the kelp forests, reefs and shallows of Riparia by night. Built not slender but agile, they hold half an ocean as their territory, hum it awake at every dawn, wait for bales and flotillas to settle in the shallows, and carry the story of their lives in scars that never heal.

Written by Dynasty Realism

Profile inspired by jaguars, saltwater crocodiles, great white sharks, Tyrannotitan, pliosaur paleobiology and creative liberty.

%% banner Overview
**Apex Nocturnal Aquatic Carnivore**
Sachicasaurus may take part in all activities other than hunting during the day. Females may travel by day, and courtship may take place by day.
Fishing does not count as hunting. Sachicasaurus may fish by day and by night.
Sachicasaurus form no lasting bonds. A male mates with only one female at a time. After courtship, the pair group until the dawn after their first full night together, and may hunt together, then each goes their own way.
Sachicasaurus males are territorial. They claim one half of an ocean POI as their preferred territory, or, when they find none, a secondary territory in the channels. See ✦ Territories.
A territorial male allows one solitary female, or one drift of females, within his territory, and permits them and any offspring he fathers to share his kills.
Males without a territory are known as wanderers. Wanderers do not hunt. They only fish. See ✦ Wanderers.
Sachicasaurus females are nomadic. They raise their young in the nursery at Tallbrush Coasts, where males never come, and defend their own nest there.
Sachicasaurus males have an aggressive nature towards ungrouped Sachicasaurus males, towards unguarded Adolescents that are not their own, and towards Tylosaurus, and a passive nature towards females outside courtship.
Sachicasaurus females are tolerant towards other species, and passive towards males outside courtship.
Sachicasaurus live in the kelp forests, reefs, shallows and beaches of the oceans. They avoid deep, open water, except for the high rock ledges of the Abyssal Depths. Of the saltwater channels between the islands, they only enter Volcano Islands and Twisted Forest, where the secondary territories lie.
Sachicasaurus are not scavengers. They hunt, and they fish.
Sachicasaurus tolerate albino and melanistic individuals.

## Profile Specific Unique Feature
Sachicasaurus scars never heal, and other Sachicasaurus read them. A heavily scarred female has mated many times, a heavily scarred male has fought many times. See ✦ Scars.

## Group Limits (In-Game Group)
Up to 2 related Sub/Adults of the same sex.
Male pairs (brothers, half-brothers, or father and son) are known as a brace.
Female pairs (sisters, half-sisters, or mother and daughter) are known as a drift, and can be joined by unlimited offspring.
A territorial male, alone or in a brace, may group with one of his sons from the middle of the Adolescent stage, after the handover. See ✦ The Handover.
Unlimited Offspring

Adults of the opposite sex only group for the pair night after a successful courtship. They never group when nesting. See ✦ The Pair Night.
ALL Sachicasaurus that group together must log their relations in #🩸・bloodline-logs before they can group. A mated pair does not need to be related.

## Engagement Limits (Defence & Hunts)
Up to 2 Sachicasaurus may take part in a hunt or a territory challenge.
Sachicasaurus may hunt up to Apex tier.
Wanderers do not hunt. Only a male who holds a territory, preferred or secondary, may hunt.
Any 2 Sub/Adult Sachicasaurus may work together to hunt an adult Leedsichthys, related or not, of either sex. They do not group in game to do so, and share the kill. See ✦ The Leedsichthys Call.
Sachicasaurus flee from adult Livyatan. As a Giant, an adult Livyatan cannot be hunted by Sachicasaurus. See ✦ Livyatan.
Sachicasaurus may hunt adult Deinosuchus and Spinosaurus, alone or as a pair, but are never required to.
Adolescent Sachicasaurus do not assist with hunts, except a son who lives with his father. See ✦ The Handover.

### ✦ Defence Against Other Species
Up to 2 Sachicasaurus may work together in defence against being hunted. In a brace or drift, their primary defenders will always be each other.
A brace may stand its ground against a Livyatan in defence, but loses against a grown one. In the kelp, in the shallows and on the high rock ledges of the Abyssal Depths, Sachicasaurus are safe from it.
Females always defend their grouped offspring of Adolescent and below.
A father who holds a territory defends his grouped son. A father who has lost his territory may not defend him until he holds a territory again.

### ✦ Territory Challenges Against Other Species
Males claim and defend their territory at night. During the day they may patrol it in small amounts, and defend it if challenged (1.4.1).
Males challenge Tylosaurus out of their territory. This begins with a broadcast (:broadcast_call:), and then three aggressive (:threaten_call:) calls (6.7).
At Palm Islands, Volcano Islands and Twisted Forest, males also challenge Deinosuchus males and Spinosaurus, and respond to their challenges.
Males do not challenge Kaiwhekea bales, Eurhinosaurus shoals or Leedsichthys. These pass through freely by day, and become prey by night.
Males do not challenge Livyatan.
If a male or brace loses a territory challenge, any female or drift they allow in their territory either follows, or leaves the POI without them.

## Preferred Prey
Sachicasaurus prefer Kaiwhekea, especially individuals resting at the edge of a sleeping bale. They are the only hunters strong enough to seize a grown Kaiwhekea.
Eurhinosaurus, Leedsichthys pups and the young of any species are the next choices.
Between hunts, Sachicasaurus fish.
Sachicasaurus like to hunt Deinosuchus and Spinosaurus at Palm Islands, Volcano Islands and Twisted Forest, but may also leave them be.
Adult Leedsichthys that pass through a territory at night are prey for 2 Sachicasaurus hunting together.
In the secondary territories, Sachicasaurus may hunt Xiphactinus.
Tylosaurus that refuse to leave a territory may be hunted.
At night, Sachicasaurus may hunt terrestrial species that swim, or stand within one body length of the water. Adult Apex terrestrial carnivores are only hunted when they are swimming in water. See ✦ Beaching.
Sachicasaurus may third party engagements that enter the water, or come within 1 body length of the water.

### ✦ Offspring Behavior (Nested)
Sachicasaurus hatchlings, known as nippers, hatch in the nursery at Tallbrush Coasts. They stay hidden in the shallows, among rocks and weed.
Their mother guards them at night and rests close by during the day. She stays with them until they reach Adolescent.
Nippers learn to fish by watching their mother. They copy how she lies still above the weed, and how she snaps when a fish comes close.
Juveniles follow their mother on short night trips through the nursery, and learn to lie still and wait.
At Adolescent, their mother leads them out of the nursery. From now on, they are only safe at her side. See ✦ The Leaving.
From the middle of the Adolescent stage, a son may go to his father. See ✦ The Handover.
At Sub-Adult, the young leave their mother. Young males become wanderers, often together with a brother, until they find a territory. Young females may stay with their mother as a drift.

### ✦ Orphaned Behavior (Wild Spawn)
Orphaned nippers make their way to the nursery if they can, and hide in its shallows.
They only come out at night to fish, and grow up quiet, cautious and solitary.
An orphan who leaves the nursery is unguarded. Until Sub-Adult, it keeps out of the territories, or crosses them by day, when their owners do not hunt.
%% banner General Behavior
Sachicasaurus are creatures of the night. By day they rest in kelp forests, reef caves and under cliffs, and barely move.
As dusk falls they leave their shelter and patrol the kelp, the reefs and the shallows, where bales and flotillas come to rest. They sense the nearest fish from far away, and fish between hunts.
Sachicasaurus are not slender, but they are agile. Their four flippers let them fly through the water, turn sharply and roll. Their huge head and short neck give them the strongest grip of any aquatic hunter.
Sachicasaurus need more air than most aquatics, and suffer badly without it. They keep to coastal waters where the surface is never far away, and avoid the deep.
They are at ease on beaches at night, and haul out on the sandbanks of Palm Islands.
They are quiet hunters. The night belongs to silence, and the only song of a Sachicasaurus is the hum at dawn.

### ✦ The Dawn Hum
At every dawn, when the hunt is over, each territorial male hums. The hum tells rivals that the territory is taken, and tells females that a male lives here.
A male never hums before the night. His voice would warn the prey he is about to hunt.
**The hum:** a (:broadcast_call:), cut short with a Bite, then a second (:broadcast_call:).
**The answer:** A female who is interested in him answers with one (:broadcast_call:), and then comes to visit him. Since Sachicasaurus may do everything but hunt by day, she may travel to him by day. See ✦ Courtship.
A female who is not interested does not answer.
Males in a secondary territory hum as well, to keep rivals out. Females do not answer them.
**The silence:** A territory where no hum is heard at dawn is free. Its owner has died, or gone. A wanderer may claim it with his own hum at the next dawn. An owner who returns must win it back in a territory challenge.

### ✦ Body Language
At night, Sachicasaurus speak with their bodies, not their voices. A call carries far through the water and would warn the prey. Every signal is slow and quiet, and never a display that startles prey.
- **The periscope:** lying motionless at the surface, only the top of the head showing. It means "I am hunting, keep away and keep still." A brother, a sister or a son who sees it stops and waits.
- **The wide arc:** passing another Sachicasaurus slowly, in a wide arc, with the head turned away. It means "I mean no harm." Females crossing a territory, mothers leading their young, and wanderers passing through use it.
- **Flank to flank:** a brace, a drift or a mated pair rests side by side, flanks touching. It shows that they belong together. It is not the flank display of courtship, which is given at the surface.
- **The nudge:** a mother nudges a nipper with her snout to make it follow her or hide. A father does the same with his son.
- **Turning away:** a Sachicasaurus that turns its back and swims off slowly ends a meeting.
At night, calls are only given in a territory challenge, in the courtship duel, for the Leedsichthys call, and in danger.

### ✦ Territories
A male's territory is one half of an ocean POI, like the segmented territories of Spinosaurus inland.
**Preferred territories:** There are ten, two in each ocean:
- Western and Eastern Kelp Vale
- Northern and Southern Coastal Bluffs
- Northern and Southern Palm Islands
- Northern and Southern Stonebed Shoal
- Northern and Southern Abyssal Depths
**Secondary territories:** There are five, in the saltwater channels, cut like the segments of Spinosaurus:
- Northern, Southern and Eastern Volcano Islands
- Western and Eastern Twisted Forest
The territories are marked on the profile map.
Land within the same POI is not part of the territory. Terrestrial species there are not affected.
**The Abyssal Depths:** The deep water is Livyatan's home, and too far from the surface for a hunter that needs air. A male who claims one of its halves keeps to the high rock ledges that rise between the deeps.
In the Abyssal Depths, Sachicasaurus place themselves on one of these ledges, like a jaguar lying on a branch above a river. The surface is close, and from there they watch the deep water below for prey, and for the Livyatan. Livyatan do not follow them onto the ledges.

### ✦ Secondary Territories
Secondary territories are unpopular. Kaiwhekea rarely rest in the narrow channels, and the channels are too narrow to hunt land creatures well.
No courtship takes place in a secondary territory, and females rarely come there.
A male only claims a secondary territory when he finds no free preferred one. From there, he may challenge the owner of a preferred territory at any time.
The secondary territories overlap the territories of Spinosaurus and Deinosuchus. As at Palm Islands, they fight hard over territory there.
An old male who has lost his preferred territory often ends his days in a secondary one, where young rivals seldom bother to challenge him.

### ✦ Wanderers
A male without a territory is a wanderer. Most are young males who have just left their mother or their father, and males who have lost their territory.
Wanderers do not hunt. They fish, and travel in search of a free territory, or an owner they can challenge.
Wanderers do not hum. They cross territories with the wide arc and keep moving. An owner tolerates a wanderer who passes through, and challenges one who stays.
A wanderer who finds no free preferred territory settles in a free secondary territory. If none is free either, he challenges an owner.
A brace of brothers may wander together.

### ✦ Downtime Behavior
By day, Sachicasaurus rest motionless in kelp forests, reef caves, under cliffs, or on the high ledges of the Abyssal Depths.
Mothers in the nursery rest close to their nippers during the day, and guard them at night.
A brace rests flank to flank, and wakes together at dusk.
A father rests with his son close beside him.
%% banner Social Interaction
Males are solitary or live in a brace with a brother. Braces hold their territory together, and share every kill.
Females travel alone or as a drift with a sister or daughter. They move between territories, and choose the male whose territory offers the best hunting.
A territorial male shares his kills with the female or drift he allows in his territory, and with his offspring.
Sachicasaurus form no lasting pairs. After the pair night, each goes their own way.

### ✦ Braces
A brace is a pair of related males: brothers, half-brothers, or a father and his son.
Braces hold one territory together, defend it together and share every kill.
A son who grew up with his father may stay with him as a brace at Sub-Adult.
In courtship, each brother of a brace may court one sister of a drift. A single female is courted by only one brother, and the brothers settle between themselves which one.

### ✦ Drifts
A drift is a pair of related females: sisters, half-sisters, or a mother and her daughter.
A drift travels between territories, and may live together in the territory of one male.
When a drift gives the agreement call to a single male, he courts only one of the sisters, never both, usually the one with the most scars. Her sister goes without.
The sister who goes without waits close by during the pair night, then may come along to the nursery, and help guard the nest and the young.
Old females often stay with a daughter in a drift.
%% banner Aggressive Interaction
Sachicasaurus are primarily aggressive to unrelated males of their own kind.

### ✦ Territory Challenges with other Sachicasaurus
Rival males are met with open hostility.
A male who finds another ungrouped male staying in his territory gives the broadcast call (:broadcast_call:) and three aggressive calls (:threaten_call:), then attacks.
**The fight:** It lasts until one of them drops to 50% health. He gives the (:heart_call:) and has lost. The winner stops at once.
- If the owner loses, the challenger takes the territory. The old owner leaves the POI, and is a wanderer until he finds a new territory.
- If the challenger loses, he leaves the POI.
- A brace fights as two. The side whose last fighter drops to 50% has lost.
If a male or brace loses the challenge, any female or drift they allow in their territory either follows, or leaves the POI without them.
A son living with a defeated father loses his protection. See ✦ The Handover.

### ✦ Unguarded Young
Like male crocodiles, territorial males kill young of their own kind.
At night, a territorial male may kill an unguarded Adolescent in his territory that is not his own. This counts as a hunt.
An Adolescent is guarded while it is grouped with its mother, or with a father who holds a territory. Below Adolescent, the young are in the nursery, where males never come.
This is why mothers stay with their young after the nursery, and why sons are handed over to their father, not sent alone.

### ✦ Between Females
Females drive away other ungrouped females from their nests, but otherwise avoid each other peacefully.
In the nursery, mothers with fewer scars give way to the most scarred mother when choosing nest sites.
%% banner Cross-Species Interaction
Sachicasaurus pay little attention to other species by day. By night, nearly everything in the shallows is prey or rival.

### ✦ Kaiwhekea
Kaiwhekea are Sachicasaurus' favourite prey, and Sachicasaurus are the only hunters that can seize a grown one.
Kaiwhekea flee from Sachicasaurus like from any Apex. A seized Kaiwhekea is lost: its bale does not come to help.
Kaiwhekea escape onto high rocks, where a Sachicasaurus cannot reach them. Flat beaches are no refuge from a Sachicasaurus.
By day, bales cross a territory unharmed while its owner sleeps.
By night, the bale's resting place decides everything. Experienced bales rest outside known territories, and keep a watch.
A Kaiwhekea bale resting in the shallows, or hauled out on a flat beach, is the target Sachicasaurus wait for.

### ✦ Eurhinosaurus & Flotillas
Flotillas are a Leedsichthys travelling with its Eurhinosaurus. Unless two of them hunt it together, Sachicasaurus leave the Leedsichthys alone, and take the Eurhinosaurus sleeping around it.
Eurhinosaurus are taken when the chance arises. Their loud calls often betray the hunter's position.

### ✦ Leedsichthys
Pups are easy to seize and drag away.
An adult Leedsichthys is too much for one Sachicasaurus. Two may hunt it together, like Tyrannotitan mobbing an Argentinosaurus. See ✦ The Leedsichthys Call.

### ✦ Tylosaurus
Tylosaurus are their equals and their rivals.
Tylosaurus rule the open water, with more stamina, more speed in long chases and faster healing.
Sachicasaurus rule the kelp and the shallows by night, with the grab and the bone-breaking bite.
Tylosaurus are challenged out of a territory. A Tylosaurus that stays may be hunted.

### ✦ Livyatan
Livyatan are the only animals Sachicasaurus truly fear.
In open water, its clicks find them wherever they hide.
A Sachicasaurus that hears clicking stays in the kelp, in the shallows or on a high ledge of the Abyssal Depths, where the Livyatan does not follow, until it has gone.
Palm Islands are the only ocean Livyatan never enter. They never come into the channels either.

### ✦ Spinosaurus & Deinosuchus
At Palm Islands, Volcano Islands and Twisted Forest, Sachicasaurus and the two river giants fight hard over territory.
Sachicasaurus like to hunt them, but may also leave them be.
In open water a Sachicasaurus out-turns both river giants. It wins by staying mobile and breaking bones, never by trading bites, because in a standing fight both bite harder.

### ✦ Xiphactinus
Xiphactinus live in the saltwater channels. Sachicasaurus only meet them in the secondary territories.
There, a Sachicasaurus can seize a Xiphactinus, and may hunt them by night. Xiphactinus do not flee from Sachicasaurus. They only flee from Tylosaurus.

### ✦ Predator Reaction
Sachicasaurus flee from adult Livyatan, into the kelp, the shallows or onto a high ledge of the Abyssal Depths.
Up to 2 Sachicasaurus may defend themselves against other hunters. A brace or drift always defends each other first.
Mothers defend their nest and their nippers in the nursery against any hunter.
%% banner Hunting Strategy
Sachicasaurus win with the grab.
1. **Lurking:** In the kelp or on the reef near where a flotilla or bale has settled for the night, motionless and silent, in the periscope.
2. **Breaking the formation:** An Aileron Roll darts into the sleeping group and knocks the sleepers apart.
3. **The grab:** Clamp seizes a Kaiwhekea or Eurhinosaurus at the edge of the group. A seized animal cannot flee, however fast it is.
4. **Dragging away:** The hunter drags its prey into the kelp or the shallows, away from the defenders, chewing and thrashing as it goes. Every bite breaks bones, more so at night.
5. **Holding on:** Kaiwhekea and Eurhinosaurus flee from Apex carnivores, so a seized animal gets no help from its group. The hunter holds on until its prey is dead, or until its own stamina runs out and it must let go.
  - Prey that reaches a high rock is out of reach.
  - Nests defended by both parents are avoided.
6. **Other prey:** Larger prey is met with Breach, Lunge and Bone Snap, and worn down by broken bones.
7. **From the ledge:** In the Abyssal Depths, the hunter drops from its ledge onto prey passing below: flotillas on their trips into the deep, and old Leedsichthys. Then it returns to its ledge.

### ✦ The Leedsichthys Call
An adult Leedsichthys is only hunted by 2 Sachicasaurus together.
1. **The call:** A Sachicasaurus who wants to hunt a Leedsichthys raises its head above the water, and gives a (:broadcast_call:) followed by a (:threaten_call:).
2. **The answer:** One Sub/Adult Sachicasaurus who hears it may answer the same way, and join the hunt. A brother, a sister or a mated partner may join without a call.
3. **Who may call:** Any Sub/Adult who may hunt: a territorial male, or a female. Wanderers and Adolescents never call, and never answer.
4. **The truce:** The owner of the territory never challenges his hunting partner during the hunt and the meal. Afterwards a male partner leaves the territory. A male never answers a call from another male's territory, unless that male gave it.
5. **The hunt:** The two hunters do not group in game, and share the kill. A Leedsichthys is stronger than both of them in a standing fight, so they strike from both sides, break bones and let it tire, never trading bites head-on.

### ✦ Beaching
Sachicasaurus follow the same beaching rules as Tylosaurus.
Sachicasaurus may hunt creatures within 1 body length of the water, but will always target those they can easily clamp first, in order to drag them into the water.
Adult Apex terrestrial carnivores are only hunted when they are swimming in water.
Sachicasaurus may stalk the shore at night, looking for individuals drinking from or intending to swim across the water. They lunge out of the water and use Clamp or Bone Snap to pull the target back into the water with them. This is dangerous, especially against carnivorous prey.
Sachicasaurus will not crawl further than a body length from the water in pursuit of prey.
Sachicasaurus avoid staying beached on land for long. The further they go on land, the higher the risk of drying out and dying to the heat.
Sachicasaurus may third party engagements that enter the water, or come within 1 body length of the water.
%% banner Courtship & Parenthood
Sachicasaurus court like great white sharks: females are larger than males, and grown females carry the scars of mating.
A Sachicasaurus does not circle or inspect its partner. It judges with its eyes, at the surface, and reads the scars.
A male only ever mates with one female at a time. After the mating bite, the pair group for one pair night. They never group when nesting.

### ✦ Courtship
Courtship only takes place in a preferred territory, never in a secondary one.
1. **The hum:** In the courting season, territorial males hum at every dawn. A female who is interested answers with a (:broadcast_call:), and comes to visit him, by day or by night. Females travel from territory to territory, judging the male and his territory. See ✦ The Dawn Hum.
2. **Meeting:** When a female enters a male's territory and the two see each other, the male recognises her as a female by her build.
3. **The flank display:** A female ready to mate turns her flank to him at the surface and gives the agreement call. He answers in the same way. He may also display first, and she answers. Then both lie still, side by side. This is all the judging there is: she shows him her scars, and he shows her his.
4. **No display:** If she does not answer and swims on, he tolerates her in his territory. After one night she travels on, and accepts this.
5. **The kill:** If a fresh kill of his own lies in his territory, he may lead her to it, and she may feed from it.
6. **Her choice:**
  - **The duel:** Usually, she gives a (:threaten_call:), and he must prove himself in the duel.
  - **Accepting him outright:** She may spare him the duel and repeat the flank display, but only if he has shown her a fresh kill of his own that is a Kaiwhekea or above Medium tier, such as a Tylosaurus, or if he is heavily scarred, a male who has clearly fought many times. This is always her choice. A male never has a right to it.
  - **Moving on:** She swims on, and is tolerated for one night.

### ✦ The Duel
- Both fight. Bite only, one bite per pass, aimed at the flanks and flippers, the way great white sharks bite. No other abilities are used. Movement is free.
- Whoever drops below 50% health gives the (:heart_call:) then at the latest.
- If she gives the heart call first, he has proven himself, and she accepts him.
- If he gives the heart call first, he has failed, and she travels on to another territory.
- At any moment of the duel she may stop it with the flank display. He has convinced her, and she accepts him.
- He stops at once at every signal she gives. A male who bites again after her heart call or her flank display has lost her for good: she never courts him again.
- There is one duel per pair per day-night cycle.

### ✦ The Mating Bite
Every successful courtship ends with the mating bite, with or without a duel: he bites her once on the flank or a flipper, and she holds still.

### ✦ The Pair Night
After the mating bite, the pair group for their pair night, to strengthen the bond between them.
- The group lasts through the first full night after the mating bite, and ends at the dawn that follows it. Then she travels to the nursery.
- During the pair night, the two may hunt together in his territory, as a pair. They count as two grouped Sachicasaurus, like a brace or a drift.
- By day, they rest flank to flank in his territory, and wake together at dusk.
- Only the mated pair groups. A brother or sister leaves the group for this time, but may stay close in the territory. When a brace courts a drift, each couple groups on its own, and the two couples never join the same hunt.
- After the pair night, each goes their own way. They never group when nesting, and males never enter the nursery.

### ✦ Scars
Scars never heal. They are how Sachicasaurus know each other.
A heavily scarred female has mated many times, and has many clutches behind her. Her scars come from the duels she yielded, and the mating bites that followed. Males seek her out, and other mothers give way to her in the nursery.
A heavily scarred male has fought many times: in duels, against rivals, against Tylosaurus and hard prey. A female may spare such a male the duel.

### ✦ Nesting
Female great white, lemon and bull sharks give birth in nurseries: shallow coasts, mangroves or rivers where the large hunters do not go. Sachicasaurus do the same.
After the pair night, the female travels along the coast to the nursery to nest. The wounds of the duel heal while she sits on her nest, and the scars stay.

### ✦ The Nursery
The nursery lies in the shallows of Tallbrush Coasts, the northern coast west of Kelp Vale.
All mothers share the nursery. A mother defends her own nest, and drives other females away from it, but not out of the nursery.
The most scarred mother chooses her nest site first, and mothers with fewer scars give way to her.
Males never enter the nursery.
Livyatan never come here, but the nursery is still dangerous. Spinosaurus wait at the river mouths, shore hunters roam the beach, and Tylosaurus may come in from the sea.

### ✦ Parenthood
The mother stays with her nippers in the nursery until they reach Adolescent. She guards them at night, and rests close by during the day.
While she raises them, a mother eats whatever she can get. She fishes the shallows, and at night hunts land creatures that swim, Kaiwhekea that come to the coast, and any other animal that strays into the nursery. She does not leave the nursery to hunt.
Her nippers watch her fish, and learn it from her.
Nippers below Adolescent die if beached for more than 5 minutes. Their mother must push them back into the water, at considerable risk to herself.
Fathers take no part in raising the nippers.

### ✦ The Leaving
Once her young reach Adolescent, the mother leads them out of the nursery, along the coast and into the open ocean. It is the most dangerous journey of their lives.
Territorial males kill unguarded Adolescents that are not their own, so the mother stays with her young. She leads them around territories with the wide arc, and crosses them by day when she can.

### ✦ The Handover
From the middle of the Adolescent stage, a son may go to his father.
1. **The journey:** His mother leads him to his father's territory. She never sends him alone.
2. **The handover:** At his father's territory, the father decides. With (:friendly_call:) he accepts his son. With (:threaten_call:) he refuses him.
3. **Accepted:** The son groups with his father. His mother leaves him there, and travels on.
4. **Refused:** The son stays with his mother until Sub-Adult.
Only a father who holds a territory, preferred or secondary, may accept a son.
**Living with his father:** The father teaches his son to hunt, and defends him. A son may take part in his father's hunts. A son with a brace learns by watching his father and uncle, because no more than 2 take part in a hunt.
**A defeated father:** If the father loses a territory challenge, he flees from the territory, and may not defend his son until he holds a territory again. Until then, the new owner may kill the son, as long as he is Adolescent.
A father without a territory does not hunt, and cannot teach it.

### ✦ Offspring Maturity
At Sub-Adult, the young leave their mother.
Young males become wanderers, often with a brother, until they find a territory. A son who grew up with his father may stay with him as a brace.
Young females may stay with their mother as a drift.

### ✦ Adoption
Sachicasaurus do not adopt. Orphaned nippers make their way to the nursery and grow up alone.
%% banner Elderly
Old males can no longer hold a preferred territory against younger rivals. An old male who loses his often ends his days in a secondary territory, where young rivals seldom bother to challenge him. Without one, he is a wanderer, and only fishes.
Old females stay with a daughter in a drift.

### ✦ Death
When a Sachicasaurus dies, it sinks into the kelp of the cove it knew best.
A sister or daughter of its drift stays with the body until the next dawn.
When a territorial male dies, his hum is missing at the next dawn. His neighbours hear the silence, and know that his territory is free.

Sachicasaurus hum at dawn, never at dusk, and speak with their bodies at night.
Sachicasaurus are the only hunters that can seize a grown Kaiwhekea.

%% banner Rules & Clarifications
The nursery at Tallbrush Coasts is not a territory. Mothers challenge nothing out of it. They only defend their own nest and young.
The courtship duel is an engagement within the species with its own limits, not a hunt and not a territory challenge: Bite only, one bite per pass, flanks and flippers only, and a heart call below 50% health at the latest. Each stops at once at the other's heart call, and the male also at her flank display.
Territory fights between Sachicasaurus end at 50% health. The one who drops to 50% first gives the heart call and has lost. Biting after the heart call is a rule break.
Wanderers do not hunt. A male who loses his territory may not hunt or defend his son until he holds a territory again.
Killing an unguarded Adolescent counts as a hunt: only at night, and only by a territorial male in his own territory.

### RULE EXEMPTIONS
A Sachicasaurus territory covers one half of an ocean POI, or one segment of Volcano Islands or Twisted Forest, water only. Terrestrial species on land within the same POI are not affected. (proposed water territory rule)
Any 2 Sub/Adult Sachicasaurus may hunt an adult Leedsichthys together without grouping, like Tyrannotitan mobbing an Argentinosaurus.
Sachicasaurus may hunt within 1 body length of the water, and third party engagements that enter the water or come within 1 body length of it, the same as Tylosaurus. (3.17)
Males may defend their territory during the day if challenged. (1.4.1)

## Profile - Quick View
Sachicasaurus are Apex Nocturnal Aquatic Carnivores. They may do everything but hunt by day.
Sachicasaurus can group up to 2 related Sub/Adults of the same sex: a brace of males, or a drift of females with unlimited offspring. A territorial father may take in one son from the middle of Adolescent. Adults of the opposite sex only group for the pair night after courtship, and may hunt together then.
Sachicasaurus males are aggressive towards rival males, unguarded young that are not their own, and Tylosaurus. They hold one half of an ocean POI as their preferred territory, or a secondary territory in Volcano Islands or Twisted Forest. Males without a territory are wanderers, and do not hunt. Females are tolerant, and travel between territories.
Every territorial male hums at dawn. Interested females answer, and visit him. At night, Sachicasaurus speak only with their bodies.
Sachicasaurus live in the kelp, reefs, shallows and beaches of the oceans, and avoid deep water. In the Abyssal Depths they keep to the high rock ledges, where Livyatan do not follow.
Sachicasaurus may hunt up to Apex tier, and hunt by night. They are the only hunters that can seize a grown Kaiwhekea, and wait for bales and flotillas resting in the shallows. Any two may hunt an adult Leedsichthys together after the hunting call. At the shore they follow the same beaching rules as Tylosaurus.
Sachicasaurus flee from Livyatan. Tylosaurus are their equals: the Tylosaurus rules the open water, the Sachicasaurus the shallows by night.
Sachicasaurus court only in preferred territories, with the flank display at the surface, where both show their scars. Usually the female then tests the male in a duel, bite for bite, until one gives the heart call below 50% health. Every courtship ends with the mating bite.
Scars never heal: a heavily scarred female has mated many times, a heavily scarred male has fought many times. Physical dimorphism is mandatory: females are the bulkier and larger sex.
Females raise their young in the nursery at Tallbrush Coasts, where males never come, and lead them out to sea at Adolescent. Sons may be handed over to their father from the middle of Adolescent.
