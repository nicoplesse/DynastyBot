# Yunnanosaurus – Offspring Behavior

Hatchling Yunnanosaurus are precocial; they have clear vision and strong muscles almost immediately after hatching. Young that are slow to rise are encouraged to their feet by their mothers, who back away from the nest and call softly - pushing them to follow. Yunnanosaurus hatchlings are raised entirely by their mothers and any females they are travelling with. Their fathers are not permitted to show interest in their young, and instead spend their time guarding the area and keeping a watchful eye out for danger. Young are raised communally by all females in the group, and they see all adult females as caregivers, but show some instinctive knowledge of which individual is their biological mother. Males are chased away, until offspring reach Adolescence. From the start, Yunnanosaurus offspring are plucky and confident, calling out loudly whenever they are hungry and staying close to the females in their group. By juvenile stage, Yunnanosaurus develop even more confidence, and venture out from beneath their mothers’ feet, exploring the environment and investigating interesting objects. Female juveniles use this time to start building friendship bonds with any other similar age females; bringing gifts to them and grooming one another. As the young reach the end of their juvenile age stage, mothers teach their offspring how to balance in a bipedal stance, using their tail for balance. This is an important step for youngsters, so that they learn how to reach low hanging branches from the desert’s flora, and how to present themselves to their full height to intimidate threats. They demonstrate by stretching upwards onto their hind legs and calling out for the young to witness and copy. Young Yunnanosaurus are often frustrated trying to learn stability, and will throw small tantrums if they repeatedly lose their balance. They often need comfort and encouragement from the patient adult females of the group. A frustrated youngster might try to flee should the lesson not be going their way. Adolescent females present within the group often try to help these frustrated youngsters, they teach them patiently and guide them to the correct stance. Adolescence is an important time for a Yunnanosaurus; males will branch away from their mothers and start to look for a male role model to imitate. If they hatched in the Dry Season, into a female-only group, young males will follow one another and stick to the outskirts of the group. Rainy Season males start to loosely follow their fathers around - not differentiating between either male if two are present - mimicking his behaviors from a short distance away. Maturing males are sometimes quite defiant, and perform displays of strength including neck smacking, drumming their chests, and mock charging at any siblings or similar-aged offspring. They have no trouble taking advantage of their terrain and often shove their brothers into rocks, or bushes! Adolescent females begin to demand respect from their siblings, and make a great effort of trying to keep their brothers in check. If a young male grows too rowdy, their sisters may chase them and call aggressively towards them in an attempt to scold them. It is from this point that young males start to grow respect for the females in their group. Female adolescents may even deliver a blow towards a brother if he is particularly annoying, but will never damage him to get below half health. When young males reach sub-adult, offspring will behave differently depending on the season and their current grouping. In the Rainy Season; the young males will disperse from the group as there is no space for them. Sometimes two brothers that have bonded closely will disperse together and form the beginnings of their own clade. In the Dry Season; the young males will disperse out of the rove they hatched into, and strike out to find a ramble to join. If two brothers have bonded closely, they may disperse together. When young females reach sub-adult, they will behave differently depending on the season and their current grouping. In the Rainy Season; the young females will disperse from the group to avoid any inbreeding. Sisters with established bonds will always disperse together. In the Dry Season; females will always try to stay with the rove they hatched into, solidifying bonds between other females. If there is no room, they will disperse in groups.
