# Yunnanosaurus – Predator Reaction

Yunnanosaurus is intolerant of any Large or below carnivore species. In particular, Yunnanosaurus will attack Daspletosaurus and Torvosaurus on sight in the rainy season, without any warning, if they are noticed. Yunnanosaurus will only give chase if the carnivore refuses to flee from them. They will otherwise launch aggressive calls and threaten the species, before turning back to their group when the carnivore begins to leave. Yunnanosaurus accept forfeit calls from these species, but only if they flee immediately after. If the carnivore continues to linger within 3 body lengths of the Yunnanosaurus, they may continue to be attacked until they flee further. Despite their usual aggression, Yunnanosaurus ignores Apex predators, as they have an innate understanding that they are unlikely to win an engagement with them. If an Apex predator approaches a Yunnanosaurus, they will flee. Up to 2 Yunnanosaurus may take part in an engagement to defend or aggress another species. When threatened, Yunnanosaurus takes an aggressive stance, raising themselves up onto their hind legs to show off their towering height. Following this, they beat their chest rhythmically to produce a loud drumming noise. In a clade, male Yunnanosaurus are inherently more aggressive than their female counterparts and are always the first to respond to danger. They sometimes mock-charge at their opponent, stopping clear of actually causing any harm - to showcase their strength and fitness. As the primary line of defence, males will not stray far from their group when travelling; a male is always ready to protect the clade or lead male ready to protect the ramble, if required. Lead females are the first to step up and defend their rove, if threatened by a predator.
