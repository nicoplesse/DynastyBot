# Yunnanosaurus – Orphaned Behavior

In the unfortunate situation where a newly hatched Yunnanosaurus has hatched into an abandoned nest, they face great difficulty surviving into adulthood. Orphans are born with the same precocial nature as offspring with parents, but on realizing that they have no family to raise them, their voices instinctively fall silent to avoid being detected by opportunistic predators. Orphaned Yunnanosaurus will always try to seek out a herbivore herd to follow, hoping to have some form of protection while they are still small and vulnerable. By adolescence, surviving Yunnanosaurus orphans have developed the necessary skills to adapt themselves to their environment, and establish a small network of routes to fresh water and delicious berry bushes. They typically do not explore much beyond the POI they have hatched into, and settle into the familiar grounds until they have grown in both size and confidence. If an orphaned Yunnanosaurus discovers a group of adults, they are so eager to join that they always try to appease them by bringing gifts, and calling out with a friendly voice. As adults, previous orphans are often very skittish around predators, even if they are within the protection of a larger group. These adults also experience some challenges socially; without the close bonds of friendship that are created between similar aged offspring, they are sometimes seen as a bit of an outsider. In rare cases, orphaned Yunnanosaurus that mature into adults will choose to live a solitary life, falling back into the familiarity of their hatching ground and preferring to eat berries into their adulthood rather than switching to roots, nuts, and flowers as other adults would.
