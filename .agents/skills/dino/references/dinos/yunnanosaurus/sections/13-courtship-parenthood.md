# Yunnanosaurus – Courtship & Parenthood

All courtship for Yunnanosaurus occurs during the day, regardless of season. Rainy Season During the Rainy Season, mature Yunnanosaurus experience an increase in fertility, and instinctively move from same sex groups to mixed sex clades to provide courting opportunities. Males take the lead by first claiming and securing a Medium-sized territory. Males in the same group work together in pairs to fend off rival males (and other species), and maintain a safe and protected area for their clade to raise future offspring. If there are two males present in the clade, they first challenge one another to determine which will hold the dominant status, before any courtship begins. Both males will rise up on their hind legs, beating their chest and aggressively bellowing at one another as they slowly circle one another, drawing closer together. If neither male backs down, this escalates to a battle: fought entirely by smacking their necks hard against each other repeatedly. The fighting continues until one of the males reaches half health or less, which forces the weaker male to submit. Once a dominance challenge has been won, the dominant male flushes his body by shedding his skin into brighter colors, signifying his new status. He maintains his status for the whole of the nesting season - but can be challenged against next time, or by other males. A nesting season starts when the group wishes to nest, and ends when the offspring from that nesting are grown to Adolescent, or a previously un-courted female wishes to nest. After losing a challenge, the less dominant male keeps his harmony with the clade and continues to work together with his male companion to defend their claimed territory. His position means that he is not allowed any courting rights to females, but he can challenge again in the future. Once the dominant male is established - either by default, or by winning a challenge, that male will parade around his territory, broadcasting loudly to draw the attention of any nearby females which may already be in his group, or may be watching nearby. Interested females will approach the male, inspecting him carefully. Delighted, he circles around them, continuing his loud calls, and standing up tall while beating on his chest to show off his strength. If a female is not impressed with his display, she will turn her back on him and move away. Some unimpressed females will even leave the territory and group entirely, in search of a new, more exciting male. If there are carnivores present in the male’s territory that he has not yet driven out, females will reject all courting advances until those predators have been driven out. If she accepts his advances, she will call back to him affectionately, and seat herself nearby. The male continues courtship calling until he is certain that all females nearby have decided either to accept him, or to ignore his advances. The male will assist nest construction with any females that have accepted him as a mate. If there is a less dominant male present in the clade, he may choose to assist nest building in the hopes of gaining favor with the females and increasing his courting chances next season, if he wins a future dominance challenge. Typically, the other male ignores all courting and nesting activity and will focus on patrolling the territory borders while his clade is busy.
