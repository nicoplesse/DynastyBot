# Pachycephalosaurus – Offspring Behavior (Nested)

Hatchling & Juvenile Baby Pachycephalosaurus which are between the growth stage of hatchling and yearling are known as kids. Kids hatch in a concealed nest away from prying eyes and other Pachycephalosaurus. Usually, their start to the world is in turbulence if their mothers nesting spot is challenged by another nanny. As soon as they hatch, kids chirp, rumble and call loudly for their mothers attention. Responding in turn, she nudges them to their feet, almost pushing them out the nest. Kids follow their mother loyally, calling to her and sticking close. When their mother leaps out of reach, kids panic and attempt to follow in vain, crying out at the last spot she stood. Experienced mothers know to lead her kids along cliffs they can follow without being separated, but first time mothers take time to understand why their offspring do not follow her. A young Pachycephalosaurus's early life consists of loudly calling to their mother and following her as she leads them to food and water. Offspring do not bond with one another in their early lives - their entire focus is staying close to the nanny who hatched them or adopted them. Offspring Pachycephalosaurus will attempt to eat everything they are led to. Their bellies are designed to digest specific food types, but their minds do not comprehend what's acceptable food and what is not. Anything inedible will come right back up and the youngling will quickly learn which food types satiate them and which do not. Adolescent Around adolescence, Pachycephalosaurus are given the full freedom and encouragement to move around as they please and are introduced to other kids in the band. Both males and females play through butting heads and leaping after one another. Kids begin to form bonds as their personalities start to show - some take a liking to a particular individual and follow them around, mimicking their behavior, while others are independent spirits and explore their immediate vicinity with confidence and interest. Nannies virtually lose all interest in their offspring around this time, and happily teach any kid who dotes on them. From early adolescence, to late yearling, Pachycephalosaurus begin to settle into their personalities. As a species that matures slower, a kid may take longer to mature than their peers. The time varies based on personality. See General Behavior for more.
