# Pachycephalosaurus – Death

Given their cliff-dwelling, hill-scaling lifestyle, uncoordinated individuals may misstep on steep terrain or unstable rock. A bad fall - especially during territorial disputes or predator chases - can result in fatal fractures or internal injuries. A loss of stamina or failure to reach high ground in time leaves them vulnerable to predators, particularly at night when vision and response time falter. Solitary individuals, or those pushed from groups, face a much higher risk of being picked off without the protection of the band. Yearlings are the most vulnerable - unless they are skilled jumpers, they often fall victim to predation if they cannot flee to safety in time. Understanding male groupings: Multiple ungrouped adult Pachycephalosaurus males follow a band from a distance, waiting for the moment to challenge the ruling buck for his position in the group. Adult males are solitary, and cannot join a group with another adult male. However, yearling males are often mistaken for females and may be temporarily accepted into the band. If a challenge for the lead buck position is won, the defeated male must be removed from the in-game group and the victor added. Females remain with the band and cannot abandon it to follow the losing buck; he must wait out the cooldown timer before attempting to reclaim his position. Disputes between females/the buck over resting spots are strictly one-on-one and rely solely on the use of kick and headbutt  abilities. Flighty reaction:  Pachycephalosaurus can attack when bitten but afterwards MUST flee to an elevated rock, cliff, or at cave entrance when there is an opportunity to do so. If trapped or unable to flee properly without dying, they may continue fighting on the ground, but must attempt to move to their "territory" throughout the fight.
