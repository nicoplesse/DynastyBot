# Spinosaurus – Pairbonds

Spinosaurus yearn for long-term, monogamous bonds. If separated from their partner, an individual may seek out another mate to avoid remaining solitary. Although they generally stay with one partner for most of their lives, they can still nest with another if circumstances prevent them from reuniting with their original mate. Spinosaurus do not group socially outside of their pairbonds - as their strong monogamous inclination prevents them from forming bonds with related family members or strangers. When ready, an individual will court another with the intention to lay eggs together as a test of compatibility. Sometimes the pairing does not yield eggs - and the acceptance of this loss depends on each individual. Some are indifferent to the eggs not hatching, while others see this loss as genetic incompatibility and end the pairing in favor of seeking a more fertile partner. Bonds form over time, with both individuals forming trust through time spent side-by-side; basking, swimming, patrolling territory and defending each other from rivals and predation. Pairbonds are broken for various reasons; infertility, fearful reactions to threats in their shared territory, unwillingness to care for young and adoptees, poor hunting skills, or simply undesirable company. When pairbonds begin to fade, both individuals distance themselves from each other and when approached - react defensively. Repeated aggressive reactions to each other sparks resentment and incompatibility. When the pairbond ends, an individual might opt to move on - either back to Triad Falls to find a better partner, or stake a claim to their own territory. Sometimes, a partner may attempt to push out their pairbond, evicting them from the water and forcing the separation.
