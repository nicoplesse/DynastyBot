# Spinosaurus – Adoption

Some Spinosaurus are doting parents, while others are not. This individual preference impacts whether adoption is more or less likely to occur. Be it in a pair, or solitary - a Spinosaurus might accept an orphan Spinosaurus or Suchomimus as young, but equally may choose to aggress them away. If choosing to adopt an orphan Spinosaurus or Suchomimus, usually the most parental individual quickly approaches the adoptee, calling softly :friendly_call: and aiming to lure it closer to them. With both parents inspecting their new child, it is led into the water and gently prevented from straying too far. If rejecting Suchomimus or young Spinosaurus, the would-be parent roars with displeasure :broadcast_call: . They snap at the air with their jaws, and chase the orphan out their territory. Suchomimus All Spinosaurus seem to recognize these sailless species as vulnerable Spinosaurus young, and adoption of them is common in Spinosaurus that have strong parental drive. These "orphans" get food provided to them, and Spinosaurus will defend them from threats, which serves as a very useful lifeline for Suchomimus - one which many of them learn, over time, to use to their advantage. If the Spinosaurus has same-species young or orphans, it's not uncommon for Suchomimus to cull these rivals when the adults are not around. Spinosaurus parenting style does not permit offspring (and adoptees) from hunting prey that is not fish or critters, they do not tolerate their offspring joining engagements until they are considered mature enough to survive on their own (subadult). If spotting their adopted Suchomomis in a hunt or being attacked, Spinosaurus pairs immediately step between the fight and aggressive their adoptee away. Forcing them to abandon the fight and retreat. In these situations, Spinosaurus parents do not hunt the species engaged with their offspring and instead return to their territory. If the species is within the territory borders, Spinosaurus parents first ensure their offspring are safe before aggressing and pushing out the rival creatures. Suchomimus are still at risk of being pushed out, though some are cared for indefinitely. It is not uncommon for Spinosaurus to grow tired of constantly caring for these sly "offspring" and opt to reject them. They employ fierce aggressive displays to push their once-accepted offspring out of the territory, ruining the Suchomimus' chances of an easier life. Spinosaurus who already have adopted sub/adult Suchomimus may still adopt the young of these species. Adult Suchomimus who are seen continually displaying aggression towards the younger adoptees will be driven off by Spinosaurus. As Spinosaurus age, their interest in territoriality wanes, and many individuals end up travelling to Triad Falls more frequently, preferring a slower pace of life. Much more time is spent lazing around and showing affection to their pairbond  - should they have one. In male and female pairs, elder Spinosaurus frequently face issues of infertility, which leads to a far lower likelihood of eggs hatching successfully. Single egg clutches are common with advanced age. Spinosaurus territory is considered 10 body lengths from the water's edge in claimable territory areas. They have unique territory sizes, specific to this profile. Each claimable territory on their map is labeled as separate water spots with darker or lighter green colors. Some territory areas will overlap, this is unavoidable and up to both players if they wish to continue engaging each other or move out of reach while they heal. Both pairs and solitary Spinosaurus claim and defend territory. During engagements: Spinosaurus can use any water to flee into and continue attacking from. However, this does not include healing water. If fleeing into water during a territory challenge with a territorial terrestrial, Spinosaurus automatically forfeits the land and must now abide by resource challenge rules for the water territory. Hunting other players can ONLY be done from territory areas. This is the water as highlighted on their map, and 10 body lengths from the waters edge. Outside of these areas, Spinosaurus CANNOT hunt other players. They must eat fish or critters and corpses. They may however defend themselves when attacked. Just not initiate hunts. Adoption is optional and can be revoked at any time. Spinosaurus do not bring their offspring and adoptees into their courtship grounds at Triad Falls when seeking a new partner. Vocalization when Hunting Profile Rule: To avoid misunderstanding. If choosing to hunt, Spinosaurus cannot broadcast or aggressive call before or during the hunt. Territory challenges (When chasing things from their water): Spinosaurus must drive out everything from their water source when in claimable territory areas. To begin this defense, Spinosaurus FIRST display their sail and issue x3 aggressive calls :threaten_call: If ignored for 10 seconds, the offender begins (or continues) drinking from the water source, or is challenged back, Spinosaurus will engage - only stopping pursuit once the target leaves 10 body lengths from the waters edge. If losing its challenge against species that do not claim the water source as territory, Spinosaurus must permit that group to drink before pushing them out again. Intervening in Suchomimus engagements: Spinosaurus cannot hunt the players that their adopted Suchomimus were engaged with. If the fight is within the Spinosaurus territory, it can push the rivals out. Sub/Adult Spinosaurus may not group with related family members. Adult groupings for Spinosaurus are pairbonds - not loose groupings. Spinosaurus may hunt other players if 75% or below hunger in the courtship grounds.
