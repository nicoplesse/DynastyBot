# Dilophosaurus – Dominant Males

Within a leash, all members defer to the dominant male and his breeding group - but particularly his leadership. His call brings all members to attention, and he leads the leash along his chosen migratory paths, as well as directing hunting parties. The dominant male determines when individuals are to be shunned or exiled from the group, and is the first line of defense against predators. His calls carry weight and he holds the highest respect above all group members. Those who defy him quickly leave the leash of their own accord. If the dominant male dies, a leash will often fall into complete disarray, and dissolve completely. Those strongly bonded together may opt to remain together, and sometimes a new leash forms if a subordinate male is able to take on the new role. This male holds the top position within the social hierarchy, and his breeding group falls second in his command. Siblings and offspring of the breeding group are the last to eat. Many other Dilophosaurus, including his own offspring, approach him submissively crouched until he greets them. Despite the position of seniority and power that he has over the entire leash, he still may greet his favored offspring, children, and breeding group members fondly. Dilophosaurus very rarely, if ever, show aggression to another Dilophosaurus within its leash or mask, unless it is a dominant reynard correcting a subordinate’s behavior. Any aggressive behavior shown by a subordinate towards another subordinate results in harsh correction by a member of the dominant breeding group. Leash Aggression Towards Solitary Dilophosaurus A dominant male will swiftly deliver harsh attacks and challenges to any solitary Dilophosaurus who is ungrouped with him that he sees. With aggressive calls as a warning, he will chase them out of the territory, nipping at their ankles and tail. If the intruder does not flee, they risk being killed. An individual in a leash who is not the dominant male may more likely alert the dominant male to the presence of the intruder, rather than challenge the Dilophosaurus themself - unless they are interested in sneaky courtship.
