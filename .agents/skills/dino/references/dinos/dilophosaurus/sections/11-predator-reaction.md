# Dilophosaurus – Predator Reaction

Dilophosaurus always flee from Apex carnivores when hunted. They are skittish in their presence, and do not attempt to push their luck and hunt them. When hunted, if the Dilophosaurus group does not have enough members to hunt their attacker, the group will flee. In the event of this predator managing to catch a Dilophosaurus, or corner it - any other Dilophosaurus within the group who are within LOS may rush to their packmates aid. If the Dilophosaurus is cornered, or the hunter is a tier which can be hunted with the current amount of group members, Dilophosaurus excitedly call to one another, reassuring each other of their presence. Dilophosaurus typically have some members as bait, with the remainder dart to land sharp bites on their hunter. Those acting as bait loudly hiss and posture, constantly chirping and calling to distract the hunter. If the carnivore gives up, Dilophosaurus can continue the hunt, as long as they are able to hunt that size tier, and are of sufficient hunger to do so in the POI. When a group member dies while being hunted, Dilophosaurus immediately flee towards the nearest hiding place silently to watch their ally be eaten from afar. Once sufficiently hidden, they  cry out in grief and sit together for comfort. A solitary Dilophosaurus always chooses their own life above all else, and will not take part in a hunt if they believe that the risk is too great and death likely. Dilophosaurus are vocal hunters and call excessively to distract their target and encourage their groupmates. Each grouping has their own set of calls with their own meanings. While leashes tend to be the most coordinated, masks struggle as most individuals in the group are still adapting to each individuals unique calls. Dilophosaurus' hunting strategy is quick darts to wound their target, before fleeing out of range. As coordinated hunters, they take turns landing harsh bites or claw attacks that cause their prey to bleed. An ideal hunt is taking the opportunity when prey panics in a blind stampede - the most exciting chase for these eager predators. While groups tend to form their own hunting strategies, the core structure of these hunts begin with an individual revealing themselves as bait and call loudly to draw the attention onto themselves. Concealed nearby, packmates dart in and out of cover to land bites and confuse prey. Dilophosaurus may attempt to hunt Apex carnivore offspring, but must always flee if adults come to protect it.
