# Thalassodromeus – Courtship & Parenthood

Thalassodromeus that have not yet found a mated pair and desire to court will travel away from their normal flock, and travel to the mountainous area in Whistling Columns, a universal courting ground for Thalassodromeus seeking out a mate. Once they have grouped there and there are other Thalassodromeus within their sights, males will find a good spot to perch on, and begin a series of loud screams to lure females towards him. From his spot, the screeching and squawking noises are accompanied by a dance, where the male will twist and turn from his perch, spreading his wings and bobbing his head to advertise his stamina, size and health. The flashier the male is, the more likely it is his performance will be successful, as even the weakest of dances can be overlooked if a male has particularly attractive colors. Interested females will watch from above, gliding through the air so that they may get a better view of all males. If a female is especially enamored by a male, she will fly down to settle beside him, chirping affectionately towards him. If he accepts, he will stop his dance and they will groom each other before flying off. If he does not, however, the female will be rudely pecked, and the male resumes his courtship performance. Same sex pairs still act out the same courtship rituals - though more submissive males tend to act as the interested female partner, and more dominant females put on the courtship display for other females. Even pairs that have been paired together before will still travel to this region before nesting and initiate this courting behavior. The only difference is the female will usually sit beside the male from the start, and the male's courtship dance will be much more brief. On rare occasions, especially if a clutch has been unsuccessful more than once, a male may reject his female, or a female may fly away and settle to pair with another male. In these instances, the rejected partner is often left fairly hurt by this experience, and may end up ignoring and shunning their former partner and new mate. Once pairs have been settled, Thalassodromeus will fly together to find a branch each in Dried Lake. Collecting these first two branches are symbolic to the pairing, and are used by both individuals to construct their nest. Even same sex pairs will still construct a nest, and follow the typical route to Dried Lake to collect branches. Branches secured by both partners, they travel on to Big Quill Lake to find a suitable place together to nest. Thalassodromeus only nest in this region, and usually opt for somewhere on the island in the middle of the lake. Pairs opt to nest reasonably close by to other pairs, but must take care not to get too close to comfort. Nesting within 2 body lengths of another pair can provoke a battle that will result in one of the pairs being forced on from the nesting spot. Pairs that have formed friendships with other pairs, however, deliberately seek to place their nests as close together as possible, enjoying the closeness between the pairs. Pairs take it in turn to leave the nest and hunt or fish for food. They don't bring back food for their partner, and instead focus on being as quick as possible so that the other can switch with them on their return. Opposite sex pairs rarely adopt, but same sex pairs readily welcome orphans to raise. In cases of extreme closeness between friendship pairs, an opposite sex pair may opt to donate one of their eggs to the same sex pair to hatch as their own. This is only ever one egg, and only to those they have close friends with. However, once chicks hatch, both parents fly back and forth in what seems to be a constant need to ferry food back to their very loud offspring. The louder the offspring, the better the chances the hatchling has of being fed. Parents always feed their noisiest chicks first, and seem to almost neglect the more subdued chicks they have in the nest. Chicks that fall from the nest before juvenile may still be cared for by parents if the parents can hear it, and it doesn't get disturbed by other dinosaurs. Parents encourage offspring to begin flying once they reach juvenile, and will nudge and chirp at their children to tempt them into the air. Once their offspring reach adolescence, the flock will take to the skies and leave the nesting grounds, and resume their nomadic lifestyle. Parents continue providing their offspring with food until they reach Sub-Adult. After which, their offspring are permitted to stay within the flock, but parents may prefer to go back to indulging in their friendships with other pairs, rather than showing lots of affection to their newly adult offspring.
