# Thalassodromeus – Social Interaction

Thalassodromeus are somewhat selfish creatures who gather together in large groups, called flocks. There is no overall pecking order with Thalassodromeus - individuals tend to pair off into mated pairs, and keep their select few friends and relatives closest to them. Thalassodromeus pair deeply with another individual - usually opposite sex, but same sex pairings also form - and prefer to spend all of their time with them. Along the way, they collect an assortment of friends. Thalassodromeus are quite standoffish with others, so they tend to keep close friends to another pair or two at most, but once bonded and having nested or raised young alongside each other, their bonds deepen, with multiple friendship pairs roosting close together and showing each other affection. Offspring is readily welcomed and shown affection by parents - but once they reach adulthood, Thalassodromeus pairs sometimes end up preferring the company of their partner and friendships - resulting in them even driving offspring away from their perch when the flock settles to rest, in favor of their friends. Thalassodromeus group together for mutual protection and general company first and foremost. No Thalassodromeus wants to be alone long term, and even those without a pairbond or friendships instinctively start to long for company and warm up to other strangers. Typically, most Thalassodromeus are quite docile and social with others - but are prone to holding grudges. If another Thalassodromeus offends another in some way, it can take quite some time for them to warm back up to each other. In extreme cases, individuals can remain cold towards one another indefinitely. This is most commonly seen when former partners are caught nesting with others. Both the new mate and the former partner will be ignored by the original Thalassodromeus, and any future offspring they produce are chased away from its personal space.
