# Ceratosaurus – Social Interaction

Males and females are relatively equal in a pack, females prefer patrolling with company while males prefer patrolling alone but this depends on individual preference more than set gender roles. Ceratosaurus is not an overly-affectionate species, but pack loyalty runs deeply in their veins. Pairs will exhibit loving and caring behaviour only during courtship and towards whelps that are adolescent and younger. At dawn, the pack gathers at their chosen den-site and curls up together, greeting one another with friendly calls. Within their pack, Ceratosaurus are vocal and friendly with one another, communicating in chuffs, grunts, growls and snorts. Playful individuals engage in mock fighting with youngsters, and sometimes supplement their greeting grunts with a cheeky head bobbing. Friendships are built within the pack through teamwork; hunting together, sharing food, and taking care of whelps. Individuals that have a strong connection with one another spend more time in each other’s personal space, eating directly next to one another, shaking their heads against each other’s back, and communicating with soft calls. While Ceratosaurus do not pair up other than temporarily for courting, two individuals may form a closer bond than friendship and this can include same sex pairings. Ceratosaurus engage in frequent play fights as a way to bond and sharpen their skills. These interactions include roughhousing, shoving one another, and playful nips. Sometimes in these play fights they will push each other into lakes or rivers, turning their games into chaotic splash-filled brawls. When the play gets too intense, other pack members will push between the fighting pair and alarm-call, ending of the scrap and ensuring no life-threatening injuries occur. Despite their aggressive play style, Ceratosaurus hold no grudges, maintaining harmony in their pack structure. A lone individual or pair wishing to join an existing Ceratosaurus pack will wait at the edge of the POI for the right introduction opportunity. Ceratosaurus packs are generally inquisitive about new members, and will broadcast in excitement at finding a potential rival or prospective new member. If there is space in the group for new members, a Ceratosaurus pack will usually accept new members after a careful inspection. New members bringing gifts of meat are welcomed more readily as they have proven themselves a successful hunter who will contribute to the pack’s survival. Neutral interactions between two packs (see Aggressive Interaction) can very rarely result in a swapping of members - if the other pack has space for them. One member may be impressed with the strength of the other pack, and choose to leave their current pack to seek more courting opportunities. The pack member who wishes to switch packs will move forward between the two packs, and stretch out their body, lifting their nose into the air. The other pack watches this carefully and either accepts with friendly calls, or rejects with alarm calls. Before the Ceratosaurus switches their pack loyalty, they turn back to their old pack and the members gather around shaking their heads softly against their body in farewell, wishing them well with their new adventure.
