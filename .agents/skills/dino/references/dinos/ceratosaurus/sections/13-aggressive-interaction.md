# Ceratosaurus – Aggressive Interaction

When two packs meet, they bunch together in their separate packs and perform fierce displays to one another from a short distance away. These displays include biting at the air with a dramatic delayed bite, broadcasting their voices, and aggressively calling. Any whelps under the age of Adolescent remain hidden in the den-site. A smaller pack will always back away and retreat, shaking off the encounter, and choosing to move to another POI to reside in. If pack numbers are equally matched, they will broadcast repeatedly and eventually both retreat in separate directions. Packs are proud, but ultimately respectful of one another and will always keep to separate POIs to prevent clashing for hunting opportunities. Ceratosaurus packs never actually engage with one another; they are not territorial or aggressive in nature, but equally they do not share a POI with other packs due to the availability of prey. Aside from play fighting, no serious aggression exists within the pack; individuals that are courting are always given their privacy and there are never any challenges over mates. Ceratosaurus do not kill one another, except with orphaned offspring that are discovered outside of the den-site. The corpse is not eaten, as they are not cannibalistic.
