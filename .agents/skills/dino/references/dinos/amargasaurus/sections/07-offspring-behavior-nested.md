# Amargasaurus – Offspring Behavior (Nested)

Young Amargasaurus are delicate and highly vulnerable to accidental trampling. Immediately after hatching, they are mobile and bleat loudly for a response. Hatchling in dense forests, hatchling Amargasaurus are temporarily separated from their caregivers. After locating the direction of their calling herd, the hatchlings scamper towards the friendly sounds of adult individuals. As social animals, young Amargasaurus frequently call out for reassurance. Once introduced to their caregivers and herdmates, they begin establishing their place in the group through playful activities such as chasing, mimicking adult behaviors, and picking up objects with their mouths. These early bonds are vital for their social development. Offspring that struggle to play or interact with others often elicit frantic cries from attentive adults, which can alarm the entire herd. This causes the group to investigate in confusion and concern. Other herd members may attempt to help the frantic adult, taking turns encouraging the seemingly sickly offspring to eat and move. Amargasaurus grow exceptionally quickly, transitioning from Hatchling all the way to Sub-Adult in a short time. By early adulthood, young Amargasaurus are already well integrated into the herd. Both male and female youngsters exhibit feisty personalities during this stage, using head-rearing and tail-whipping to draw attention. Females, in particular, tease others - whipping their tails and calling affectionately before darting away to entice a chase, seeming to enjoy the attention when pursued. As they mature into adulthood, this playful flirtation may persist or diminish, depending on the individual.
