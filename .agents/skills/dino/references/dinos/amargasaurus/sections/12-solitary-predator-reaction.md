# Amargasaurus – Solitary Predator Reaction

When alone, an Amargasaurus may use another sauropod species as a meat-shield if they are available - keeping them between the Amargasaurus and the predator. If they encroach on another sauropod's personal space and are aggressed away, Amargasaurus always distance themselves to avoid being hit - often opting to try to flee or hide in nearby woodland. If no other sauropods are nearby, a solitary Amargasaurus will react anxiously, rearing their head and whipping their tail in displeasure. If attacked by a predator, the individual will respond in their own unique way; some turn and flee, while others stand their ground. Amargasaurus - when alone - do not chase down fleeing predators, but may charge to drive them away from them, backing off once they retreat. They sometimes take the opportunity to move towards a more defensible position if it might increase survival chances.
