# Amargasaurus – Herd Behavior

Amargasaurus are herd animals, typically traveling in large groups alongside other sauropod species. They prefer the company of larger sauropods, relying on their size for protection by using them as a "meat shield" against predators. Both male and female Amargasaurus are friendly with each other and harbor no ill will or resentment. Individuals bond with those they spend the most time with, and readily respond to their calls. The closer the bond between individuals, the more likely they are to forage side by side, sleep with their backs resting together,  and play games of chase. This can be between females, males or a mixture. Amargasaurus does not necessarily have a single, dedicated leader. The herd roles shift, with different individuals taking the lead in different situations. Some are relied on during times of predator reaction, while others are followed to food and water. Amargasaurus that form close friendships with others readily follow their friends and the herd movements fluidly shift as allies rejoin the migration.
