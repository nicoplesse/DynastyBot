# Amargasaurus – Parenthood & Adoption

When the first offspring hatches, its initial movements in the nest prompts its siblings to begin emerging from their eggs. Once all hatchlings have pipped, they call out for adults, who respond with excited wails to guide them. Following the herd's voice, the hatchlings stumble out from the dense foliage and greet their waiting herd. All adults rush to greet the new generation and particularly broody individuals or pairs may try to lure young away with them. Not every Amargasaurus is fond of offspring, but those who are happily mimic the chirps of young. Both males and females adopt these newly hatched Amargasaurus and form no paternal bonds with the eggs they laid. All hatchlings are an equal opportunity to parenthood. Hatchlings closely follow their chosen caretaker, who provides constant guidance until they reach adulthood. Adoptive caregivers play an active role in teaching young how to forage. They begin by “hand-rearing,” gently nipping off small clusters of berries to feed each offspring, helping them learn how to gather food on their own. This attentive care ensures the young develop the skills necessary for survival.
