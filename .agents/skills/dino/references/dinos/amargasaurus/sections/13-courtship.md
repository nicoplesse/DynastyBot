# Amargasaurus – Courtship

When ready to breed, Amargasaurus fill their bellies with berries in preparation for laying eggs and courting. The berries contain astaxanthin, which is transferred from their stomach to their flesh and skin. This biological release of chemicals is what makes broody individuals flush into vibrant colors along their skin. When their colorful skins are fully flushed, Amargasaurus begin the instinctual migration to their nesting grounds. All Amargasaurus return to the same location each time and instinctively follow those leaving to lay their eggs. Albino and melanistic Amargasaurus, unable to flush into vibrant colors, experience difficulty during courtship, but still display with calls during the migration and may still be accepted as mates. Not every Amargasaurus participate in nesting, depending on how much food they managed to consume beforehand. Along the way, males mingle between migrating females, hoping to impress them with their bright colors and calls. If a female is impressed by an approaching male she will move slightly away from the herd - if he follows, the two briefly pair with affectionate calls and walk together for a while, before rejoining the herd again. Amargasaurus form promiscuous mating bonds, with females often accepting the advances of multiple males. As a result, a single clutch of eggs can hatch offspring with different sires, increasing genetic diversity within the herd. Amargasaurus are not picky with whom they bond with, and do not grow jealous of each other. Even if a friendship is between individuals of the opposite sex, this does not hinder their herdmates chances of nesting with others. Even if arriving at the nesting grounds unpaired, a courting individual may still find another within this area, sometimes even waiting multiple days before moving on unsuccessfully.
