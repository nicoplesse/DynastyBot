# Amargasaurus – Orphaned Behavior (Wild Spawn)

As a friendly species, orphaned baby Amargasaurus cry out in search of other sauropods or herbivores it can keep up with. When another herbivore species is identified, it rushes towards them, quickly falling in line behind an adult. Unless aggressed, Amargasaurus orphans are persistent at following whatever herbivore species will accept them. If they manage to come across other sauropods, they will ditch other herbivore species in favor of any sauropod species that welcomes them. Orphaned Amargasaurus follow herbivore migration routes, constantly searching for companions to bond with. As an adult, Amargasaurus orphans face no social or behavioral difficulties. Amargasaurus thrive in a variety of biomes, from floodplains to semi-arid regions and dense woodland forests. When not traveling alongside other species, they tend to migrate along sauropod migration routes. Preferring to graze during the day, Amargasaurus have teeth well-suited for pulling berries from low bushes and swallowing their food whole. Young Amargasaurus often struggle with foraging and rely on their caretakers for guidance. Adults will bring small mouthfuls of food to their calves, almost mimicking hand-feeding, and encourage them to pick off smaller portions, making it easier for the young to swallow. Grazing Amargasaurus are highly social and vocal, frequently calling out to one another. They are uneasy when alone and require constant reassurance of their herd's presence. During the warmer parts of the day, they particularly enjoy walking through and wallowing in shallow water, letting it cool their skin, and usually choose to nest nearby.
