# Kentrosaurus – Predator Reaction

The very first reaction all Kentrosaurus have when spotting a predator is to spit. Spits are aimed directly at the predator as the Kentrosaurus runs from it to alert the rest of the bristle or herd, swinging its tail behind it. Once the herd is alerted to the presence of the predator, babies and elderly/injured members also spit, before running and scattering, trying to find somewhere nearby to hide. Babies or weak/elderly individuals may run towards Miragaia if part of a stegosaurid herd, and the Miragaia are adopting their defensive stance. This provides a quick opportunity for young and the weak to get immediate protection, surrounded by these larger herbivores. Kentrosaurus keep moving when faced with predators. Staying in one spot - unless hiding as a baby or weak individual - is usually fatal for Kentrosaurus, so they make sure to stay on their feet, swinging their tails and spitting at their opponents. If faced with Apex carnivores, and in a Kentrosaurus-only group, all Kentrosaurus will opt to flee rather than defend. Kentrosaurus utilise Stegosaurus as meat shields where possible, enjoying their protection when part of a stegosaurid herd. When any member of the stegosaurid herd is hunted, up to 4 Kentrosaurus may join the engagement. When faced with Apex carnivores, Kentrosaurus in a bristle and not grouped with other stegosaurids prefer to flee, only fighting back when cornered. Offspring are always defended by their mother.
