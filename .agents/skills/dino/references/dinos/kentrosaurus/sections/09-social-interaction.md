# Kentrosaurus – Social Interaction

Kentrosaurus travel in relatively loose social groups with a roughly even gender ratio. Sometimes, this gender ratio can be heavily skewed towards one gender. This is tolerated for the majority of the time, but may cause groups to break up if they encounter others of the opposite gender, and form new groups. Both males and females show equal levels of affection to each other - including towards others of the same gender as them. Mutual nuzzling, or gentle prodding with their snouts is a common sign of tenderness. Same sex pairs do form, with those individuals opting to spend the majority of their time together. They may still nest with the opposite sex from time to time. Even adults enjoy playful behaviors - with individuals landing a well-placed tail swipe on another's rump before sprinting away, prompting the target to turn and chase them. Both parties seem to thoroughly enjoy this play, and have even been observed taking it in turns, almost like a game of "tag". Bristle members take it in turns to catch naps throughout the day and night, often closely watched over by another who will patrol around them, ready to signal for threats. Typically, smaller pairs can start to form within a bristle - but these are not mated pairs; instead pairings based around friendship. When traveling, the bristle opt to stay close together, corralling young and elderly individuals towards the middle to keep them protected from threats. Kentrosaurus are wholly social with one another - but sometimes have a sudden mood change, prompting an otherwise gregarious individual to suddenly withdraw, and take themselves to a quieter place away from the others. This behavior is confusing for other Kentrosaurus, but all Kentrosaurus experience these mood swings throughout their lives - even as adults. It is thought that a fluctuation of hormones - usually before nesting periods - is responsible for these temporary changes. Just as quickly as they change, however, the mood dissipates, and Kentrosaurus resume their normal gregarious selves.
