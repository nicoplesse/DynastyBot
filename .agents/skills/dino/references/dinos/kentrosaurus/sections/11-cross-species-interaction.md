# Kentrosaurus – Cross-Species Interaction

Kentrosaurus form close bonds with other stegosaurid species - such as Miragaia and Stegosaurus. Miragaia may group with Kentrosaurus up to their own species group limit, and are frequently welcomed by Kentrosaurus bristles to form a stegosaurid herd. Once part of a herd, Miragaia are considered faithful group members, and defended the same as if they were Kentrosaurus themselves. Miragaia are a very parental species, and Kentrosaurus encourage their young from juvenile age to flock towards them, enabling them more eyes and ears to keep tabs on rambunctious offspring. Kentrosaurus willingly share food and space with Miragaia, and defend them from threats if there are no Stegosaurus protectors. Stegosaurus may group with Kentrosaurus up to their own species group limit, and are frequently welcomed by Kentrosaurus bristles to form a stegosaurid herd. Once part of a herd, Stegosaurus are considered faithful group members, and defended the same as if they were Kentrosaurus themselves. Stegosaurus are impeccable heavyweights and a highly valuable contribution to a stegosaurid herd. Kentrosaurus benefit under the protection from their stronger cousins, and repay the favor by staying alert, and acting as a sentry species. Kentrosaurus willingly share food and space with Stegosaurus, and defend them from threats if they are alone. When another stegosaurid is spotted: Kentrosaurus will respond with a broadcast call :broadcast_call:  when alerted to an ungrouped stegosauruid spotted near their herd. Kentrosaurus do not get involved in squabbles between Miragaia and Stegosaurus for positions in the stegosaurid herd. They merely watch from a distance, and do not intervene. Challenges between two stegosaurid herds: These are fought between the lead Stegosaurus bulls of the herd. Grouped Miragaia and Kentrosaurus stand at the sidelines in support of their lead bull. If no Stegosaurus are in the challenging herd, the herd with the stegosaurus will win by default and the loser must abandon the POI. If no Stegosaurus exist in either herd, the Miragaia fleet will challenge in their own profile specific aggression reactions to determine which herd wins the right to remain in the POI. Aggression towards other species by the stegosaurid herd: The stegosaurid herd does not tolerate other carnivores and herbivores spotted in line of sight of the group. When found, all members of the stegosaurid herd aggressively ward off the invader with bellows and threatening displays. If the other species responds with aggression, does not respond at all, or does not move out of LOS, the entire stegosaurid herd will engage in a challenge for the right to exist in the POI. If either side forfeits ( :friendly_call: ) or a body has dropped - the engagement ends and the loser is forced to abandon the area and move on. Aggression towards other species by a Kentrosaurus-only group: All other species - including small carnivores and herbivores - are met with aggression. Any species that is spotted within line of sight of the bristle is aggressed with aggressive :threaten_call: calls, and if not responsive enough to leave line of sight they will be chased out by up to 4 Kentrosaurus. Should the chasing not work, they may enter an engagement. They can forfeit at any time, but are forced to forfeit when one member of the group reaches deathscars. If the other species is territorial, the bristle is forced to leave the POI. If they are not, and only wish to consume resources in the area, Kentrosaurus cannot push them out again until the species timer is up, and is forced to tolerate their presence near them or move on themselves. Kentrosaurus do not attempt to do this with Apex sauropod species, such as Apatosaurus, Ampelosaurus or Argentinosaurus. They recognize the potential threat these species could pose to them in an engagement, and begrudgingly tolerate them in their vicinity. They will, however, still aggressively call at them to show their displeasure. Allosaurus provoke a highly aggressive response in Kentrosaurus, and are the priority target for Kentrosaurus to drive away from a stegosaurid herd if multiple species are within the POI.
