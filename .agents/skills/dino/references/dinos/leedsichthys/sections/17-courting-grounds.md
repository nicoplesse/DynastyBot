# Leedsichthys – Courting Grounds

When driven to nest, adult Leedsichthys migrate towards their established courting grounds, found within the waters between Big Tree Overlook and Cedrus Forest. This interest is particularly palpable immediately after heavy rain. Males usually arrive first, and begin searching the courting grounds for the best location to place their nest. Once selected, a male remains close to his nest and begins displaying to attract the attention of passing females. A nest placed close to a source of krill is considered highly desirable. Courting males become considerably more vibrant, flushing their colours brighter in an attempt to make themselves more noticeable amongst competing males. Females exhibit little interest in offspring and do not possess the strong parental instincts seen in males. Instead, reproductive instincts periodically draw adult females towards the courting grounds, where they seek males and eventually lay their eggs. Males entice females towards them and their nest through elaborate swimming displays. They circle around their nest, weave back and forth below it, and slap their tails above the surface. Females move slowly throughout the courting grounds, inspecting both the males and their nests before making their choice. They may linger around particularly interesting displays, before continuing on to inspect other available mates. A female interested in a male approaches his nest, calling affectionately. If they both accept one another, the female swims onto her side (? emote) and circles around his nest. Females are not monogamous, and may choose several different males during the same nesting period. A female chooses how many eggs she may lay with a particular male, up to her egg limit for the season. Some females may lay all of her eggs with one male, while others may opt to leave eggs with multiple males. Males, likewise, readily accept courtship advances from multiple females that show interest in them.
