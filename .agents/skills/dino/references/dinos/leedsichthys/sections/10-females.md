# Leedsichthys – Females

Females are observed to be generally more sociable than males, although there is still variation between individuals. Some females develop a preference for travelling alongside another female, forming long lasting companionships as they migrate between feeding grounds. Female companions often swim alongside or within sight of one another, following each other as they continue their migrations. These bonds are passive and non-competitive. Neither female is dominant over the other, and either may determine the direction which she needs to travel. Affection is loosely shown by parallel swimming, gentle nudges and bodily contact, and deliberately remaining near one another during rest periods. Females express interest in anything that is brightly colored. Leedsichthys females will observe brightly colored species from a distance, even briefly bringing their heads above water to observe species that linger on the shore, or following behind other aquatic species that exhibit bright colors. Objects, on the other hand, are picked up and collected in hoards. Females have particular favor for items that are colored similarly to males they have nested with, or those they have bonds with. In relation to this behavior, females are observed occasionally breaching above the water in order to observe their surroundings and to spot bright colors.
