# Leedsichthys – Rhamphorhynchus

Leedsichthys are more than happy to receive a thorough scale cleaning from these small fishers, often drifting to sleep in the moment. At times, Leedsichthys may often find themselves gifted with a foreign item during interactions with Rhamphorhynchus. Lacking sense and overcome with intrigue, Leedsichthys’ first reaction to these gifts are an attempt to consume them, with varying levels of success. One Sub/Adult Leedsicthys may enter an engagement in defense of itself or grouped offspring. Leedsicthys offspring of Adolescent and below always flee with other offspring within their nursery group, flocking together in order to form a school. Using their matching coloration and speed, offspring dart away from predators until they are out of sight. If a predator reaches the center of the school, the school will always part and flee in two separate directions. All Leedsicthys offspring prioritize moving away from predators, not returning to their school or parent if separated. Leedsichthys males - if their offspring are attacked - attempt to block the predator’s path or chase after it, though often this proves futile, as their large bodies struggle to keep up with more agile species. Leedsichthys adults who are hunted often have no choice but to defend themselves. Using the force of their muscular tails and heads, they attempt to ward off their attackers. The usually slower speed Leedsichthys maintains changes rapidly, able to curve and bend in on itself quickly, much to the surprise of predators who may assume an easy meal of Leedsichthys. Leedsichthys is defensive in nature and has no need to continue a hunt that has been forfeit by a predator. If Leedsichthys is attacked near deeper waters, they may attempt to lure the fight further into the depths of the ocean as some creatures cannot remain submerged long enough to continue to pursue Leedsichthys - instead forced to return to the surface for air. Leedsichthys offspring (Hatchling, Juvenile, and Adolescent) do not gain the tier of their parent when within two bodylengths of them. Adolescent Leedsichthys are still considered Large tier. Hatchling and Juvenile Leedsichthys have no tier and can be hunted by any carnivorous species.
