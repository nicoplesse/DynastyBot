# Suchomimus – Offspring Maturity

As their offspring reach sub adult, they drift away from them, but will continue to tolerate them, unless there is a claimed Spinosaurus present. In that case, they will chase the offspring off-- but with very little aggression, if they can help it. As Suchomimus age, they begin to grow less and less active, preferring to sleep and rest for extended periods on the shore. They become less overconfident and bold and grow quieter, preferring to find peaceful and warm resting places rather than to contest others for stomping grounds. More often than not, older Suchomimus rely on the support of their social bonds in order to feed and take care of themselves. Lonely or solitary Suchomimus will drift towards Spinosaurus for this protection as they did in their youth, and while tolerated in most cases, it depends on the Spinosaurus whether or not it will actually lend its care and support. Older Suchomimus may fade in color as a result of their age; often, Suchomimus may go deaf or blind from age, primarily relying on the support of others around them. When a bonded or young Suchomimus passes, Suchomimus will express a great deal of grief, nudging, nuzzling, and vocalizing towards the body to get it to stand. They prefer to not feed from the bodies of a loved one, and will often choose not to, even when hungry. None
