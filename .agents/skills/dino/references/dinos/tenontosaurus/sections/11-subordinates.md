# Tenontosaurus – Subordinates

Subordinates are other does within the glade. All subordinates must be submissive to the matriarch. When attempting to join a glade, newcomers attempt to win the matriarch’s favor by lowering their heads and avoiding eye contact, careful to stay low to the ground, highlighting their submission. If the Matriarch deems them to be suitably submissive, she will welcome the other female to live as a subordinate within her glade - though she is careful to keep an eye on the interactions the new female has with the bucks. Subordinate females must accept this position in order to remain in the glade, or else leave to start a glade of their own. Plenty of does are content to remain as a subordinate indefinitely - despite their lower status, they gain safety and access to food, alongside socialization with others of their species. Subordinates hold respect for the matriarch, and rely on her to keep the glade organized and safe. Subordinates undertake the majority of responsibilities concerning territory maintenance. Because patrolling the territory can be arduous, subordinate does often work in pairs to increase coverage while ensuring mutual safety. Throughout their patrol of the territory's edge, does claw, kick and rub against trees and trunks, ensuring rivals or any other threats are aware of the danger that they would face if venturing further into the territory. Through patrolling in these duos, subordinates may begin to form bonds. These bonds are built on respect for the other's behaviour rather than affection. Two does who have continuously maintained trust with each other may be more affectionate. Upon arrival of a new doe, the current subordinates are cold initially and avoid contact - sometimes even nipping them away from food. Despite the coldness, the new female will be invited to patrol so they may observe her behavior. Subordinates may refuse to patrol with does they deem as incompetent or immature. Subordinates bond easily with bucks, and happily engage with their playful antics. They especially spend time with males that have not gained the matriarch's favor. In the matriarch's temporary absence, the most respected subordinates take on the task of organizing patrols. Within a glade, the bonds between does are fragile and usually quick to break. This is due to their temperament and natural aggression, which - while serving as the main way they protect themselves - can also cause a hindrance to maintaining social relationships within the glade. Subordinates fall victim to the outburst of another subordinate most often. Arguments can arise over decisions, sleeping spots, or food - and sometimes even over the company of a buck. Easily irritable, they become increasingly annoyed by their supposed companions if they feel they have been crossed too often. Though vocally threatening, these arguments are generally harmless - a few swipes may be exchanged, but otherwise, any nearby buck quickly steps in to soothe the does. Some more aggressive subordinates struggle to listen to reason, and hammer one another with harsher strikes. This usually requires the Matriarch to step in, or else does decide for themselves how far to take an argument.
