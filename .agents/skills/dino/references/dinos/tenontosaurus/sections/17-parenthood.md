# Tenontosaurus – Parenthood

After the matriarch's nest has been built, bucks move to place one of their own in a defensive position, surrounding the primary nest. Does are indifferent parents, and focus more on maintaining the group's wellbeing and leadership. In contrast, bucks display an innate parental drive, and actively engage in nurturing behaviors, often forming strong bonds with offspring. They are attentive to the needs of the glade's young, offering protection, guidance, and affection, and play an active role in their upbringing. Bucks prioritize the well-being of offspring and are willing to make sacrifices to ensure their survival. Although males consistently showcase a more nurturing side, does occasionally check up on offspring to ensure they are well groomed and fed. Signs of neglect are immediately met with aggression towards the bucks responsible. A doe will begrudgingly show a more parental side towards young if the situation requires it of her, but always opts to leave this in the hands of the bucks. However - if the matriarch notices pale or sickly offspring under the age of Sub-Adult, she will cull them. Likewise, males will still shun and chase away fawns that behave sickly, to prioritize the rest of the group.
