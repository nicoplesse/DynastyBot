# Tenontosaurus – Aggressive Interaction

Overthrowing a Matriarch If a stranger encroaches on another doe's domain with the desire to take her glade, she will approach the glade with her head held high, broadcasting loudly to draw the attention of the Matriarch. The Matriarch, enraged by the intrusion, will greet the newcomer, and the pair will begin circling, letting out low, aggressive rumbles. After some time vocalizing and circling, both females clash physically. They ram into each other's bodies, slam their tails at each other, and nip and kick to battle their rival into submission. These battles continue until one female either willingly forfeits, or reaches deathscars and is forced to withdraw. The female that forfeits is chased off by the victor and the rest of the glade. Despite this, these types of disputes are rare, and the majority end before an engagement begins. Heavily scarred does, or does leading very large glades, are seen as more experienced, and very intimidating. Subordinates expelling their matriarch Subordinates within a glade only chase out the matriarch if she shows repeated and severe demonstrations of inexperience. Otherwise, subordinates deeply respect a well-behaving matriarch. If, collectively, the subordinates decide to expel their matriarch, all does will band together as a united front, nipping and swiping at the Matriarch to chase her from the territory. After the matriarch is chased out, the glade falls into a chaotic and unorganized state until a respected doe emerges to take over her duties as the new matriarch. This is not a violent affair, as subordinates are overall submissive, and adverse to losing their spot within the glade. Instead, the glade naturally gravitates towards the most trusted female. It may take the glade a few days to decide, making them particularly vulnerable to outside does forcefully taking over during this time. Intruders If an intruder is spotted on patrol, the subordinates call out in distressingly loud calls, echoing through the territory to warn the matriarch. If the matriarch perceives the warning call, she quickly spreads word to the bucks and any young they might be raising before moving to the spot of danger. In the process, the Matriarch loudly expresses her position, serving to inform the other subordinates of the threat, and to be ready to follow and defend against it. In the event that a warning call is left unanswered, the does will risk splitting: one will warn the matriarch, while the other tries to keep the threat distracted. However, this option is considered to be extremely risky, sometimes ending with the subordinate facing the threat alone, and becoming gravely injured in her attempted defense. After carefully assessing an intruder, the matriarch communicates her decision to the glade. Intruders deemed too dangerous are initially given an intimidating display with repeated tail slams and loud calls. Unfortunately, this display sometimes fails, allowing the intruder to continue to infiltrate the territory. Should the matriarch be unreachable, the subordinates decide how to handle the threat alone. Both subordinates and the matriarch will destroy all unattended nests found within their territory.
