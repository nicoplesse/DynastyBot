# Tenontosaurus – Courtship

Courtship is a fairly passive process for Tenontosaurus. The matriarch may choose up to 5 males to nest with depending on the season, and number of suitable males. She does not exceed the egg limits for the species, but may opt to have split paternity for her nest to ensure genetic diversity for her fawns. The matriarch is the only female which may openly nest, and she will expel any subordinates caught attempting to nest. Each buck within a glade bids for the matriarch's attention. In attempts to gain her favor, they take care to spend time grooming her, bring her gifts of food or interesting objects, and show off their fitness with stretches and small dances. A common behavior of bucks is crouching low to the ground and nibbling at the matriarch's chin, in a gesture known as "cobbing", which serves additionally as a show of submission. When the matriarch rests or settles down to sleep, bucks excitedly push to be the first to lay next to her. They briefly shove and kick the air near each other to battle for the best spot, before settling down beside her. If bucks become too rowdy as the matriarch tries to rest, she will irritatedly grunt and move away from them, refusing to lay next to the males. Bucks act similarly whenever greeting the matriarch as she returns, crowding around her and shoving against each other to cobble and show her affection. Males are never overly aggressive when they scuffle towards the matriarch, as those which attempt to cause harm or excessively shove will never be selected as mates. The matriarch assesses and chooses her mates based on various behaviors she observes in day-to-day life. She searches for parental and gentle behavior, submission, care for the glade, and playful bucks that possess healthy behaviour. She particularly enjoys males that are the first to greet, lay next to her and groom. Bucks that show aggressive behavior within the glade, behave recklessly, or do not have a parental nature are always rejected and even expelled from the glade in extreme cases. The matriarch does not tolerate a lack of submission, and considers such males unfit to raise young. After choosing her bucks, the matriarch approaches each one individually and crouches to the ground in front of them, before reaching her head up and rubbing her neck against them. The males begin following her after this action, as she will lead them to the nesting site within the territory, urging the males to gather resources. All bucks - even those that were not chosen - pitch in to help construct the nests to try and gain favor.
