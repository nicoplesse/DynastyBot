# Austroraptor – Parenthood

Once all the offspring are invited to the nest, both parents will abandon their nest to go fishing. This is to provide their offspring with their first sufficient meal upon hatching. Once both have found a fish or a suitable critter, parents will then return to the nest to invite offspring in the nest to group. Both parents take part in raising the young and do not rely on the rest of the siege to rear their offspring. First time parents may take longer to soothe their chicks, overwhelmed by continuous wailing, if none of their attempts soothe them. They may sometimes become frustrated and exhibit this by leaving the nest to go fishing with their mate. More experienced parents are quick to try every method they can, often rotating between preening, offering different types of food, and keeping the chicks warm. Austroraptor are protective parents, often eyeing others that come near the nest. Only when they are within a body length with they immediately hiss at the offending Austroraptor who will immediately move away. Should a predator approach the nest, Austroraptor will pick up their chicks and carry them in their mouths to rush them to safety, whether it is swimming into water or rushing to hide them in foliage. Austroraptor parents will both simultaneously curl around chicks in a nest in order to ensure they stay warm during rain, nighttime, snow, or other cold weather. If offspring below juvenile enter the water, now stressed parents immediately run to snatch the child out, dropping it back into the nest. They will aggressively preen the chick to ensure the water does not seep into their down and freeze the chick. Once chicks are adolescent, parents teach them the essential fishing, swimming, and hunting behaviors that will help them grow to be capable adults. This is often done through “games” and even pushing chicks into the water to get them past their initial nerves. Parental Stress: Clutches that exceed 4 eggs cause stress in pairs. This results in selective reduction by parents, either by chasing individuals from the nest or slaughtering them outright. Pairs may also attempt conspecific brood parasitism if they have more chicks in their nest than they feel able to comfortably support. Conspecific Brood Parasitism: When an Austroraptor nest exceeds 4 offspring, pairs will become extremely stressed. In an attempt to reduce the stress and increase the survival of their offspring, Austroraptor take part in conspecific brood parasitism. When pairs nest at the same time as another pair, a pair with 5 chicks will likely seek an opportunity to place their extra offspring in another pair's nest. Gently clamping the newborn chick, one of the parents will take the opportunity when the other pair have left to fish, sneaking the offspring into the nest and fleeing. A chick who has been grabbed and deposited in another nest will not struggle or flee the nest they were placed in, comfortable with being picked up and moved into another warm nest. If there are already 4 offspring in the nest when the biological pair bring their offspring over, they will swap it with one of the host offspring. After selecting one of these offspring, Austroraptor will take the chick to nearby water, submerging it in an attempt to drown it or shaking it to death. Chicks who are grabbed from their nest do not attempt to escape the grasp of an adult, trusting the adults around them. Other members of the siege do not interfere with this behavior. Should the owners of the potential host nest catch the biological parent attempting this, they will begin a Standoff. In order to do this, the parent carrying the offspring must drop the offspring, which does not move away and remains where it was placed. Should the biological parents lose the standoff, the chick will be killed by the potential hosts and fed to the potential host’s nest. Should the biological parents win, the chick will not be killed by the potential host and will be attempted to be placed into another nest. If no other host is available, the parents may resort to killing the chick. Other members of the siege do not interfere with this behavior.
