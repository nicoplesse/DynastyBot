# Austroraptor – Sieges

Austroraptor commonly gather together, remaining in the same area within a POI. These larger groups are called sieges. While Austroraptor only take comfort in their pair, sieges offer protection in numbers. When spotting a predator in the area, members in a siege begin to call out, alerting the other Austroraptor in the area of it’s presence. This alarm system can either indicate a larger meal, or danger. Sieges are advantageous for Austroraptor should larger prey be available. Four Austroraptor will group temporarily to work together in taking down prey. Once the kill is complete, the individuals who participated in the hunt will eat first. Other Austroraptor in the siege may attempt to eat from this body at the risk of being aggressed or nipped at by the other Austroraptor. If aggressed, the scavenging Austroraptor will move away and do not persist in feeding from the body. Pairs and individuals, if outside the courting grounds, do not intrude on each others personal space unless they have recognized each other as family. Sieges do not hold a strict hierarchy, though dominant individuals are often seen staring harshly at others. Most commonly occurring when themselves or their mate is approached by other Austroraptor. Submissive individuals will often turn their heads away from these harsh stares and make sure to give the other a wide berth when moving about them.
