# Austroraptor – Mated Pairs

Austroraptor rely heavily on their mate, as this is the individual they remain closest to through their lives. They are constantly close to one another, and rarely if ever leave one another’s sides. Mated pairs are often seen fishing and swimming together, diving and manoeuvring with each other in unison. Austroraptor does not find value in gifts that are not food and will not use alternative items as prizes to impress their partner. Preening and soft chatter is frequent, with each pair having their unique way of communication. Males in a mated pair may even flare their feathers as they did in the courtship grounds, which is always appreciated by his mate. With prompting from the female, males may even become very excited and replicate their performances from the courtship grounds; which is always appreciated by his mate! Mated pairs are always extremely protective over one another, and do not tolerate offenses to their mate. This can result in grudges held against other species, especially those that injure their partner. Mated pairs always ensure that the other is well fed, and take turns fishing for one another or presenting critters. Mates constantly reaffirm their bonds through frequent chatter that is unique between the pair. Dominant x Dominant Personality Pairs: Mated pairs of Austroraptor who are both more dominant in personality are much louder together, frequently broadcasting to draw one another’s attention and to show off. These pairs will claim the best fishing spots in an area, and are very choosy about where they nest. They will defend these spots fiercely from outside interference. Dominant x dominant personality pairs will also snap at one another as a form of play. If any nips land accidentally, the offender rushes to make it up to the other, preening them apologetically, bringing them lots of food, and nuzzling them often. These pairs are often seen playing via competing with one another; whether it is running short distances, finding the biggest fish, or otherwise. Despite both being extremely confident and noisy, neither are found without the other, and will adamantly go searching if unable to find one another. Dominant x Submissive Personality Pairs: Pairings of both a dominant and submissive personality balance each other the best of all of the pairings, eventually helping the other to grow in confidence or to learn where the line is drawn. The more dominant of the pair typically learns better restraint, guided by the influence of their more careful and quieter mate; while the more submissive of the pair learns more steady confidence, willing to assert themselves where needed. Within this pairing resides a healthy balance, where both are able to fiercely defend their personal space and nesting areas, but are simultaneously capable of being loving, doting parents to their offspring. These pairs are inseparable, and are rarely seen apart. Submissive x Submissive Personality Pairs: Pairings consisting of more submissive personalities are the most quiet of all Austroraptor pairs, preferring to remain completely isolated and only with one another. These pairs often find comfortable resting places to snuggle up to one another and preen, their most consistent form of bonding. Submissive pairings form long lasting bonds and may exhibit anxiety when separated from one another, particularly if they have not heard one another’s flutes for an extended period of time. These pairs are also typically the best parents, constantly nuzzling, preening, and fussing over their offspring.
