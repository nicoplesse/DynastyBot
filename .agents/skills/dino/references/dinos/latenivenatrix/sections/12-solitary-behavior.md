# Latenivenatrix – Solitary Behavior

Although social by nature, Latenivenatrix are capable of functioning when solitary for extended periods. Solo individuals become quieter and more reserved, and show less communicative chatter or singing as opposed to those within a rookery. Solitary Latenivenatrix often trail behind herbivore herds or Apex carnivores that tolerate them. When permitted, they utilize opportunities to scavenge from kills, and patiently observe their surroundings. They remain nomadic, preferring not to settle in one location for long. They still place a nest down during the day which they rest in, but tend to place this in a less exposed - and more hidden - location for their own safety. Prolonged solitude often results in restlessness, and even a depressed nature. Latenivenatrix thrive with others of their kind, and instinctively seek to join a rookery when possible. Sometimes, extreme prolonged loneliness can result in reduced appetites - with individuals often refusing food, or showing little interest in hunting. Achillobator companions help to stave away feelings of loneliness, and solitary Latenivenatrix adapt better when accompanied by these larger carnivores.
