# Latenivenatrix – Rookeries

Latenivenatrix are an intensely social species, and most comfortable when surrounded by others of their kind. They form large, loosely structured groups known as rookeries, which can range from a handful of adults to larger groups of up to 16. Rookeries have no overall leader, and Latenivenatrix do not rely on any rigid hierarchy structure. Authority is situational, with individuals instinctively deferring to older and more experienced adults during moments of uncertainty. Decisions are made as a group within the rookery. Movement, resting sites, and response to threats are shaped through group reaction, as opposed to dominance and control seen in other species, such as Deinonychus. Social bonds within a rookery are fluid, but persistent. Individuals recognize and seem to remember one another, even after long separations from each other. Male and females show similar temperaments, with little differences in behavior between the genders. Aggression within a rookery is never tolerated. Hostility, prolonged bullying, or unnecessary violence is quickly corrected by the rest of the group, with episodes of extreme aggression resulting in expulsion from the rookery. While communal, Latenivenatrix form close knit pairs - or sometimes trios - within a rookery. These bonds are stable and often become lifelong, with bonded individuals resting and travelling closest to each other.
