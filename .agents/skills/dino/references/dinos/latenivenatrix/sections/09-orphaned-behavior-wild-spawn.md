# Latenivenatrix – Orphaned Behavior (Wild Spawn)

Orphaned Latenivenatrix show higher levels of caution and anxiety in comparison to chicks raised in a creche setting. From an early age, they are alert and subdued, and more hesitant to engage with unfamiliar surroundings. Without adults or siblings to assist them in thermoregulation, chicks instinctively seek physical contact with any creature willing to tolerate their presence. This is sometimes mistaken for a form of imprinting behavior in Latenivenatrix, but it is purely a survival-driven response to conserve body heat. If lucky enough to find a rookery, Latenivenatrix chicks are often swiftly adopted and placed within the communal creche. These chicks go on to suffer no ill effects of their earlier orphanhood, and integrate quickly into rookery life. Unfortunately, those that do not tend to develop exaggerated attachment behaviors, even as they mature. They persistently follow specific individuals, attempting to solicit grooming or feeding. Individuals that previously relied on another species for warmth may display similar attachments towards that species - or individual - later in life, though this does is not considered true imprinting behavior. Orphans mature faster out of necessity. Forced with being self-sufficient at a young age, they end up highly observant individuals - preferring to watch and assess situations carefully before acting. Play behavior in orphans is reduced or absent entirely in comparison to those with parents. Even when siblings remain together, their focus is placed firmly on survival, resulting in heightened vigilance and less time for play. By Adolescent, most orphans are capable solitary hunters, able to bring down small prey and critters in order to feed themselves. Latenivenatrix are a highly intelligent and alert species that have a constant awareness of their surroundings. Even at rest periods, individuals remain attentive, tracking movement and sound with their senses. Curiosity is a defining trait for this species. Latenivenatrix are drawn to novelty, and frequently investigate unfamiliar objects, species or environmental changes. They are known to move items both with their jaws and their arms - lifting and inspecting bones, branches, foliage and rocks with apparent interest. They show a fondness for dust baths, particularly along dry riverbanks and sandy clearings. These behaviors serve as both helpful feather maintenance and sensory stimulation. Vocalization is near constant. Healthy groups seem to echo one another in songs - communicating in a series of clicks, chirps and chattering noises. When one group member begins to "sing", it causes a ripple effect in the rest of the group, prompting even the most reserved member to join their groupmates. Nimble and restless, Latenivenatrix rarely remain still for long during their active period at night. Short bursts of movement are common, and help to hone their agility. Uniquely, Latenivenatrix often form a bond and mutualistic relationship with Achillobator. More can be found under Cross-Species Behavior.
