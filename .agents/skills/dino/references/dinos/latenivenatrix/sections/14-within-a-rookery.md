# Latenivenatrix – Within a rookery

Aggression between members of a rookery is rare, and strongly discouraged by social norms within the group. Most conflicts arise from resource pressure - especially when a rookery is large and space/food is limited - or jealousy over mates. These disputes are brief, and usually consist of screeches, hissing, posturing, and a few exchanged nips - as opposed to prolonged fights. On the rare occasions these disputes escalate into violence, the rest of the rookery quickly steps in to separate the fighting individuals, keen to smooth over relations before lasting damage is done. Those in pairbonds - or trios - are highly protective of one another. Any challenge or dispute aimed at their partners are met with a united response from all bonded members. Aggression displayed by those Adolescent and below is treated very differently. Young Latenivenatrix are forgiven easily for overstepping boundaries, as minor aggression is viewed as a natural part of their development. Such behavior is corrected gently through vocal reprimands, brief physical redirection, or a temporary withdrawal of attention - all of which are far more effective than a harsh correction. Violence towards chicks from adult members is never tolerated. Any adult that directs serious aggression or damage towards offspring is immediately confronted by the rookery, and may face exile if the behavior persists. Individuals who repeatedly disrupt group harmony - or are otherwise excessively aggressive to others - are not tolerated. If corrective behavior fails, the rookery drives the individual away, resulting in expulsion from the group. This begins with a form of shunning. Rookery members will turn their backs when approached, refuse grooming or vocal "chatter", and seek to body block the individual when the group is feeding. Over time, this escalates to aggressive calling, with the final outcome being bites and chasing until the individual leaves the area. Following any aggressive interaction, all members of the rookery show a strong tendency towards soothing behaviors. Group members gather closely together, engaging in mutual grooming and cooing for reassurance.
