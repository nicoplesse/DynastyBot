# Alioramus – Downtime Behavior

Alioramus are extremely quiet in the nighttime, communicating primarily through soft clicks, huffs, and body language. They stop migrating at this time, and often search for dens to take claim of in order to sleep in. If no dens are available, the next preferred choice is deep shrubbery or a cliffside. During inactive periods, males and females often separate entirely to sleep and socialize; with females remaining with their close bonds and offspring and males taking positions in order to watch over them. Males in a mixed-sex group do not spar at this time - those that attempt to are quickly corrected by the females. Females are often seen cuddled up as close as possible together - even if it means incidentally forcing their offspring a bit further away. Males may court females via giftgiving and preening during downtime. Alioramus exhibit strong social dimorphism, with males and females fulfilling distinct roles that stabilize one another within a pack. Balanced packs rely on the presence of both sexes and when one is absent, social cohesion deteriorates. Male-only groups trend toward escalating aggression and internal conflict, while female-only groups become overly passive, lacking the assertiveness required for defence and structure.
