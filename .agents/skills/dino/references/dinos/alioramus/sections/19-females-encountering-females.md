# Alioramus – Females Encountering Females

When bonded female pairs encounter other females, they take the lead in responding to the situation. Females approach the new females while males remain behind them, observing but not intervening. These encounters are tense, but restrained. The leading females posture, vocalizing warnings with sharp hisses and deep growls, and pace in front of them, showing a clear threat if the rival(s) do not leave. If warning displays are met with aggressive responses - or ignored entirely - females launch into a rare display of violence, engaging with the rivals directly to drive them out. Should the females lose this challenge, the pack destabilizes. Males may choose to disperse entirely and follow the new females instead, while others will stay loyal to the losing females and leave the area with them. Solitary females may be tolerated if they maintain significant distance and do not attempt to approach or integrate into the pack. Never grouping with them, the bonded pair may turn a blind eye to their presence, but will immediately aggress them should they get too close. This context - as well as during nesting and rearing offspring - represents the rare circumstances in which females display aggression to others. Solitary females encountering other solitary females approach cautiously, but show affection quickly if they appear to have similar or complementary temperaments, enabling them to form a lifelong bond.
