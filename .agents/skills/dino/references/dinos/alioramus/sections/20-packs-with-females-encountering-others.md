# Alioramus – Packs With Females Encountering Others

Encounters between 2 mixed sex packs are cautious and tense if neither are willing to leave the POI. Both groups slow, reducing their vocalizations, and allow their female pairs (or solo female) to assess one another from the front. Bonded females lead all aggression in these circumstances. Males remain behind their respective female pairs and do not posture or advance. If the other group is simply passing through, they may seek to avoid conflict and show non-hostile intent. Females lower their posture into crouches, offering soft coos to indicate submission rather than a challenge. Such displays are always respected. When submission is shown, the resident females accept the signal without escalation, and both groups disengage peacefully and continue on their way. Should the rival Alioramus be a solitary male - or group of males - how they are considered depends on whether the pack has space for more males - and how willing they are to accept new members. If the pack has no capacity for additional males and no males are willing to disperse, the bonded females take the lead in driving them off with warning displays - or direct the pack to leave the area, depending on the size of the other group. If these warnings are ignored or met with aggression, the males of the group step forward, launching into a vicious attack on the other group. The outcome of the conflict determines the result - should the resident pack lose, they are forced out of the POI. If the rival males lose, they are chased out of LOS by the resident males. If the pack has space for additional males, the females step forward to assess the males, and usually welcome them in quickly with soft shakes and gentle chuffs. Only when the females have accepted the males do other males allow them within the group.
