# Alioramus – Females

From the moment they hatch, female Alioramus are instinctively driven to find a lifelong female companion. These bonds - stronger even than those between mother and offspring - are most commonly formed between sisters raised together. However, unrelated females who cross paths and find comfort in one another can also develop this deep connection. Even orphaned or solitary females feel an undeniable pull toward companionship, and seek out another lonely female to form the bond they crave. Females exhibit heightened anxiety when isolated from a female companion, and actively seek a bonding opportunity throughout their lives. Bonding is reinforced through frequent physical contact. They walk close together, brushing against one another's sides, nuzzle faces and engage in quiet, cooperative feeding behaviors. They may hunt critters together, or take chunks from corpses and move a distance away to feed in privacy. More reserved by nature, females thrive on gentle companionship, and never show aggression to their companion. Their relationship with their chosen partner is paramount, and they remain fiercely bonded for life. The loss of this bond is devastating - when one female dies, the surviving partner may enter a prolonged mourning state, refusing to eat and - in some cases - succumbing to grief herself. Others, though deeply affected, will eventually seek a new female companion to rebuild the bond.
