# Alioramus – Parenthood - 1 Female

Solitary females are capable of raising offspring alone, but experience considerably higher stress levels throughout nesting and early cub rearing. Solitary mothers spend significantly less time with their offspring than bonded females. They leave the nest frequently and for extended periods, often to feed, patrol, or simply distance themselves from the stress of constant care. Cubs raised by a single female are frequently left unattended, resulting in heightened distress and increased vocalization when she returns - adding to her stress levels. This stress manifests itself in inconsistent care. Solitary mothers may alternate between brief bouts of affection and long periods of outright avoidance, ignoring calls or physically distancing themselves from their young. If stress levels are extreme, solitary mothers may abandon their young entirely, snapping at them should they follow her, and permitting males to interact with them. Cubs abandoned by their mother are immediately considered orphaned, and trigger orphan behavior patterns if they survive.
