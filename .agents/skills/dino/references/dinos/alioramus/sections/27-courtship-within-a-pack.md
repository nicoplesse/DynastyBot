# Alioramus – Courtship Within a Pack

Courtship occurs at rest stops, usually after a long migration. It is initiated by the males of the group, who begin seeking the attention of one of the females. They approach with a crouched stance, calling softly towards her, and attempt to walk close, mirroring her movements. The ultimate goal is to earn permission to walk beside her and her female partner (if she has one), proving he can match a more restrained pace without disrupting the cohesion between the pair. Movements are kept slow and deliberate, careful not to make the female nervous. Sudden movement or approaching too quickly can disqualify a male from a female's interest, and prompt her to turn her back and move away from him. Males that attempt to separate a female from her pair - or deliberately block her path - are immediately rejected for this breeding season. Should she be interested, she may allow him to travel beside her or her pair for a short distance, quietly assessing his suitability for courtship and nesting. If the male is deemed suitable, the interested female will temporarily peel away from her partner and move away with the male, calling to him softly. They settle beside each other in a secluded spot, cooing and preening at each other's feathers. Only one male may attempt to court a female or the female pair at a time. Multiple males wishing to court must challenge one another for the right to do so. Challenging males walk straight up to the courting male, maintaining a head on stance and issuing short hisses and low growls. They do not pursue or address either female at this time. If the courting male has lost a spar against the challenging male the same day, he must forfeit his right and step aside. Otherwise, a fierce battle ensues, with its outcome determining who has the right to court the females. The fight ends when one male submits, retreats, or reaches half health. The losing male may not attempt to challenge another male for courtship rights again during that rest period. Winning a challenge grants a male the right to attempt courtship only. Females may still reject him by turning away or leaving, and dominance does not guarantee acceptance. However, other males are not permitted to attempt until he has had his chance. Females consistently appear to prefer dominant males - though others may prefer a male that is capable of showing great restraint. One or both females may choose to court the same male if they wish, but some opt to choose different males.
