# Alioramus – Orphaned Behavior (Wild Spawn)

Orphaned Alioramus cubs initially display behavioral patterns similar to those raised by a solo female - in particular, heightened distress and instability. With their emotional needs not fully met, orphans are often visibly unsettled in early life. Whereas offspring reared by a single female tend to become loud, demanding and persistent in their distress - frequently calling, following and attempting to demand care from their mothers - orphans quickly discover that such behavior goes unanswered - or is actively punished by drawing danger to them. As a result, after repeated neglect or negative reactions, orphans reduce their vocalizations dramatically - often leading to much quieter adults. They learn early that noise draws unwanted attention - from both predators, and potentially dangerous adult males - and adapt by becoming quiet and observant far earlier than typical cubs. Orphans become independent at a young age. Play - even between siblings that remain together - declines quickly, with curiosity changing to caution and more skittish behavior. Orphans may attempt to scavenge from kills before Sub-Adult. They will quickly flee if aggressed by the owner of the body, and - depending on when this happens in the development - can result in a long lasting fear of the species for the rest of their lives if bitten. Orphans may be adopted by female pairs that are interested, but are ignored by males. Males with no females do not group with orphans. Males never adopt. Females in same-sex pairs - or those with similar aged young - will adopt orphans easily. Solitary females do not adopt. For an orphaned Alioramus cub, the approach of unfamiliar adults is not immediately interpreted as comforting. Instead, it triggers acute fear responses - something which forces adult females seeking to adopt to tread very carefully. However, whilst frightened at first, almost all cubs can be won over with gentle encouragement, and eventually settle into adopted life with their new caretaker. Alioramus are intelligent, agile and swift carnivores, capable of traveling great distances in pursuit of the best hunting opportunities. Unlike their more powerful relatives, they lack a strong bite force - and so avoid direct conflict with apex predators wherever possible. Their survival strategy favors speed, awareness and adaptability over brute strength. Maintaining an active lifestyle, Alioramus are almost constantly on the move - pausing only to sleep, eat, or nest. They remain relatively quiet when traveling, only chattering briefly amongst each other so that they may scope their surroundings for threats or prey undetected. Alioramus are highly vocal creatures otherwise, and communicate often in a series of growls, grunts and chuffs. Each pack develops their own unique vocal signature, often showcased by a particular greeting when pack members meet. They only broadcast to alert the rest of the pack, call for others, or during hunts. Broadcast is a universal summoning call for other Alioramus, or displaying heightened excitement during a hunt. Sunbathing is a cherished pastime for Alioramus. They often take brief naps in particularly sunny spots, relishing in the warmth against their feathers. During these rest periods, they engage in deep self-grooming rituals. Closely bonded female pairs meticulously clean each other, removing insects, dirt, and remnants of past hunts from their feathers. Males, by contrast, groom themselves, lacking the same cooperative bond as their female counterparts. When not sleeping or grooming, Alioramus are highly alert, observing their surroundings and commenting quickly to alert others if danger is spotted. They sniff frequently, especially when encountering a new object of interest. (This can be accomplished in game by using the :look: emote and cutting it off.) Alioramus show an aversion to deep or fast moving water. While able to swim, Alioramus prefer to avoid entering rivers and lakes whenever possible. Sudden immersion in water is distressing for Alioramus - water weighs down their feathers and dulls their agility, leaving them temporarily sluggish. A dip in the water or a heavy onset of rain render Alioramus soaked through, prompting these carnivores to pause for rest, shaking their feathers dry. Alioramus spend a lot of their time tracking and stalking herbivore herds. While rarely interested in immediate confrontation, they trail these herds at a distance for long periods, studying their migration routes and looking for potential weakness in the group. This behavior is as much an information gathering event as it is a precursor to a hunt - packs or individuals may shadow a herd for a long time without ever attempting a hunt, while others may stalk until the moment is right before striking. While stalking in this manner, Alioramus keep their distance, careful not to trigger confrontation until the right moment. Some Alioramus never hunt the herd they stalk, and may move on to other prey as necessary.
