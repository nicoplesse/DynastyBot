# Tyrannotitan – Infanticide / Culling

Both boars and sows may commit infanticide or culling of unrelated offspring they encounter. There are no hunger requirements for offspring of Juvenile or below, but the offspring must be unprotected, and without a parent present. Adolescent Tyrannotitan are high priority prey options, and may be hunted at appropriate hunger levels. If not at appropriate hunger, they must be challenged out of the POI. If offspring are protected, they cannot be hunted or culled. If an infanticide or culling attempt has already begun and a parent steps into their defence, the attempt must stop and the adult Tyrannotitan must be challenged instead. Boars that successfully take over a POI from a previous male and his nesting female have the opportunity to commit infanticide under certain circumstances. This is detailed in Courtship and Parenthood. Through the night, Tyrannotitan are fearsome predators. Boars do not tolerate any Medium and above carnivores within their claimed territory and will challenge them out if they are unwilling or able to hunt them. If these carnivores have a claimed body, Tyrannotitan must wait to challenge them out. Tyrannotitan do not challenge out Austroraptor from their nesting sites and are tolerant of them around these sites. Sows who are not nesting do not exhibit this behavior and are not required to challenge non-rival Apexcarnivores from the POI. Nesting sows are more likely to challenge these carnivores. During the daytime, their energy shifts and Tyrannotitan grow tired; the heat of the day turning these tyrants into lazy creatures, unwilling to rise or expend too much energy unless loud sounds or heavy steps wake them. Typically tolerant of Compsognathus and Rhamphorhynchus, this extends to other scavengers as well if Tyrannotitan is sleeping near its kill. If the scavenger is too noisy and alerts Tyrannotitan to it’s presence, Tyrannotitan will aggress it away from the body before going back to sleep. Creatures such as Anodontosaurus, Peloroplites, or Pachyrhinosaurus bring confusion to Tyrannotitan during the day, drawn to the scent of potential prey and unsure when they are greeted by an aggressive rock. If these rocks lash out, Tyrannotitan whimper at the sudden smack and back away disgruntled before sleeping again. Tyrannotitan do not challenge Austroraptor for their nesting sites, as they are not viewed as a real threat to territory or resources, and are understood to flee to water when outright threatened by a Tyrannotitan. They may, however, hunt Austroraptor. Tyrannotitan, particularly young Tyrannotitan, may be briefly observed curiously investigating other baby Austroraptor in nests, if not immediately corrected by the mother of either side.
