# Tyrannotitan – Setts

Setts consist of a pair of bonded brothers or a father and son. These pairs, despite constantly contesting one another for dominance or having an established dominant male, have an extremely strong bond. Some males are particularly possessive over the other male in their sett, and do not tolerate their interest in their adolescent sons. Boars initiate games of more aggressive play with the other male in their sett, running around and swinging their tail at each other’s faces and sides. Boars may also nip at each other’s bodies and tails, and at times practice courtship challenges amongst each other. Boars do not hold grudges against each other with successful courtship, but may attempt to “correct” one another’s behaviors, especially if one has been established and more dominant than the other. Some boars are naturally more aggressive or flippant than others, and require calmer, dominant males in order to correct their behaviors. This is particularly common amongst fathers and sons, but may be observed between brothers as well. Boars may be observed standing alongside one another, circling each other, and mirroring each other’s behaviors in order to show dominance and to reinforce their bonds. Boars may be observed lazily lounging in sunbeams together. Boars patrol their territories together and defend them and each other fiercely. Boars will work together to take on any threat and any prey, and may be observed utilizing strategy to do so. Boars typically prefer to remain close to one another, but may patrol opposite sides of the territory in search of food, potential mates, and to better survey for threats. In the cases where they are far from one another, they use unique roars and bellows to communicate and alert one another. Two boars in the same sett may never claim two separate territories; they will only move into different POIs together.
