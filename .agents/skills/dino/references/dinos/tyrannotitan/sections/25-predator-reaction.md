# Tyrannotitan – Predator Reaction

Tyrannotitan are not often deterred by other carnivores that attempt to hunt them, but repel them aggressively, charging onwards and bellowing loudly. They soak themselves both in their own blood and the blood of their enemies, making a highly intimidating opponent. Within a pair, Tyrannotitan may opt to stay close together, snarling and roaring at any predators that come close. Up to 2 Tyrannotitan may work together in defense against being hunted. If there are 2 Tyrannotitan in a sett or stand, their primary defenders will always be each other. If there is a solitary courted pair within the same POI (one boar and one sow), and they are within LOS of 30 seconds of the hunt starting, they may defend each other. If there is a solitary female and a male pair, only the male who has courted her may act as her second defender. Any individuals who are not grouped and are not related, and have not successfully courted, may not defend each other. A solitary male is not protected by pairs of females. Female Tyrannotitan always defend their grouped offspring of Adolescent and under from threats if they are within LOS. If the father of a clutch is within LOS of his offspring being hunted, and is able to reach the engagement within 30 seconds of the hunt starting, he may defend them. Once the mother reaches the engagement, she may also defend with him. Up to 2 bonded Adolescent Tyrannotitan may defend each other if attacked, but must flee if their parents arrive to defend them. Tyrannotitan, with their serrated teeth and wide bite, prefer to tear and shred their prey, bleeding them to death. Naturally an endurance hunter, Tyrannotitan will tear pieces from their prey and puncture their hides, pushing and pursuing fleeing prey over great distances in an attempt to secure a meal. Tyrannotitan often have woken prey before coming across it - their loud, booming calls alerting others to its presence. Oftentimes, this means their prey is already on alert. In some instances, Tyrannotitan is lucky and undiscovered by its target. More experienced individuals may opt to ambush their prey if given the chance, creeping as close as possible before charging from their cover. This behavior is most commonly seen near water sources, where some individuals prefer to lie in wait behind trees or bushes as they wait for unsuspecting prey to approach. These charges are sometimes paired with bellows as they crash through the foliage, spooking their prey and causing them to panic, giving Tyrannotitan a greater opportunity to launch their attack. Tyrannotitan may hunt any tier, but will always hunt Therizinosaurus, Parasaurolophus, Eotriceratops, Apatosaurus, and Barsboldia before all other prey. Tyrannotitan may also mob Argentinosaurus with 2 or 3 individuals. This typically involves a pair and a courted individual. Tyrannotitan who have not courted or are not paired may not mob Argentinosaurus together. Once the kill has been made, all Tyrannotitan who participated in the hunt claim the body. If challenged for this body, 2 Tyrannotitan are permitted to defend. Tyrannotitan may not hunt healthy adult Giganotosaurus and Tyrannosaurus unless the rival is Albino, deathscarred, or aged Adolescent and below. Albino Tyrannotitan may be hunted at any time regardless of hunger. Tyrannotitan does not hunt other adult Tyrannotitan, but may eat from bodies that fall during a challenge.
