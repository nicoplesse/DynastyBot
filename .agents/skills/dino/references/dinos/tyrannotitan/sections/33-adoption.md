# Tyrannotitan – Adoption

Tyrannotitan do not adopt and will cull unrelated, unattended offspring of Juvenile and below. Adolescent Tyrannotitan are regarded as potential prey. Tyrannotitan who are more advanced in age begin to exhibit signs of slowing down– tending to completely avoid aggression with other Tyrannotitan, beginning to lose interest in courtship, and moving slower in migrations or when patrolling territory. Older females have less tolerance for the antics of young offspring, and often abandon them entirely sooner, lacking the energy to deal with their rowdy behaviors and feed them at the same time. Tyrannotitan often go blind with age completely, due to repeated exposure to sunlight throughout their long lives. It is very rare that a Tyrannotitan makes it to an old age, but these are most commonly the ones that are riddled with scars, but know when to avoid conflict where one is at risk. Tyrannotitan with a bonded family member often survive longer than those who do not.
