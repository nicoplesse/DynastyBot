# Anodontosaurus – Offspring Behavior (Nested)

Anodontosaurus hatchlings, known as pups, are immobile and vulnerable as soon as they're born. Clutches of Anodontosaurus offspring usually hatch at the same time, with siblings breaking out of their eggs one after the other. Hatchlings - not yet fully developed - do not stray from the safety of the nest, and stay sheltered in the thicket crafted by their parents. At this age, they are completely reliant on their parents for protection and food. Once they mature into Juvenile, the muscles in their legs become strong enough to support their heavy bodies - and with gentle encouragement from their parents pups begin to wobble to their feet. Instinctively, young Anodontosaurus stay close to their parents, often pressing against their legs for reassurance and comfort. Until pups gain confident mobility on their legs, the tumble usually remains in the same area, allowing young to build confidence before beginning any journeys. This lasts until offspring are halfway through their Juvenile period - or until food becomes scarce in the POI, forcing the tumble to move on slowly. Anodontosaurus pups are playful, often breaking into short runs and chasing one another while swinging their tails in tandem. This behavior helps them develop crucial coordination skills and agile reflexes, and is actively encouraged by their parents, who can sometimes be coaxed to engage in play with them. While they rarely intend to hurt one another, tail smacks occur between siblings - but this sparring never pushes one below half health. By Adolescent, pups have absorbed much of their parents' behavior, and begin to wander slightly further from the group. This is further enabled by their parents, who stop prioritizing food resources for them - even aggressing them away from food sources they are eating from to encourage independence. Some offspring remain somewhat oblivious to these warnings - and often end up more prone to infighting and causing offence when joining another tumble, or encountering other Anodontosaurus in general. Anodontosaurus are fully independent once they reach Sub-Adult, and usually break away from their parents and siblings to travel alone, unprompted. Some offspring require pushing out by their parents if they linger too long.
