# Citipati – Elderly & End of Life (Optional)

Citipati that grow old and become elderly spend more and more time resting in the sun, basking in open areas, and taking naps whenever the flock pauses for a moment. As they age, the social interactions that bring so much color to a flock’s dynamics become less important for the elderly Citipati. They experience hearing loss and without listening to the calls of others, they do not reciprocate and their own voice falls silent over time. Elderly Citipati rarely have the energy to take part in harem activities. Hens will stay in a harem, but lose their fertility and rather than nesting, they spend more time helping their harem-sisters to raise their chicks. Roosters will struggle to keep up with the demands of harem-life, they do not have the energy or attentiveness that hens prize, and hens will overlook them if they do display themselves to hens. If a rooster is challenged by another for a hen, he will always submit to him and very rarely engages with any fighting or aggressive behavior of any nature. Elderly hens stick to the relationships they have built over their life, and avoid making new acquaintances, preferring to maintain what they already have and relax in the company of familiar individuals. When a threat presents itself, an Elderly Citipati will struggle to raise the alarm with its silent voice. They remain quick on their feet, but become tired more easily and their stamina wanes with their increasing age. An Elderly Citipati is unfortunately an easy target for a clever predator.
