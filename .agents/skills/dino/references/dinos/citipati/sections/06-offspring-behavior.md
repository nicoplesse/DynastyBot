# Citipati – Offspring Behavior

Chicks are able to run about a few moments after hatching, staying very close to their mother. Imprinting behavior is prevalent in Citipati, they bond strongly to the first individual that they see and follow them closely throughout their childhood. This is almost always their mother, chicks that hatch from abandoned nests may not be so lucky. Chicks are playful and inquisitive, investigating everything in their immediate area. Singing and trying out different calls is common place in Citipati nests, chicks are never quiet, even snoring softly as they sleep! Their exploration of the world is steeply curbed by their instinctual desire to stay very close to their mother, they very rarely move more than a body length away until reaching the safe age of Adolescent. On reaching Adolescent, chicks grow in confidence and socialize with other flock members more frequently. Young roosters start practicing their posturing and courting calls, preening their feathers keenly and hoping to make a good impression on the rest of the flock. Young hens pick up on mothering instincts from their adult female relatives and start mimicking nurturing behaviors such as carrying around objects, like they might carry a future chick. At Sub-Adult, Citipati offspring leave the safety of their harem and are no longer considered part of the harem group. Parents are no longer protective over their offspring and push them out to encourage them to find their own harem to join.
