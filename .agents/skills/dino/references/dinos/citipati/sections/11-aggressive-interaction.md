# Citipati – Aggressive Interaction

Roosters fighting for hens Roosters that come to a dispute over hens, for example wishing to add the same hen to their harem, will initiate a dominance fight. Nearby hens gather to watch from a safe distance. The roosters face each other off and call aggressively, hissing and displaying their plumage. They rush forward at each other and launch upwards to deliver a vicious kick. While maintaining eye contact at all times, they pull back a few steps, ready to launch forward and kick again. Turning away from the opponent is seen as an immediate forfeit. This is repeated until one member is below half health or until one submits by turning their back to the opponent. The winning rooster takes a moment to recover, and struts over to his new hen to bond with her. Chick stealing If there are no chicks within a hen's harem, hens will start to feel jealous of mothers with chicks in other harems and watch carefully to see if one strays too far from its mother, or if there are any orphan chicks to adopt. Broody hens will quickly pick up and steal a chick from another harem and run back to their harem, hoping that the mother hasn't noticed. They will never target chicks that are within a body-length of their mother, and will only try to steal those that are outside of their harem’s protection. These hens are fiercely defensive of their stolen brood, even going so far as to start a fight with the mother of the chick if she comes to retrieve her wayward child. Hen fights over stolen chicks consist only of pecking attacks. The mother and broody hen fight viciously until one gives up, or one falls below half health. The losing party may sometimes mourn the loss of the chick for a short time. Mothers that have chicks stolen from them are more protective if they have a small clutch. If one of many is taken, she may not fight very hard to reclaim it and may allow the thief to keep the child after only a few frustrated pecks.
