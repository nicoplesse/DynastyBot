# Eotriceratops – Downtime Behavior

When the herd rests at night, bulls patrol. Each bull is only active for about 15 minutes of the night; split down the middle if there are two bulls, or spread across the entire night in short chunks if the only bull. Otherwise, Psittacosaurus may take a bull's spot in "patrolling"! Females sleep curled around offspring and each other, with the matriarch choosing the best and most comfortable place to do this. Some cows may stay up slightly longer to keep an eye on calves if they are being active. Courtship and migrating does not take place at night. Bulls do not challenge other bulls for their herds at night, and instead prefer to only challenge during the day, when it is safer to do so. All Eotriceratops determine what social group suits them the best. Some bulls prefer solitary lives, refusing to have competition in courting cows – while some bulls tolerate or even enjoy the presence of another in order to take part in that competition. Matriarchs are always able to determine whether or not they want one or two bulls to join their herd, and which females and offspring are permitted to travel with her. Eotriceratops social dynamics are complicated – but at the core of it all is the matriarch. She determines who she groups with, which bulls are impressive, and disrupts any conflicts between members within the group. She is extremely important to the success of a matriarchy or herd, and her death can result in a herd utterly dissolving – especially if there are no other heirs to take her place. Eotriceratops very rarely, if ever, form strong bonds between sexes, and tend to form the strongest bonds with other cows or bulls over the course of their lifetime.
