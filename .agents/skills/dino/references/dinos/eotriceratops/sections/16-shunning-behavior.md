# Eotriceratops – Shunning Behavior

If a matriarch begins to shun a female, she will quickly vacate her from the herd. This applies to bulls as well - a matriarch who does not like a bull’s behavior will quickly leave him behind, and move on as a matriarchy rather than a herd if his behavior is too much for the group to bear. All members in a group trust the matriarch (or the dominant bull in bull groups) in their opinions of other members of the herd, and will base their responses to others on the response of the leading individual. Matriarchies, herds, and bull groups form walls in order to push shunned members away from the rest of the group, following the lead of the matriarch or dominant bull to drive out individuals that are being shunned. Bulls do not take part in this in herds - but still avoid or aggress the shunned member if approached. On the other hand, when in a bull group and shunning other bulls, males simply aggress and bull fight the other bull - even going so far as to kill him if his efforts to drive him out are ignored. Bulls do not shun cows – instead of performing aggressive behaviors, he would leave the herd in order to find other, more suitable opportunities.
