# Eotriceratops – The Matriarch

The matriarch has many responsibilities and is essential to the success of a matriarch and a grander herd. While she leads migrations and determines her own herd’s migration paths, and steps up to defend when no bulls are present, she is also responsible for the social health of the herd, and the success of other cows. This role is not suitable for all females – but once achieved by a female, is not challenged for by any other female in the group. The only time a matriarch changes is when the previous matriarch passes, leaving her inheritor to take her role. The matriarch is extremely particular with who her inheritor will be - it tends to be her eldest daughter whom she keeps very close to her in order to teach her how to be a matriarch. The inheritor may take over some matriarchal duties if the matriarch has determined she can, if the matriarch is needing help. The matriarch determines who joins her matriarchy or herd, and also determines who leaves. This is done by circling and inspecting the prospective female, and is done with no aggression. If she accepts the female, she will issue a friendly call, and then go with her to bring her to the other females. If she does not accept, she will issue a loud broadcast call, and move on without the female. Each matriarch is different, but generally matriarchs tend to prefer her own daughters and sisters, adoptive or otherwise, and have lower preferences for unrelated albino and  melanistic cows. Cows who follow the matriarchy after being rejected risk being aggressed away – so if not selected, a cow will typically migrate elsewhere, in order to find a different matriarchy to accept her. In the same vein, a matriarch is responsible for choosing the bull or bulls her group will nest with. This is detailed more in Courtship & Parenthood. A matriarch may choose to accept a solitary bull or a pair of bulls. If a solitary bull joins a herd, a second bull cannot join. Only previously established pairs can be the two bulls in a herd. A matriarch will lead migrations even when bulls are present. She establishes a regular migration path, picks which nesting ground the matriarchy moves to, and is the first to place a nest where nests will be constructed, as detailed in Courtship and Parenthood. The matriarch is a peacekeeper in many fashions – while conflicts between cows are rare, she will quickly run between the two and drive one of them off gently to ensure they both get space. Where baby fights go too far, she will intervene. If a bull attempts to charge or is too aggressive with one of her females or a calf, she will aggress him in return, and quickly drive him off if it goes too far. The matriarch is a fierce guardian, and acts as the primary defender of the herd if no bulls are present. She also defends females and offspring from the bull, and will monitor him while he is with the herd to ensure he does not behave too aggressively towards any females, and that he does not successfully kill any offspring. This is detailed in Aggressive Behavior. The matriarch will, after all offspring in a matriarchy at a nesting ground have reached juvenile, take the calves and present them to the bull. She will gently correct calves who are behaving badly with nudges in order to allow the bull to approach and investigate them, and will defend him by pushing him away with block if things go wrong. This is detailed further in Courtship and Parenthood. Matriarchs are very present in the babyrearing, teaching new mothers how to parent, watching over offspring, and if a bull is unwilling or not present, teaching baby bulls bull behaviors.
