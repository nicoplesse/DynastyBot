# Eotriceratops – Death

Elderly Eotriceratops who are ready to pass make their final migrations to Dried Lake in Gondwa or Dry Fang Canyon in Riparia, surrounded by the individuals they love and care for. Strongly bonded bull pairs often pass simultaneously, if they have made it that long. They go quiet and peacefully, laying down their large frilled heads, and closing their eyes to rest permanently. Mother Eotriceratops may defend the bodies of calves Adolescent or below for up to one minute immediately following their death. The rest of the herd quickly move away from the situation, bellowing and calling for her to try to snap her out of her wrathful grief. A mother Eotriceratops defending her calf's body in this way will not charge or chase for more than one bodylength away from the body. She takes a defensive stance over her calf's body, headbutting the air and aggressively calling. As she realizes her calf is dead, she will look down to it and nuzzle it, giving it one more loving call before fleeing back to her herd in grieving panic. If the matriarch of a herd or matriarchy dies to a predator, the entire rest of the herd immediately panic and stampede away from the body and the predator, calling desperately until they are out of LOS of her, ignoring all other species in the meantime. Then, they stand in shocked silence, until the inheritor steps forward to comfort the herd and take her role as matriarch. If no such inheritor steps forward, the terrible grief of losing a matriarch may force a matriarchy or herd to split. The death of a matriarch is a terrible loss for a matriarchy, and if no female steps forward to take her role, the matriarchy struggles to find unity. All Eotriceratops form very strong bonds within their groups, and will mourn and remember places where their friends and loved ones have died for the rest of their lives. Mourning Eotriceratops may take a flower in honor of the memory of a loved one and take it to Dried Lake, which is treated as a universal Eotriceratops resting place. Solitary bulls and bull groups do not enter the nesting areas in Broken Tooth Canyon, Rainbow Hills, Rockfall Hill, Green Valley, Tallbrush Coasts, or Hollow Hills unless they have successfully courted females that are nesting there. However, matriarchs will not allow bulls within 10 body lengths of nests. Bulls that form strong bonds with another bull disperse from larger bull groups, limiting their grouping to just the pair of them. They no longer tolerate other bulls joining their group. When not grouped with females (migrating to the nesting grounds or encountering a group of females while migrating), Eotriceratops bulls will not defend them. However, if a solitary bull is hunted on the way to the nesting grounds, the matriarch may step up to defend him. Bulls do not use block in bullfights, instinct and hormones amping up their aggression, and fully immersing them in the engagement. Bulls who use block are less preferred by matriarchs than those who do not use block. If females join the matriarchy after bulls have been accepted, but before nesting has occurred, they may also take their pick of the available bulls, introduced by the matriarch. If bulls have temporarily ungrouped in order to bullfight, but are in a bonded pair, and one individual in the pair is attacked, both bulls may regroup to defend one another. If bulls are attacked while in a bullfight, but are not bonded in any way, the bull who is not attacked must immediately move 10 bodylengths from the engagement. Bulls may only group with cows when there are offspring present. Once the youngest calf reaches Sub-Adult, he will disperse. Bulls do not commit infanticide to related individuals of Adolescent and older. However, bulls will commit infanticide on all ungrouped individuals of Adolescent or younger. When bullfighting, both bulls must leave their in game groups and fight solo.
