# Eotriceratops – Cows

Female Eotriceratops form incredibly strong bonds with one another, particularly with their mothers and the matriarch of the group, whether related to her or not. They bond with one another by sleeping alongside each other, gentle brushes against each other's sides, and frequent low, affectionate calls. These bonds are lifelong, and females shunned from their original groupings or having lost them grow lost and at times even skittish, seeking the comfort of another Eotriceratops as quickly as possible in order to fill the void. When reunited with individuals they have been separated from, they happily broadcast to one another, shaking their frills and briefly locking horns in brief greeting ceremonies. Cows take part in a behavior called allomothering – where all females in a herd or matriarchy work together to tend to offspring. This is another way that females build bonds – by providing for one another's calves, they establish a strong and healthy support system and a strong foundation for calves to fall back on. Older cows take a particularly strong role in herds, continuing to teach other adult cows and supporting the matriarch. These older cows tend to become "inheritors," who will rally and reunite a matriarchy post a matriarch passing away. If a cow within a matriarchy or herd is sick or injured, other cows and the Matriarch slow down to allow them to keep up, even stopping more frequently to allow them to rest and heal. When a group of cows encounters a bull naturally while travelling across Gondwa, they may greet him politely and shake their frills at him, but do not group up with him. However - if the matriarchy has calves of Juvenile or below present, they will not greet him, and instead attempt to give him a wide berth in order to protect their calves.
