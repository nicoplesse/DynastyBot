# Eotriceratops – Orphaned Behavior (Wild Spawn)

Orphaned Eotriceratops born in Broken Tooth Canyon, Rainbow Hills, Rockfall Hill or Green Valley have a very strong likelihood of survival. All matriarchies will take in any orphaned Eotriceratops, and will fiercely defend any calves nearby them. Herds will only adopt orphaned calves if a female in the herd has recently lost a calf, due to predators or due to one of the bulls in the herd lashing out. It is vital that this grieving female or the matriarch sees the calf first, as it must be quickly ushered to the protection of other females before the bull sees it and kills it. Bulls will kill any unrelated Eotriceratops offspring they see on sight. However, calves born outside of these areas face a lot of risks, even from their own species. Adult bulls will attempt to kill any offspring they do not recognize, or those that are not attended by an adult female. Orphaned Eotriceratops proceed through many similar milestones to their nested counterparts, but display different behaviors as a result of their solitary upbringing. Female Eotriceratops tend to be more timid and private, preferring matriarchies of exclusively individuals that they have formed extremely close bonds with – these relationships being their daughters and granddaughters. Male Eotriceratops who grew up solitary tend to have been socialized improperly, resulting in them struggling in social situations with other Eotriceratops, particularly other bulls. They also generally display more aggression – which can even, at times, be taken out on females that intrude on a male’s space. However, they do benefit from older bulls bringing them into bull groups. This over aggression is quickly corrected by other females in the herd, is never fatal, and results in complete rejection by that matriarchy. Eotriceratops have distinct behavioral dimorphism between sexes. While females tend to be more gentle giants - so long as their calves are not threatened! - bulls are an aggressive, powerful, and unwavering presence in Gondwa. Matriarchies have organized social structures, while bulls form groups as either strong duo bonds, or as loose, competitive bachelor groups. Eotriceratops have incredibly strong memories of each other and of locations. Matriarchies will often seek out the same bull or bulls, especially if he has proven himself a worthy protector, father, and caretaker. Eotriceratops matriarchies are almost always on the move, other than when being courted by a bull, nesting, or sleeping. Led by a matriarch who determines migration paths and determines who remains as a member of the group, they are incredibly bonded, and slightly exclusionary of outsiders. Matriarchs are incredibly important individuals within Eotriceratops social structures, and lead many behaviors – such as migrating, the introduction of calves to a bull, selection of a bull, and determining which females join a matriarchy. This is detailed in Social Behavior. Eotriceratops bulls sometimes form strong bonds with one other bull, typically when they have fought many battles together and won, and whom fiercely protect their territories. With a constant balance of deferring to one another’s dominance, and working together to impress females, Eotriceratops bulls, while being some of the most aggressive herbivores in Gondwa’s ecosystem, are capable of wieldling extremely powerful bonds with each other. When a bull forms a strong bond with another, they no longer tolerate other bulls to group with them. Most bull groups are simply groups of bulls who travel together to grow more experience with Gondwa, practice bullfighting, and defend one another. These groups are typically a band of brothers or a father and his sons, but also may be a group of unrelated bulls. These are only very loosely bonded groups, and spend a lot of their time challenging one another and playing as they make their way to the courting ground. Eotriceratops herds are temporary groupings of a matriarchy and their chosen bull or bulls. Only deeply bonded bull pairs may take over a matriarchy together, if the dominant bull is selected by the female. Bulls will only group with females when offspring are present, and are between Juvenile and Sub-Adult in age. Bulls are highly territorial with their herds, and 5 bodylengths surrounding the herd. Herds travel with the matriarch at the front and calves in the middle of a wall of females, who work together to keep predators and the bulls away from their calves. In the Rainy Season, bulls flush with additional testosterone, making them more unpredictable, and willing to go further during bullfights. Due to this, a higher number of male calves are born in clutches laid in the Rainy Season. In the Dry Season, more female calves are born on average.
