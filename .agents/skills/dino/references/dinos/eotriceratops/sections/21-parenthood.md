# Eotriceratops – Parenthood

Eotriceratops females take part in a behavior called allomothering – meaning that all offspring are tended to by all females in a herd. This is a behavior taught to them from a very young age, as even adolescent females will assist with the raising of offspring. The matriarch is considered the mother of all mothers within a matriarchy, and is often left to babysit or attend to offspring while new mothers get food and water. She helps new mothers learn how to take care of their calves, and will quickly adopt a calf if its mother dies. While their biological mother is the first to tenderly greet calves as they hatch, guiding them and protecting them from the moment they arrive, all cows in the herd will excitedly greet the newborn calf, nuzzling it and affectionately calling it. Bulls are not tolerated any closer even when offspring hatch, and are aggressively driven off by the cows - and matriarch - if they get too close. Youngest calf reaching juvenile As calves reach juvenile, they begin to grow more confident, and the group opts to resume migrations - this time with the bull(s) now in tow. As the youngest calf reaches juvenile, the bull(s) are permitted to finally approach the nesting area, and form a new in game group - known as a herd. While the calves will receive tender affection from any cow in the herd, bulls are not allowed close to calves until they are properly introduced. Until the matriarch arrives, females move with their babies away from the bull and towards the matriarch. Bulls have no real paternal instinct or desire to be close to the calves, and do not protect them if the calf is attacked near them. If the calf attempts to use the bull as a meatshield, and does so continuously despite being aggressed, he will attack it. Matriarchs take this opportunity to introduce the calves to the bulls to minimize the chances of infanticide. This is done by the matriarch gathering all offspring alongside her, and gently nuzzling them and comforting them as the bull (or bulls) approaches. She stays calmly beside them, encouraging them to lift their heads and stay still, while she keeps her eyes firmly fixed on the bull(s). This forces the bull to be on his best behavior. In the case that he decides to be aggressive towards the calves, she will rush forward, using her massive frill to block the bull and continue to shove him back. Experienced bulls will abide by these gestures, and younger, more aggressive bulls may attempt to ignore her – with risk of being shunned from the herd. If the bull shows aggression, cows quickly escort calves to a defensive circle in order to protect them from the bull, aggressing him when he comes near, until the matriarch deems it safe to disperse by approaching and shaking out her frill. Eotriceratops cows are very protective over babies, and may refuse to let bulls close even after the calves have been introduced, especially if the female has lost a calf to a bull.
