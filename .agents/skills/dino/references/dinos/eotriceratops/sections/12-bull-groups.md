# Eotriceratops – Bull Groups

Most bull groups are a small collection of loosely bonded bulls that train, spar, and play together. The two most dominant bulls lead the group on where to go and are the main defenders. These groups are constantly doing mock bull fights to practice - which never go below half health, even in the dry season. These groups are most frequently bands of brothers, a father and his sons, or a group of bulls on their way to the courtship grounds. Bulls in these groups take it in shifts to patrol at night, one at a time. Sometimes, bull groups slim down to just 2 bulls, known as a bull pair. Bull pairs are very strongly bonded bulls, with an established bond – either father and son, brothers, or unrelated bulls with one more dominant personality and one more submissive personality. These bulls are very close, and will fight to the death for one another, and defend the other’s body if they fall, for up to one minute. These bulls do not often hold grudges with one another, especially as they get older and more experienced. Some pairs may have established power dynamics – others may allow dominance to pass back and forth between bulls, allowing both bulls to continue to pair with matriarchies. These bulls will rarely, if ever, split once bonded, and bulls that lose this bond will never bond with another bull in the same fashion again. Bulls sometimes opt to follow matriarchy groups (with offspring or without) at a distance in order to encourage the matriarchy to migrate towards the courtship grounds.
