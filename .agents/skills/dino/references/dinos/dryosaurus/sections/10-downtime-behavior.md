# Dryosaurus – Downtime Behavior

Dryosaurus sleep in turns, carefully coordinated and directed by the dominant pair to ensure there's always more Dryosaurus awake than asleep at any given moment. They prefer longer, infrequent naps, as opposed to many short ones. Dryosaurus often sleep at the same time as their mate and offspring, where they huddle together and reaffirm bonds through mutual preening. Misbehaving members are often made to sleep alone as punishment by the dominant pair. The dominant pair rarely sleep at the same time, ensuring one or both individuals are always available to lead and supervise the warren. Other Dryosaurus, looking to prove their loyalty to the dominant pair, rush to act as sentries for them, standing guard near the cave entrance or nest. If during a rest period, a Dryosaurus sounds the alarm for an intruder, all sleeping Dryosaurus rush awake, and need little time to fall back into their usual aggressive nature.
