# Dryosaurus – Adoption

If either sex encounters an orphaned Dryosaurus, they will happily take it in and adopt it as one of their own. This occurs even if they already have kits. As Dryosaurus enter their older years, they lose the drive to attain or maintain a dominant status within the warren. When a dominant Dryosaurus becomes elderly, their colors naturally begin to fade. This change triggers other members of the warren to compete for the now-vacant position of dominance. Dryosaurus that are nearing the end of their life begin to act in a lethargic manner. They can be observed sleeping in dens, bushes and caves. All members of the warren begin to treat this member as if they are a hatchling, bringing them berries; something the dominant members of the warren permit. Toward the very end of their lives, elderly Dryosaurus will often isolate themselves from the group. This instinctive behaviour ensures they do not draw attention to the rest of the warren. Upon it being discovered that the elderly member of the warren has met their peaceful demise, other members of the warren will cover their body with foliage. None Dryosaurus are not territorial over water sources. Dryosaurus are bold and do not flee from engagements except when those engagements do not appear to go their way. Dominance fights must stop at deathscars. Healing calls may not be used during dominance fights.
