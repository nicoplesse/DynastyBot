# Dryosaurus – Warren

Dryosaurus have a relatively loose social structure. The most dominant and aggressive individuals end up as the dominant pair of the warren. With a dominant status comes access to the favored food resource: berries. Non-dominant individuals are not permitted to eat berries by the dominants, unless they are below Adolescent. The dominant pair can be of either gender and do not have to be a mated pair. The pair team up together to rule the rest of the group and claim resources for themselves. This pair run a tight pecking order that other Dryosaurus are expected to fall in line with: they control where the warren nests, where they claim cave territories, where to forage for food, and when to migrate. With this added responsibility, many Dryosaurus are happy to remain as submissives under the dominants, but may attempt to steal berries when the dominants are not looking from time to time. Some are equally as respected as the dominant pair, and the position may be battled for, with leaders changing frequently. See Aggressive Interaction for more detail. All Dryosaurus are generally affectionate to one another, engaging in mutual preening of feathers and quills. This not only reinforces relationships between individuals, but also reduces any lingering social tension in the group. Dominant individuals use theatrical displays to establish or reinforce dominance, such as hissing, aggressive posturing, and teeth baring. They may go so far as to land nips to keep them in line. More often than not, aggressive postures are enough to subdue a potential rival. Dominant Dryosaurus maintain power by forming alliances within the warren, often grooming or feeding their supporters to ensure loyalty. These alliances are particularly critical during disputes or challenges to dominance. Lone Dryosaurus attempting to join the warren are met with intense scrutiny. Outsiders are often harassed and examined closely by the entire warren, which can appear frightening to a particularly nervous individual. Once the dominants are content that the new addition will be submissive to them, they are welcomed into the warren. The warren moves together in loose formations, with dominant individuals leading from the front, and submissive members following behind. Dryosaurus often rest in close proximity to one another, touching or leaning for warmth and comfort. Both adults and young communicate frequently with a range of chirps, hisses, and trills to signal food sources, danger, or maintain group cohesion during movement. Dominant Dryosaurus within the warren enforce a strict regime, pecking away subordinates from berry bushes. They frequently use their :threaten_call: aggressive hisses to intimidate submissive members that may be pushing their luck, but are swift to soothe tensions with affection after their boundaries are respected. All Dryosaurus - even dominants - show remarkable tolerance and gentleness with kits. Dominant Dryosaurus react harshly toward adolescent and older members of the warren who attempt to eat berries. These individuals are aggressed and bitten away from these prize resources - and brutally attacked if they do not take the message. These battles are never fatal, and once the submissives back away from the berries, the dominants show them affection again.
