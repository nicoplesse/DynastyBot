# Dryosaurus – Joining a Warren

Lone Dryosaurus that wish to join a warren are firstly subject to an interrogation by the existing warren members. The group gathers around the newcomer in a circle, blocking all exits, and performs an intimidation display: chirping, calling loudly, hissing, and mock-attacking. This process tests the outsider’s temperament - and if they wish to be accepted, they must remain submissive throughout. Submission is shown through avoiding direct eye contact, keeping crouched low and looking down or to the side. They should remain calm and quiet throughout the displays. If a new Dryosaurus reacts with aggression, confrontation, or attempts to break out of the circle - they are rejected. The warren members shriek in outrage and immediately chase the outsider out of the immediate area, sometimes delivering a sharp nip but never outright attacking or killing them. Once a new Dryosaurus has been accepted, they are under probation and expected to respect the warren’s hierarchy and avoid aggravating the more dominant members. This means they should remain unobtrusive; keeping out of the way when more dominant members are eating or leading, and not challenging or showing aggression to others within the warren. Over time, the new member of the warren will earn more respect and can climb their way up the social pecking order. They will have proven their worth to the warren by repeatedly showing submission to more dominant members, and respecting other members of the warren. This earns them favor, and higher standing within the group. Orphaned youngsters (Juvenile and below) are the exception to the intimidation process; they are adopted without question, and welcomed regardless of their origin.
