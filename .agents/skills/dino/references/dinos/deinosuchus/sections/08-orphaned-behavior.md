# Deinosuchus – Orphaned Behavior

Orphaned Deinosuchus that manage to make it past the mid hatchling stage have a much better chance at survival in comparison to some other apex carnivore species. However, they still lack the privileged upbringing that those with parents (or at least a mother) are granted. Orphans below the age of adolescence that encounter an adult female with young may find themselves unexpectedly lucky. If they are close in age to her existing brood, they can often slip in among her offspring and be accepted as one of her own. The adult male in the territory does not distinguish between biological and non-biological young. As long as the orphan remains near the adult female, he will not drive them out, and any offspring in her care are considered his by default. When adoption is not an option, orphaned Deinosuchus often band together with other orphans of a similar age, forming a dispersal group known as a pod. These small gatherings offer safety in numbers and shared vigilance, though competition over food remains fierce. Conflicts are much more frequent in comparison to parented Deinosuchus, but serious injury is rare, with a pecking order quickly establishing within these groups. Pods remain together until Sub-Adult before dispersing - with individuals of the same sex pairing off if desired, or individuals leaving alone. In cases where a mother dies while raising her young within a male's territory, the offspring face a new and sudden danger. Adult males do not tolerate young in their territory without their mother around, and will immediately begin driving them out.
