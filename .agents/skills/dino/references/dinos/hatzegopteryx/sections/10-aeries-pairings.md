# Hatzegopteryx – Aeries & Pairings

Hatzegopteryx are a social species, but selective on who they lavish their attention on. They bond very closely with their partner, showing high levels of affection which includes preening, soft coos and cuddling in close. Pairs - and pairs with an adult offspring in their grouping (aeries) - share kills with each other, with no hierarchy on who gets to eat first. Food is shared between them equally. Grooming and preening each other is an activity that takes up a lot of time for Hatzegopteryx. Both individuals seem to find the experience enjoyable and - where there are more than 2 in the group - sessions of group preening takes place, with all individuals huddled together to nibble at each other's feathers. Hatzegopteryx are emotional and bond deeply with both their partner and all offspring they produce - with the exception of albinos. During their annual migration to Red Island, Hatzegopteryx enjoy seeking out the company of their former chicks, and show them great affection. This is in stark contrast to when they patrol and maintain their territories - where even previous offspring will be chased out.
