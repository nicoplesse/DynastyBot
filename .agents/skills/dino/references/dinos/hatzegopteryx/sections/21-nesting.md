# Hatzegopteryx – Nesting

Hatzegopteryx always choose to nest somewhere that is either inaccessible to terrestrial dinosaurs, or otherwise very difficult for them to access. This results in the majority of Hatzegopteryx nests being built on top of cliffs, mountains and rocky areas. Hatzegopteryx lovingly construct nests together before the mother lays her eggs. They decorate their nests with leaves, bones, and shrubbery in order to preserve warmth for their chicks, and will often try to find a place sheltered from the rain to minimize the chance for them to freeze to death. Once the nest is built, the female is responsible for the vast majority of brooding over the eggs - which is aided by the male bringing her food, and taking over brooding responsibilities while she leaves to get water. However, she is always quick to return, and shuffles him back out of her spot. As chicks start to hatch from their eggs, both parents spend an incredible amount of time preening them, nuzzling them and cuddle in close to keep them warm. The mother primarily opts to brood over them, while the male flies off to hunt and bring back food.
