# Hatzegopteryx – Albino & Melanistic Behavior

Albino individuals are universally shunned by all Hatzegopteryx - including their parents. Albino chicks will be ignored by their parents or abandoned entirely if they are the only chick in the nest, and do not have food provided for them. Albinos that make it to adulthood may never find a partner - even from another albino. They too make the same courtship migration to Red Island, hopeful to find another, but face consistent disappointment, leading to a highly lonely existence. Adults ignore albinos, never forming partnerships with them and driving them out of their territories. Albinos do not understand that they are albino, and find the treatment they receive to be confusing. Ironically, they also treat other albinos in the same way. Melanistics, meanwhile, face little issue when it comes to forming pairs. Females are happily accepted as mates, but males seeking an opposite-sex pair may find difficulty during courtship. Melanistic males interested in a same-sex pair struggle due to being perceived as female by other individuals. Hatzegopteryx - once paired - are highly aggressive to all other Hatzegopteryx once they form territories. The only exception to this is the migration to Red Island, where Hatzegopteryx are largely tolerant towards each other. They immediately chase out other Hatzegopteryx from their territories, leading this into a hunt if the other Hatzegopteryx do not leave the POI. These "challenges" are engaged upon with the owners of the territory swooping in with aggressive calls, and attacking immediately. There is no grace period during a territory challenge for Hatzegopteryx. If the other Hatzegopteryx fights back, or refuses to leave, the group will become more aggressive in their attacks, killing the other if at all possible. They will not give up pursuing until the individual (or group) leaves the POI. If the group becomes severely injured, they may choose to flee and abandon their territory, leaving the POI for another entirely, rather than risk death. If the group has offspring that cannot fly, both parents will take one baby each and carry them to the new location. If they have an adult offspring with them, this offspring will assist in carrying an additional chick. They will return afterwards to try and collect any babies that had to get left behind, but often return when it is too late. Hatzegopteryx do not swap mates and do not engage in challenges for a mate, except at Red Island, where the challenge will be started before a pair is formed. When solitary, Hatzegopteryx largely avoid others of their kind until they form a pair. They do not claim territory, and do not chase out others of their kind or other aerial species, with the exception of Quetzalcoatlus.
