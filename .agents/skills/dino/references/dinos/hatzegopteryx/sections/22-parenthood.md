# Hatzegopteryx – Parenthood

Hatzegopteryx parents are doting and affectionate with all their chicks, but do grow to select a favorite. Both parents always pick the same chick as their favorite. They make sure to feed the favorite chick first, and show it the most affection, but are still loving and affectionate to all of their chicks. Chicks will work hard to be considered the favorite, by giving gifts and generally being affectionate with their parents. The role of 'favorite' can change over time, but is a firm choice once the chicks hit adolescence. This favorite child is usually the one from the clutch that stays behind with their parents if they do not already have an older sibling in the group. Albino offspring, sadly, are neglected by both parents. They do not get brought food, and are not shown affection. If an albino chick is the only one in the clutch, both parents may abandon it and the nest entirely. In the event one (or more) of the chicks falls from the nest and manages to survive, parents will fly down to attend that offspring and bring it food and affection. They will continue to do this until their chick is able to fly - and at which point, it will be expected to return to the nest by itself. Hatzegopteryx parents watch their chicks closely. When the mother leaves the nest to get water, the father makes sure to stay with his chicks at the nest. They prefer never to leave chicks unattended, and get stressed if both parents have to get called away at the same time. In the event where eggs hatch but only one parent has survived, that parent eagerly endeavours to provide for their chicks, but end up struggling to provide for themselves and the demands of hungry chicks at the same time. Chicks are often left unattended while the parent flies off to hunt and bring food back, and are at a higher risk of predation. When parents start to notice their chicks hopping and flapping their wings, both of them will pick up a chick and fly high into the air before dropping it, forcing their offspring to take to flight. If for some reason a chick’s wings don’t catch, Hatzegopteryx parents will land and fuss over their chicks, nuzzling them and affectionately calling to them before picking them back up in their beak and returning them to the nest. They will, in these circumstances, wait another day before they encourage the chick to fly again. As chicks continue to grow, chicks will do mock attacks and dives at their parents and siblings. Chicks who excel in this area will be the most favored by their parents, even if speckled with gifts from other chicks.
