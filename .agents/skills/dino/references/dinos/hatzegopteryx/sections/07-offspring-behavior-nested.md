# Hatzegopteryx – Offspring Behavior (Nested)

Baby Hatzegopteryx, known as chicks, are extremely vulnerable after hatching and require the loving care from their parents to have the strongest chances of survival. They are born wet, blind and deaf, and take several moments to recover from the ordeal of hatching. Once their eyesight clears and their hearing develops (at around midway through the hatchling stage), chicks imprint on the first adult Hatzegopteryx (or Quetzalcoatlus) that they encounter. In almost all cases, this is their mother or father. Now aware of their surroundings, chicks huddle close to their siblings if they have any and nestle deep into the warmth of their mother's body. They call out to their parents for affection and happily return it when it is given - which is frequently! Chicks are relatively quiet in most instances, with or without a parent nearby. However, when one of their parents returns to the nest with food, chicks begin to squawk loudly, competing with their siblings to be the first to eat. The additional noise is largely unnecessary - Hatzegopteryx chicks live a fairly cushioned life, with parents that dote on them. Chicks are highly inquisitive, pecking and grabbing at whatever objects that are within reach of them. They can be observed inspecting a wide array of items, and squabbling over especially exciting ones. Parents choose a favorite chick - which siblings unfortunately notice over time: observing how the favorite is fed first, and given the most attention. Though all Hatzegopteryx offspring get lots of tender loving care, chicks are always desperate to be the favored child with their parents, which leads to displays of over-affection, and offering their parents gifts when able. Hatzegopteryx, in turn, can become quite possessive of objects they find and fiercely defend them from their siblings, in order to be the one to present it as a gift to their parents. Chicks that have no siblings have an extremely cushioned life. They get access to all resources from both parents and are fiercely defended. These chicks develop later than those with siblings, and take longer to mature. Hatzegopteryx chicks are grounded throughout all of the hatchling stage and lack the proper strength in their muscles to take flight. This lasts up until mid juvenile, when they begin flapping their wings and showing more interest in copying their parents. Chicks that fall from the nesting site before they are able to fly are still lovingly attended by their parents. They tend to remain close to the area they fell from, hiding in bushes or undergrowth and only emerging when a parent returns to bring them food. As their parents start to notice this willingness to fly, chicks are often snatched up by their mother or father and flown high into the air. From this height, their parents drop them, and chicks instinctively spread their wings and take their first flight. The first few sessions of flight can be rough for babies, and these clumsy sessions are kept short. However, by adolescence, Hatzegopteryx develop into keen and skilled fliers. At adolescence, chicks begin flying around their parents' territory. They perform mock attacks on their parents and siblings - swooping down as if they are going to crash into them, and then moving just at the last moment. This type of play seems to be mutually enjoyed by both parents and siblings, and assists Hatzegopteryx young in developing crucial hunting skills. At sub-adult, chicks begrudgingly leave their parents' territory. The favored chick may remain with their parents if there is not already an older sibling present, but the rest will disperse - often sticking together until they can find a mate of their own.
