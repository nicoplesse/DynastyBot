# Sarcosuchus – Orphaned Behavior

Orphaned Sarcosuchus face a tough battle for survival from the moment they hatch. Without the guardianship of their float, they lack both protection and food provision. They instinctively chirp to call their parents, crying out fearfully from hunger, or from being alone in the world. Without any response, the young Sarcosuchus sometimes call more loudly and boldly, attracting predators and bringing about their demise. Those that survive do so by hiding deep in root tangles or drifting with debris, attempting to blend in with their surroundings and calling more softly and infrequently. As their eyesight improves by Juvenile - and driven by hunger and the threat of starvation - orphans begin fishing as soon as they are able. They sink to the riverbed, moving only when necessary, and spend much more time in water than they do on land. Orphaned Sarcosuchus may band together in a group with others if they have siblings or encounter other youngsters of Adolescent age or below. Though they do not defend each other, they feel safer in numbers, and prefer to rest close to each other for comfort. Those lucky enough to find a lone adult male - or all male group - may find solace if they are welcomed into the float. Adult males, especially those without recent offspring, may tolerate or even adopt an orphan, offering them protection as well as food. Orphans that successfully integrate into an all-male float remain submissive to their new caregivers, and quickly learn to respond to his vocalizations with affection, and avoid competition with him for food. However - they must remain cautious when adults of their kind approach them. Females never adopt them and may see them as an easy meal. If an orphan ventures too close to a float containing females, it risks being attacked, driven away, or even eaten.
