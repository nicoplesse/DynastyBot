# Sarcosuchus – Cross-Species Interaction

Sarcosuchus does not hunt healthy adult Deinocheirus, but may attempt to hunt unprotected Deinocheirus offspring, up to adolescent. Should an adult return to protect the offspring, Sarcosuchus will forfeit the hunt. Unhealthy Deinocheirus are death-scarred or albino. Sarcosuchus tolerate scavengers. Sarcosuchus in a float of 2+ members do not tolerate solitary Deinosuchus in their immediate POI. These large crocodilians prove a threat to Sarcosuchus - and so, they will either seek to drive them out, kill them, or leave for different waters themselves. Solitary Sarcosuchus that encounter 1+ Deinosuchus, or floats that encounter 2 Deinosuchus, always opt to leave the POI for a safer water source. They will attempt to hurry their offspring away if they have a nest nearby, but will defend themselves if attacked. Adult Spinosaurus are always kept at a distance where possible. Experienced individuals may learn over time that Spinosaurus are highly aggressive and territorial, and opt to flee them if they get too close. Sarcosuchus do not hunt adult Spinosaurus and do not accept territory challenges, and opt to flee engagements from them. They may defend themselves, but must try to escape as soon as an opportunity presents itself. Scavenger species are tolerated if they provide grooming abilities and remain slow moving. Sudden bursts of speed may prompt Sarcosuchus to lunge - sometimes without meaning to - and clamp the unwitting species in their jaws. Often, they release them afterwards, before settling back down to receive more grooming attention, should the small creature not be too alarmed. Sarcosuchus open their jaws wide for these species to clean at pieces of flesh and gristle caught between their teeth. Their small, pointed snouts prove effortless in stripping any remnants left behind here, and appear to be greatly enjoyed by Sarcosuchus.
