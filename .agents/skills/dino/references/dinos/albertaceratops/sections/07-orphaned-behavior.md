# Albertaceratops – Orphaned Behavior

Orphaned cubs instinctively seek out the dominant female or another ceratopsian species to fill the void left by their missing maternal figures. They linger near these individuals, mimicking the behavior of other cubs to blend in as seamlessly as possible. Sows within the sett are known for their nurturing nature and will readily accept orphaned cubs that approach them, integrating them into their own broods or treating them as their own. Even offspring of mixed-species herd mates are warmly welcomed and fiercely protected by the sows until they are mature enough to survive independently. Orphans raised without the guidance of a social structure often grow into dependent adults, displaying submissive behavior in their quest to be accepted into a new sett’s hierarchy. Orphan boars frequently miss out on early breeding opportunities, as their lack of resolve makes them less successful in courtship battles. They often go too far in battle, resulting in reprimands or fatal wounds. In contrast, orphaned females gravitate toward the dominant sow, eager to support her and earn her approval, reinforcing their place within the social fabric of the herd.
