# Albertaceratops – Predator Reaction

Albertaceratops are a bold species, quick to announce the presence of any carnivores in their vicinity. When a predator is spotted, the first individual to notice alerts the herd with a broadcast call, prompting other sett members to turn toward the threat and echo the warning. In mixed-species herds, Albertaceratops are usually the first to announce a threat. With better vision than their herd mates, they can spot the shape of a carnivore from some distance away. Once the alarm is raised, most individuals group together, bunching closely if young are present or standing nearby loosely for smaller predators. A group of up to 4 individuals, which always includes the dominant sow & boar, move to the outer edges of the herd to act as a buffer between the danger and the rest of the group, performing as active defenders. If the predator draws closer, the active defenders instinctively mock charge toward it to intimidate and drive the predator away from hunting the sett. Issuing warning calls, they attempt to draw attention to themselves in an effort to distract the predator from their groupmates and young. When outmatched, the dominant sow directs the rest of the herd to flee, prompting them to abandon their defensive stance and move away from the engagement. As many as four skilled individuals create a barrier between the herd and the predator, charging around the outskirts, headbutting, and kicking to keep the danger at bay. This always includes the dominant pair. Unlimited Albertaceratops form a defensive circle formation when faced by a predator or threat. Up to 4 Albertaceratops may actively chase predators while in LOS of their herd. When choosing to abandon the formation, the 4 defenders may still continue to chase down predators while the herd flees.
