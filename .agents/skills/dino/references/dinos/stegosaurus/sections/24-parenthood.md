# Stegosaurus – Parenthood

Females are highly protective over their young and do not tolerate ungrouped species nearby (within 10 body lengths). They keep platelets close to them, and guide them to different food sources. Females alert the male to the threat, broadcasting to gain his and the herd's attention. This aggression evolves into a territory challenge around the herd, should the intruder continue to approach. This radius does not exceed 10 body lengths and Stegosaurus does not challenge outside of this. Miragaia may seek to help assist mothers (and fathers) in offspring rearing, but initially Stegosaurus mothers prefer to keep their platelets close, and shake their heads at approaching Miragaia, blocking them access to their young with their bodies. However, once platelets reach Juvenile, mothers relax significantly, and more willingly allow their young out of their sight - often towards the loving care lavished upon them by Miragaia. Mothers - even those that have platelets being cared for intensely by Miragaia - viciously defend their young when danger approaches them, and are the first ones to leap to the defence when a predator comes. If there are no Miragaia in the herd - or a mother is particularly protective (especially for a first clutch) - mothers lead their young to food and water sources within the territory, helping to teach them what is safe to eat, and which food stuffs to avoid. Even overprotective and first time mothers will willingly accept childcare from Miragaia when feeling overwhelmed - which is especially common with large clutches of needy young. However, the care provided may be temporary, depending on the nature of the mother. When she feels recovered enough, she will call out to her offspring, who will obediently return to her side. Mothers tend to ignore playfights between platelets and move away from them when they do - but fathers keep a close watch on all the young within the zeal, carefully stepping in to prevent further injury if he deems that offspring have gone too far in their playful tussles. If the stegosaurid herd opts to move, any nests will be destroyed by mothers before they leave - sometimes with the assistance of their young, who delight in the destruction and crushing of old egg shells.
