# Stegosaurus – Offspring Behavior (Nested)

Baby Stegosaurus, known as platelets, are highly dependent on their parents and closely follow other Stegosaurus, Miragaia and Kentrosaurus - but instinctively bond with and follow behind their mothers first and foremost. Platelets emerge from their eggs small and vulnerable, with soft, rounded plates and stubby tails. Their mother is usually there to greet them after hatching, and they call out affectionately to her as she nuzzles into them during their first few moments of life outside the egg. Platelets immediately rise onto their feet shortly after hatching. Their attempts to move at first are clumsy, with frequent stumbling as they struggle to coordinate their legs and tail at the same time. Mothers patiently stand beside their young, gently nudging them upright as they stumble, and encouraging them to follow them. Once more confidently mobile - typically by the end of the hatchling period - platelets tend to adopt an attitude of "strike first, ask questions later". Their way of exploring the world is based upon experiences - and confident platelets will smack their tails against new objects, testing it with their small but powerful tails. Platelets are naturally cautious, having a tendency to freeze in place when they hear or spot something that alarms them. This behavior is instinctual, and allows adults in their group a better opportunity to move to protect them. Despite their cautious nature, platelets quickly show a curious character, bleating and chirping at new objects and individuals they encounter. At juvenile, platelets begin having playful tussles with one another. Individuals of both sexes turn away from each other and smack at the other with their tails, practising the movements they will rely on later as adults. Both babies seem to enjoy this play, and never take each other below half health. These games often end in snorting and clumsy tumbles, but serve as important exercises in balance and coordination. Platelets only eat one to two different types of food throughout their lives, and the basis of their diet is entirely guided by the adults around them, and their exposure to different food types. They rely on the adults in the stegosaurid herd - and not just their mother - for guidance on what is safe to consume. As they age, baby Stegosaurus naturally drift more towards Miragaia if they are lucky enough to have one present within the stegosaurid herd. Though they will always defer to their mothers, Miragaia are recognized and respected as excellent caregivers, and platelets seem to enjoy any interactions and affection a Miragaia may offer them. Approaching adolescence, platelets' freeze responses become less and less common as they mature and develop into their more adult personalities. Somewhere midway through this age phase, adolescents begin to start taking more defensive and aggressive postures - and by the time they reach Sub-Adult, the freeze response is gone entirely. At this time, a more noticeable difference emerges between males and females. Adolescent males spend the majority of their time sparring with other adolescents - and even their father, if permitted - in a desperate attempt to develop dominance, and highlight their strength and stamina. Females, meanwhile, remain more social, binding together to socialize with other females in the group, and grazing alongside each other. At Sub Adult, all offspring disperse from the zeal - including females, even if there is room. Sisters often bind together and stay together permanently if they forge strong enough bonds. Sub Adult female offspring are permitted to trail behind their parents' zeal (un-grouped) until their parents either nest again, or they find a male of their own to form their own zeal with. Any Miragaia or Kentrosaurus offspring that age up out of the stegosaurid herd may choose to follow along with the newly mature sisters, forming a new stegosaurid herd. Males split off into a solitary lifestyle, keen to find their own females to carve out their dynasties. Brothers may loosely follow each other for some time (un-grouped), but will never defend the other, and quickly battle for control of any females they encounter.
