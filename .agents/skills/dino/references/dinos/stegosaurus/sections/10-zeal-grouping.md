# Stegosaurus – Zeal grouping

Stegosaurus form groups of one male and up to three females. While the females in the zeal can be related to each other, none of them are related to the male, who only tolerates potential breeding partners. As such, related females to the male are not permitted to remain in a zeal, with males seeking access to potential breeding partners only. Females prefer to stay with their male once a zeal has formed, but may opt to leave the zeal at any point - even after three clutches raised together and stronger bonds are formed. However, once a strong bond has formed between a male and a female, it is much less likely a female will leave the group. If a rival male challenges for the rights to the female, the female will still leave to go with the winning male if he is successful - strong bond or not. If strongly bonded, though, she may pine after her previous male, and look to find her way back to him after a while - should the new male not meet her standards after some time. Males therefore spend a lot of time showing the females in his zeal great affection. They work as the primary protector in the group, always leaping to the defence of his females during engagements, and even to the defence of the other stegosaurid species in their wider herd. Females bond socially with other females, and tend to group closely together, shadowing each other and tending to have offspring at the same time. Abandoned or orphaned young are readily swept up by both male and female alike, and integrated into the zeal until they come of age. Orphaned females may be permitted to remain if there is space, while orphaned males may stay only if there is not already an adult male in the group.
