# Stegosaurus – Orphaned Behavior (Wild Spawn)

Platelets that hatch alone - or lose a parent - often do manage to make it to adulthood, providing they do not encounter too many dangers along the way. Instinctively, they move to follow - and perhaps even imprint - on any other stegosaurid species, such as Stegosaurus, Kentrosaurus or Miragaia. Happily for them, they will usually be adopted by any of these species they find - and this normally results in the best outcome for an orphaned platelet. Those that do not manage to find others of their kind or similar still fare decently, but may more quickly lose the freeze response that is so instinctual in Stegosaurus young. Stegosaurus are a cathemeral species, but are the most active at night. During the daytime, they bask in the sun, walk between food and water, and defend their chosen resource from other species and rival Stegosaurid groupings. Though they seem to love to sunbathe, this basking behavior is very beneficial to them, and helps them to recharge their energy for a night spent engaging in more active behaviors. Stegosaurus are highly sluggish during the day time, and do not sprint unless in an engagement or actively fleeing an engagement. At night, Stegosaurus are their most active, spending the majority of this time socializing, migrating and courting. Stegosaurus are notoriously grumpy individuals, prone to aggressive posturing when approached by other species. They only ever share their space with the rest of their zeal members, and Miragaia and Kentrosaurus they group with to form a wider stegosaurid herd. Stegosaurus seem to have an excellent memory, with highly experienced lead males careful to remember good areas with lots of resources for their herd. Stegosaurus sleep in short bursts - mainly throughout the day when they are lazy and more sluggish, but also at night during times of quiet. Sometimes this can be taken standing up, and at other times laying down for a more comfortable session of sleep. Stegosaurus deeply enjoy bathing -  especially during the day, when the heat of the sun can feel exhausting. They are not the best swimmers, so prefer to remain in shallower pools, or on the edge of a riverbank.
