# Therizinosaurus – Offspring Behavior (Nested)

When a Therizinosaurus chick hatches, it is fragile, wet, and almost entirely blind. Peeping and wiggling, Therizinosaurus chicks are greeted by their parents, who affectionately call back to them. Therizinosaurus chicks imprint based on the sound of the first adult it hears, whether it be their mother or father. Chicks learn their parents’ voices over time, and become so attuned to the sounds of their shifting feathers, footsteps, and calls that even if a chick were to remain blind for the rest of its life, it will always be able to identify its parents. Therizinosaurus hatchlings remain in the nest beneath their father’s feathers, huddling close to him and their siblings for warmth and comfort. Unless they are sleeping or there is a threat facing the sect, Therizinosaurus chicks are almost constantly making noise - especially if left alone in a bush without a parent’s warmth or attention. Once a Therizinosaurus reaches Juvenile, it finally collects the strength in its legs to stand and waddle around, weighed down by its own heavy, downy feathers. At this point, chicks are able to see, but are still highly sensitive to light, and will duck back under their parent’s feathers to avoid the pain of the rising or setting sun. Chicks will begin to nervously explore the shrubbery and area surrounding the nest they were hatched in, with any startling noise, sudden movement, or parent that strays too far sending them careening back to their nest for comfort. They may, at this point, begin to follow the sect during patrols. Juvenile Therizinosaurus chicks continue to peep and chirp for their parents - these noises may evolve into alarmed shrieks if they have not heard or seen them for an extended period of time. Once Therizinosaurus chicks begin to follow the sect, if they choose to follow their mothers, they will be quickly ferried away and back into their father’s care. While chicks do not fear their mothers, they are quickly corrected when she outstretches her claws, always returning to their father’s tail as a show of obedience. During patrols of a sect's territory, Therizinosaurus chicks can be seen following their fathers in a straight line, only clumping beneath his tail or walking at his sides when frightened or seeking comfort. If a Therizinosaurus chick does not have a father, they may be adopted by a different male in the group, or will be forced to maintain a steady distance between themselves and their mother. Juvenile Therizinosaurus may play with their father and other chicks in the same clutch as them, but show great anxiety when approached by unrelated chicks or adult Therizinosaurus that are not their parents. They will always maintain their personal space from unrelated Therizinosaurus and will flee if their personal space is intruded upon. Therizinosaurus chicks do enjoy running, playing in water, and picking up items of interest. As they grow older, they grow braver and more independent, and are more likely to stray away from the sect in order to play games by themselves. Some Therizinosaurus chicks may choose to bring gifts to their preferred parent or sibling. This behavior is exceptionally common in males, with females preferring to be the recipient of the gifts. Male Therizinosaurus chicks tend to stay closer to their fathers even as they age, with females being willing to push farther away from the sect to mimic their mother’s behaviors - scratching trees, calling, and stretching their claws at critters and other species that may be considered a threat to a chick's "territory". A Female Therizinosaurus chick may at times choose a bush, a parent, or sibling to defend as her "territory," and will fiercely defend it from other Therizinosaurus in the group - unless yelled at or corrected by her mother or another adult female in the group. Upon reaching Adolescent, Therizinosaurus chicks will no longer take shelter beneath their parents' tails, having likely been shooed out from beneath them a few times now. They will now begin to curate and defend their personal space bubble (1 body length), even with their siblings or parents. They, however, will often not aggress a family member out of their personal space bubble - it simply depends on the Therizinosaurus. Adolescent Therizinosaurus have by now also grown out of their constant peeping, and have learned to be quieter, only calling to their parents if they stray too far from the sect in order to find them again. Adolescent Therizinosaurus also begin to more directly mimic their parents’ behaviors - females with leading patrols, marking and defending territory and males with trying to impress females. Adolescent female Therizinosaurus may engage in territory challenges against species different than their own - but if too hurt, will quickly flee back towards the sect or her favored bush for comfort. An adolescent female will never initiate a territory challenge against another species. Once Therizinosaurus chicks reach sub-adult, males often choose to leave the sect in search of unrelated females to impress. Females who were born to the Dame of the group are permitted to stay, if there is room - otherwise, they may choose to leave or are driven out by the Dame, in search of their own group to lead.
