# Therizinosaurus – Orphaned Behavior (Wild Spawn)

Orphaned Therizinosaurus hatchlings are blind, cold, and fragile, peeping helplessly for a voice that will never call back. They remain in the position they hatched in, peeping incessantly and moving their heads around in search of the sound of movement or warmth. Orphaned hatchling Therizinosaurus will imprint on the first voice that calls back to them - whether it be another herbivore, carnivore, or Therizinosaurus. Even if imprinted, hatchling Therizinosaurus are unable to move, entirely blind with weak legs that cannot support the weight of their wet, dense feathers. They will call frantically if they stop hearing the voice they imprinted on, but remain unable to move until Juvenile. Juvenile Therizinosaurus, if imprinted on another species through calls, will take their wobbly first steps in search of the voice that called for them. Constantly peeping, and at times frantic, they will search the areas surrounding for days, trying to find the sound again. If not imprinted, juvenile Therizinosaurus will begin to wander in search of a sound. Juvenile Therizinosaurus will imprint on any species it hears, but often will prefer the presence of other feathered dinosaurs - including Citipati, Struthiomimus, and Deinocheirus, and at times, even the carnivorous Deinonychus, Kelenken, Utahraptor, Achillobator, Latenivenatrix, or Yutyrannus- which frequently results in a tragic end. No matter the species a baby Therizinosaurus imprints on, it will always prefer to hide under its tail as a source of comfort. Orphaned Therizinosaurus are clingier with their parents and other members of their species as Juveniles and Adolescents, and tend to show more stress and anxiety when not part of a group, or when the area surrounding it is too quiet. Adolescent Therizinosaurus will mimic the behaviors of the species it has been imprinted on, if imprinted. However, it will begin to grow slightly more independent of its parent species, wandering farther and exploring more of the area surrounding it. Adolescent Therizinosaurus who have been adopted by another species are typically not as defensive of their personal space bubble as their nested counterparts, and will allow others to get close when necessary. As orphaned Therizinosaurus reach Sub-Adult, they begin to drift away from any parents that are not the same species as it. They will go out exploring at night and will not return to their adoptive parents, seeking the comfort of their own species as they did when they were hatchlings, but now able to find them independently. Therizinosaurus are an odd, fiercely territorial, and defensive species, who prefer to keep a decent amount of distance between themselves and all other creatures. Therizinosaurus have eyes that are highly sensitive to light. During the daytime, if moving around for any reason, a Therizinosaurus may close its eyes to protect them, relying on routine and its strong sense of hearing to move to where it needs to go. OPTIONAL: Juvenile Therizinosaurus who open their eyes for the first time during the day time may be rendered entirely blind due to light sensitivity. Therizinosaurus will prefer to stick to heavily shaded areas during the daytime, if forced to move. Their feathers are a strong source of pride, especially amidst females who use their tails and colors in order to attract and receive affections from potential mates. Females tend to their feathers by taking baths in the healing waters across Gondwa. For this reason, many Therizinosaurus females avoid mud, non-healing water, dust, and rain when they can help it. This behavior is not observed on Riparia, instead supplementing this behavior with dust baths. Therizinosaurus may be seen standing idly in the healing ponds in Hunter’s Thicket, Hot Springs, and Whistling Columns. Their legs carry a lot of weight from the feathers that cover their bodies, and while being a method of relaxing and cleaning themselves, the healing water soothes soreness from daily patrols and migrations. Therizinosaurus may migrate to healing waters before courting, in order to clean themselves and ensure that their feathers are ready to be displayed. Therizinosaurus are nocturnal, and will only partake in active behaviors such as courting, patrolling, nesting, and building nests during the nighttime. However, if a Therizinosaurus is awoken by another species: approaching a resource they are sleeping next to, or making loud noises, or otherwise being disruptive, a Therizinosaurus may wake up and challenge that creature away from that resource and out of their territory. Only the Dame and one other female may partake in these challenges. Therizinosaurus are slow moving dinosaurs, but when provoked they are powerful. Therizinosaurus within the same Sect will challenge each other to games of running to food and water first, and will always work to beat all other herbivores to resources. While male Therizinosaurus opt to then shyly back away from the resource if challenged, females will defend her winnings fiercely, calling loudly and flexing her claws. Hens are extremely protective of their tails, taking time to meticulously preen and maintain their feathers. Therizinosaurus hens work hard to ensure that their tails stay out of carnivorous reach– however, bear scars on their tails to indicate strength and survival. A hen may only allow its lifelong pairbond to groom its tail; even then, if it becomes irritated with its mate’s behavior, it may shoo them off again with pecks and squawks. Therizinosaurus hens are also significantly more confrontational than their male counterparts, and may take part in combats against other species, sects, and other female Therizinosaurus. A male will only ever take part in an engagement if his mate or chicks are attacked. A dominant female, known as a Dame, will lead a Therizinosaurus sect. This position is highly coveted by other females within the sect, and may be challenged for - detailed in Aggressive Behavior. Therizinosaurus roosters are a bit more easy-going and friendly, socializing happily with other smaller feathered species and other Therizinosaurus within their groupings. They are more likely to shyly allow intrusions into their personal space, either opting to move away or stand there frozen if unsure of what to do. Therizinosaurus males tend to be mainly responsible for raising chicks and tending to the females within the groups.
