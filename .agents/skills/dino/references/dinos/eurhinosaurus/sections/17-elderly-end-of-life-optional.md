# Eurhinosaurus – Elderly & End of Life (Optional)

As Eurhinosaurus age, they spend less time in the open ocean and stay close to rivers and coast lines. They lose the ability to shed their skin to camouflage themselves, and show more white or pale colors in their skin as their patterns and original colors fade. The pale colors often make elderly Eurhinosaurus stick out and become a target for predators. When the shoal migrates to breeding territory, elderly males will beach themselves to display, but sometimes struggle to return to the water quickly and become a bigger target for predators. Companions of elderly individuals will sometimes stick close by their side, guiding them to feeding areas and encouraging them to keep up with the shoal. Eventually, elderly Eurhinosaurus lose their swimming strength and float gently to the bottom of the sea or river bed, in their final resting place. Closely bonded individuals will mourn the loss of their friends for a short period of time, before moving on with their shoal and forming new bonds.
