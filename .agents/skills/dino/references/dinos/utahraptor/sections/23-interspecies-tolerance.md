# Utahraptor – Interspecies Tolerance

Utahraptor do not tolerate and have a deep hatred for Dryosaurus - this small feisty herbivore has a habit of invading nesting spots and attacking young. All Utahraptor in a family pack, of any age, will aggressively engage with Dryosaurus, regardless of hunger or claimed corpses nearby. Utahraptor do not tolerate scavengers. Utahraptor are excellent and co-ordinated hunters, with a hunting party organized by the Alphas to carefully balance skills and ability. Each member has strengths in the family pack, and young are encouraged into the hunting party to teach them the pack’s hunting strategy.  Family packs prefer to hunt within their territory where possible, but packs that desperately need food can move further out in search of prey. Solitary individuals are seemingly seen before they are heard. Quiet by nature when alone, they curiously follow herds of sauropods, using the herd to alert them of any dangerous predators that may enter the area and wait for easy hunting opportunities in calves during breeding seasons. Utahraptor may not start a hunt against other species if other Utahraptor are present in the POI. They must be chased out before a hunt can be started on other species. Utahraptor prefer to hunt up to Large tier where possible, and will not hunt Apex tier if there are viable Large tier or smaller prey options available in the POI. Only 1 hunting party per family pack may engage in a hunt at a time. Utahraptors are skilled social hunters when hunting other species, and family packs maintain close bonds, with the offspring of the alpha pair holding the highest rank. These individuals, considered Betas, lead the hunts, while Omegas typically fill the remaining roles in the pack. Utahraptors employ endurance hunting tactics, first circling their target and using growls and snapping jaws to frighten and intimidate. The Betas take turns running through prey groups to separate and confuse the animals, aiming for a weaker target. Some packs prefer to hunt adults over young, as it provides more sustenance for a larger Utahraptor family. Younger and smaller packs, lacking the confidence, often simply target the easiest meal while they are still establishing themselves in the area. The hunting party divides its efforts between those who bite the prey and those who bluff, using their numbers to confuse and exhaust the target. When prey is successfully killed, the higher ranks and their mates eat first, filling their bellies while standing guard over their kill. This feeding order is not challenged once learned, and respected by all ranks. Individuals may correct others if they forget their place with growls and well timed nips. When interested in courting another individual, Utahraptor have a few options available to them. One Utahraptor may opt to temporarily disperse from their natal pack to roam to the territories of other packs. They will not allow any of their siblings to come with them, as more than one Utahraptor entering the territory of a different pack risks an aggressive response. If preferring to stay within the proximity of their natal pack, Utahraptor will roam at the edges of the territory, sniffing and searching for dispersals seeking a mate. If without a natal pack, Utahraptor will roam in search of other solitary individuals to court with, or travel to pack territories.
