# Utahraptor – Orphaned Behavior (Wild Spawn)

Orphaned Utahraptors, being weak and small, face extremely low survival chances. As easy prey to most predators, they typically seek refuge in small dens where they remain hidden until they are strong enough to defend themselves. Their soft, fluffy plumage provides some warmth, but without a caregiver, they must fend for themselves. They attempt to survive by scavenging from abandoned corpses or hunting small critters. Orphans do not group up with other orphans, and instinctively avoid other Utahraptor until they are physically mature, and wish to form a pack of their own. Utahraptors are cautious towards their own kind and do not share resources or space. Family packs are committed to securing and defending their nesting area against rival packs of ungrouped individuals. The area which they have claimed to nest in is referred to as the pack territory: a chosen location guarded by an entire bloodline. Although Utahraptor in a pack may venture beyond their chosen territory borders in search of prey, doing so is always a calculated risk. Only preferred POIs can be claimed as territory, which is only defended from other Utahraptor. Each POI is listed as either a compact territory, or a grand territory. Compact Territories are limited to 5 Utahraptor per side in an engagement, whereas Grand Territories have no engagement limits. Smaller packs gravitate towards compact territories that are more manageable for their numbers. With fewer mouths to feed compared to larger packs, the area holds enough resources to sustain them. Smaller in size, these territories are easier to patrol and manage, offering a safe haven from overpowering bloodlines who crave grand territories to support their larger numbers. Once outside their territory, Utahraptor are mindful of their surroundings and move quietly from place to place, aware that they could be encroaching upon enemy pack's territories. When pushed to venture beyond their preferred POI, family packs will travel together closely, following the lead of their Alpha pair. Utahraptor only leave their preferred POI when starvation threatens their pack and territory is abandoned in a desperate attempt to find food. Once their bellies are filled, Utahraptor quickly retreat into familiar regions, eager to return to their territory. Utahraptor always posture and growl when an unfamiliar individual approaches. Therefore, when an individual is separated from their family and returns months later, their arrival back to the family territory is done with caution. The returning individual shakes out their feathers and calls softly, standing some distance away to wait for the recognition of family through their scent. When recognized, both will welcome each other happily, chirping and grooming to establish bonds. Utahraptor are keen preeners - using self-grooming as a measure of self-soothing, as well as general hygiene upkeep. Individuals may groom one another as a sign of affection and an attempt to bond, especially after a squabble.
