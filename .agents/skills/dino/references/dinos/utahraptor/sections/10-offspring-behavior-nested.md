# Utahraptor – Offspring Behavior (Nested)

At birth, baby Utahraptors, referred to as chicks, are weak and fragile. They remain hidden in the nest, cared for by trusted packmates who provide protection during their vulnerable early weeks. Unattended chicks are blind to danger outside the den, so the entire family fiercely guards them. The clutch a chick hatches into determines their roles within the family pack. Utahraptor family packs do not permit every rank to nest - only offspring to the Alpha pair (Beta), siblings to one of the Alphas (Delta) and grandchildren (Delta) are permitted to nest. Omegas are forbidden from nesting, and any pair ranked as Omega caught breeding are exiled from the pack immediately. Offspring born from a clutch sired by both Alpha parents become betas. Betas who breed will produce offspring who rank as deltas, and offspring of delta become omega rank. Once taking their first mouthfuls of food, chicks are big enough to begin exploring their immediate area. Chirping at their clutchmates, they crawl over each other and investigate the nest they hatched into. As chicks, there is no defining behavior difference between which rank chicks are born into. The realization of rank only comes into play during adolescence where chicks are led on patrols and hunts by different family members who share their skills with the younger generation of their rank. As chicks transition into the juvenile stage, they grow stronger and more restless, prompting their parents to introduce them to various objects around the den. Curious and exploratory, juveniles use their teeth to investigate their surroundings, engage in rough play with siblings, and occasionally nibble on their parents' tails. Utahraptor chicks mimic the movements and sounds of their parents and family to strengthen social bonds. They chirp softly to imitate adult calls, practicing their future pack communication skills. During supervised moments outside the nest, chicks explore under the watchful eye of their pack. They occasionally stray outside the realm of the territory, but are quickly herded back with gentle nips or growls from adults. When it rains, chicks display highly playful behaviors such as pouncing on puddles or playing with dripping water, and shaking their feathers in the rain. This natural engagement helps them become accustomed to weather changes and terrain. Within their family pack, chicks are chaperoned around by older offspring to the Alpha pair. Their role as Beta permits them to oversee skill development of the younger family members and start forming bonds early. These relationships last a lifetime and forge alliances that help establish one’s position within the family, and provides support when disagreements between packmates require support. Once offspring reach adolescence, their curiosity and fast metabolism is no longer suited to staying at the den . They follow their older siblings out the nesting area, mimicking their behavior and learning the ins and outs of survival. If the territory is challenged for, chicks will hide quietly under shelter, nervously watching the engagement. If their pack forfeits or dies in the challenge, babies will flee, skittish and flighty until they are reunited with a familiar face. Offspring that end up a different color palette from their bloodline will find themselves exiled from the pack by the entire family upon maturity. Though confused and upset by this sudden rejection, they have no choice but to leave and try to survive on their own. Once all the offspring are subadult, the Alphas invite chicks to join them on hunts, taking turns with each youngling to teach them hunting strategies and group co-ordination. Oftentimes, the caregiver who helped raise them attends these hunts, keeping a keen eye on their chosen sibling and helping them learn how to feed the family. Young adults and packmates are shown critter burrows and water sources as well as where the borders of their territory end by older, more experienced members of the pack. Keeping each member fit by patrolling and swapping individuals out in regular hunting parties is a way Alphas ensure their territory is well defended.
