# Utahraptor – Parenthood

Once eggs hatch, the parents begin by caring for their offspring by themselves - but all members of the pack participate in child rearing, and help one another with the raising of young. Alphas support nesting efforts within their pack by organizing members into rotating hunting groups to bring back food for those caring for young. When fewer family members are present, both parents alternate between hunting and caring for the eggs or offspring. They incubate the eggs by carefully covering them to keep them warm without crushing them, mimicking modern bird behavior. Some packmates, specifically elder individuals, help those nesting by bringing roots, flowers and reeds to warm the chicks. They also serve as primary caretakers of young when other adults - particularly parents - are out hunting, or defending territory. Nesting is particularly stressful for the entire pack, especially for the Alpha Pair. The Alphas must coordinate hunting parties to ensure a steady food supply for their growing family while also defending their territory from potential thieves. Raising chicks is mainly the job of parents, elders and omegas. Caregivers regularly bring food and various objects to be used as toys back to the nesting spot, which are eagerly explored by chicks and fought over jealousy. Offspring are kept in line by the entirety of the pack. Those that wander too far are quickly corrected, and at least one adult always has eyes on the young, acting as a babysitter. Raising young is far easier with more pack members, and becomes less stressful for the entire pack when there are more hands on deck to assist in providing for offspring.
