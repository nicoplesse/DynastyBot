# Lurdusaurus – Social Interaction

Lurdusaurus have a dominant male who acts as the leading individual for the float. He has won this position through winning bullfights and earning the most favour from the females. His position often makes him the most favourable to the females, and it is as such highly sought after between the males. Other males only challenge him when trying to take over leadership. The dominant bull often spends a great amount of his energy patrolling the territory and showing off to cows. He showcases his strength by leaping above them or above rocks, brings them gifts, and even tries to flick mud onto them to encourage bonding games. Other males are regarded as the subordinates. They behave more submissively than the dominant male, until one of them gains enough confidence to try and claim his position. These males are by no means meek -  they engage in brawls, races and games to improve themselves, always working to become stronger while the dominant male is distracted tending to the float in general. Females greatly enjoy watching the males spar, and will often settle down together to witness it. Finding the males amusing and great entertainment during the downtime. All males work together to protect the territory, cows, and calves within the float, under the dominant bull’s leadership. Cows are incredibly individualistic, they prioritise the needs of themselves and their own offspring above anything else. Cows that are in a float with their related female family members tend to reject unrelated females from their personal space. When near resources, older females often show aggressive behavior to unrelated females that get too close or attempt to eat at the same time as their family unit. They will nip at the unrelated female, and rake their large thumb claws across the other’s sides. If the other female retaliates, the spats will continue until one reaches half health or backs down. As such, they prefer to band together with females they know. Sisters, mothers, daughters, or even unrelated females they were raised around. This makes conflict less likely, and play behaviors more common. Females in a float will also show close bonds with each other so long as they were raised together or are related, often using each other as scratching posts due to their itchy skin, wallowing in the mud together, and even playfully fighting to keep their own skills sharp. Should the float ever need to move from their territory, they travel together warily. The dominant male will lead, while the subordinates will patrol around the outskirts of the float, the females and their offspring bound together in the middle. Oftentimes he will migrate them through waterways, only ever travelling on land through the shade or during the cover of night, and only if truly necessary such as a result of threats - or being expelled by an enemy float. During times of relaxation, Lurdusaurus enjoy flicking mud and water onto one another, often running or swimming away moments later, inciting a small game where they chase each other around. Males become obnoxious and far more playful during the night, when many Lurdusaurus heighten their activity. They practice their bullfight or acrobatics, usually taking it onto land. It's common to see a pair of males stretching their arms wide and posturing at one another, letting out loud bellows. Albino individuals are shunned from a float and condemned to a more lonely existence. Individuals will turn their back to them at first, push them away from resources, and if ignored, will face them directly and call aggressively. If these warnings are still ignored, the float will take further action by attacking them and driving them away.
