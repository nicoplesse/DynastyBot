# Lurdusaurus – Predator Reaction

Lurdusaurus always flee from Deinosuchus or Spinosaurus hunting them unless there are at least 5 sub/adults in their group, in which case they will attempt to defend themselves. When hunted by other semi-aquatics, bulls always come to the defence of the rest of the float, but cows will step in if there are no males present, or not enough to be able to defend effectively. They will try to intimidate the hunters away by standing their ground and bellowing. Slapping the surface of the water, letting out loud warning calls. When hunted by terrestrial species, Lurdusaurus will retreat to the water’s edge where bulls take the lead in defence. These herbivores feel empowered and confident at the shoreline; they stand in the shallows or on the edge of the shore, bellowing and trying to intimidate their hunter. Lurdusaurus attempt to kick, bite, shove and even claw the target until they forfeit or the Lurdusaurus is too weak and chooses to flee into the water. Lurdusaurus will never try to chase down or kill a fleeing hunter unless they are near the edge of the water, at which point they will attempt to finish them off, never running more than 10 body lengths to kill a fleeing hunter. Members of the float that are not involved in a hunting defence engagement will move away swiftly, but stay close to one another; avoiding making themselves an easy target for the predator to switch to.
