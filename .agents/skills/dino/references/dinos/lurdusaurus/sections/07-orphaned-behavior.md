# Lurdusaurus – Orphaned Behavior

Orphaned Lurdusaurus are left to fend for themselves without their mothers guidance or teaching. They are slow to awaken after hatching, and even slower to find their way to water, often stumbling about and crying out in hopes for their mother to hear them. Once they realise they’re alone, they will wobble about mostly in silence, occasionally snorting or honking in hopes of another Lurdusaurus finding them. Once they realise they are truly alone, instinct will lead them towards the nearest water source. They will learn quickly how sensitive their scales are to the sun, and will be seen hiding beneath the shade and trying to roll in mud on their way to water. Orphaned calves lack a lot of the confidence that their float-raised counterparts hold. As soon as they find water they spend most of their time hiding amongst branches, rocks, and aquatic plants. As they grow in Juveniles, their curiosity kicks in and they venture out into the water and can be seen playing with fish and riverbed rocks. Much of their time is still spent hiding, moving onto land occasionally but keeping to vegetation to avoid predators. Even in Adolescence, an orphaned calf will keep out of sight as much as possible. If they come across other young creatures, they will follow them around from a distance at first while they work out whether they are a threat or not. Orphan calves that find themselves with other herbivores will avoid drawing too much attention to themselves. Occasionally playing with other youngsters but remaining shy and cautious in behavior, always choosing to observe from a distance first. Mid way through adolescence, Lurdusaurus develop instincts to seek out others of their own species, searching river banks, coasts and the surrounding areas for floats to join. Female orphans usually have a difficult time differentiating social cues from other females, sometimes leading to them accidentally invading the personal space of others, and crying in confusion when they are aggressed away. This can even result in them becoming overaggressive later on in life, valuing their own personal space and nipping without warning at any females who come too close. Male orphans on the other hand have an easier time, while they lack the combat prowess of the other float-raised bulls, they tend to be more agile and excel at swimming. Sub-adult Lurdusaurus that started life as an orphan will hopefully have found themselves a float to join at this point, as they are typically accepted as adults into existing groups. Males remain less skilled at fighting, and females remain less socially aware. Adult orphaned Lurdusaurus that have not joined a float will seek to follow herds of Iguanodon,Tenontosaurus and Lambeosaurus as they feel the need to have company. Lurdusaurus raised in an orphaned setting will sometimes appear friendlier to other species than the average Lurdusaurus, often investigating other species up close unless aggravated and pushed away, as they never learned personal space, even for other species.
