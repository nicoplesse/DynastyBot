# Lurdusaurus – Cross-Species Interaction

Lurdusaurus are incredibly territorial towards other semi-aquatics in their claimed territory. Medium and Large semi-aquatics are aggressed at all times if they enter into the float's territory in the water, and will be continually aggressed until they leave the claimed territory. Deinosuchus and Spinosaurus however, pose a higher risk to the float. Unless there are at least 5 sub/adults present and ready to defend, the float will not risk attacking or driving them out; they will be ignored, and avoided where possible. Deinocheirus are also a high risk species, so Lurdusaurus will only attack and drive them out when there are at least 5 sub/adults present. In the Rainy Season, non-aquatic species are largely ignored unless they pose a threat to any calves that are present in the float. Defensive attacks will be led by the dominant male and always aim to remove the threat by chasing them out, rather than fighting to the death. In the Dry Season, all Lurdusaurus - even those not in a claimed territory - become aggressive to other species that approach the water's edge - including even Apexes. They utilise the water to flee from these engagements if the situation appears to turn unfavorably for them - but have no qualms with starting them. When retreating back into the water, however, they cannot aggress the same species again until 15 minutes have passed, following normal engagement rules. Lurdusaurus are relatively passive towards Rhamphorhynchus. The tiny aerial carnivores pose no risk to the herbivore, and are rather perfect for clearing off stuck shed or water ticks clinging to their scales. Iguanodon are greatly admired by Lurdusaurus, and are one of the few species that Lurdusaurus will not aggress away from their territory. They approach these herbivores when they spot them and grunt at them affectionately, keen to socialize with these similar-looking species. Females that have failed repeated clutches or simply desire to adopt offspring may sometimes attempt to adopt Iguanodon calves. This behavior can be very frightening for an Iguanodon calf - particularly when the female attempts to encourage it into the water, seemingly oblivious to its panicked cries. Males strictly intervene if noticing females in the float exhibiting this behavior, and aggress the Iguanodon calf away and back onto land - ideally back towards its own kind. Iguanodon calves that accept their adoptive parent and follow them into the water often die by accidental drowning, attempting to become aquatic like their family. Camptosaurus and Tenontosaurus are terrestrial hadrosaurs that look uncannily similar to Lurdusaurus, and males especially easily mistake them for odd looking Lurdusaurus with stunted growth. Both sexes sometimes mistake them for small versions of Lurdusaurus, and during courting if a Camptosaurus is nearby, they may accidentally choose to court them. Young of these species are sometimes mistakenly adopted into Lurdusaurus floats and will be guided into the water by the adoptive female, and may accidentally drown if they swim too far from the shore. Lurdusaurus bulls will put a stop to this behavior if they notice it, driving away adoptive offspring of a different species with aggressive calls and pushing them back onto land towards their true species. Friendship with Yunnanosaurus Despite Lurdusaurus being so territorial and aggressive to most other species, Lurdusaurus adore Yunnanosaurus. Lurdusaurus share a kinship with Yunnanosaurus; both species have similar smooth skin and long bodies, and are intolerant of most carnivores. When a Yunnnanosaurus crosses the water, Lurdusaurus greet their terrestrial cousins with calls of joy and sometimes leap out of the water to perform for them. Both species find one another entertaining, Lurdusaurus greatly enjoy the sight of their long necked friends with their odd appearance, and Yunnanosaurus find great amusement in the parading of Lurdusaurus in the water, sometimes pausing by the shore in hopes that a Lurdusaurus will leap for them. Lurdusaurus often encourage Yunnanosaurus to join them in the water, even going so far to knock them in by launching forwards through the mud once from shore. Pushing them into deeper bodies of water is great fun for Lurdusaurus, but any alarm calls from Yunnanosaurus are respected. Lurdusaurus will sometimes even remember their Yunnanosaurus friends and seek them out when the Yunnanosaurus return in the Dry Season to exchange friendly calls and spend a few moments together. As Yunnanosaurus travel often during the Dry Season, Lurdusaurus may attempt to follow them through rivers, or may simply bid them farewell until they meet again. Friendship with Halszkaraptor Lurdusaurus happily welcome Halszkaraptor as part of their group, showing uncharacteristically tolerant behavior towards them. Halszkaraptor make excellent sentries for Lurdusaurus, and the small dromaeosaurid frequently collects and shares a number of food resources, helping to smooth over relations with Lurdusaurus.
