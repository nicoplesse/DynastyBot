# Compsognathus – Orphaned Behavior (Wild Spawn)

Orphaned chicks lack some of the boldness of those raised with parents or in a swarm. They spend their time darting between the undergrowth and make much less noise than those raised in a brood. Orphans will eagerly rush to any older Compsognathus that they come across, hoping to be adopted and receive some safety amongst numbers. Orphaned Compsognathus chicks that mature into adults often prefer to stay solitary and tag along behind different carnivores, rather than forming groups with their own kind. Orphaned Compsognathus enjoy following Tyrannosaurus, Giganotosaurus, and Torvosaurus and will sometimes join up with a few other Compsognathus to form a small group. Compsognathus are highly social dinosaurs, and live in well organized swarms that have a hierarchy between members. Swarms are ruled by a dominant male, who maintains general order amongst the group and chooses the direction in which they travel, and several dominant females, who like to take charge when it comes to courting and nesting. Compsognathus work together to secure resources and track down larger carnivores that are worth following. The dominant male leads the rest of the swarm towards their next migration point and determines whether the swarm should pursue a carnivore or not. Compsognathus are extremely vocal, using an array of squeaks, chirps and squawks to alert the rest of the swarm to danger or potential food opportunities. Compsognathus may “choose” a particular carnivore to follow for their entire life. They are not possessive over these carnivores, but they follow them relentlessly and tend to them, alerting if they detect threats and screeching joyfully if they see a potential target. Intelligent carnivores may heed their Compsognathus companions and tune in to their calls to listen out for prey. Compsognathus does not intentionally lead carnivores to prey, but is noisy and excited about creatures it discovers. Sometimes, a Compsognathus can accidentally alert their chosen carnivore to a dangerous situation rather than suitable prey. Compsognathus is not always the smartest when it comes to sizing up prey, and will squeak and call out just about anything it sees. Compsognathus are fairly intelligent for their size, and have high emotional understanding. If a chosen carnivore migrates through POIs, a Compsognathus will follow along dutifully, trusting their carnivore to provide them with scavenging opportunities. With their high emotional intelligence, Compsognathus will not hunt other Compsognathus. However, in the case that a member of their swarm dies, they may choose to eat from the body. The little scavengers are known to be highly resourceful, but their intelligence gives them a moment of pause to pay respect to their fallen member before gratefully consuming the meat left behind. Compsognathus do not attempt to hunt babies of their chosen carnivores. If their chosen carnivore dies, they may eat from the body, similarly to how they feed from their own species. They will stay close by the body and will fiercely attempt to defend it. These relationships between hunter and scavenger will last the Compsognathus’s whole life in most circumstances, and often end in the Compsognathus passing peacefully alongside their larger friend.
