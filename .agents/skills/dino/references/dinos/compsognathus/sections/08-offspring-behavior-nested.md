# Compsognathus – Offspring Behavior (Nested)

Compsognathus offspring, known as chicks, often hatch in large numbers from typically poorly constructed nests. These nests are usually situated under little foliage, in an open environment. Born fully equipped for survival, chicks are on their feet immediately, quickly taking to following the adults around them. They instinctively copy older individuals, mimicking their movements and behaviors, much to the older Compsognathus' annoyance. Both mothers and fathers check up on their chicks, encouraging them towards food and occasionally showing parental affection. Chicks have keen survival instincts - while they are extremely curious about the world around them, they are quick to scatter into the undergrowth at sudden noises, movements, or any other perceived threats. Chicks that start to fall behind in growth or appear sickly end up neglected by their parents, and are eventually pushed to the outer ring of the swarm's social circle. Chicks begin to show their intelligence and their ability to work together around adolescence, when they begin to collaborate on hunts against critters. While chicks are not permitted to join hunts against other dinosaurs until they are full grown, they take great delight and pride in taking down a critter, and will screech happily to alert all nearby of their success, before feeding from the corpse. As a swarm rarely kicks out any of their members, chicks that age into adulthood are permitted to stay. They start at the bottom of the social ladder, and must navigate their way up the dynamics of the swarm.
