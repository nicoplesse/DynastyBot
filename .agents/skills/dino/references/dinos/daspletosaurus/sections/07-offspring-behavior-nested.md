# Daspletosaurus – Offspring Behavior (Nested)

Daspletosaurus offspring, known as cubs, hatch completely blind, deaf, and defenceless. Mothers keep their babies hidden away for the majority of their hatchling phase. Daspletosaurus cubs do not make a sound while in the nest, remaining quiet and unassuming; instinctually in tune with their own self-preservation. Mothers often leave their nest unattended to hunt for food, draw predators away, or patrol the area around their nest. By Juvenile, cubs are capable of standing up and moving around, and start to leave the safety of their nest - under their mother's watch. Young cubs explore the world with their mouths, nipping at their surroundings, including the tails of other clutchmates and similarly aged young. A nipped tail often results in a cry for their mother, who comforts the cub. Cubs do not playfight with each other at this stage. Daspletosaurus juveniles instinctually start to herd and hunt critters they encounter, with full encouragement from their mother. In larger clutches, some cubs are inclined to act as the ‘herders’ and drive critters towards the ‘hunters’. Despite this, cubs do not participate in active hunts or engagements with their parents, and flee for cover. As they reach Adolescent, Daspletosaurus cubs grow bolder in their actions, sometimes even chasing smaller dinosaurs of Small tier or lower - but never attempt a hunt against these dinosaurs. Adolescent male Daspletosaurus branch away from their mothers and begin to mirror the behavior of their father - though their father never pays them much attention; beyond protecting them if attacked. Adolescent female Daspletosaurus mirror the behaviors of their mother. Adolescent Daspletosaurus are more inclined to fighting with each other to establish dominance. Males in particular use this to bond with others in their clutch. These fights rarely reach below half health, and mothers issue corrective bites to any offspring that show signs of hyper-aggression. These bites are never fatal. As cubs reach Sub-Adult, they are almost fully independent from their parents, and at this point males usually have established bonds with others in their clutch, if any. Sub-Adult Daspletosaurus often disperse from their parents' pack to pursue courting or socialization outside their family group. A male may opt to stay if he has bonded with his father enough to become his dominant male partner.
