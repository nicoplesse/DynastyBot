# Daspletosaurus – Dominant Males vs. Ungrouped Males

Dominant males will not tolerate ungrouped males in their vicinity, and will attempt to force them into their pack as a submissive male - or chase them out entirely. Males greet each other by lifting their heads high and showing off their fitness and vibrancy. Ungrouped males who posture back are quickly chased out, as  dominant males do not tolerate other dominant males around their pack. If the male is submissive or not intending to take over the pack, he will crouch and make soft calls at the dominant males. If they accept his display of submission, they may allow him to join his pack. This is, however, never guaranteed - and dominant males may still decide to chase out a submissive male if they have existing submissive males in their pack already. Ungrouped males that want to take over a pack posture and broadcast to show their intention to take over the pack, lifting their heads high and turning to the side to display the bright flashes of colors on their body. Only the dominant male pair will engage in these challenges. If the dominant male is solitary, only he will partake in these challenges. Dominant males of the pack always position themselves between the males and their pack, showing their position and separating the males further from their females. A battle will ensue, with both parties fighting until one forfeits or until a male of either side are killed in battle. Daspletosaurus pack takeovers typically end in bloodshed. Despite this, if a male forfeits during the battle it is always respected, with the winning party maintaining or taking position as leader of the pack. If the dominant male is in a pair, his partner will also forfeit the challenge. If a pack takeover has occurred, females who have bonded with the original males may opt to disperse and follow their preferred males. Daspletosaurus aggress any carnivore in 5 body lengths of themselves or their pack; displayed as posturing, roaring, and snarling. This is not done if the Daspletosaurus are attempting to hunt the carnivore. Unless solitary, Daspletosaurus fear no other dinosaur. Solitary Daspletosaurus avoid Apex carnivore pairs entirely. Daspletosaurus tolerate scavengers when they have eaten their fill of a catch. If a scavenger attempts to scavenge while they are still eating they will aggress them away. Daspletosaurus are less likely to tolerate Quetzalcoatlus scavenging from their kills. Daspletosaurus are not aggressive towards any herbivore that is not aggressive towards them. However, all individuals have an instinctual need to herd, and may often surround herds even if they do not wish to hunt them. They will always stay 5 body lengths away from the herd and will only get closer if they intend to hunt.
