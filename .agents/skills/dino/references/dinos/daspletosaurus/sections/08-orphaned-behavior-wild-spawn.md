# Daspletosaurus – Orphaned Behavior (Wild Spawn)

Orphaned Daspletosaurus cubs rarely survive. Without the regular care of their mother, they are extremely vulnerable. These orphaned offspring do not move for the first portion of their life until they reach Juvenile. Because of their instinctual independence, orphaned Daspletosaurus do not seek out packs to join initially, and if encountered by a pack are chased away. Orphaned Daspletosaurus are never adopted. They spend the majority of their time hiding in dense undergrowth, and sneaking past larger dinosaurs. Orphaned cubs typically grow up more cautious and aware of their surroundings. By Adolescence they have gained confidence enough to start hunting Small tier and below dinosaurs and target these species first. Daspletosaurus Adolescents flee from all dinosaurs Medium tier and above and attempt to evade them by hiding. They will only turn to defend themselves if they are cornered. By Sub-Adult, orphaned Daspletosaurus have gained enough confidence in themselves to defend themselves and begin to seek out a pack of their own. Daspletosaurus are generally very haughty and confident individuals that spend most of their lives learning and coordinating their hunting tactics. This species is very intelligent and strategic, but relies heavily on the bonds it makes with other individuals to achieve hunting success. Males, in particular, form strong bonds with their fathers, brothers, or even other unrelated males in order to maintain claims of and court females. These dominant male pairs will also lead the pack in hunting, and may opt to have a submissive male or a female assist, acting as a “herder.” Daspletosaurus trail herds for long distances, especially Hadrosaurus herds consisting of Parasaurolophus or Lambeosaurus. They are careful to not be noticed by these flighty species. Daspletosaurus exist in dense forests and rocky cliff regions. Regions like this provide ample hunting opportunity and difficult terrain to hide and raise their young. Because of this, Daspletosaurus possess strong and muscular bodies that help them traverse this terrain. Daspletosaurus are not naturally curious, but younger individuals are more likely to seek out experiences or dinosaurs they have never seen before. This is especially prevalent among Adolescent Daspletosaurus, who sometimes get themselves into dangerous situations. Their parents, however, always come to their aid. When not hunting, Daspletosaurus spend time playfighting with other individuals in their pack - a mutually enjoyable action that helps to strengthen their bonds. However - this playful sparring is kept strictly to same sex playmates, with males and females unwilling to spar with one another. Daspletosaurus are deadliest when the pack grows silent. Their roars, which often echo through the cliffsides they favor, are replaced by huffs and sniffs of the air in search of prey. When looking for prey, Daspletosaurus often search for high boulders or cliffsides that are easy for them to climb up on for vantage points. Males are often much more outright dominant than females, and show off amongst each other often. Females, while quieter than males, still act boldly, and do not back down when threatened.
