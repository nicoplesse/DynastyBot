# Daspletosaurus – Adoption

Daspletosaurus ignore unattended and unrelated offspring. They do not adopt. Orphans will be aggressed away if they linger for too long. Daspletosaurus males, as they age, become less effective in their performances for females. They begin to move more slowly, and often start to dull in color. They end up unable to keep up with the needs of the females of their pack, which often means females dispersing on their own terms to find more suitable males. Due to this, more often than not, elderly males have only their bonded male to rely on, both when it comes to hunting and protection - or they become a submissive male. Females, on the other hand, are continued to be provided for even as they age and begin to lay less eggs. They continue to assist other females in their pack with their cubs even if they have none of their own, and males continue to attempt to feed them and take care of them in the same fashion as if they could properly court her. Daspletosaurus who are older in seasons may be observed taking longer naps more often, moving more slowly, and not being as willing to stray from preferred areas as their younger counterparts.
