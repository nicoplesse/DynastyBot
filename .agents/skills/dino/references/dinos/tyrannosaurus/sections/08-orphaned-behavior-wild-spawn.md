# Tyrannosaurus – Orphaned Behavior (Wild Spawn)

Orphaned Tyrannosaurus cubs face a brutal and often short-lived existence. Without the protection of at least one parent, their odds of survival drop dramatically, particularly within the first few stages of their life. Hatchlings and young juveniles left without parents rarely survive long. Their vocal cries for their parents sometimes draw the wrong kind of attention, and serve as a beacon for opportunistic predators. Orphans that lose their parents fare better. They remain in a group with each other, but do not defend one another, and stick together purely for the safety that numbers bring. Those that do survive into Juvenile grow significantly quieter than parented offspring. Through necessity, they avoid unnecessary vocalizations and rarely interact with other Tyrannosaurus. Adults of their species are especially dangerous, and frequently cannibalize unrelated offspring of Adolescent and below. Adults mature into more cautious but stealthy individuals, and tend to prefer a more solitary lifestyle, taking more time to carefully assess potential mates before committing. Tyrannosaurus is a highly aggressive and territorial species that claims large territories consisting of an entire POI. Once established in their territory, they patrol it ferociously, and quickly drive out rival carnivores with fierce battles. Patrols are carried out both day and night, following slow and deliberate routes that are repeated regularly, and accompanied with lots of sniffing to hone in on different scents. These routines help reinforce scent trails, and establish a visible presence to deter rivals. Mated pairs sometimes patrol side by side, but often split up to patrol separate areas, ensuring more ground is covered. When raising offspring, mated pairs never patrol together. Tyrannosaurus have an infamous ability to nearly disappear in areas of high coverage and foliage despite their massive size. This is utilized heavily to ambush prey, and Tyrannosaurus can lay in wait for many hours for the right opportunity to strike. Tyrannosaurus establish their own routines in regards to courtship, nesting, hunting, and sleeping. Each individual has their own schedule that they abide by, which only truly adjusts when they begin to bond with their mate, and when having to feed and care for their offspring. Tyrannosaurus may also adjust their schedule to better align with potential prey in the area; for example, sleeping during the day time to attempt to ambush a Parasaurolophus herd at night. Tyrannosaurus enjoy taking long naps, especially when it is the Rainy Season. This behavior is seen most often following a successful hunt, and additionally when it rains - if not using the rain as a cover for ambush. Tyrannosaurus rub themselves against trees, rocks, cliffsides, and their mate in order to scratch themselves and leave their scent. Tyrannosaurus may develop favored trees or objects to serve this purpose within their territory. Tyrannosaurus - especially younger Tyrannosaurus - are especially curious and will investigate new objects, scents, and locations via smell. They lower their noses to the object and sniff until it can be identified. Tyrannosaurus may develop favorite scents over their lifetimes, with the most popular ones being the scent of carrion, meat, or their mates. Tyrannosaurus bulls, typically of a smaller stature, are more agile than their female counterparts. They set up elaborate ambushes in order to chase prey towards their mate, while females - larger and stronger - wait until prey gets into her proximity before swiftly lunging and landing crushing, bone-breaking bites. Tyrannosaurus enjoy basking in sunlight when not directly attempting to hunt. In the Dry Season, they can be observed resting in shallow banks of water in order to cool down, drawing their muzzle through the water. Tyrannosaurus are not adverse to swimming across rivers, lakes, or etc in order to find other hunting opportunities within their territory or carry on with their patrols. Tyrannosaurus are capable of moving almost silently, dropping to a crouched position and moving slowly and steadily so as to not be detected by prey.
