# Tyrannosaurus – Pairs

Tyrannosaurus are not social in the traditional sense but - once bonded with a mate - form strong, lifelong pairbonds with their partner. These pairs are monogamous and remain together year round, hunting cooperatively, patrolling territory together, and rearing offspring as a family unit. Within a bonded pair, interactions are subtle, but deeply communicative. Low, rumbling vibrations, coupled with soft calls to each other are common, with each pair developing their own method of communication over time. Pairs show deep affection towards one another. They enjoy resting beside one another closely, shaking their heads and bodies, and touching muzzles. When greeting each other, pairs frequently lift their heads in a synchronized fashion, flashing their throats and crooning gently. [:stretch_emote:] Trophies from kills are frequently gifted to each other, and always warmly received. Pairs sniff each other often, seeming to take comfort in each other's scent. A bond, once established, is near impossible to break, often only dissolving when one of the pairbond dies. Pairs are never composed of related or same-sex individuals, and siblings - even parents and offspring - treat each other aggressively if they encounter them again in adulthood.
