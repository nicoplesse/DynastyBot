# Lambeosaurus – Orphaned Behavior (Wild Spawn)

Fawns who wake up alone spend most of their early lives hidden, only feebly bleating if something suitable to be a mother approaches. This often takes the form of other Lambeosaurus, Iguanodon, Barsboldia, and Parasaurolophus; but may also be Camptosaurus and sometimes even Citipati. If any other species approach the fawn, it remains extremely still and silent, following natural instinct. A fawn only begins to show panic if aggression is shown to it, and bolts from all threats and danger when this happens. If a fawn is orphaned while a part of a herd, it quickly becomes adopted by any other interested individual in the herd. Fawns who grow up alone struggle with integrating socially into herds, often straying far behind or pushing far ahead, and showing less interest in courtship and nesting. These behaviors tend to settle after their first successful courtship and nest, and they quickly begin to move as one again with the rest of the herd. Lambeosaurus are quirky and simple, thriving in a group setting with other Lambeosaurus. With a very limited sense of personal space amongst themselves and other friendly hadrosaurid species, Lambeosaurus does are highly tolerant and social, staying very close to members of their herd. Lambeosaurus bucks are shyer than does and are more submissive. They tend to be more nervous, being the first to alert the herd of any perceived threats with sharp barks and stomps. Lambeosaurus herds move together as one unit - as when one Lambeosaurus begins to lead, the rest of the herd instinctively follow, keeping their bodies and young close together. Without the security of a herd, whether it be Lambeosaurus or another herbivorous species, Lambeosaurus begin to struggle, and may even become ill from stress. Lambeosaurus have an excellent sense of smell and hearing - they can detect the sounds of a predator's footsteps from a great distance. In many situations, this results in the entire herd silently bolting, rushing fawns to safety and not wishing to draw the predator’s attention. Lambeosaurus are comforted by the sight of other Lambeosaurus nearby, and seek eye contact often. This means that some of the most bonded pairs spend long periods of time silently gazing at one another, affectionately nuzzling and shaking themselves at the other on occasion. Lambeosaurus herds prefer to live and nest in dense forests along waterways. Migration routes typically follow rivers, allowing Lambeosaurus to flee into the water if being hunted or if a threat is seen. Lambeosaurus may be seen briefly rising onto their back legs to reach prime food sources. Lambeosaurus can be very picky about their food sources, taking extra effort to find the freshest shoots, leaves and berries - ignoring trampled areas. Lambeosaurus have scent glands beneath their eyes that they use to leave their scent on favored food sources, areas, family, and friends. Lambeosaurus prefer to avoid open areas, and will not spend much time out in open areas, if possible. [OPTIONAL]: In the Dry Season, some Lambeosaurus herds can be observed migrating to Red Island en masse, in order to find new individuals to court and nest with, as well as to take advantage of the resources there. Very few species are able to make the long swim. Once their young reach Sub-Adult, they are ferried back across the sea to return to Gondwa, and continue their regular migrations there.
