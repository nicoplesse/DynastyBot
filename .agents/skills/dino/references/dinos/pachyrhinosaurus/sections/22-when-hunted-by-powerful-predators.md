# Pachyrhinosaurus – When hunted by powerful predators

Pachyrhinosaurus, while poor in eyesight, have long-lasting memories. Previous encounters with powerful predators (apexs, multiple larges, semi-aquatics, etc..) are not forgotten easily. Instead of forming a defensive formation, individuals might opt to flee, putting as much distance between themselves and the perceived threat in a frantic stampede. It's during these fearful moments that sometimes Psittacosaurus gets caught under thundering feet, falling prey to the confusion. If the predator begins an engagement while the migration attempts to flee, Pachyrhinosaurus might halt and form their signature defensible circle or wall, or opt to abandon bitten individuals. Males and bonded females are known to turn and defend their bitten ally, but once the target has taken significant injury (half health) and is already being defended (3 are already defending it), they are forced to retreat. They anxiously watch their fate from some distance away (10 body lengths).
