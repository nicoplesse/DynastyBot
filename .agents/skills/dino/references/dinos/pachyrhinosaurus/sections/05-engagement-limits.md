# Pachyrhinosaurus – Engagement Limits

Predator Reaction: Defensive Unlimited Pachyrhinosurus form a defensive formation when a predator the herd deems as dangerous approaches within 10 body lengths of themselves. Individuals may step forward a total of one body length to land a hit on the predator, but then must step back into the formation. Up to 4 adults can break the formation to charge the predator. Mixed species protection When migrating: Unlimited Pachyrhinosaurus, Styracosaurus and Albertaceratops form a defensive formation when a predator the herd deems as dangerous is within 10 body lengths. Up to 4 adult individuals (of any species) are permitted to step outside the formation to attempt to chase away the predator. Once the predator is chased 10 body lengths from the herd, the defenders must retreat to the herd. Psittacosaurus may use the herd as a meat shield to hide from threats. When in nesting grounds: Pachyrhinosaurus that have claimed a bush do not permit other species into their small territory. Other species they herd with are shoved outside of hitting distance, and are not protected. Territorial Defence When challenged in their nesting grounds, Pachyrhinosurus harems and courting males form separate formations around their resources/nests. Females actively nesting with these males can choose to retreat into his formation, or join the main herd. If a harem or courting male(s) is attacked, the rest of the herd may leave the nesting grounds and begin their migration earlier. If non-nesting individuals or the matriarch is attacked, they form a defensive formation in much the same way as their predator reaction. However, those uninvolved (males and harems in a territory) do not flee and remain in their territories during the engagement. In all engagements; hatchlings, juveniles and adolescents do not count towards the engagement limit, and their biological or adoptive mother becomes the 4th engaged. Preferred Diet Flowers, nuts, roots, fruit (forager)
