# Pachyrhinosaurus – Courtship

Migrating cows court around the same time. Led by the matriarch, the migration changes their path towards a well liked nesting ground when the eldest female feels ready to lay her eggs. When they arrive at her chosen nesting grounds, cows take to relaxing in the warm sun, cooing to each other or following loud bulls with interest. Males claim small territories to impress females: Bursting with confidence, bulls become competitive over food resources nearby, and shields sniff out the best bush to claim as their own. A shield (up to 3 related males) will work together to claim a small territory around a bush. Males not in a shield work alone to claim a bush territory. Territorial when nesting, shields forcefully challenge rival shields from their chosen nesting site. The size of these territories vary based on the amount of females in the migration - with smaller harems splitting into tinier spaces, and larger harems claiming more space. Once bulls have established their territories, they bellow loudly, pacing the length of their chosen spot. Rival shields, seeing the quantity of resources claimed, will approach the shield and paw the ground, scraping dirt with their beaks and sizing their competition up. If the challenge is accepted, a whole shield of up to 3 bulls will work together to defend their territory from rivals. Charging forward, they each target a male in the rival shield - or, if the rival is solo - the whole shield targets the challenger. These bullfights are loud and often end sooner when crippling blows land on exposed hides. The fight consists of charging towards their rival and landing heavy blows, then retreating backwards for a distance to repeat the forward charge. These bullfights are always fought with the challengers head facing their rival. Looking away or turning tail signals defeat, and the loser is forced to walk away, seeking a secondary territory to claim. During these challenges, other bulls may watch, assessing the skill of these shields. A territory will only ever be fought over once per nesting season, as males do not wish to wound their collective herd defenders, but rather stand firm on their space when nesting. If a territory has already been challenged, shields find alternative spots, preferring not to upset the fragile group by overstepping their bounds. During bullfights between shields, cows wander between the territories, assessing each shield's chosen spot. Typically, it's the resources that win them over - but a cow always prefers more heavily scarred males. These males are seen as stronger and more likely to defend the territory while her young are weak and fragile in their hatchling phase. Cows may choose any male within a shield to nest with. More often than not it is the most active male; favoring his scarred hide - an indication of age and wisdom.
