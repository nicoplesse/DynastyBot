# Metriacanthosaurus – Parenthood

Metriacanthosaurus parents take part in rearing their young equally and immediately search for mushrooms their hatchlings can consume, beginning to condition the scales to the poisonous coat they will have later in life. Beyond the initial offering of mushrooms, Metriacanthosaurus are typically uninvolved parents throughout the rest of raising their offspring - only acting if necessary. Parents expect polliwogs to follow them almost immediately, and rarely look back to ensure their offspring are close by. Those that linger are at risk of being eaten by their larger siblings. Parents who witness this behavior will intervene. However, offspring that are successful in killing a sibling without them witnessing it are not scolded, and parents do not seem to mourn the fallen polliwog. While bullying is common between siblings, this is rarely stopped - and sometimes even encouraged. Parents will only intervene if their young is nearing deathscars. Throughout their lives, parents seek out mushrooms to eat by habit, a behavior that their offspring quickly pick up on and mimic. Whilst this is not traditional teaching, it serves a valuable learning opportunity for their young. Metriacanthosaurus parents do not show affection to their offspring, and do not allow them to eat from their kills while they are eating. However, Metriacanthosaurus may still defend any offspring they have that are Adolescent and below from threats. The older their offspring is, the less likely some parents are to defend these offspring. Only once parents have eaten do they allow polliwogs below Adolescent to feed from their meals. Offspring of Adolescent and above are aggressed and nipped off the meal if they attempt to eat.
