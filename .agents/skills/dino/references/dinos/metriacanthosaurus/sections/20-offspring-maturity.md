# Metriacanthosaurus – Offspring Maturity

Once Adolescent, Metriacanthosaurus young begin to distance themselves from their parents as they are pushed off meals and mostly ignored. Some Metriacanthosaurus will completely disperse from their parents at this age and leave the vicinity their parents reside in. This does not seem to affect their parents, who by this stage are indifferent - or perhaps even irritated - by their offspring's presence. By Sub Adult, offspring must disperse quietly in an attempt to disappear. Any form of overstaying results in their own parents challenging them viciously from the territory. Metricanthosaurus hunt, court, and nest, all the way through their elder years. If an individual does not have a consistent mate, they may stop courting entirely, and prefer to live the end of their lives solitary. As Metricanthosaurus age, the toxin in their flesh begins to degrade. This often results in a much lower quality of life for a Metricanthosaurus, as well as its intensely bright colors beginning to fade. Metricanthosaurus struggling with their toxins fading may dehydrate faster than usual, and may easily bake in an intense Dry Season afternoon, if not properly sheltered. When a Metricanthosaurus female chooses to have her final clutch - with a much lower toxin level unable to provide the sufficient level of toxin for her offspring - she may attempt to cannibalize her mate. Many males, especially those that have been paired with the female for most of their lives, willingly accept this, and do not fight back. Males who do not simply flee from the POI entirely. In the case she is unable to consume her former mate, Metricanthosaurus females will only lay one egg in her final clutch. When the offspring is born, she will raise them alone.
