# Metriacanthosaurus – Orphaned Behavior (Wild Spawn)

Orphaned polliwogs take a moment to orient themselves, listening for the telling footsteps of their parents. Hearing none, these hatchlings follow their noses in hopes of the scent of a fresh kill. If lucky, the hatchling will attempt to scavenge from a body unnoticed. Without the security of parents, polliwogs prioritize concealing themselves in shrubs or tall grass, though a bold polliwog may trail other creatures from a distance out of curiosity. By Juvenile, orphaned polliwogs are openly curious about their environment. Unusual objects are inspected thoroughly, and other creatures are trailed for short periods of time - often until they are spotted and aggressed away. At Adolescent, polliwogs begin to take interest in faster moving creatures, attempting to catch anything of Small tier and below for prey. Once they reach Sub-Adult, orphaned Metriacanthosaurus may display lesser skills with hunting, depending on the success rates they made whilst growing. Parented Metriacanthosaurus have the benefit of observing successful hunts from adults - something that helps hone their own hunting skills. Metriacanthosaurus are usually solitary by nature, and comfortable on their own. While Metriacanthosaurus do not rely on other individuals, they may tolerate one other to travel with them for the purpose of convenience or courting. Any additional Metriacanthosaurus are viewed as potential meals. Metriacanthosaurus has an extremely poisonous hide covering its body which serves both as a deterrent and a defence mechanism. While immune to their own species' poison, most species find them too toxic to consume and suffer side-effects after eating from a Metriacanthosaurus corpse. Only Compsognathus, Ceratosaurus, Maip, Allosaurus, Deinosuchus, Quetzalcoatlus, Megalania, Rhamphorhynchus and other Metricanthosaurus may successfully feed from a Metricanthosaurus body. Without maintenance of these toxins, Metricanthosaurus begin to fade in color. These toxins are primarily replenished by consuming a large amount of mushrooms, though sometimes can be substituted by feeding from a Metricanthosaurus corpse. Metriacanthosaurus’ most identifiable feature is their bright scales, which serves as a warning for predators. The brighter an individual, the more poisonous they appear to be. These warning colors are a concept known as aposematism. Due to Metriacanthosaurus diet, their mouths quickly become toxic with the amount of mushrooms, noxious meat, and carrion they consume. Depending on the diet of a Metriacanthosaurus, their bites may inflict others with a toxin which can build quickly. Metriacanthosaurus is tolerant and extremely observant, unnervingly staring down other creatures. They remain still, moving their heads only if necessary. The only time they do not is if they come across an Apex carnivore. Carnivore groups are of interest to Metriacanthosaurus, who will mimic their  movements at a distance while the group hunts. Metriacanthosaurus take specific interest in feathered creatures. Should one pass their field of view, their attention is grabbed immediately, crouching quickly as their head swivels in the creature's direction. Feathered species attract such great attention in Metriacanthosaurus that some may even forget their surroundings, fixated only on its potential prey. Metriacanthosaurus particularly enjoy eating the young of these feathered species, though know their limitations as well. Should Metriacanthosaurus find attended Therizinosaurus young, Metriacanthosaurus may fixate on this in hopes that the offspring wanders off - though will flee if aggressed by an adult. A visible Metriacanthosaurus typically poses no threat. When hunting, Metriacanthosaurus attempt to conceal themselves and bide their time stalking their prey as silent, patient hunters. Metriacanthosaurus favor coastal islands, swamps and caves - and are most comfortable in areas with dense coverage. Metriacanthosaurus only hunt at night, keeping to dark and shadowed areas where their bright coloring is less evident and they are able to creep up on their prey. During the dry season, Metriacanthosaurus prefer to remain in caves throughout the day, as their scales are sensitive to the dry heat.
