# Kelenken – General Behavior

Kelenken enjoy a variety of areas to live in - from vast open plains, to more rocky biomes, to thick forests, Kelenken readily adapt to their environment and typically prefer to stay in a POI that resembles that of their childhood. With their sharp eyesight, Kelenken are instantly attracted to anything that moves. A fast, small dinosaur is a prime target and can prompt a Kelenken into a sudden chase, even if it ends up unsuccessful. Taking dust baths is a common activity for Kelenken. They shake their feathers into dry, loose dirt and patches of sand helps to dislodge unwanted critters from their skin, and smooth out their feathers, which keeps them well groomed. Kelenken spend nearly their entire lives on their feet, from the moment they can stand. They are rarely seen at rest, and during hunts they pursue their prey effortlessly - taking advantage of their excellent stamina while pushing their prey to their limits. When resting, Kelenken retreat to areas of dense vegetation for protection. If a Kelenken is in a pair, typically one member of the pair will stay awake, keeping a protective eye over their sleeping companion and swapping through the night to ensure they are both well rested. Both males and females perform this behavior for one another. Within the POI they currently occupy, Kelenken patrol with a keen eye, scoping for danger or potential prey. When in a full cast, the patrolling Kelenkens keep a firm look-out for other Kelenkens, and drive them out immediately. Solitary Kelenken: Solitary Kelenken are nomadic, which means they do not stay in a particular POI for a long. They spend the majority of their time migrating between different areas in search of other Kelenkens to form a pair or cast with - or simply to seek out better hunting opportunities. Some Kelenken prefer to remain solitary throughout their lives; lacking any natural drive to form pairs or even join a cast. These independent individuals are well-adapted to thrive on their own - capable of self-sufficiency and survival without social bonds. However, this behavior is rare, and largely depends on individual personalities as most Kelenken prefer the companionship of a mate or group. Albino and Melanistic Kelenken: Male Kelenken that exhibit albinism or melanism are often mistaken as female due to their coloration. They can experience difficulties in attracting a female mate, in comparison to males that display the typical dimorphism with bright and contrasting colors. Females with these mutations are tolerated by their peers and face no difficulties during courtship.
