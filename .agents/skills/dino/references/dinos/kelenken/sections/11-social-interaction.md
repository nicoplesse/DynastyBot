# Kelenken – Social Interaction

Kelenken form deep, affectionate bonds that can occur in both opposite-sex and same-sex pairs. These relationships often begin as temporary arrangements for companionship or child-rearing but frequently evolve into lifelong pairbonds. Once a lifelong pairbond is formed - either by mutual decision, or raising offspring together successfully 5 times or more - they mate for life, only taking another if their original mate dies - but only after a grieving period is observed. Up to 4 pairs, or 8 individuals of any relation make up a co-ordinated group known as a cast. A cast claims a large territory and work together to keep any other Kelenken out of their territory. Within their chosen POI, pairs will take turns patrolling and monitoring the territory. Pairs within a cast stick to each other like glue - even when the relationship is young and still considered a temporary pairbond. They are rarely seen far apart from one another, and often mirror each other's behavior. This synchronicity helps to strengthen their bond and maintain cohesion within the cast. Kelenken will only groom their mate, and will not tolerate others grooming their mate, or their mate attempting to groom others. With extreme jealousy, they will hiss and posture angrily, and aim to chase the other Kelenken away from their mate, even if the offender is part of the cast. Despite the process being lengthy, bonding with another Kelenken to form a pair is a core aspect of a Kelenken’s life. These pairs, once closely bonded, will fight to the death for one another, even going as far to briefly defend their body if their mate dies. The corpse of a dead mate may be defended for up to 1 minute after they have died, before relinquishing it to the hunter that has killed it. While the core of a Kelenken’s social structure relates to their relationship with their chosen mate, they also enjoy interacting and socializing with other pairs within the cast. Casts typically consist of up to 4 adult pairs - fostering a small, close-knit community, but may also include individuals that have not yet formed a pairbond. Pairs form friendships with other pairs within the cast, often sharing responsibilities such as guarding hatchlings, scouting for prey, or providing comfort during stressful times. These friendships are vital to the overall harmony of the cast, as they create a sense of collective responsibility and mutual trust. Social gatherings are a key part of Kelenken life. These include ritualistic group displays such as synchronized vocalizations, elaborate preening sessions, and communal dances that reinforce the cast's unity and hierarchy. These displays also serve as an opportunity for juvenile Kelenken to observe and learn social cues from the adults.
