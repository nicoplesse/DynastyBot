# Allosaurus – Courtship

Though criteria differs between individuals, Allosaurus are most keen on individuals who showcase independence and competency when seeking out a mate. Those capable of providing for themselves without issue translates to a mate that is able to provide for a family with more ease than others. As such, solitary individuals often receive a surprisingly high amount of attention from others. Allosaurus are commonly known to be fickle when it comes to choosing a mate, but extremely devoted once that choice has been made. Once a female and a male has entered the courting stage, they may group up in-game as a pair. Females become increasingly persistent once they are interested in nesting. They walk side by side, in front of, and trail behind a chosen male, doing all they can to remain in his personal space. At moments of pause, females continue their pestering behavior by rubbing their face against the male's side and neck, purring frequently. This behavior persists for several hours, often acting as a major distraction to the male. Some females end up completely ignoring hunting opportunities or threats in favour of continuing to passively court, much to the frustration of the male. Males that start to lose interest begin to politely reject this persistence, continuing to avoid and swerve the female's attempts, and creating distance where possible - sometimes even using trees and rocks as a barrier. Though disheartening, this behavior is not a rejection, and a female may still attempt to win the male over through continuous efforts. If a male grows annoyed with a female's attempts, and loses all interest in courtship with her, he will opt to defend his personal space once more, calling aggressively to ward her away. This is a clear sign of rejection, and females - though disappointed - always respect this. After this stage, the pair will split off from one another, seeking their previous groups or returning to a solitary lifestyle. Otherwise, males charmed by a female’s behavior slowly begin to reciprocate her actions, pacing himself to walk side by side with her, and returning her purring calls. Males who continue to maintain an interest in courtship finalize their choice by initiating a game of chase. With a light nip to the female’s tail, males flee in the opposite direction, calling affectionately to encourage her into chasing him. The game continues for a long while, with both individuals taking turns to chase after, and flee from the other. At last, when play is considered complete, individuals come face to face, leaning forward to rub alongside each other's face and neck, symbolizing complete acceptance of the other. After this point, the pair are inseparable, walking side by side at all times, even if it means squeezing past tight spaces or stumbling over each other's feet. The female typically leads during this period, seeking out a suitable den site to raise offspring within.
