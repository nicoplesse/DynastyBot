# Allosaurus – Adoption

Allosaurus females may adopt, but must be quick in their decision, especially if grouped with or close to males. Males do not adopt, and may cannibalise unrelated Allosaurus of adolescence and below, even with a female nearby. If the female defends the cub from him, he will begrudgingly back down. Female Allosaurus can remain defensive of the adoptee towards males in their group if she deems him a risk, eyeing the male and placing her body between him and the offspring. Aging Allosaurus primarily experience issues navigating complex terrain, and begin to lose their hearing. Elderly Allosaurus are much quicker to concede in challenges for territory, exhaustion wearing them down quicker than any rival could. These individuals prefer to remain within a pair or coalition, finding the companionship more beneficial to securing meals, and receiving protection. The pursuit of adult sauropods is daunting to these aging and scared individuals, no longer as agile as they once were. Other grouped members often adapt to this change in energy by slowing down or resting more, but otherwise do not treat the aged individual any differently.
