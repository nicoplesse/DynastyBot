# Deinonychus – Death of the Mother

The death of the mother is the most destabilizing event a dynasty can experience. As the core of its structure, her absence throws the entire group into uncertainty and disorder. Without her presence, the dynasty loses its center. The feeding order collapses, sleeping arrangements are disrupted, and formerly submissive females begin testing boundaries. Vocalizations increase sharply as females posture and aggressive call at one another, with pacing and challenging increasing. Adult daughters, sisters and other close female relatives begin vying for control. These power struggles are not fatal - females fight until one is reduced to deathscars, forcing the other to submit. However, these conflicts are numerous, often with one single female fighting the entirety of the rest of the group to grapple for ultimate control. If no clear successor emerges, or the infighting becomes too intense, the dynasty may split entirely. Small factions form around strong female personalities, with daughters and sisters to each other grouping to form several smaller dynasties. Once a new successor emerges, the new mother must exile more distant relatives. Only the new mother's sisters and offspring are permitted to remain. Nieces, nephews, brothers and any other relations must be exiled from the dynasty. In rare cases, a new mother may allow her father back into the dynasty if she has bonded with him well since youth. He receives no special role and can be pushed out like other related males if the mother deems the group full, but may return at a later time. The father to the mother is not permitted to court any females, as he is related to all of them. Males prefer to leave quietly during the turmoil of a new dynasty formation. They band together in a protective group, waiting on the outskirts so as not to get involved in any disputes. If the Mother has offspring under adolescent These orphaned young experience extreme neglect, and are either killed or exiled by their adult sisters and female relatives. The surviving orphans have to become as independent as possible, and learn scavenging and hunting in order to stay fed but may not remain in the dynasty if below adolescent. Deinonychus are extremely noisy and rude neighbours to almost all other dinosaurs in their vicinity. When groups come across another dinosaur in their travels, they launch into a loud and aggressive display while keeping a safe distance. This involves posturing, loud calls, and mock-attacks into the air. Deinonychus are all bark and no bite in these situations - unless planning to hunt the other species. As soon as the other dinosaur starts to approach, they flee the scene and retreat to the safety of a cave or cliff edge if possible. Caves and cliff edges are temporarily claimed by Deinonychus and any dinosaur challenging their resting spot or attempting to enter will be met with deadly force of the entire group. These resting locations are protected defensively while in use, and Deinonychus will not chase dinosaurs further than LOS of the area; retreating back if the dinosaur leaves. If a spot they have chosen already has an occupant, they will attempt to root out the dinosaur so that they may use it for resting at night, or nesting. Deinonychus will attempt to follow Tyrannosaurus and Giganotosaurus where possible in the hopes of being able to scavenge from their kills. The larger the dynasty, the less likely they are to be tolerated - though this doesn't stop them from trying! They will heed warnings when given by these Apex carnivores, and keep their distance if threatened. Deinonychus scatter and flee at the sight of Hatzegopteryx or Quetzalcoatlus. They almost never engage with either of these unless cornered, with a body or at a claimed nesting site. Deinonychus do not tolerate Compsognathus, Rhamphorhynchus or any scavengers, and aggressively chase them away. If lucky enough to discover an unattended nest of Kelenken chicks under adolescent, Deinonychus can slaughter the entire brood without incurring species timers. However, if an adult comes to defend them, Deinonychus gives up this feat and flees. Deinonychus are especially adept hunters of Tenontosaurus, and always target this prey species first if multiple prey options are in the POI. Deinonychus may attempt to provide grooming opportunities to Anodontosaurus and sauropods, if tolerated. Latenivenatrix are not tolerated in the same POI. If a Deinonychus group is resting in a POI, they will try to chase out any Latenivenatrix. Solitary Deinonychus avoid conflict with Latenivenatrix.
