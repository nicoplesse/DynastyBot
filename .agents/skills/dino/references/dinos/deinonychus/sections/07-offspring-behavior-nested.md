# Deinonychus – Offspring Behavior (Nested)

Baby Deinonychus, known as fledglings, hatch out of their eggs either deep inside protected caves or up high on cliff edges. Fledglings require grooming shortly after hatching by an adult to help clean their feathers. Hatchling fledglings are quite noisy to ensure they are attended to, but settle down quickly after being cared for. They remain in the nest after this, recovering from the ordeal of hatching. By mid hatchling stage, fledglings begin to find their feet and start investigating other fledglings within the nesting territory. They nip at each other (and adults), play fight, and pick up whatever objects they can find. Fledglings do not leave the cave or away from the cliffside unless accompanied by an adult, and have food brought to them. Thus, when adults return to them, all fledglings mob them, squawking and begging for food. However, some persistent fledglings can be quite mischievous, and still attempt to leave the safety of the nesting site when they can sneak past. They are swiftly corrected and reprimanded by adults if this is spotted. The safest time of a Deinonychus' life is when they are a fledgling and born to the mother. These fledglings do not have to wait for the feeding hierarchy, and are permitted to eat as soon as the mother has taken her fill. The rest of the dynasty continuously bring them toys to keep them entertained. Fledglings run around with these objects, biting and throwing them around, and fighting to take them off other fledglings. Fledglings learn through mimicry. They watch adult females posture, growl and assert their dominance. Both female and male fledglings imitate these displays on siblings and objects they discover. Fledglings quickly learn what behaviors result in the best rewards for them. By adolescence, females fully understand that dominance is highly prized by the mother, and males become driven to show their submission, crouching and offering gifts to curry favor with the rest of the group. Male fledglings get lavished with affection from the adult males in the group. This attention and grooming helps prepare them for a more gentle life within the dynasty. Females, meanwhile, are doted on by their adult female relatives, particularly when they correctly show dominant behavior. Fledglings born to the mother have the highest rank out of all offspring. They are permitted to bully and harass other fledglings, which many of them take great delight in doing. Fledglings that attempt to bully and harass fledglings of the mother are always swiftly corrected by any adult that witnesses it. Fledglings who grow up without much adult care - such as those born to females that are not the mother, or orphaned at a young age - tend to be more skittish, opportunistic, and self-serving in adulthood. Forced to be more independent, they are more likely to develop rogue behaviors like hoarding food, and avoiding direct confrontation. Fledglings are permitted to leave the nesting site once they hit Adolescent stage, and are expected to fall in line with the rest of the dynasty to pull their weight. This includes participating in hunts, and following the strict feeding hierarchy after a kill. Any fledglings born to the mother's daughters or sisters must be introduced to her once they reach adolescence. Their mother must bring them forward, allowing the mother to inspect them - often with visible scrutiny - before begrudgingly accepting them into the dynasty. If fledglings are not properly introduced, they risk immediate exile or even death upon discovery. Any fledgling may be rejected by the mother - such as if she feels spited by said female introducing their offspring to her, or if the mother simply does not approve of the female's offspring. If these fledglings are born outside the dynasty, they are never accepted back into the original dynasty. Once fledglings reach Sub-Adult, they remain in the dynasty if the mother decides there is enough space. However, if the group reaches the mother's decided limit, or if the individual prefers to leave, the mother will drive them out, starting with: any related males, granddaughters, and nieces, in order to prioritize her daughters and potential breeding males. Sons of the mother may sometimes return to the dynasty if a space opens up, but they are always the first to be exiled again when room is needed. Grandsons and nephews, once exiled or dispersed, are not permitted to rejoin a dynasty. They may still share brief affectionate moments with their own mothers if encountered during migrations. Many related young males form troops, and travel together in search of new dynasties. Females strongly prefer to stay within the dynasty. The mother gives her daughters the highest priority to remain when she feels space is limited. If a daughter or sister leaves, she may return later if there is space, but must prove herself to regain the mother's favor. However, any daughters or sisters who have offspring outside the dynasty will find their young permanently rejected by the mother. Their children will not be allowed to join the dynasty under any circumstance and might get killed if introduced when they are still under adolescent. Granddaughters and nieces are not permitted to rejoin the dynasty that they dispersed from or were exiled out of. However, if their own mother (or sister) forms a dynasty, they may be permitted to join that dynasty.
