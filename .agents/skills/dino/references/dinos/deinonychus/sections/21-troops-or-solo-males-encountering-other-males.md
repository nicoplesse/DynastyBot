# Deinonychus – Troops or solo males encountering other males

Male encounters are cautious, but much less aggressive than introductions involving a female. Full troops of 10 members do not engage, preferring to observe briefly before moving on. If open to newcomers, the oldest or most confident male approaches first, chirping and shaking to gently inspect him. The newcomer does not crouch as he would for females, but offers friendly headshakes and soft calls in return. The rest of the troop then approach, and if accepted by all parties, the troop include the new male in their group. They settle somewhere nearby and begin a ritual of deep, group-lead grooming, with individuals focusing on the newcomer. If a male with head feathers attempts to join a troop, the troop reacts with confusion and will immediately flee, unwilling to risk being misidentified as a dynasty or drawing aggression from females during a possible encounter. Mothers lead their dynasty with an iron fist and expect submission at all times. Individuals that show disobedience or lack of respect towards the mother are sharply scolded, and repeat offences may cause them to be kicked out of the dynasty. If the offending female has fledglings, the mother may choose to slaughter them if she feels she has been slighted too many times. The mother chooses favorite daughters, and will prioritise them when sharing food. The decision for how a mother chooses a favorite daughter is down to the individual - but usually bullying and beating the other females in the group and proving themselves as adept hunters is the easiest way to secure the position. Favored daughters often develop preferences among their own offspring, investing more time and resources into certain daughters who they believe have the best potential. These young daughters are frequently groomed, defended, and encouraged to rise through the dynasty's hierarchy. However, only the Mother decides which granddaughters are granted the status of favored granddaughter. Favored granddaughters can only come from the offspring of favored daughters, and enjoy increased protection, access to food and higher influence within the group. This can sometimes create tension between the Mother and her favored daughters. A favored daughter may invest heavily in one offspring, only for the Mother to show preference towards a different granddaughter. In these situations, the daughter is forced to support the Mother's chosen favorite regardless, as opposing the decision risks damaging her own standing. A favored granddaughter's position - like a favored daughter's position - is never guaranteed. Should she repeatedly disappoint the Mother, her favored status may be revoked. In extreme cases, the Mother may also question the judgement of the favored daughter who produced and supported her, potentially resulting in both granddaughter and daughter losing their favored status within the dynasty. Because favored granddaughters gain their status entirely through their mother's position, they immediately lose their standing if their mother loses her place as a favored daughter. Deinonychus females, as a result, display bullying behavior to one another, vying for a position as a favorite female under the mother's eye, as well as over access to resources and particular males. Bullying involves shoving, getting in their personal space, air snapping, stealing meat, blocking their path as they move, rushing to take a preferred sleeping spot, flirting with their courting partner, and generally being annoying and disruptive. Some females may even go so far as to nip potential rivals or males that have provoked their irritation. This is a carefully measured action, as others that witness this may step in to defend the individual - or prompt the individual into challenging back. Bullying is always met with either submission or bullying back, but if one individual has had enough of repeated instances, it escalates to a quick fight known as a spar, detailed below. In extreme cases the bullying may escalate to the deliberate plucking of head feathers. This is considered severe social punishment and can leave a female partially or entirely bald, increasing the risk of her being mistaken for a male. While head feathers may eventually regrow, the process takes time - at least one full in game day cycle. Females lacking head feathers for extended periods are heavily bullied and will be deranked to as low as being treated similarly to albino individuals. During this time the female switches to the Antirrhopus sub-species to showcase their lack of head feathers. Some females may attempt to bully or harass the mother’s preferred male when the mother is not present, testing his position or venting jealousy. If the mother discovers this behavior, she responds with severe aggression, often resulting in serious injury to the offender. Repeated or bold offenses can result in immediate exile from the dynasty, but after a while this female may be allowed to return.
