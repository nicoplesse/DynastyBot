# Deinonychus – Nesting & Parenthood

Deinonychus always nest inside a cave or the edge of a cliff side. These locations are fiercely protected by all members of the dynasty from any unwelcome intrusion. Deinonychus surround their nests with thick ferns. The mother gets the most desirable nesting site within the cave, and will never share a nesting spot close to other females. If an area only has one spot, the other females must wait until she has finished using it, or find a new location nearby in order to nest in their offspring. Other females never challenge the mother for an area to place their nest. However, they may challenge one another in the form of a sparring match. Once the mother's fledglings are born, all members of the Deinonychus dynasty help out with rearing them. They bring them food and gifts, accompany them to water, and defend them from any threats. Mother fledglings essentially live a very comfortable and protected life. Other females that are nesting are not permitted to mix their fledglings with the mother's until they are at least juvenile. They receive no help from other dynasty members in rearing their babies, and are expected to pitch in with the mother's fledglings as well as their own. Any female seen prioritising her own brood rather than the Mother's will receive harsh punishment from the rest of the dynasty - such as shunning behavior or even aggression from the mother herself. Due to having to provide for both the mother's fledglings and her own, a female that nests at the same time as the mother may only be able to raise one or two fledglings as the others may starve. In extreme circumstances, a female may opt to reduce her brood through culling to ease the pressure. Males - especially males that have courted and nested in offspring this season - assist by bringing prey and scraps for fledglings, collecting small objects as makeshift "toys", and gently grooming and playing with young to build early trust. Males lavish affection on male offspring especially, helping to guide them with submissive behavior in order to win over the approval of females. Fledglings that tumble out of the nest may be gathered up by males, but sometimes get rejected by the mother. This rejection is not a death sentence, but forces fledglings to work harder to gain her favor. Once fledglings reach Adolescent, they are treated as adult members of the dynasty. They are expected to help raise other fledglings, hunt, and socialise with the other adults.
