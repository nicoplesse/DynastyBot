# Deinonychus – Submission & Gift Giving

Within a dynasty, males earn favor from females through visible acts of submission, and presenting gifts. Not all males participate in gift giving, but those that do more quickly earn favor. Submission is displayed by crouching when approached by a female. Females frequently test this behavior by standing over a male, waiting silently. If the male crouches promptly, she rewards him with an affectionate headshake. If he hesitates - or worse, fails to crouch at all - she will hiss at him, fanning her tail in irritation. Males only show this behavior towards females. Males do not perform submissive acts to other males, but may still gift other males as an act of affection. Males are active gift-givers, and bring interesting items they find to females, offspring, and even other males, as a form of social bonding. These gifts can include shiny stones, colorful flowers, nesting material or scraps of food - each chosen for its visual appeal or scent. Preferences vary widely between individuals, pushing males to be observant and creative with gifts they choose. When given a gift, males respond with warmth, offering grooming and gentle chirps of appreciation. Females are more selective. If pleased, a female takes the item with her and lavishes the male with affection in return. But if the gift disappoints her, she will rudely discard it, often by flinging it aside, and express her annoyance by flaring out her feathers, and tail-fanning in the male's direction. For males, successful submission and gift giving are often the key to acceptance, and winning favor with females.
