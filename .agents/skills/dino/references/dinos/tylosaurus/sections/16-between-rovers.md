# Tylosaurus – Between Rovers

Rovers are extremely aggressive towards solitary bulls and other rovers. Rover pairs often “claim” pods or matrilines in attempt to court them and do not tolerate other rovers - or solitary males - within the POI. Rovers are only aggressive towards solitary bulls when they have no room for other bulls in their group. However, rovers do not view males within matrilines as threats, as males within their mother’s matrilines do not court the females in that group. Rover groupings challenge each other with loud and aggressive calls. They swim back and forth, viciously lashing their tails, and displaying their shape, colors, and scars in a threatening manner. This posturing continues for at least 10 seconds before a challenge begins, unless one side forfeits with a fear call and flees. Rovers do not chase other rovers after a forfeit, but instead swim back and forth in front of the matriline or pod that they have won, eager to ensure no other rovers are nearby. Winning a contest in this way is often satisfactory to cows for courtship, and - if the females are interested - may result in them initiating courtship.
