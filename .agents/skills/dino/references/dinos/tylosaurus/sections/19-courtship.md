# Tylosaurus – Courtship

Courtship begins with Tylosaurus cows surfacing and broadcasting to attract any unrelated bulls to the area. Bulls, in response - if ready to court - breach the surface and return their calls eagerly. This is particularly easy to initiate when a pod has two unrelated matrilines traveling together, or matrilines with accompanying rovers. Receptive cows lead bulls underwater, and spin to indicate their continued interest. Interested bulls follow eagerly, checking to make sure no other bulls have followed. Should more than one bull follow the cow below, bulls initiate a challenging display to win the right to court the cow first. The challenge consists of bulls performing fierce headbutts against nearby rocks or cliffs, with the winner being the bull that creates the most impactful force. The winning bull earns the right to perform his display first - and does so by circling, creating an intricate dance that highlights his strength and fitness. During the dance, bulls offer friendly calls towards the observing cows, demonstrating good temperament. Bulls swim in a circle and spin for a minimum of at least three times, before sinking slowly to the bottom, awaiting for the decision from the cows. If a cow chooses a bull, she will swim to the bottom in front of her chosen bull and begin to reciprocate his dance, swimming in circles and spinning to showcase her own vitality. Her dance imitates his own, utilising similar movements and patterns. Cows may reject a bull even if he wins a display if he fails to impress them with his dance. Loser bulls are allowed to perform their dances only after the winning bull has courted successfully - or been rejected by the remaining cows. Bulls are able to court up to 3 cows at a time.
