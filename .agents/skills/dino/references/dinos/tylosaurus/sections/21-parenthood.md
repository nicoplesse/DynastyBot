# Tylosaurus – Parenthood

From the moment that offspring hatch onto the kelp beds, mothers feel incredible attachment to their offspring, and quickly encourage them to climb off of the kelp beds and into the warm, safe water. Mothers clean their young first by flicking her tongue over them repeatedly, before tucking them against her sides or beneath her belly to ensure they are covered from aerial predators. The entire matriline works together to shelter offspring and to ensure they are fed - with siblings, aunts and uncles swimming out to the deeper seas to find prey to return to young offspring, before mothers are comfortable bringing them into the deeper parts of the sea. Offspring are very fragile, so all members of the matriline move slowly within their nesting areas to ensure that offspring are not accidentally damaged with the movement of adult bodies. Once offspring are juvenile, they are brought out of the shallows and into deeper water, where mothers initiate play by spinning and swimming around them, rubbing up against them, and nudging them up towards the surface of the water. Mothers play hunting and stalking games with offspring in kelp forests and tunnels, ensuring that offspring develop the skills they will need later in life. Mothers remain very close to their offspring throughout their early lives, and through this foster extremely strong bonds with them. If offspring accidentally beach themselves, it is the responsibility of the mother or one of the nearby adults in the matriline to rescue the offspring. This poses high risks on the mother, who may be hunted in her time out of the water. Despite this, many mothers still rush to the rescue of her calves, using her body to push them back into the safety of the water. At adolescent, mothers begin to allow calves to travel further away from them, but still keep a close eye on them. Mothers teach their offspring the hunting strategies and the unique vocal calls that they will use throughout their entire lives, encouraging them to hone their skills via hunting critters and fish. All cows within a matriline express interest in each other’s calves, and help ensure all calves are fed and safe. Bulls show less interest in the calves, but still initiate play and take calves on small hunting trips to prey on fish. Up to 1 adolescent may assist with hunts, but will flee from the engagement if brought below half health. This may also result in the rest of the hunting party forfeiting as well, in an attempt to defend the calf.
