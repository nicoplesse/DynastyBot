# Tylosaurus – Rovers

Rovers consist of either related or unrelated males. These can be bulls without a living mother, bulls that have been shunned from their matriline, or bulls temporarily breaking away from their matriline in order to court unrelated cows. Being removed from their matriline often makes a bull unwilling to socialize with others, and go through a very strong grieving period. These bulls rely on the bonds they have with a brother in order to prevent themselves ending up entirely solitary. Bulls in rovers are aggressive towards bulls in rival rovers, and do not tolerate them in their POIs. These bulls pose as competition for cows the groups encounter. Bulls in rovers may court cows within matrilines, as long as they are not related to any cows in the matriline. Rovers compete with other rovers for the right to follow a matriline or a pod, and do not tolerate other rovers within a POI. Time spent together helps forge bonds between bulls within a rover. They begin to form bonds, and show more interest in remaining together in their rover grouping. Even with closer bonds, bulls still display and spar often - a form of play that also hones their hunting and fighting skills. Rovers do not adopt offspring, and will turn tail and swim away from them. If the offspring continues to follow them, the rovers may kill and eat them if sufficiently hungry. This is treated as a hunt.
