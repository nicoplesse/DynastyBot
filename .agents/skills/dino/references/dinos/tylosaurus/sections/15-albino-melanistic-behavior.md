# Tylosaurus – Albino & Melanistic Behavior

Melanistic Tylosaurus risk overheating at a much faster rate than their non-Melanistic counterparts, and cannot beach without risking death from the heat of the hot sands and sunlight. It can take less than 5 minutes for a Tylosaurus to become too exhausted to drag itself back to the water - meaning that if a melanistic Tylosaurus becomes stuck, its only chance of surviving is having an ally push it back off of the beach and onto land. Melanistic Tylosaurus are accepted in courtship and as members of hunting pairs. Albino Tylosaurus are much more fragile than other Tylosaurus, and also risk overheating - this time paired with painful sunburns that leave Tylosaurus unwilling to rub up against or be too close to other Tylosaurus. Albino Tylosaurus are much slower both in water and on land than non-Albino Tylosaurus counterparts, and are as a result typically chosen only if there is no other options available for a hunting pair. Albino Tylosaurus are also - as a result of their lack of speed and therefore inability to provide for offspring - chosen last during courtship. Neither Albino or Melanistic Tylosaurus are treated any differently otherwise, and may still happily bond within their matrilines, sisterhoods, and as a part of rovers.
