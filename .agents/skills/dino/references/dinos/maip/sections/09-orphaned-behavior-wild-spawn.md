# Maip – Orphaned Behavior (Wild Spawn)

Orphaned Maip pups hatch much the same as non-orphans, and are exceptionally noisy to draw approval from their parents, but quickly quiet down when they realize they are alone. This quietness tends to persist into adulthood, and while it makes the individual much better at setting up ambushes and hunting, it may become troublesome when it comes to interacting with other Maip. In order for an orphaned pup to sneak into a clutch of parented pups, the orphan will watch from afar, selecting the weakest and most submissive of the pups to target. At the right moment, they will try to kill this pup and take their place. Some Maip pups, especially males, may choose to avoid the conflict of killing another pup to infiltrate. If able to, they will slip into the nest unnoticed by any lingering parents. With the successful infiltration, this pup will roll in the nest as much as possible to cloak their old scent in the new scent, ensuring their safety in the clutch. If unable to roll in the nest, they will often rub themselves against the fresh corpse of the slain pup. Maip pups that already dwell within the nest may react to infiltration differently depending on the method. The murder of a sibling may trigger the brood to cry out in fear, alerting the parents. However, the pup who infiltrated will mimic these panicked cries, hiding their identity among the brood. An infiltrator who slips in without killing an existing pup must hide their scent in the nest quickly. Otherwise, present pups of the parents will move away and alert them of the newcomer. Maip parents will often never discover this infiltrator if they are not alerted, or do not see it happen, and will treat it like the rest of their pups. If unable to find surrogate parents, orphaned Maip will readily group with others in their situation, preferring safety in numbers. Maip are nocturnal, and choose to find secluded and covered places to sleep during the day, becoming most active at night. They have scales and eyes that are very sensitive to light and take care to shade if caught out during the day. Excessive exposure to sunlight can cause sunburns, and in extreme cases can cause blisters, or even blindness. Maip enjoy mud baths or a soak in shallow water, rolling around to remove external parasites or any shedding skin. Maip are very bold creatures, with high curiosity and next to no fear. They often stroll right up to other dinosaurs, laughing and cackling as they nip just behind their heels to see how they react, or chasing small herbivores for fun. They often use their many unique vocalizations to scare other dinosaurs, seemingly fully aware of just how unsettling their voices can be in the night. However, unless hunting, if they are met with aggression by the object of their entertainment - and the species is their tier or higher - they back off, preferring a safer form of entertainment. Should the other species start to run, Maip cannot help themselves but engage in chase - even if they have no intentions of hunting the other species. They air snap, release cackles and loud screeches, relishing in the thrill of terrifying the other species. They can be heard screeching through the treeline, their piercing shrieks and howls echoing throughout the POI as they stalk under the shadows. Maip are also extremely intelligent, and can be seen seeking out small carnivores such as Compsagnathus, Rhamphorhynchus or solo Latenivenatrix for grooming opportunities, and sometimes reward their little companions with scraps from their hunts. If a Maip has not sought out a cleaner scavenger, they will torment them like any other small creature before they kill and eat them. ONLY one temporary companion may be permitted to eat from a kill, with others are aggressed away. Male Maip are very shy. Encountering one another may reduce their anxieties, as they introduce themselves with a submissive roll before realizing they’re of the same sex, and exchange some passive pleasantries like grooming and socialization before continuing on their way. Female Maip, however, are very dominant. They often bully males outside of breeding season (and more submissive females), toying with and spooking them until they flee. If a female encounters another dominant female, the two engage in a vicious brawl until one backs down. If a solitary female Maip becomes notorious enough in an area, she may be avoided entirely by other Maip until the Dry season, when breeding is most common. Lone males in the area may keep tabs on her scent, ready to seek her out for courtship opportunities when the time is right.
