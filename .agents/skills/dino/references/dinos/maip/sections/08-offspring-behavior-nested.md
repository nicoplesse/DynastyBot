# Maip – Offspring Behavior (Nested)

Once hatched within the confines of the nest cave, Maip offspring, known as pups, are first greeted with a celebration consisting of a cacophony of hoots, laughs and cackles as they emerge. The health of the pup is gauged closely by their parents to see if they add their own voices to the celebration. If a Maip pup is hatched and silent, they are usually considered sickly, and can be cannibalized by their parents to preserve the wellbeing of the other pups. However, if the silent Maip pup is the only pup in the clutch, it will not be cannibalized. Instead, this solitary pup has extra care put in to ensure its survival. If the efforts seem in vain, or no improvement is observed, the pup may then be cannibalized. Maip pups are born with poor eyesight and very soft, vulnerable scales. While their eyesight begins to develop better halfway through the hatchling stage, their scales do not properly harden until close to the adolescent stage. Once pups have enough strength, they are considered one of the most rambunctious species' young. They are noisy and boisterous as they play, roughhouse and hunt their parents, who seem to enjoy this behavior from their young. Maip pups, with their playful nature and steadily growing intelligence, often pick up play behaviors from their parents, or make up their own games. This can come in many forms such as a simulation of predator-prey dynamics - often resembling hide and seek! - copying one another’s vocalizations, or presenting their siblings with unfamiliar objects, ideally to provoke a reaction. These games extend to parents as well, who may even encourage sparring and play-fighting amongst the brood. These fights sometimes become overzealous - resulting in injury, which parents are quick to correct with a low hiss if the fights become too intense. Maip parents never tolerate a scrap between their offspring to go to deathscars. Pups remain inside the cave until they reach the mid-Juvenile stage - and only once nightfall lands are pups allowed to begin venturing outside of the nesting area. First time parents, however, are extremely protective of their offspring, and may wait to allow them out until they are absolutely sure they are safe - leading to frustrated and rapidly disobedient pups. At adolescence, Maip pups are taught to hunt for themselves, being guided to critter burrows by their parents, who encourage their offspring to play with their prey to draw out hunts and wear down their stamina. Parents provide less and less food for their offspring, until pups are able to feed themselves without assistance. Adolescent pups begin accompanying their parents on hunts integral experience for hunting experience and learning. These young pups - up to a maximum of 2 at a time - help to overwhelm prey and experience tactics their parents employ.  Adolescents can only assist in hunts against solo Large prey items and below. Adolescent pups may also be permitted to take more of a leading role in hunts, targeting solo Medium and below prey, as well as lone offspring that have strayed too far from their parent’s watchful eyes. Once pups mature at Sub-Adult, they no longer get to enjoy the benefit of their parents' kills and protection and prepare to be sent away. Before this happens, parents take some time to consider all the pups they have raised, determining whether one may stay behind as a favored offspring, forming a family trio.
