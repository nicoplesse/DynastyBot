# Maip – Death

Solitary Maip nearing the end will often hide themselves in a safe den, or a particularly well-shaded patch of foliage to rest and peacefully pass away - if starvation, predation or illness doesn't claim them first. When one member of a pair passes away from old age, the other is quick to follow, usually suffering extreme stress from the loss of their mate. If only one part of the pair remains after the passing of the other, this heartbroken Maip may fall into a deep depression - with extreme cases provoking a prolonged loss of appetite, leading to their eventual death. Maip pairings rearing offspring may find themselves with the loss of a pup due to predation, sickness, or other natural causes of death. Parents do not mourn their offspring until they reach Adolescence - with cannibalization common on sickly offspring to protect the health of the rest of the brood. Bonded siblings of Maips from the same clutch will mourn the death of their sibling very deeply, their grief taking them down a path similar to the loss of a mate. Due to having a lifelong bond with their companion, Maips spend every waking moment with one another once they form a pair. Because of this strong bond, if they lose their partner, Maip often become inconsolable and uncharacteristically silent, guarding the body for as long as they can before scavengers close in. Maip will mourn their partners, refusing to eat or socialize for up to 3 days. After they begin to feel better, they’ll slowly care for themselves once more before instinct drives them to seek out a new relationship.  Some individuals, however, may never form another pair again. If a Maip's partner is lost during combat, they can defend their mate’s corpse for up to 30 seconds before they must abandon the body to scavengers or predators. After this, they will mourn for up to 3 days. Maip may not attempt to hunt other players in the courtship grounds and must primarily feed from critters or carrion.
