# Maip – Predator Response

Maip are a species that do not take kindly to the hunting efforts of other species against themselves. If a Maip detects another carnivorous species stalking or attempting to hunt them or their group, they aggressively scream in an attempt to ward off the predators. Experienced, older Maip may opt to wait until the hunters charge in to attack before they leap into action, focusing their efforts to target one specific hunter and bearing down on them in order to end the fight quickly. This is especially true for groups of Maip, who will not hesitate to run down an attacker to kill it. Younger Maip take longer to recognize the signs of an approaching predator. If alone at the first sight of danger, they may opt to flee in an attempt to outspeed or outlast their pursuer, or lose them in complicated surroundings such as forests or rocky outcrops. If hunted and unable to flee their attackers, Maip will fight back relentlessly until their last breath. Maip avoid Tyrannotitan due to the Apex carnivore's speed and ferocity, fleeing when they can. However Maip will always protect their closely bonded individuals from Tyrannotitan. Maip are intelligent, cunning, and even cruel with their prey. Built for endurance and speed, Maip take great pride in their hunting strategies, which may differ between pairs depending on familial strategies and learned experiences. Maip pairs prefer to use their numbers against their prey, flanking their target from the sides and startling them with hoots and laughs before closing the gap and landing their devastating blows. If a pair or family trio are hunting together, one Maip often positions themselves as a distraction to bait carnivores or cause herbivores to flee, allowing the others to attack their chosen prey from the brush. Then, the “bait” Maip will join the fight until they bring down the creature. Solo Maip are much more likely to bite then run, rather than stay and kill their prey. In order to protect themselves, they will chase down their prey and use their teeth and claws to inflict bleeding wounds before fleeing a few body lengths, repeating the process until their prey is felled from blood loss. Utilizing their razor sharp teeth and gouging claws, all Maip bleed their prey out swiftly.  As endurance hunters, they are primed for long hunts that devastate and tire their prey. Maip that are in a Cackle or Skulk - either parents and their favored pup, or three closely bonded siblings - are a prime example of using learned tactics for hunting, especially against Apex prey. These groupings focus on larger prey in order to feed the entire group. Maip that have experienced hunting Apexes in the past develop efficient hunting strategies, with the knowledge of just how dangerous these targets can be. These swift hunters often form a pattern of attack, with each member taking turns to rush in, slash at their target to open gaping wounds, and flee before they themselves can be injured. No matter the number of Maips in the hunt, they always celebrate with a loud cackle before they eat, excited by the scent of blood and the temptation of fresh meat. After they eat their fill, they often groom eachother to clean one another’s wounds, and roll after gorging themselves on their kill.
