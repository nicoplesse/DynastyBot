# Maip – Nesting

The signal to nest comes specifically from the female in an opposite-sex pair, with her behavior becoming broody and unusual. Oftentimes the female signals to her male companion her eagerness to nest by collecting nesting materials and stowing them away - stashing them into various hides, ranging from logs to rocky crags. These hoards contain nesting materials such as reeds, roots and branches, and are placed near to - or inside of - caverns that would serve as ideal nesting locations. Males are very quick to catch onto this, and begin assisting them with gathering materials. Sometimes, males may can be more selective with nesting locations, and attempt to lead their mates to safer, more covered areas away from the harshness of the sun. The soon-to-hatch brood is supervised from afar by Maip parents, keeping a careful distance as to not draw attention to the den site. Due to their more aggressive nature, females will return to the nest less frequently than the males do, as fathers take a majority of parental care in the early stages. Same-sex Maip pairs cannot nest, and do not adopt.
