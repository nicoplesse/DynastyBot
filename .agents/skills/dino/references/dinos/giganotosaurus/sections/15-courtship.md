# Giganotosaurus – Courtship

Females who are ready to court will shake and rub their chin on trees, bushes, and rocks, leaving their scent to indicate to males that they are ready to court (:stretch_emote:). When males pick up this scent, they open their jaws and begin to flush (by using :question_emote: , and changing the colors on their skin). Males flush colors into their crest, necks and faces in order to indicate readiness to court. Females show immediate aggression to males whose faces are not flushed in this manner if they approach her and they are ungrouped. This is especially troublesome for low testosterone and melanistic males, who must attempt a different method of courtship. Males, with flushed crests, broadcast (:broadcast_call:) at a female from a distance of a minimum of five bodylengths, in order to let her know of his presence. The further the space between them, the longer the male will have to slowly approach the female - so experienced males will take their time and approach her before broadcasting to declare his intent. If the female is unappreciative of his attempt, or otherwise does not want to court, she will issue an aggressive call towards him and turn away. The male then must move on, or issue an aggressive call (:threaten_call:) in return to initiate a fight to push the female out of the POI. If the female is interested, she will turn to face him and examine him, before doing a light shake in the male’s direction. Being approached by a stranger male is extremely stressful for young females who have never courted before, and even so for experienced females. As a result, they will yawn often to release stress. Males must posture, stretch and display their colors, scars, and crests. Females are most impressed by the strongest, largest, and most experienced males, but they also reject males that move too quickly towards them, or those that show any aggression toward them at all. A male must slowly approach a female, taking his time, and being careful to not rush. He must cautiously observe her body language, noting that frequent yawning is a sign of stress - an indication to slow his pace - and that affectionate calls or shaking at him means that she is interested or entertained. If he is able to successfully approach her without being aggressed by her, the two will touch noses and nuzzle before affectionately calling to one another. They then set off to build a nest, having established a temporary courtship bond. If a solitary Giganotosaurus comes across a courting pair, they opt to give the forming pair a wide berth, and will not initiate conflict, in order to avoid being driven off by both individuals.
