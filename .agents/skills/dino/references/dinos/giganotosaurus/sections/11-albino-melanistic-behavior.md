# Giganotosaurus – Albino & Melanistic Behavior

Melanistic Giganotosaurus, while not outright cannibalized like their Albino counterparts, live slightly more challenging lives and hold slight differences to other Giganotosaurus. Melanistic males, in particular, struggle with courting easily, as they are unable to flush their crests in order to attract females. Instead, they are forced to take a different route, as detailed in Courtship Behaviors. Melanistic individuals are more temperamental than other non-melanistic Giganotosaurus, meaning that individuals are more likely to form quick bonds that break up quickly over longer lasting ones. Melanistic individuals benefit from being able to slip into the dark shade of forested areas easily, but also struggle with overheating, especially during the dry season. As a result, melanistic individuals prefer to primarily keep to the shaded areas along forests and tree lines - a choice that has them risking an encounter with Tyrannosaurus, who will not tolerate them in their POI. Albino Giganotosaurus are cannibalized upon discovery if nested into a clutch, and cannibalized immediately if discovered in adulthood. As a result, albino Giganotosaurus are completely unable to successfully court or nest. Pairs of Giganotosaurus are always aggressive towards other pairs and solitary Giganotosaurus, unwilling to share resources with them or exist in the same POI as them. If unwilling to fight, they must immediately leave the POI. Solitary Giganotosaurus are rarely, if ever, completely aggressive to other solitary Giganotosaurus - unless uninterested in courtship or grouping.
