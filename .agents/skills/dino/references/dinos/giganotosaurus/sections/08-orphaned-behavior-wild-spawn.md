# Giganotosaurus – Orphaned Behavior (Wild Spawn)

If one or both parents of a kit dies, they risk being completely neglected. While they may stay banded together as a temporary alliance until they reach Sub-Adult, survival for them is fraught with stress as they have no parents to feed, take care of, or nurture them. Kits who are born orphans or hatch alone live similar lives. From the beginning, they have to be independent, teaching themselves how to hunt, hide, and survive Gondwa. Kits avoid other adult Giganotosaurus instinctively, and do not approach other adult Giganotosaurus willingly. Orphaned kits grow to be fiercely independent and mostly solitary adults, struggling to trust any other individuals with their lives when it comes to hunting or courtship. Giganotosaurus thrive in solitude, but may form temporary groupings with other Giganotosaurus of any relation in order to achieve goals or to protect oneself better from threats. While most of the time bonds remain loose, the strongest typically form between a mother and her daughter, or two brother Giganotosaurus. However, both of these pairings disperse from one another if interested in courtship. Pairs of Giganotosaurus do not tolerate other pairs of Giganotosaurus within the same POI as them, and either challenge the offenders out or leave themselves if unwilling to fight. Individuals rub their cheeks up against objects to mark them as theirs. Giganotosaurus may develop favorite trees, rocks, or landmarks that they rub their cheek on every time they pass. This also serves an additional use of being a flag to other Giganotosaurus that others have passed through the area. Giganotosaurus are more aloof, showing little interest in their offspring or other Giganotosaurus they group with. In this way, they are methodical and unstoppable, pushing prey and other predators to their limits without showing much effort or strain. Their massive size intimidates both members of their own species and other predators away, making them suitable defenders for when nesting occurs and a temporary nesting site is claimed. Sometimes, a solitary Giganotosaurus approaching too quickly will result in aggression from the other, who will land a few bites if the individual is in their personal space, and then flee. Giganotosaurus display stress or discomfort by yawning frequently. Giganotosaurus enjoy resting under the shade, whether it be under a tree or beside a cliff side, in order to temporarily escape the heat. On the other hand, some Giganotosaurus prefer to entirely travel along the coast, letting the sea wind rush over their scales. Beaches are a popular courting place for Giganotosaurus, as they are open and allow great visibility for females.
