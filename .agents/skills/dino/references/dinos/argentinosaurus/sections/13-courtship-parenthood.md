# Argentinosaurus – Courtship & Parenthood

Males interested to breed will flush bright colors to the tops of their skins in a vibrant display for any interested females. Mature females come into season from his display and, eager to show her desire to breed, stomp her back legs into the ground (Back Stomp ability) to alert these males of her desire. Bulls become excited and stomp both front legs into the ground (Dual Stomp ability) once - to catch her attention. Nearby males also dual stomp to show their interest in the female. Noticing a rival's interest, bulls will pair off and stomp their front legs (Right Stomp ability) to intimidate their challengers. If neither back down, they engage in a shoving contest, alternating between stomping their right leg into the ground and neck swiping against the rival, standing shoulder to shoulder. Bull fights may not use the Dual Stomp ability when within a body length of a rival male while fighting for a female - this ability is only used once to signal interest. The bull fight commences until one forfeits with :fear call:, turning away and leaving that female. Approaching the female, the winner will continue stomping his right front leg into the ground, and she continues her eager displays as well. The pair, circling each other, come together to body shake and cement their bond for the season, necks entwining. Throughout the contest over a female, rival bulls may shed into bright colors to encourage nearby females to come into season as well, hoping to pull her away from the courting individuals while both bulls are distracted. Once a bullfight is won, any number of cows can choose to go with the victorious bull and form a tower, up to the limit of 4 cows per bull. Cows who have close female companions are more likely to bring their companion alongside them, as separating can prove to be detrimental. Taking over a Tower Harem Bulls may even opt to take over a Tower that has already been formed. This challenge is initiated similarly - with the challenging bull approaching his rival stomping his front legs and ending the challenge by rearing up on his hind legs, only to slam down aggressively in a Dual Stomp. Choosing to defend his cows, a lead bull will mimic the males intimidating displays and fights shoulder to shoulder with aggressive neck lashings and right leg stomps. The winning bull will drive out the loser or take control of the harem. However, this act may prove pointless, as the cows of his harem can choose to either accept or reject the newcomer. Rejecting the newcomer means they will whip their tails to push him away and abandon him to rejoin their injured bull. After a bull has taken over a rival's tower, or pushed out their challenger: Cows reinforce their decision in him by approaching and gently shaking their bodies against his, signifying their interest. If all 4 cows are receptive at the same time, the bull will lead them to a suitable nesting location. This is always in one of their dedicated nesting locations in their preferred POIs. Bulls do not actively assist with nest building, but will keep a watchful eye as the cows prepare for young. Since they are huge animals, Argentinosaurus are often fond of Bonapartenykus, who provide great assistance to them in nest construction by ferrying resources back and forth. Cows show loving and tender affection to any offspring they raise. They lavish them with soft, affectionate nuzzles and are very careful to walk slowly (even moreso than usual!) beside their calves, lest they accidentally trample them. Mothers opt to keep their young close, and preferably behind them - making it easier to not accidentally tread on them. Bulls assist with parental care once offspring reach juvenile, helping lead offspring to food and water, taking great care in socializing and bonding with his calves - whether or not they were sired by them. Cows will always adopt other orphaned sauropods they come across, welcoming them into their group (unless in a village - where they must split from the group in order to adopt the young) and treating them just as if they were their own young. Once calves reach Sub-Adult, they get pushed out of the tower grouping, but are allowed to remain nearby, socializing and grazing beside their parents and other relatives.
