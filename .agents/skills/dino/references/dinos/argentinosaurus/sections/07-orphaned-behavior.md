# Argentinosaurus – Orphaned Behavior

Orphaned calves, if in a sibling pair or trio, become very attached to their sibling for emotional and physical comfort. Losing one sibling often leads to the loss of the second. However, calves that have found themselves without companionship are at risk to many different predators. These calves will usually stay in the same area they hatched in for the sake of safety, only leaving if the area has run out of resources - or has become too dangerous. Orphaned calves may encounter other orphaned sauropod calves, forming unlimited groupings for protection until they hit adulthood. After which, they leave in search of new opportunities. Orphan life is extremely lonely for a species as social as Argentinosaurus. To curb this loneliness, orphaned calves are drawn towards other sauropods eager to follow closely for company. This proves to be the best result for an orphaned calf who cannot find their own kind. Once grown, previously orphaned Argentinosaurus are usually more vigilant and nervous than those raised in a tower. They constantly wander a POI, scanning the horizon for predators. If they are in a sibling bond, this behavior is lessened, using their sibling as extra eyes and emotional support to soothe their anxieties.
