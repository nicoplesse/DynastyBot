# Argentinosaurus – Aggressive Interaction

While Argentinosaurus are passive by nature, they do exhibit aggressive behavior in specific situations: Contests for Food During the Dry Season, Argentinosaurus become possessive over abundant food sources and large enough water sources to sustain their groups. They do not challenge other herbivores immediately, but will broadcast their displeasure and walk toward other herbivores to drive them away. Should the warning not be heeded (at least 3 aggressive calls must be issued), Argentinosaurus can use an attack move. In regards to their own species, Argentinosaurus towers defend their resources in the same way they would against other species. Villages instinctively move away, recognizing the need for this possessive behavior to secure food and water for young. When aggressed, they simply move off to find other grazing opportunities. Towers compete with each other over these areas of abundant resources. With both males and females calling to intimidate and drive off a rival tower. If ignored or challenged, both males will compete for the right to exist in the area (a challenge for territory by non territorial species). When guarding their resources from other Argentinosaurus groupings: Towers form a semi-circle barrier around their chosen source in the presence of another group, bellowing and calling to encourage them to move on. If the other group doesn’t back down or move on, the lead bull in the defending tower will move forward to meet the other tower's bull, aggressively calling out. If they refuse to back down yet again, the two will stand parallel to one another, and begin slamming their necks into their opponent’s bodies. These competitions swiftly end once one individual reaches deathscars or otherwise forfeits, in which they call out in pain and retreat to their group, losing the contest and being forced to move on. Contests for Females Argentinosaurus bulls that do not yet have a tower of their own - or have space in their tower for more cows - become restless when traveling alongside mature, unrelated cows. With the potential of courting, tensions between bulls rise, until they finally push each other aside to initiate a challenge. (See more connecting to this in Courtship & Parenthood)
