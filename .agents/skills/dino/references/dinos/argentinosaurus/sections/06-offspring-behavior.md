# Argentinosaurus – Offspring Behavior

Argentinosaurus young, known as calves, are often born in up to trios during the Rainy Season, but solo in Dry. Like many other herbivores, Argentinosaurus calves are born able to stand, see and hear in the first few hours of their lives. While roughhousing is common with all calves and hatchlings, Argentinosaurus calves are more likely to cuddle and practice grooming with one another. Calves will form very deep bonds with their siblings during this early stage, and will share lifelong partnerships with them, regardless of gender. Curious and playful, Argentinosaurus calves can often be seen picking up and playing “keep away” with objects like rocks, plants, bones and other interesting new things to play with - especially if another calf has shown interest in it. Calves tend to stay close to their mothers until they reach adolescence, and begin to branch out a little bit - though still stay within viewable distance of the tower. This is often for both the calf’s sake and the tower’s, as losing sight of the other can cause widespread panic and frantic searching. Overall, Argentinosaurus calves show genuine affection with each other, their mother, and the rest of their herd mates. Roughhousing is much more common in bull calves as they reach adolescence and begin practicing their dominance displays and combat. However, they know instinctively to stop when it gets too far, and will never take an opponent down to below half health. Even if this fighting does not stop, the adults in the group will quickly put an end to the fight with an authoritative call. Once at Sub-Adult, all calves - regardless of gender - disband from their tower to wander back into the village to seek mates or socialize with other adults. This results in a very sad departure, especially from the calf’s mother. They will often share one last grooming session and soft nuzzles and coos before the now adult calf leaves. Mothers not wishing to breed again often leave the tower and join a village with their newly mature children.
