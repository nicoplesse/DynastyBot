# Pycnonemosaurus – Adoption

If an orphan approaches a drove, it is usually welcomed in and a sow will claim it as her own, she will treat it as if it was her own offspring. If the group does not have any sows, a boar will step up and claim the offspring, guiding it through it’s youth as best he can. Orphans raised by boars are often more rough with their play, and more bold when interacting with others. Elder Pycnonemosaurus are less active and rely on the group for protection and food. They tend to linger near nests or resting areas while younger members take on the majority of hunting responsibilities. With their old age, they find themselves with less energy to interact well with other drove members. They may be looked over when others are seeking playmates, and often feel neglected or ignored by the rest of their group. Without any malicious intentions, the group may accidentally leave behind elder members - not realizing they have stopped to snooze, or cannot keep up with the energy of the rest of the group. After being left behind, elder Pycnonemosaurus may break away from the group and continue life as a solo individual.
