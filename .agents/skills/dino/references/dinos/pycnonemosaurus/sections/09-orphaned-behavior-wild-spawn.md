# Pycnonemosaurus – Orphaned Behavior (Wild Spawn)

Orphaned Pycnonemosaurus shoats often have rocky childhoods. Their distracted but enthusiastic nature can sometimes lead them into harm's way. Without the protection of a drove, orphaned shoats are less confident, but equally keen to explore. They often dart about between bushes, and peek curiously at other species that pass by. If they are lucky, they will come across a drove of adult Pycnonemosaurus, which will usually adopt them. As adults, orphaned Pycnonemosaurus are not as adept at social interactions within a drove; they enjoy the rough play, but often back out too soon or shy away from all-out play fights. They’re unsure of their standing, and haven’t grown up with siblings or family members to get them used to the proper way of things when shoving one another about. Pycnonemosaurus are very bold and loud animals; commonly referred to as "bullish". They travel their preferred hunting grounds frequently - never really settling in one POI for long unless courting or nesting in the area. This fun loving species has a very short attention span. Pycnonemosaurus are very easily distracted and enjoy following different dinosaurs around - posturing and swaying their heads playfully to jest around, and often confusing them. Pycnonemosaurus do not bond with other species, but see them as entertainment and may focus on individuals for a short period of time before moving on. Pycnonemosaurus have a natural drive to keep moving, searching for food and favorable resting spots. Their curiosity sometimes leads them into dens, caves, or thick foliage, where they explore - just because they can. When not playing or sniffing out prey, Pycnonemosaurus never bypasses the opportunity to bask in the sun, and take a quick swim in shallow water as the weather cools. Pycnonemosaurus love sitting and laying in mud to cool off and protect their skin from sun and parasites. This often turns into a playful social event, with individuals bumping and shoving each other for the best spot. Pycnonemosaurus are cathemeral, meaning they are awake and active at most times of the day. They take short, frequent naps - especially in a warm spot where they can bask.
