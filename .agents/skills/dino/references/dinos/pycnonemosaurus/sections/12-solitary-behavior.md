# Pycnonemosaurus – Solitary Behavior

When alone, Pycnonemosaurus continue to engage in typical behaviors. Solitary individuals may display to their reflections in water or toward other species they encounter, still partaking in head bobbing and low vocalizations. Though they lack the social reinforcement of a group, these displays may serve to entertain themselves or rehearse for future interactions. Without peers to roughhouse with, they may push against trees or roll fallen logs to simulate familiar play. Solitary Pycnonemosaurus are also more vulnerable and cautious, always choosing to flee from Apex carnivores, a stark contrast to the confidence they display when backed by a drove. Once a solitary Pycnonemosaurus encounters a drove, both parties typically approach one another with curiosity, bobbing their heads and inspecting each other. If the drove has space and is not at its social limit, the solitary individual is usually accepted without resistance. If there is no space in the drove they will usually just move on after the short interaction - but if the drove has enough individuals, some of them might choose to go with the newly met individual.
