# Styracosaurus – General Behavior

Styracosaurus are nomadic creatures that form huge herds, roaming through dense forests or arid grasslands in search of food. Styracosaurus are sociable but very stubborn, which generates rivalries and small squabbles within the herd  - though these never turn lethal. Styracosaurus enjoy the company of other ceratopsians in their group, and can often be seen together at nesting sites - even choosing to nest close to others in the herd. Despite this, and the fact that they even herd with these other species, Styracosaurus are still possessive about their food, often refusing to share it with other species. They tend to be one of the more aggressive ceratopsians in their mixed ceratopsian herd - often referred to as "attack dogs" of the group. They fearlessly charge around their companions - only hesitating when the opponents are Daspletosaurus or pairs of Apex carnivores. Styracosaurus like to sharpen and scratch their horns on rocks and trees, leaving marks that show their passage through an area. Some males compete passively against others, trying to leave marks higher up than other males, emphasizing their great size and strength. Styracosaurus like to cool off in shallow water during the hottest hours, even rolling in the mud to protect their skin and wounds. Although they are not territorial creatures, there will always be at least one adult member (usually a male) watching in case of danger, while the rest of the herd is resting.
