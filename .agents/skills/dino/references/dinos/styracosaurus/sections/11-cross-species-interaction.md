# Styracosaurus – Cross-Species Interaction

Styracosaurus herd alongside Albertaceratops, Pachyrhinosaurus and Psittacosaurus. They will opt to defend these species if attacked - permitting them to join the defensive formation (or shelter behind it), or up to the active engagement limit of 4 defenders of any species (including the defended individual). Styracosaurus have no tolerance for other herbivores they do not group with, and are quick to protect food sources for the wider ceratopsian herd if they have a lot of mouths to feed. These challenges over food begin with full herds moving in front of the claimed food resource and aggressive calling at least three times to the approaching herbivores. They utilise their bodies to block access to the food, and shake their heads fiercely. The herd will begin to move forward slowly, trying to drive the other herbivores away without escalating into a fight. If successful and the other herbivores leave, Styracosaurus relax and go back to eating. If they do not, and a battle ensues, all members of the herd can engage in the challenge over food. Does primarily form a defensive line around the food bush and do not break formation - only moving as far as 1 body length forward to land a hit, before returning to the formation. 4 active defenders charge around the outside of the formation - usually all stags, including the dominant stag, if available - and chase down their opponents to land hits, utilising their speed and agility to dart away in time for another member to land another, preferring to use "hit and run" tactics to wear down their opponents. This battle lasts until one member reaches deathscars - this forfeits the challenge for the entire herd, and forces them to give up and move on. If the other herbivores forfeit and leave, Styracosaurus do not chase them down to finish them off. Killing their opponents is not the intention of the challenge - only to drive them away from their food. Styracosaurus avoid conflict with Eotriceratops, Apatosaurus, Ampelosaurus and Argentinosaurus. They do not begin food challenges with these species.
