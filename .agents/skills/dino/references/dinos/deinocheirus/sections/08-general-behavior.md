# Deinocheirus – General Behavior

Deinocheirus are intriguing creatures that exhibit aloof behavior, yet have a strong need for socialization. While they primarily form strong pairbonds rather than large groups, they enjoy spending significant amounts of time together near bodies of water with other members of their species. Pairs engage in a variety of water-related activities that enrich their lives, such as swimming and diving in search of food and resources or simply for the joy of it. They also forage for fruits and berries along the shores, explore the coastline for shellfish, fish in the water, and take leisurely naps on the shore. During these moments, they often bask in the sun or enjoy the tranquility of moonbathing at night. Deinocheirus are cathemeral, meaning they are active both during the day and at night. However, they tend to be slightly more energetic during sunrise and sunset, when the light conditions are optimal for foraging and socializing. This adaptability allows them to make the most of their environment and resources, ensuring they have ample opportunities for feeding and interaction. It is quite common to observe pairs of Deinocheirus grooming each other and spending quality time together, which deepens their bonds. Despite their aloof nature, multiple pairs can often be seen mingling in the same area, engaging in social interactions without forming a cohesive group. These interactions serve to reinforce social ties among the pairs and create a sense of community in their environment. In terms of parenting, Deinocheirus excel at caring for their young and any adopted offspring. They are attentive and dedicated parents, often seen spending extensive time with their babies, nurturing and protecting them as they grow. Their commitment to their offspring is evident in the way they prioritize the well-being of their young, ensuring they have everything they need to thrive. Deinocheirus also have unique dietary habits, particularly enjoying chewing on lakeweed to absorb essential nutrients. It is not uncommon to see these creatures with strands of lakeweed in their mouths throughout the day. In addition to this, they may occasionally peck at rocks to ingest small stones that aid in their digestion. Deinocheirus have a strong inclination for scratching themselves frequently. This behavior is not only a form of self-care, but also a way to bond with their companions. They often seek the help of other Deinocheirus, especially their lifelong mate, to assist in scratching hard-to-reach places using their claws and beak. In addition, they may turn to their Struthiomimus companions for assistance, further strengthening the social bonds within their small community. When no help is available, Deinocheirus make use of their surroundings, rubbing against trees, logs, branches, and rocks to relieve any itch or discomfort. Over time, they tend to develop a preference for specific scratching spots in their environment, returning to these familiar places for comfort. This behavior becomes a daily routine and a common sight near bodies of water where Deinocheirus gather, blending practicality with social interaction. Although Deinocheirus are sedentary, they are sometimes forced to leave their usual water hangouts - either from a lack of resources, or high levels of predation. When migrating, Deinocheirus opt to move as one large group where possible, hoping that their numbers might dissuade predators from hunting them while they locate to safer waters. Deinocheirus may migrate both on land or in water, and may make short stops in smaller water sources on the way to their new destination.
