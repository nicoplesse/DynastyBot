# Deinocheirus – Predator Reaction

When a predator attempts to hunt Deinocheirus offspring, they won't face just one defender - but both parents, who act in unison to protect their young. One exception is with Adult Spinosaurus. Deinocheirus flee with their offspring, and only turn to fight back when escape is impossible. This fierce loyalty even extends to their adopted Struthiomimus companions. Deinocheirus rise without hesitation to defend these smaller allies as if they were their own - though this is limited to 1 Adult, as opposed to 2 for Deinocheirus offspring. The moment danger strikes, one (or both) adults instinctively move to shield the vulnerable, positioning themselves between the attacker and their charges to form a living wall of defense. In most cases, Deinocheirus are discerning enough to assess a threat before engaging. When confronted by an Adult apex predator like Spinosaurus, they opt to flee as quickly as possible, recognizing the immense danger such a rival presents. Only when escape is impossible will they fight back, with just 1 protector. Suchomimus provoke a defensive response in Deinocheirus - especially if they have young present. Suchomimus are one of the few semi-aquatic predators who might threaten a Deinocheirus, and put these species on edge. Though they're generally ignored by healthy Deinosuchus and Sarcosuchus, Deinocheirus remain cautious around these ambush predators. Their watchfulness increases significantly when near water or when caring for young, aware that crocodilians may seize any opportunity to strike at the injured or vulnerable. If a terrestrial predator approaches near water, Deinocheirus may employ aggressive splashing - rearing up and slamming their forelimbs into the surface to send up powerful sprays of water. These soaking strikes can drench and disorient land-based hunters, giving the Deinocheirus time to escape or reposition. In some cases, Deinocheirus can be observed fleeing to land, where they shake off water and dry themselves in preparation for a counterattack. With their feathers no longer waterlogged and footing restored, they may turn and charge at the predator - especially if it continues to pursue or threatens the group further. While not naturally aggressive, Deinocheirus are fully capable of delivering a punishing defense when their family is endangered.
