# Deinocheirus – Orphaned Behavior

Orphaned Deinocheirus are naturally very skittish and cautious, and usually constantly on high alert to avoid attracting the attention of any nearby carnivores. Despite their emotional dependency on their parents, orphans quickly adapt to their circumstances and become quite self-sufficient. They are capable of foraging for food and finding water on their own, using their instincts to navigate the environment. However, their heightened caution often means they prefer to stay hidden, blending into their surroundings to avoid danger. Even though orphaned Deinocheirus are highly self-sufficient in terms of survival and exhibit asocial behavior, they still need to fulfill their social needs. These young creatures crave the company of others to feel a sense of family, especially during their formative years. This need for social interaction often drives them to seek out the companionship of other orphaned Deinocheirus. It is common for these young individuals to gather near bodies of water, forming loose dispersal groups that exist primarily to meet their socialization needs. Although they enjoy each other's company, orphans lack the skills or strength to defend themselves or others in the event of danger. When faced with a threat, their natural instinct is to scatter and run, relying on their speed to escape rather than attempting to confront or ward off predators. This instinctual behavior highlights their vulnerability as they navigate the challenges of survival without parental protection.
