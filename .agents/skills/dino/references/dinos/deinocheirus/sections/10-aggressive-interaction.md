# Deinocheirus – Aggressive Interaction

Conflicts between Deinocheirus are quite rare, as they tend to prioritize avoiding confrontation and respecting each other's boundaries. However, if an individual exhibits unprovoked aggression towards its own kind or towards the Struthiomimus they live alongside, the entire community at that body of water will shun the aggressor, and, if necessary, they may even exile them. This same rule applies to aggressive Struthiomimus, who will be ostracized by both the Deinocheirus and their own species' communities. When confronted with aggressive behavior from other herbivores, Deinocheirus prefer to avoid conflict by calmly retreating into the water, showing little urgency as they try to defuse the situation. However, if another herbivore attempts to steal their food, continuously harass them, or pose a threat to their offspring, these usually peaceful giants will not hesitate to display their powerful claws in a defensive stance. Though they aim to avoid lethal confrontations, they are more than capable of standing their ground when necessary. When encountering carnivores, Deinocheirus will generally try to stand their ground if the situation appears favorable. They assess the danger carefully, and if the threat seems too great, they will swiftly flee, retreating into the water or on land depending on where they feel safest in that moment.
