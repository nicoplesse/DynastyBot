# Deinocheirus – Courtship & Parenthood

When the Deinocheirus breeding season begins, unpaired females start to gather along the shorelines of lakes and rivers, forming loose-knit groups where they rest, forage and observe. Unpaired males, meanwhile, driven by instinct and anticipation, become more vocal and energetic, positioning themselves around the female gathering. In attempt to stand out from the competition, males stand up tall, spreading their feathered arms wide, and stretching their necks skyward in exaggerated postures. They bellow deep, echoing calls across the water, interspersing these with dramatic splashes using their arms - sometimes startling fish or other semi-aquatic species in the process. These water displays not only demonstrate strength and vitality, but also highlight the male's alterness, suggesting he would make a capable protector during nesting season. Females watch carefully, evaluating each male's stamina, presence, and awareness of his surroundings. If a female shows interest in a particular male, she selects a rock as a symbolic offering, akin to a wedding ring in humans. The female then approaches her chosen male calmly, presenting her gift to him and calling softly. If the male accepts the gift, the pair gently intertwine their necks in an affectionate bonding ritual, sharing low vocalizations and grooming one another. Together, they then bury the rock in a special place along the waterline, forming a lasting memorial of their union, and marking the area as a special place. Males that do not accept the female's advances, however, will simply ignore her and continue his display. When it's time to nest, the pair always split off from the communal group, and form a separate group with just the two of them, and any adopted Struthiomimus or Halszkaraptor companions. They drift away from the main group, and select a well-hidden location near water to build their nest, which they construct together. The mother incubates the eggs while the father stands guard, vigilantly protecting their territory and keeping watch even when the mother needs to leave the nest. Once the babies hatch, both parents tirelessly work together to raise their young. It's common to see several pairs nesting near the same body of water, allowing the young to socialize and interact with one another, fostering a sense of community among the offspring. If the pair have Struthiomimus in their group, Struthiomimus will build their nests close to those of the Deinocheirus. This proximity allows them to assist in surveying the area for potential dangers while also benefiting from the protection offered by their larger companions. Once their offspring reaches Adolescent, the nesting pair opt to travel back towards the wider gathering group, and integrate their offspring with the others. Deinocheirus are renowned for their nurturing instincts, making them exceptional parents. It is quite common for adult pairs to adopt orphaned Deinocheirus they encounter and raise them as their own. This parental instinct to care for and protect young, even those not biologically related, speaks to the deep familial bonds these creatures form. Orphaned individuals who are adopted receive the same care, protection, and guidance as the biological offspring of the pair, giving them a second chance at survival within a supportive family structure. Same sex pairs within Deinocheirus are also seen, with all male pairs and all female pairs equally common. Their courtship starts similarly to opposite sex pairs, with either individual taking on the "male" performance or "female" gift giving, depending on individual personalities. Same sex pairs willingly adopt orphans and rear them together, often building a nest for their adopted young to settle in.
