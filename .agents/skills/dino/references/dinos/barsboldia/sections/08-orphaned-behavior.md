# Barsboldia – Orphaned Behavior

Orphaned Barsboldia are blind when they are born, incessantly calling for the presence of a mother that will never come to them. As they begin to realize that no one is there to warm them, they go quiet, and wait for their legs to gain the strength they need. Many calves perish due to cold, particularly in the Rainy Season. Orphan calves fair better in the Dry Season thanks to the warmer temperatures. Orphaned calves are even more skittish than their nested counterparts, and take a significant amount of time to warm up to new experiences and individuals they encounter. The earlier they manage to find others of their species - or other suitable herbivore surrogates - the earlier they grow out of their skittishness. Orphans who do not meet any other Barsboldia - or at least a friendly herbivore face - as they grow up can retain some of this nervousness into adulthood. Without a mother to guide them to try other foods, orphaned Barsboldia take longer to try other food sources, with extreme cases resulting in adults stubbornly sticking to berries and fruit only. Without parents, orphaned Barsboldia will search for any similarly-shaped herbivores (such as Iguanodon, Tenontosaurus, Parasaurolophus, Lambeosaurus) to follow along with for comfort. However, they may only group with their own kind, Struthiomimus and Bonapartenykus. In adulthood, orphaned Barsboldia may initially struggle with courting. While females who were orphaned are more difficult to impress and get close enough to for a bull to court, a bull who was orphaned often does not know how to bugle his first courting season - so may opt to join a different group as a subordinate bull or be courted by another, more experienced bull instead. Barsboldia require a high level of salt in their diet once they reach Sub-Adult. To better maintain an electrolyte balance, and aid them in water retention (which is crucial to the more arid environments they inhabit), Barsboldia are frequently migrating in search of Salt Rocks. Barsboldia have an incredible sense of smell, and are capable of tracking down the location of salty environments over long distances. They can be frequently seen pausing and sniffing the air to detect different scents. Barsboldia are a cathemeral species with a high appetite, requiring them to continuously graze in order to stay satiated. Finding enough food can prove difficult - particularly in the Dry Season - but Adult Barsboldia have adapted to eat and consume a wide range of vegetation. In the terrible midday heat of the Dry Season, Barsboldia can be spotted standing closely together, pressing their bodies side by side to avoid excess heat from the environment. This is due to Barsboldia having lower body temperatures than the atmospheric temperature during the scorching heat of Dry Season. Barsboldia do not go out of their way to remain in massive herds like Iguanodon and Parasaurolophus, and maintain small, close-knit family groupings over making connections with new, unrelated Barsboldia. This helps them prioritize resources for themselves, their closest friends, and family members. Barsboldia are constantly chewing. This behavior comforts them and keeps them fed, so particularly stressed Barsboldia often immediately rush to find something to graze on, filling their bellies and giving them something to chew on for the next few hours. When food is limited, Barsboldia opt to chew on branches instead. Barsboldia absolutely love rain, and take it as a great opportunity to cool off. They lay in rain puddles and lift their faces to the rain, exposing their necks and chests to cool themselves off quicker. Barsboldia, once fully mature, are a relatively calm species that do not spook easily. This is in stark contrast to their earlier years, but is something that builds over time as they age. Adults pay little attention to carnivores - including Apexes - until directly threatened by them, or if they have young. However, they do not tolerate carnivores entering their personal space and slam their tail into the ground to showcase this displeasure. Ultimately, if given appropriate space, Barsboldia remains tolerant. There is one exception to this - adult Tyrannosaurus - see Predator Reaction. Barsboldia are a highly parental and nurturing species. The bond between mother and calf is strong - and even bulls have strong protective instincts towards young.
