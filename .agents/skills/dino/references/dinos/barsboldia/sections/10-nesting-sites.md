# Barsboldia – Nesting Sites

At nesting sites, only grouped Bonapartenykus and Struthiomimus are allowed within 5 body lengths of a placed nest. Any other species that ventures too close are driven away. Up to 2 herd members - almost always bulls - start with issuing an aggressive call :threaten_call:, and break from the herd to chase the intruder away if this is ignored. These interactions are not intended to kill, but to create distance, and the bulls quickly return once the threat has moved away. Cows remain near their nests, nestled against their eggs or young hatchlings, rarely engaging unless the threat is extreme. Defending Calves Barsboldia mothers defend their calves from all other species - except other Barsboldia, their grouped Bonapartenykus or Struthiomimus, or any adopted herbivore offspring - until they reach adolescent. They defend a space of 2 body lengths around their calves. Any species that get too close are warned first with an aggressive call :threaten_call:, and may be attacked until they leave 2 body lengths of distance between themselves and the calf. Struthiomimus Struthiomimus are Barsboldia's closest allies. They tolerate these species to get very close and personal with their young, and value them as excellent sentry species. Struthiomimus are often seen assisting  Barsboldia navigate through complicated rocky terrain, which the apex herbivore tends to struggle with. Barsboldia form very close bonds with Struthiomimus or a group of Struthiomimus, and spend a long time mourning them if one of these treasured companions passes away. Bonapartenykus Bonapartenykus are also permitted to join the herd and get close to young, but Barsboldia do not connect with these species as closely as they do with Struthiomimus. Bonapartenykus have a quiet and gentle nature, something which is appreciated by Barsboldia, who dislike excessively noisy species. Other Species When solitary, Barsboldia is prone to following herds of other herbivorous species - most often Parasaurolophus, Lambeosaurus and Iguanodon.
