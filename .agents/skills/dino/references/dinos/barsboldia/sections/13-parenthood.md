# Barsboldia – Parenthood

Both bulls and cows may adopt orphaned Barsboldia, treating them as if they were their own. Cows are very dedicated mothers, and lay on top of their nests until their eggs hatch. At the beginning of their lives, Barsboldia calves are blind, fragile, and carefully tucked into the nest. As soon as they hatch, their mother leans in to nuzzle them, soothing them and drawing them towards her. Barsboldia mothers remain in one place - directly beside the nest - until the calves' eyes open and they become ready to stand. Otherwise, they risk their calves nonstop making noise without her, potentially alerting predators and risking the safety of her entire clutch. Bulls give the mothers distance at this time, patrolling the outskirts of the area and staying away from the calves. Mother Barsboldia with calves in their nest are known to be quite aggressive, flush with protective hormones that make them more reactive and violent. As a result, Barsboldia bulls view their task as protecting the area as incredibly important, as failing this task can disappoint the female and therefore leave him losing out on nesting opportunities with her in the future. If any individual other than grouped Barsboldia, Struthiomimus or Bonapartenykus approach the nest and get within 5 body lengths of it, all Barsboldia are permitted to give one aggressive call :threaten_call: as a warning before chasing the individual from the area. These engagements follow nesting challenge rules. Mothers are highly defensive of their calves, and when migrating, protect a radius of 2 body lengths around their offspring under Adolescent. This applies to any adopted offspring - including other species adoptees - that they adopt as their own. Bulls tend to be less involved than the cows, but will accept being approached, standing still and careful to allow the calves to play around him and to earn the mother’s favor. Over-excited bulls or bulls that play too rough risk being loudly chastised by protective mothers. All members of the herd treat calves fondly, and are highly protective of them. This includes bulls - both the father, and any other adult bull in the group. Any herd member can move in defense of a calf, but nearly always the mother and father protect them first. Mothers are always gentle with their calves, nuzzling and gently bellowing at them to express their affection. Calves tend to be frightened of every new experience and their mother is always the first to comfort and soothe them. As calves get more independent, their mother becomes more relaxed, allowing them to come to her for comfort and protection rather than keeping them close. Females will affectionately shoo their children off once they become sub-adult, encouraging them to go off and form their own social groups. Adult offspring may opt to stay close and form their own social groups close by their birth grouping, resulting in familial herds that roam the expanse of Gondwa for generations. Should the offspring be Albino, mothers frequently prefer to stay with them for life, caring for them until one of them passes.
