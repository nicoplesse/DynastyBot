# Barsboldia – Beginning Courtship

Cows start the courtship process, signifying their receptiveness to courtship by repeatedly performing stretching motions in sight of a bull. Bulls that notice this repetitive stretching approach the cows, sniffing the air to pick up any differences in their scent. Once it is clear that the cow is interested, bulls let out a distinctive bugle. This is a deep, far reaching broadcast :broadcast_call:  that signals their presence and intent. As they start bugling, other bulls in the herd - and even the area, if multiple herds or individuals are present - eagerly move over, calling out with bugles of their own to start a chorus of song. With multiple bulls bugling and competing for a cow's attention, disagreements can occur. Bulls seeking to be chosen start to walk deliberately in front of other bulls, attempting to block them from view of the cows. This causes the blocked bull to become highly irritated, switching his bugles to low, aggressive grumbles - an attempt to warn off the intruder. If the aggressive call is ignored, and the other bull does not move out of the way, the other bull thwacks his tail into the ground, prompting the beginning of a short and loud fight between the two bulls. These fights are quick, consist of much shoving, pushing, and hitting each other with their tails, until one is forced to submit at half health. These fights can be a prime opportunity for more submissive bulls to garner attention from a cow. While other bulls are battling, quieter bulls may approach the cow and bugle towards her, hoping to draw her attention away from the fighting bulls. A Cow's Choice Ultimately, the decision on who a cow will nest with is down to the cow. Bulls must wait to be chosen, and even a successful fight against a rival can sometimes be overlooked if a cow has a particular preference. Bulls that fail to impress are simply ignored. The cow will turn away from the bull, making it clear she has no interest. Cows only choose a single bull to court, and may choose a different bull each time she nests if she wishes. Some cows grow very attached to their bulls and over time, form a strong bond, choosing him to father all of her clutches. When a cow is suitably impressed, she approaches the bull and nuzzles against him, calling out affectionately. The bull then seals this by gifting a salt rock for the cow or leading her to a drink of salt water. The cow will follow him, staying nearby until courtship is over. Bulls may continue bugling and attempting to impress cows until all interested females have chosen a bull. After this, the group moves to find suitable nesting grounds - but this can be in the same POI if it appears safe enough. Nests are placed reasonably close together. Females courting the same bull always opt to have their nests beside one another, but females courting other bulls in the same herd prefer to make sure their nests are also in close proximity. Having nesting sites close together aids bulls in protecting them, and makes the cows feel more secure.
