# Struthiomimus – Social Behavior

Struthiomimus are highly social creatures that thrive in the company of others. They usually form large herds known as flocks, which consist of multiple males and their respective harems. Although males try to keep their distance from other males and their harems, they tolerate each other's presence within the flock, except during the breeding season, when they will aggressively defend their space. Females, on the other hand, coexist peacefully and often interact freely, even across different harems. It is common to observe males engaging in playful or aggressive sparring, delivering kicks to establish dominance. Meanwhile, females, whether from the same or different harems, often forage together, creating a more harmonious atmosphere. Within these flocks, individuals may form strong social bonds. Hens in a harem often develop close pair bonds with their male, and chicks growing up together can also forge lasting connections. As males mature, they might lose these bonds or merely tolerate each other more than unfamiliar males, while female bonds tend to persist even after reaching maturity, even if they belong to different harems. Male Struthiomimus enjoy spending time with their harem and can often be seen grooming, resting, or tending to their hens and chicks. When a male finds food, he will call his harem and offspring to eat first, even helping them reach food that might be out of their grasp. Males are also the first to leap into action when it comes to defending their harem from threats. Males are generally more vocal and showy than females. They frequently practice their calls, whether for singing or alerting the group to danger, and take great pride in preening their feathers after any physical effort. In contrast, females are more laid-back, grooming themselves throughout the day but not to the same extent as their male counterparts.
