# Struthiomimus – Offspring Behavior

All the females in a harem lay their eggs together, but it is the male who takes on the responsibility of incubating them. When the eggs hatch, baby Struthiomimus, known as chicks , are treated equally and will see each other as full siblings. Mothers do not play an active role in raising the young, although they may assist in protecting them if necessary. Once a Struthiomimus chick hatches, it must wait for its feathers to dry, remaining motionless in the nest under its father's care. After their feathers dry, hatchlings will stay close to their father, as they are fragile and rely on him for safety. Though cautious, they are naturally curious and will attempt to peck at anything within reach. As chicks grow into juveniles, they become more adventurous and begin exploring farther from their father. The father, meanwhile, tries to keep the group together, though sometimes the chicks may wander into potentially dangerous situations. At this stage, young Struthiomimus enjoy playing with one another—chasing each other and picking up objects from the ground. It is also during this phase that the father starts encouraging them to forage for their own food, fostering their independence. By the time the chicks reach adolescence, their father starts to pay less attention to them, forcing them to become more self-reliant. Though mostly independent at this point, they still remain near their father's harem. If the group is part of a nomadic flock, they will resume migrating around this time. Male adolescents begin to display more vocal and aggressive behavior, while females remain relatively unchanged in temperament. Upon reaching maturity, Struthiomimus are forced to leave their father's harem, but remain part of the wider flock. Male offspring will attempt to attract females and form their own harems, while females will evaluate potential males and choose which harem to join.
