# Kaiwhekea – Offspring Behavior

Kaiwhekea hatchlings, known as calves, hatch fully developed and ready to swim after their mother - but do not do so until they reach Juvenile. Calves are born in shallow water - either along the coastline, or in shallow pools on coastal islands. From birth they watch their mother leave the tide pools to rejoin with her bale in deeper water, and wait behind in the shallows for safety. After reaching juvenile, they make the stretch across into deeper water to meet their maternal bale. Calves are precocial, ready to survive on their own and hunt small fish. However, calves do not begin hunting until sub-adult, and solely live on fish or shared kills from their bale until then. Despite this, calves do not rely on their mothers for food, and chase around fish and provide for themselves wherever possible. No calf will turn down the opportunity to share in their mother's kill, though! Calves enjoy play fighting with other calves within the bale.
