# Yutyrannus – Orphaned Behavior (Wild Spawn)

Orphaned kits often hatch alone and abandoned, with no mother to reassure them or clean them. As a result of this stressful experience and having no one to care for them, these kits are at the most risk of developing Mange, a feather and skin disease. This is detailed in General Behavior. Orphans must be migratory much earlier than their nested counterparts, and are much quieter. They move quickly and quietly, preferring to stay within hiding places, especially if they hear other footsteps of anything larger than them. Being entirely alone is incredibly stressful for a kit, and has impacts on them socially and physically. Kits who grow up orphaned may need to observe other toms in order to understand how to perform in their adulthood, and may not understand how to sing. Mollies tend to be much shyer, fleeing from flirtation rather than approaching or initiating it. Yutyrannus are heavily plumed tyrannosaurids who roam the entirety of Gondwa. They prefer the rainy season for its more temperate or cold temperatures, and love the rain, stepping out into it and shaking their heads upwards. In contrast, Yutyrannus prefer to stay inside dark and dim dens during the dry season, wanting to avoid the heat as much as possible. Yutyrannus will select dens that they are protective of, refusing to allow other predators, including other Yutyrannus, close. In the dry season, in order to avoid as much of the cruel heat as possible, Yutyrannus become nocturnal hunters-- but may take part in all other behaviors during the day, including challenging for bodies. Yutyrannus often rub themselves up against other Yutyrannus, favored dens, nesting sites and favorite objects to mark them as “theirs.” This is most frequent between mated pairs, siblings, and parents and their offspring. Yutyrannus curl up with other Yutyrannus to preserve warmth in the rainy season, but keep space from each other in the dry season. OPTIONAL: In the transition from rainy to dry season, Yutyrannus shift from more colorful and bright pelts that are more alluring to potential mates and better match their environment, to pelts that are duller in hue and more monotone, to assist with managing their temperature and camouflage in the drier environment. Yutyrannus will lay in shade often, particularly along cliffsides that they may use to chase prey or escape predators. Yutyrannus are very playful with other Yutyrannus, chasing one another’s tails or their own, hiding, and pouncing. They are very vocal, constantly calling or singing to one another.
