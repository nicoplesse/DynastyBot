# Psittacosaurus – Ceratopsians

As the friendliest known member of the ceratopsian family, Psittacosaurus show a strong fondness for other ceratopsian species, and actively seek out their company. Most ceratopsians readily tolerate - and even enjoy - the presence of Psittacosaurus, accepting them as helpful, sentry-like companions. The main exception is male Eotriceratops, who refuse to actively defend them. As a result, Psittacosaurus tend to keep a respectful distance from adult males of this species. Psittacosaurus excitedly run around Styracosaurus, Albertaceratops, Pachyrhinosaurus and female and young Eotriceratops. These species are viewed as ideal protective companions. Should a passel have at least 8 members, Psittacosaurus will greet other ceratopsians - but prefer not to mingle too closely. They typically trail after the herd without integrating too closely into it, choosing to share space at more of a comfortable distance. As the smallest known Ceratopsian, Psittacosaurus chooses evasion over confrontation with most predators. Psittacosaurus always flee predators that are Large tier or higher. Psittacosaurus flee from Medium tier predators if there are 2 or more present. If confronted by a single Medium predator, or predators classified as Tiny or Small, the passel may choose to mob the threat. In these situations, Psittacosaurus rely heavily on their tails, inflicting venom and bleed to deter attackers. When retreating, Psittacosaurus prioritise burrowing underground and moving toward shelter. Individuals that are not yet capable of burrowing instead seek dense foliage, using concealment to avoid detection. Once hidden, Psittacosaurus remain remarkably still, appearing statuesque - only regrouping once the threat has fully passed. If a predator approaches while Psittacosaurus are travelling with larger ceratopsians, they instinctively leap toward and cling to the nearest larger individual for protection. In the presence of larger ceratopsian companions, Psittacosaurus may not flee as quickly, drawing on the confidence from the protection these species provide. However, if their larger companions show signs of fear or begin to retreat, Psittacosaurus follow suit without hesitation - or cling tightly to the sides of these larger species as the herd moves.
