# Psittacosaurus – Downtime Behavior

Psittacosaurus prefer to sleep in dense foliage, where they can huddle together under the branches and avoid being noticed by passing species. Psittacosaurus take a long time to settle down for sleep, with their focus being diverted to bonding and grooming other members of the passel, thoroughly cleaning their tails of accumulated dirt to ensure a comfortable sleep. During this period, the Quillguard, sometimes accompanied by another Psittacosaurus, take a short scout of the area, ensuring no dangers are lurking within reach of them. Upon their return,The Quillguard begins encouraging each individual to go to sleep one by one, with soothing calls and gentle nudges. Only once the whole passel has been lulled to sleep does the Quillguard settle down too. Psittacosaurus within ceratopsian herds act similarly, ensuring their companions are clean and comfortable before sleeping themselves. Within a passel exists a loose but respected hierarchy, led by a single individual known as the Quillguard. The Quillguard is typically the eldest member of the group, and is responsible for guiding the passel safely along migratory routes, as well as ensuring reliable access to food and water sources. Conflict within the passel is rare. However, when discipline is required, it is the Quillguard who intervenes, using presence and authority rather than overt aggression to correct behaviors. As the Quillguard is usually the elder of the group, they are afforded additional respect and care by their passel members. Other members may present them with choice shellfish, well cleaned roots, or rare foraged items as a gesture of loyalty and gratitude. During migration, Psittacosaurus move with a uniformed, ‘flock-like’ mentality. The passel travels as a single unit, reducing the risk of individuals becoming separated or targeted by predators. Each member of a passel listens acutely to one another. Should one member of the group screech an alarm call, all others react by burrowing or seeking shelter. Only when the Quillguard has signalled that it is safe will the group re-emerge from their hiding spots. Social bonds within a passel are reinforced through soft chirping calls and gentle, full body shaking displays. Care is taken to avoid striking another Psittacosaurus' eyes or face during these interactions. Younger or newly integrated members often mimic these bonding behaviors, learning the passel's social cues through observation and participation.
