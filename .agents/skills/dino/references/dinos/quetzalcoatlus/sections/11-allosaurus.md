# Quetzalcoatlus – Allosaurus

While Quetzalcoatlus expend no effort to actively assist Allosaurus with hunts as they occur, nor to lead them to potential prey sources, Quetzalcoatlus reap the benefits of stalking herbivore herds that Allosaurus hunt. Following the signals that Quetzalcoatlus naturally give while stalking, Allosaurus whom successfully hunt willlingly share their prey with Quetzalcoatlus, who take the opportunity to freely scavenge from their corpses. Allosaurus are, in return, not hunted by Quetzalcoatlus, even when in large groups. However, a Quetzalcoatlus may scavenge from or challenge for an Allosaurus body if found. If unable to third party or bully another carnivore off of their body, Quetzalcoatlus will hunt offspring below adolescent and deathscarred individuals first, then albino large or albino apex tier creatures when 2+ Quetzalcoatlus are present, then healthy mediums or healthy larges when 2+ Quetzalcoatlus are present. Quetzalcoatlus will never hunt a non-albino adult apex. Quetzalcoatlus are endurance hunters when solo, and co-ordinated when in a committee. However, they typically prefer to be passive hunters and are more likely to seek the easiest option. Quetzalcoatlus will always seek out the ill, injured, elderly, or young to hunt. If desperate, Quetzalcoatlus may hunt and cannibalize their young. Quetzalcoatlus may group with other Quetzalcoatlus to bully carnivores off of their corpses, but will also choose to do so independently if they think that they can feasibly do so. If a hunt proves to be too difficult (if the prey is fighting back too hard, or there's no direct in-routes, etc), Quetzalcoatlus will give up the hunt in order to seek something easier. At the completion of a hunt, if other Quetzalcoatlus are involved, they may briefly peck and squawk at each other to determine who gets the favored portions of the corpse. Repeated bickering at corpses will lead the committee to eventually break apart.
