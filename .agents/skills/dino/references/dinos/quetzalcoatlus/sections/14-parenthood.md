# Quetzalcoatlus – Parenthood

Parents are not affectionate with their chicks, solely feeding them and ensuring that they are not challenged by any predators. They will often watch their chicks squabble, in order to see who is the strongest and most worthy of raising. Father Quetzalcoatlus may leave the nest once chicks have reached adolescence, in search of other females to court and more hunting opportunities. Mother Quetzalcoatlus will remain until her children reach sub-adult; at which point, she will feed the chicks once more before then taking to the skies again. Quetzalcoatlus tend to their chicks by tossing them chunks of meat and watching them fight each other for who gets to eat first. If a chick is ill or not growing quickly, the mother may toss the chick out of the nest to avoid getting the other chicks ill, or having to feed a chick that wouldn’t survive anyway. Melanistic and Albino chicks are often rejected, and shoved from the nest. If a parent Quetzalcoatlus is starving (25% hunger or lower) they may choose to cannibalize their chicks. Weak, ill, or sickly chicks may be tossed from their nest, in favor of ensuring that the sickness does not spread and that the strongest may survive. Male-Female pairs, or solo males/females will not adopt offspring, and will often instead kill them to feed to their own offspring, or to eat themselves.
