# Megalania – Downtime Behavior

Megalania are very lazy, and spend a lot of time either cooling down in shade, warming up in the sun, or floating in water. Their favorite spots to sunbathe are rocks, and they will often choose dark, muddy spots underneath or beside the roots of trees to cool down. Megalania most often rest immediately after a strenuous hunt, eating, or during highest and lowest temperature periods during the day. This allows them to regulate their body temperature and also digest their food. Megalania who are disrupted from their rest may respond with aggression or a venomous bite, at which point they will quickly rush away if not interested in hunting the species. Megalania are solitary, but not necessarily aggressive towards others of the same variant as them. While they do not form structured groups, individuals of the same variant tolerate one another's presence, especially in basking zones, or when prey sources are abundant. Temperate Megalania are more socially relaxed in comparison to Arid variants. Though they remain solitary, they are more tolerant of other adults settling beside them at rest spots. Two or three can be commonly spotted resting together by river banks, flicking their tongues lazily. Adults that rest together do not group together in game. Arid Megalania, by contrast, are more defensive of their personal space. While they do not hunt others of their variant, they demand a wider berth from others, hissing their annoyance if other adults get too close. They form no social bonds, even between clutchmates or parents and offspring, but may show more tolerance to particular individuals. Those who have been in each other's presence for a long time may grow more accustomed to one another. Nesting pairs form a temporary alliance until offspring hatch.
