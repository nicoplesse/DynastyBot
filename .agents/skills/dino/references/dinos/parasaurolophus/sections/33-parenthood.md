# Parasaurolophus – Parenthood

Mothers are attentive and remain close by to their newly hatched offspring while they recover from the arduous task of hatching. Fawns remain quiet and spend most of their time sleeping, gathering the strength to get to their feet and begin migrating with the rest of the herd. Fathers stand guard near their chosen does or harems, patrolling the nesting ground to keep watch for potential threats at all times of day. Sometimes, stags stand watch over the nest, should the doe need to leave for a brief moment for food and water. During these early stages, does pick berries from bushes and bring them to their offspring, setting them down close to the fawns and encouraging them to eat. A doe is willing to leave her fawns temporarily to graze or drink water, but always keeps an eye on her offspring, even if from a distance. She relies on the solace of other females within her harem or her family to ensure that her fawns are well protected and cared for in her absence. New mothers are especially anxious and are quite often found sticking their heads into the nest frequently to check on her young. These females are affectionately corrected by those around her, instructed instead to remain close by and settle - generally within a bodylength. As soon as the youngest fawn in the herd reaches Juvenile, mothers and fathers are anxious to move and migrate, fondly nuzzling their offspring to their feet and leading them on their first migration. While the entire herd takes part in baby rearing, fondly teaching them boundaries and the way hierarchy within the herd works - as well as gently correcting behaviors - mothers and fathers always stay close to their offspring.
