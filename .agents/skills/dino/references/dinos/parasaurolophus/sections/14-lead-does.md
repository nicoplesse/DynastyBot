# Parasaurolophus – Lead Does

The role of lead doe, unlike lead stag, is a fluid role that changes based on the social dynamic of the herd. Lead doe(s) are typically does who are part of the lead stag's harem. If these individuals are inexperienced or show unwillingness to step into this position, a more experienced doe may step up. If the lead stag has no does within his harem present or has yet to bring does into his harem, an experienced doe, regardless of relation to him, will step into this role. These does select nesting locations alongside their Lead Stag. They may reject the stag’s chosen site if it does not meet their standards and guide him and the herd to a more preferred area. When the herd is approached by newcomers, lead does are the first to step forward and accept them into the herd. Should an individual begin to act over-aggressively, the lead does are the first to step in and correct. This is done with the support of the herd as a whole - should the aggression continue they collectively will shun the individual. These does may offer corrections to unruly fawns if their mother is unable or unwilling to do so, aiding in support to newer or overwhelmed mothers. While the lead stag typically guides the herd, the lead doe(s) offer support and can disagree with his chosen route. They are the first to stop the migration and attempt to redirect the lead stag. Disapproval is displayed by stepping in front of the stag and halting him with harsh stares ( :looking: ). Should he choose to ignore the lead doe(s), he puts his relationship with his harem at potential risk. Should multiple lead does disagree on the new direction the herd takes, the herd will follow the route which the majority of the lead does decide, though if it is an even split, the stag must make a decision. Lead does may still guide the herd during migration.
