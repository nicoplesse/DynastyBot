# Parasaurolophus – Correcting Over-Aggression

Over-Aggression is extremely uncommon. Parasaurolophus do not aggressive call each other other than when in a rut or correcting other’s over-aggressive behaviors. Parasaurolophus do not attack each other with flails or stomps unless in a rut. Persistent aggression is dealt with swiftly within the herd - and is corrected once herd members notice the behavior, with individuals stepping forward to form a barrier. Correction begins with sneezing, followed by aggressive calls and body blocking to disrupt the aggressive behavior. If the behavior continues, firm nips get aimed at the aggressor. Other members of the herd quickly rally to help, moving forward and calling aggressively to push the offender away. Usually, such an escalation is unnecessary - Parasaurolophus are often quick to respond to corrections, not wishing to upset their place in the herd. If the behavior continues despite corrections from the herd, the group will shun the over-aggressive individual for the safety of the herd. Over aggression is never tolerated or defended by other herd mates regardless of relations, and any attempt to defend the aggressive individual will lead to both individuals being shunned from the herd.
