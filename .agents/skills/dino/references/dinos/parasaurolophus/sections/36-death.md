# Parasaurolophus – Death

As they near the end of their lives, Parasaurolophus feel a quiet pull toward places of deep personal significance; favored nesting grounds, migration rests, or the resting places of bonded companions and kin. As their bodies weaken and strength begins to fade, the elder will move toward the front of the herd, nuzzling the lead stag and issuing soft, weary calls to make their intentions known. The lead stag permits them to remain at his side, allowing the elder to guide the herd toward their chosen resting place and set a slower pace, with frequent pauses as needed. Upon arrival, the elder takes great time in chosing their final resting place, selecting the site where they wish to lie. As the herd gathers closely, offering reassuring nuzzles and low calls as they settle around them. The herd remains with the elder until their passing, particularly bonded herdmates, offspring, or lifelong companions, who stay pressed close and tend to them until their final breath. Parasaurolophus form harem bonds that can turn into lifelong bonds, whether they are exclusively between a pair or an entire harem. Parasaurolophus flee from all Apexes, unless they have a nest with hatchlings. In that case, the parents, if within a bodylength of the nest, will defend it, only fleeing from Apex pairs. Parasaurolophus do not drink salt water, except to rid themselves of internal parasites. This is only done in extreme occasions, and only briefly (no longer than 5 seconds).
