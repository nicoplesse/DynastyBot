# Parasaurolophus – Downtime Behaviour

Night is a time for rest and bonding; while the herd does not migrate, court, or move much, there are some activities that are acceptable during these late night hours. Parasaurolophus do still need to get some sleep, but are no longer required to sleep for the entirety of the night. The herd often huddles together, though some may split off to rest within their harems or pairbonds away from the main group, but always close enough for the patrolling stag to watch over them. Parasaurolophus that wish to socialize during downtime may call other herd members over using the :claw_emote:. Those that respond may join them and enter their personal space. From there, they may call softly or gently rub their heads together as a form of grooming, helping reach places the other Parasaur cannot. Fawns younger then Adolescent may play during this time, unable to remain still for prolonged periods of time, they work on developing their social skills and establishing relations with other offspring close to their age. Their play way consist of playing hide and seek around sleeping adults, or chasing each other with quiet calls. Fawns that are overly disruptive may be corrected by herd members they wake up with harsh stares. Fawns that continue to be disruptive and wake various herd members will be escorted back to their mothers by the stag or doe that is currently patrolling. Parasaurolophus may do the following during downtime: Adolescents play under adult supervision while others sit nearby, socializing and watching the fawns. Night patrols with a maximum of 2 stags patrolling.
