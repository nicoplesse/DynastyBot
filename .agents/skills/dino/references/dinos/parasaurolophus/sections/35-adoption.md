# Parasaurolophus – Adoption

Both does and stags may adopt and defend orphaned Parasaurolophus, Lambeosaurus, Iguanodon or Barsboldia they may come across. While stags lack the same maternal instinct as does, they make reliable and attentive caregivers. Same-sex pairs often raise a fawn together, sharing responsibilities and acting as committed surrogate parents. Reaching the elderly stage is a huge milestone within a Parasaurolophus's life. The herd always cherish their elderly herdmates, viewing them as a source of comfort and guidance. The entire herd help care for them as they slow. Old age brings stiff and painful joints, forcing elders to settle and rest more frequently than younger herd mates - slowing the entire mobility of the herd, who are forced to stop and wait with them. Elder Parasaurolophus lose most of their desire to court or nest. Instead, they chose to play more of a nanny-like role, aiding does in raising the new generation, and mentoring young stags. Some elders continue nesting, but experience limited ranges of success. Their nests are often entirely infertile, or only produce one viable egg. As they near the end of their lives, their scales begin to lose their pigment, with colors dulling across their bodies.
