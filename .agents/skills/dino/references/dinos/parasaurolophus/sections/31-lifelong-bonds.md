# Parasaurolophus – Lifelong Bonds

Parasaurolophus, while primiarily harem creatures, sometimes opt to form lifelong bonds with individuals within their harem. They may form multiple lifelong bonds and form a lifelong harem, who they will stay close to and travel with regardless if any of the rest of the herd is present. A lifelong bond within a harem may take the form as one of the following options: Two individuals in a monogamous pairbond; Two individuals who remain together for life, but allow other does to flow in and out of the harem freely; Or, harem of individuals who all opt to stay with one another that remains unchanging. If a doe is continuously wronged, abandoned, or ignored by a stag after forming the lifelong bond, she may mournfully leave the harem. Does who have recently left a lifelong harem enter a short mourning period, where they refuse to nest or court until the mourning period is over. Stags often proudly display for their lifelong bonded does, and bring them food and other gifts to show their constant devotion. It is not mandatory for stags to rut or display in order to impress their lifelong bonded females. Instead, does indicate readiness to nest by flirting with him along with the rest of the herd when courtship begins. When a stag sees another stag attempting to court his lifelong bond, he may intervene and begin a rut or allow the behavior. Should this happen during nighttime, he may block the stag with his body and direct a sneeze in the offending stag's direction, which is respected. Should the new stag win the rut and the doe remains disinterested, she will make her choice clear in affectionately calling her lifelong partner. When a doe sees another doe attempting to court her lifelong bond, she may intervene or allow the behavior. If a doe wishes to intervene, she will place herself between the approaching individual and her lifelong mate, sneezing only once or twice at the courting attempt. These interventions do not result in repetitive sneezing by both individuals and never escalates to aggressive calls. Once the doe makes her displeasure known, the stag may opt to respect this decision and remain with his lifelong or pursue the new courting individual. Should the stag choose the new individual, the doe(s) may opt to leave that stag or allow the lifelong bond to open up and accept the individual. Should the stag deny the individual, he will turn his back to her in polite rejection. If a doe who is attempting to court a lifelong stag is persistent, he will turn his back to her and sneeze sharply at the ground. If the behavior continues, he will backup while facing her, then sneeze towards the space between them. After this, the stag is no longer pursued by the new individual.
