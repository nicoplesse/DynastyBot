# Parasaurolophus – Solitary Behavior

Solitary Parasaurolophus are rare, with almost all individuals instinctively seeking the safety of groups. However, some individuals - most notably stags - travel alone at certain times of the year, especially if recently shunned from a herd, or in search of new bonds. These males remain alert and cautious, using the terrain and coverage to their advantage. Solitary individuals in search of others of their kind are highly vocal when in open ground, hoping to hear their calls echoed back by others. They call to reconnect with familiar herd mates, or find connections in others. They only attempt this when visibility is clear and predators can be spotted in ample time. Long term, unwanted solitude results in heightened wariness and signs of stress in Parasaurolophus. They paw at the ground and appear restless, and frequently travel exclusively on their hind legs.
