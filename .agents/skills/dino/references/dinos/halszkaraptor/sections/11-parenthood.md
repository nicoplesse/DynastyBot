# Halszkaraptor – Parenthood

Once hens have finished courting, they move off to build nests, closely assisted by all other members of the raft, including other, non-nesting hens. All nests are built extremely close together. The eggs and young are cared for communally. Halszkaraptor that have formed a same-sex bond build a nest accordingly, ready to adopt any stray hatchlings that wander over. Hens then move to sit on their nests to incubate their eggs, lovingly tended to by all the nearby drakes. Same-sex pairings sit on their empty nest together, ready and waiting to scoop up orphaned hatchlings. As young hatch, they are greeted with loving coos by their mother, and are preened to clean their wet feathers from the remains of their eggs. Young are raised by the entire raft, but instinctively follow their mother - assuming she was the one who was there when they hatched and imprinted. Offspring are free to wander between all nests in the raft, and receive care from any adult present. Mothers dote primarily on their own young, but can be easily persuaded to keep watch over other nearby offspring as well. Drakes, and non-nesting hens provide loving care and attention to all offspring.
