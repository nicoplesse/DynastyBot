# Halszkaraptor – Downtime Behavior

Halszkaraptor take short, frequent naps throughout the day, most commonly after a long journey or an eventful situation. When settling down to sleep, Halszkaraptor seek out islands or driftwood floating upon the water, areas that are otherwise hard to reach for most predators. They are extremely heavy sleepers as a result, placing great trust in their chosen location. They coop up in a tight group, preening their own and other's feathers and reaffirming bonds with cheerful affectionate calls before settling down to sleep, an action that often takes several attempts due to their excitable nature. Even after silence has fallen, a single call can cause the group to reignite in chatter and preening, causing for the process to restart once more. Halszkaraptor are an affectionate species, frequently using affectionate calls :friendly_call: to reinforce social bonds. When an unknown Halszkaraptor is welcomed into the raft, they are only truly accepted after the group comes across a mud patch and witnesses the new member cleansing themselves with it. This helps reassure others that they are not bringing any feather lice into the group. Particularly close individuals within the raft will groom each other, pruning each other’s feathers. Should a Halszkaraptor become injured, other members of the raft gift precious fruits to the injured Halszkaraptor to eat to enhance their recovery time. Solitary Halszkaraptor Lone Halszkaraptor struggle to survive due to their inherently social nature. Their sole focus is finding a raft to join, or failing that, seeking temporary protection from the much larger Lurdusaurus or Deinocheirus. Solo Halszkaraptor are extremely flighty without the protection of their raft or other species they can group with. Without the safety of a raft, the Halszkaraptor sticks to the shallows, avoiding deep water where predators lurk, and flocking between dense bushes wherever possible. Internal aggression within the raft is virtually nonexistent. The raft’s survival depends on cooperation and therefore, they prioritize nurturing one another above all else. Halszkaraptor do not tolerate members of the raft that consume remains such as meat, carrion or bones. Individuals caught doing so are relentlessly mobbed and shunned. Shunning Within the raft, a member being shunned will experience mobbing. Halszkaraptor circle around the target, nipping at them and hissing to display their disapproval. Following this, they are then ignored. Raft members turn their backs, and refuse to allow them to settle beside them during downtimes. Over time, the shunned individual is slowly forgiven, and begrudgingly accepted back into raft life. Halszkaraptor are a highly curious species, and chirp and honk about most species they encounter. Halszkaraptor are fond of Lurdusaurus. The semi-aquatic herbivore tolerates Halszkaraptor in their presence. Lurdusaurus help to ward off predators, while Halszkaraptor serve as excellent sentries. Whilst searching for food, Halszkaraptor also provide for Lurdusaurus within their cross-species group. They can frequently be seen ferrying bundles of berries or reeds to their larger companions. Halszkaraptor also share a strong relationship with Deinocheirus. They enjoy the calming presence of these much larger herbivores, and can be frequently seen cuddling close to them, preening through their feathers for insects. Predator Reaction: Flighty Halszkaraptor are a flighty species and when under attack, individuals disperse in various directions - heading for water, land or rocky terrain -  whichever the predator struggles to navigate. Halszkaraptor does not necessarily flee until danger is known. Being as quick and agile as they are, they are capable of getting unnervingly close to predators without risking injury. This is often seen with Spinosaurus. Though Spinosaurus are highly territorial and do not tolerate other species in their claimed water sources, Halszkarapator seems to be forgiven in this instance - likely as Spinosaurus rarely if ever manage to catch them. Halszkaraptor aim for underwater plants or overground bushes that provide shelter. Where possible, they also enter small caves that are out of reach of larger predators. Halszkaraptor are able to defend themselves against Tiny tier carnivores such as Rhamphorhynchus or Compsognathus, and may do so in unlimited numbers. Halszkaraptor defend themselves in a mobbing style, erratically attacking the predator as a coordinated effort. This is extended to Small tier carnivores if a pair are nesting within the raft. In water, Halszkaraptor are a small yet rich meal for Sarcosuchus. As a result, Halszkaraptor never approach Sarcosuchus willingly. Even hatchling Halszkaraptor avoid them and do not imprint on these fierce crocodilians. Halszkaraptor feel comfortable sharing their space with Sub-Adult and Adult Deinosuchus, an Apex crocodilian that does not bother to hunt such a small agile creature. However, hatchlings never imprint on Deinosuchus. While on land, Halszkaraptor are particularly wary of Deinonychus. They are one of the few predators that are able to match the agility and speed of the Halszkaraptor, often working together in large dynasties to make light work of them. Survival is a daily struggle, so Halszkaraptor breed often. Nest building is a communal activity where each member of the raft contributes, further strengthening the raft’s bonds. In a raft, courtship is a communal affair, often with multiple females becoming ready to court and breed at the same time. Their promiscuous strategy means a female will court and nest with several unrelated males at a time. As a result, paternity is rarely known, and the entire raft shares the duty of raising the young.
