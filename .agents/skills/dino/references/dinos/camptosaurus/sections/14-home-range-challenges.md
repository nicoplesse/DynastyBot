# Camptosaurus – Home Range Challenges

Home range challenges and defences are a common occurrence within colonies, given their solitary and territorial nature towards each other. Camptosaurus looking to take-over another home range will intrude into it within the owner’s sights. If the owner isn’t around, the intruding Camptosaurus will wait for their return to the home range. Camptosaurus who spot an intruder within their home range show immediate disapproval, stretching and calling aggressively. They approach the intruder with slow steps, assessing their intention and status. If the intruder shows no signs of respecting their territory, and has yet to challenge, the residing Camptosaurus will broadcast and issue 3 aggressive calls, officially beginning a challenge for the home range. These fights are done head-on, with both Camptosaurus rushing forward and attacking their opponent’s face and neck with vicious bites. Fights may continue until half health, or until one side forfeits. Forfeiting is done by hopping away from the other, and fear-calling in defeat. The winning Camptosaurus claims the home range, and cannot be rechallenged by the same Camptosaurus for 30 minutes. The winning Camptosaurus may freely leave their home range, as long as they do not challenge for another territory within 30 minutes. Any Camptosaurus that turns their back on their opponent - or attempts to reposition by running away - automatically forfeit, as these are seen as signs of submission and defeat. When travelling with other species, a Camptosaurus may choose to conform to a more diurnal routine. The process can take days to truly come into effect, with the Camptosaurus becoming lethargic during this period of change. If the Camptosaurus returns to a Camptosaurus only group, they will naturally return to a nocturnal lifestyle. Camptosaurus tolerate other species within their colony ground, and do not defend home ranges from them, understanding any intruders are only there temporarily. However, they may still defend a home range against other species if they have live young within the nest, at their own risk. Camptosaurus do not challenge or accept challenges from other species for food and water, even when resources are limited. They will immediately forfeit and wait for the species to move along before returning to assess the damage. If another species passes through a colony ground, and eliminates all resources within the area, a colony can make the united decision in moving to a new colony ground, in a migration led by the dominant males. Camptosaurus follow the crowd in these circumstances, and will rarely stay behind if a majority of the colony choose to migrate. Camptosaurus who have yet to settle into a colony may choose to follow herds of herbivores instead. However, they treat them with the same amount of tolerance as they would with other Camptosaurus. Camptosaurus in particular prefer herding with Iguanodon, Lambeosaurus and Parasaurolophus. These hadrosaurs often attract the most attention, due to their large size, giving Camptosaurus the chance to flee danger when they are not the focus of a hunt. In addition, these species do alert each other of danger, a trait Camptosaurus do not share with them. Camptosaurus make the most of these warnings, instantly rising to their feet to observe their surroundings. Camptosaurus never flee a situation without confirming the danger themselves, even if alerted by other species. Camptosaurus interact minimally with these species, even when grouped with them. They tolerate them within the vicinity, but are quick to move away if their personal space is invaded or if one becomes overly friendly with them. Some Camptosaurus may still express instinctual territorial behaviours around their sleeping spots during this time too, and may go as far as aggressing another species for it. However, Camptosaurus  will back down if the aggression is returned, their disdain forming into a harmless grudge over time. Camptosaurus do not alert to threats, or defend other species under any circumstances. Camptosaurus do not adopt babies of a different species, and will ignore the presence of any that come by. Camptosaurus may accept grooming efforts from all species, at their own risk and preference. Camptosaurus fear Large+ Carnivores, and Achillobator, and always flee from these predators. Camptosaurus are an incredibly observant species, and take their time to analyze a situation before reacting to it. A displaced sound, or an unfamiliar scent, is enough reason for a Camptosaurus to become alert, with their first reaction being to rise to their hind legs and scan their surroundings. They sniff the air and listen out for further noise, their bodies tense and ready to bolt at the first sign of movement. Camptosaurus come with a fundamental flaw, in which their sight is entirely reliant on motion. A stationary, well camouflaged threat will go completely unnoticed, leaving Camptosaurus vulnerable to ambushing predators. Achillobator are treated with extreme caution for this reason, regarded as one of the species that are able to quickly seek out and exploit a Camptosaurus disadvantage.Their common alliance with the nimble Latenavenatrix leaves Camptosaurus in a great disadvantage, given their ambushing hunting strategy. If a threat isn’t found, or otherwise deemed as harmless, a Camptosaurus will opt to ignore it, but stay on alert for further changes in the environment. If attacked, or spooked by something, Camptosaurus first reaction is to flee, in hopes of outpacing the danger. They may instinctively yelp out in pain when attacked, but do not otherwise alert to danger, focused on a silent escape. Camptosaurus hope to achieve increased survival chances through this method, by drawing less attention to themselves, and often succeeding, with predators setting their sights on an unsuspecting target instead. If a Camptosaurus opts to defend itself instead of fleeing, sometimes seen when encountering small numbers of tiny and small tier, they will receive no defenders in this. This includes offspring, who will not be protected by their mothers regardless of circumstances. Offspring are expected to flee at the first sign of danger. The only exception to this is a father defending a female who is actively raising his young. The father will only defend the female and ignore his offspring during the engagement. Offspring must be already hatched, and below Sub-Adult for this exception to be valid. Males do not extend this protective behavior to Large+ carnivores or Achillobator, and will still flee from them.
