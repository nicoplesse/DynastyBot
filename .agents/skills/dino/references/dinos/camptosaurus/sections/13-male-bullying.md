# Camptosaurus – Male Bullying

Dominant males may bully other males to maintain their position in the hierarchy. These acts come about for a variety of reasons, with many being ultimately unimportant and petty. Bullying other males for eating before them, for being too close to a female, or for sunbathing in a favored spot are all common occurrences, though a Camptosaurus may be bullied for any reason, as long as the intent is clear. Bullying is done by shoving, getting in the personal space of, or nipping at another male to provoke them. Subordinate males typically react to bullying by submitting and moving away from whatever has upset a dominant male. Subordinate males who frequently submit are far less likely to be picked on by dominant males over time. Dominant males - and some subordinate males - may opt to react with aggression, shoving and nipping to showcase their refusal to back down. This reaction causes the bullying to escalate into a fight. These fights end at half health, and both Camptosaurus respect the challenge’s outcome, regardless of if they’re the victor or loser.
