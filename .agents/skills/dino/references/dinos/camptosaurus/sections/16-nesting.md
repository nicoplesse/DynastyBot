# Camptosaurus – Nesting

Females retreat to their home range after courtship and begin securing her territory for offspring. They stock the surrounding area, and especially the nest, with strong-smelling flowers, mushrooms, and foliage; a process that helps mask the smell of newborn offspring and avoid unnecessary attention from lurking predators. The male will assist during this time by stockpiling the nest with food for the offspring, bringing back heaps of berries and other soft foods that are easy to consume. This task can be extremely daunting for a Camptosaurus who has courted multiple females. Failing to support a female during this time can result in this female rejecting him next season, or if she feels particularly uncared for, she may hold a grudge for life. As such, males work tirelessly to ensure all courted females are satisfied. Past this brief period of collaboration, courted individuals do not interact with each other more than necessary. Once the male has completed his role, the two do not linger near each other, and the male will no longer be tolerated in the female’s home range. Males largely return to ensuring the male hierarchy has not transformed in his absence. He may still watch over his courted female from a distance, ready to defend her from predators if needed, but show little interest in her and her offspring aside from this.
