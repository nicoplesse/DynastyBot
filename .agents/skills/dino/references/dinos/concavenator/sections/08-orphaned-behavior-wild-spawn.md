# Concavenator – Orphaned Behavior (Wild Spawn)

Kits that lose their parents at a young age or are separated from their nesting site  face a grim and difficult upbringing. These orphaned Concavenator are extremely cautious, often avoiding contact with other creatures entirely - including their own kind. Loud noises and unfamiliar scents send them into hiding and they are rarely spotted far from cover. Orphaned kits instinctively gravitate toward swampy and marshy terrain. Shallow water edges become their refuge, and they learn quickly to vanish beneath the surface at the first sign of danger. Many survive purely by stealth and escape, relying on their swimming skills and an uncanny ability to remain still and hidden. When alone and desperate, orphaned kits approach the nests of others - especially if they detect the warmth or scent of eggs or hatchlings. In rare moments of desperation, they have been observed crawling into the nests of unrelated Concavenator or even other species, hoping for food or warmth. If no others are present, they may simply collapse in exhaustion within the safety of the nest site. As they grow, these orphans adopt a life of solitary survival. They scavenge when they can, avoid confrontations, and rely heavily on ambush tactics. Kits raised entirely on their own tend to grow into fiercely independent adults. Those grown on their own are especially possessive over food and resources. They are quick to drive off any creature - even of their own kind - that comes too close during a meal. Their distrustful nature and lack of early socialization also hinder them during courtship, as they often struggle to read signals of other Concavenator or respond appropriately. Compared to individuals raised in social groups, these orphans are less tolerant and far more guarded in every interaction. Concavenator are playful yet highly possessive creatures, often seen investigating their surroundings with curiosity, but quick to guard what they consider theirs. These can range from trinkets, food and basking spots, which are treated as valuable resources. While they may frolic or nudge each other in bouts of social play, they are not inclined to share once something is claimed. Concavenator return frequently to familiar water sources - not only to cool down, but also to forage, hydrate, and escape danger. While they do tend to wander inland most of the time, they still return to water frequently. Gangs of Concavenator often linger in areas scattered with bones, shells or other small items - and subtly mark these spaces with their scent and physical presence. These trinket zones can act as temporary hang-out spots for gangs, who may revisit them seasonally. Concavenator are alert, nimble, and tactile creatures. They are neither excessively loud nor silent, vocalizing mainly with low growls, huffs, or curious chirps when communicating within their group. Concavenator are flighty when alone, but grow bolder in numbers. There is no strict social hierarchy for Concavenator, but the most assertive or experienced individuals often take the lead in decisions regarding movement or feeding. During rest periods, dominant Concavenator position themselves in elevated or central spots, keeping watch while others sleep nearby. Vigilance is a shared responsibility, with lookout duties shifting between gang members throughout the day. While not naturally confrontational, Concavenator are acutely aware of their surroundings and quick to respond to unusual scents, sounds or disruptions in their environment.
