# Concavenator – Adoption

In instances where multiple Concavenator are nesting at the same time, orphaned kits seeking companionship will sneak into an unoccupied nest - often one belonging to a same-sex pair. These nests are typically empty, reducing the risk of being bullied by already hatched young. The orphan is then showered with attention from the pair, who are usually very excited to care for a hatchling of their own. In other cases, orphans may enter nests that already contain kits. Reactions vary: some hatchlings may bully and drive the newcomer away, while others may accept it as just another sibling. When the orphan blends in well with the existing clutch (especially if they are of similar size and age) - parents are likely to accept and raise it without issue. However, if the orphan stands out due to its size or coloration, the adults may reject it, leaving it for another pair to deal with. In rare instances, aggressive or inexperienced parents might go as far as cannibalizing the kit if they feel their brood is threatened by its presence. As Concavenator age, their skin starts to pale and their once migratory nature slips into dull indifference. Replaced is the desire to claim what is not theirs and elders will readily relocate to the nesting grounds in search of nests with valuable trinkets. Approaching a perched parent, the elder watches from a distance, waiting for the opportunity to claim the trinket pile when both parents have moved a couple body lengths away. When the moment opens, the elder will sit on the nest and aggress any Concavenator that approaches - even going as far as shooing away the resident hatchlings out the nest. If no nest is around, an elder Concavenator will steal piles of trinkets from other Concavenator, much in the same fashion. This approach is effortless as the younger generations do all the gathering work for them. If paired with a mate, both will work together to guard the pile from its original owner. Oftentimes this clever theft strategy results in elders being pushed out of gangs regularly - but a brief exile does not deter the elder and they still follow from some distance away. Persistent and annoying, almost every elder has many personal rivals.
