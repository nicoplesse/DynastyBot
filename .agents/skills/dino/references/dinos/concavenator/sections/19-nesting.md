# Concavenator – Nesting

When ready to nest, all Concavenator pairs travel to Sharptooth Marsh - a universal Concavenator nesting grounds. Gangs remain together even when nesting and only tolerate the proximity of others during this instinctual nesting season. Each individual pair spaces their nest a minimum of 5 body lengths away from other pairs. They guard their nests fiercely from other adults coming too close. The female initiates the nesting process, but both partners contribute to clearing the area, arranging materials, and guarding the space. In same-sex pairings, nest roles are flexible, and duties are either shared or alternated depending on the dynamic. Though males do not lay eggs, they remain close during this period, guarding the perimeter and bringing gifts or food to the brooding partner. In same-sex pairs, both individuals may take turns lying atop the nest or performing guarding duties. Once eggs are laid, the nesting partner becomes significantly more territorial. And are more reactive to intrusions - even from fellow group members - and chase away any other Concavenator who are not their mate or current hatchlings. This aggression fades slightly after their brood hatches, though protective instincts remain high.
