# Concavenator – Parenthood

Kits are quite loud when first hatching out of their eggs. They require immediate attention and grooming from their parent pair and will only calm down once preened by them. Hatchlings spend their first few hours seeking out warmth around them letting out soft, reassuring yelps every now and then for their parents to notice. Usually one parent stays close enough to the nest to make sure their kits don’t scatter away too far, as they tend to become more active nearing Juvenile. Sickly kits, lying motionless in the nest or unable to eat, do not receive extra care or attention from their parents or siblings, and may be left behind when the family starts moving areas. Sick kits are pushed from the group and forced to survive without the protection of a gang. Once kits reach Adolescent, the gang reconnect and leave Sharptooth Marsh to continue on their regular pathings. Parents allow their offspring to remain close to them until Sub-Adult. Despite their selfish tendencies, elder pairs who have nested together over years of courtship seasons show distress when their offspring leave or outgrow the gang. Separation or loss is deeply distressing and may lead to temporary wandering, aggression or obsessive hoarding behavior in the aftermath. Both parents partake in caring for their kits. They feed and play with their offspring often, protect them from danger, and teach them their gang's typical migration patterns. They may also gift them lesser items if they are especially fond of a particular kit. While kits rarely misbehave toward their parents, they often nip at unrelated individuals, provoking irritable behaviour and testing their boundaries. This behavior serves as a crucial learning process; kits that receive frequent reprimands generally develop a better understanding of Concavenator social dynamics compared to those who are shy, reserved, and avoid playful interactions. Attentive parents are more likely to be chosen again as mates by the same courtship partner in the future, though this is not always guaranteed. Parents do not help other pairs with raising their offspring. They allow their kits to engage with one another after Juvenile, but do permit adults nearby when young are not yet weaned from the nest.
