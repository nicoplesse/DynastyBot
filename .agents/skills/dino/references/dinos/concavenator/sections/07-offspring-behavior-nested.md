# Concavenator – Offspring Behavior (Nested)

Concavenator offspring, known as kits, hatch in secure nests built near water at the communal nesting grounds in Sharptooth Marsh. These nests are often hidden in reed beds or placed in shallow, swampy embankments. Kits remain in close proximity to their parents, relying heavily on them for both food and protection. Shortly after hatching, kits imprint on the adult who feeds them first. This figure becomes the center of their world, and they follow this parent closely, chirping loudly if left behind or to signify hunger, but otherwise remain relatively quiet to avoid drawing attention from other predators. Throughout their development, young Concavenator remain deferential to their elders. They avoid challenging larger or older individuals directly, instead learning when to withdraw, submit, or display appeasement to avoid harm. As juveniles, kits become noticeably more active and adventurous. They engage in gentle wrestling and short bursts of chasing, mostly with siblings or nearby juveniles. They often splash through shallow water, testing their balance and beginning to mimic adult hunting behaviours, including short lunges or mock pounces. Play at this stage is frequent and important for developing physical coordination and social boundaries. Juvenile Concavenator are inquisitive but cautious. They avoid direct confrontations with adults or strangers, submitting instantly with chirps, lowered heads, and flattened postures if confronted. As kits grow into adolescence, their behavior shifts. Curiosity gives way to skill development, and they begin shadowing adults on short scouting runs, or clumsily attempting to stalk and pounce on small prey. Many kits at this stage practice their swimming and diving abilities independently, taking joy in perfecting the underwater lunges that will one day define their hunting style. While playful and bold among their peers, kits are wary in mixed-age social settings. The larger kits demand respect from those younger than them, and the once wary attitude starts to fade as they reach adulthood themselves. As they mature into Sub-Adult, kits eventually leave the group - driven by instinct to find new connections, or pushed out by space and resource limitations. These individuals rarely maintain emotional bonds with the family they left behind. Upon dispersal, many form loose gangs with other Sub-Adults, learning from each other and developing a tolerance-based hierarchy. They are prone to hoarding behaviors and have low tolerance for sharing food or close social proximity with others, a habit often retained into adulthood. If not pushed out of the group, some Sub-Adults may linger if food is abundant and the family is tolerant. Those who stay behind assist in hunting or helping guard the nesting area. If permitted, they may attempt to rebuild or strengthen ties with parents or older siblings - offering caught food or interesting objects to remain within their good graces.
