# Concavenator – Gangs

Concavenator are highly social but intensely possessive individuals. They typically form loosely structured groups that fluctuate over time. There is no fixed hierarchy, though some members naturally display more dominant or submissive traits. These groups, called gangs, often form from strangers, parents, siblings or past nestmates, and while stable for stretches of time, individuals may wander off to explore or seek new companions. They sometimes return later with little fuss, if there is room for them. Concavenator do not share food. During feeding, each individual eats their fill, quickly tearing through kills. While food can be chunked for faster consumption, those who eat the fastest benefit most. Slower eaters are often forced to join a second hunt or scavenge alone. The only exception is during courtship, when a male may offer food to a potential mate or a mother may share with her young below adolescence. Concavenator are natural hoarders. They obsessively collect bones, trophies and any trinkets that appease the individual - often carrying them around for long periods of time or stashing them in hidden nests or secluded corners of their range. Some are happy with just one possession, while others collect multiple at a time. These collections are deeply personal, and individuals form powerful emotional attachments to them. If an item is lost or stolen, the owner may display visible distress, returning to the last known location repeatedly or obsessively searching nearby terrain. For this reason, Concavenator do not share items, and may react aggressively if another member of the group touches or takes something from their hoard - unless it is part of a courting gesture. Receiving a gift from a fellow Concavenator (especially one who is not family) is a rare and meaningful gesture, often signaling courtship or a powerful bond of trust. These gifts are not given lightly and may involve parting with a prized possession, making them incredibly significant.
