# Apatosaurus – Rhamphorhynchus

Alongside Citipati & Struthiomimus, Rhamphorhynchus is another species held in high regard by Apatosaurus. Favored for their grooming rituals and ability to pick off pesky parasites, Apatosaurus welcomes the presence of these tiny pterosaurs and happily permits them to latch and perch on their bodies during both migrations and rest stops. Individuals may subtly adjust their posture to accommodate them by lowering their heads, turning their vast bodies to the side and even settling onto the ground to provide easier access for grooming. Calves are especially fascinated by Rhamphorhynchus, and often watch them closely while attempting to follow their movements through the undergrowth. Struthiomimus have a natural resentment for Rhamphorhynchus, and often attempt to attack them if they are not latched onto the sides of an Apatosaurus companion. If aggressive calls or attack moves are issued towards a Rhamphorhynchus within the line of sight of an Apatosaurus by a Struthiomimus, the Apatosaurus may rush forward at the Struthiomimus with a crack of its whiplike tail, often startling the small sentry into completely avoiding Rhamphorhynchus in the future. If the Struthiomimus persists, it may result in the sentry being entirely ousted from the herd with rumbling aggressive calls. Apatosaurus are robust and not necessarily flighty. They are not bothered by many small predators but keep a watchful eye, and are quick to chase away any carnivores that begin posing a nuisance. Larger carnivorous species (4+ Medium, or any number of Large and above) that appear to be moving - or lingering - too close to a pod are met with watchful tracking, typically instigated by bulls who move to the outer side of the pod and greater herd. In the case of two Apex Carnivores, two bulls rush to the forefront to act as the primary defenders of the greater herd. If an individual within a pod is attacked, another individual from the same pod, or their mate, may be their second defender.
