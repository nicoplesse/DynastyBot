# Torvosaurus – Coalitions or multiple ungrouped males meeting females

Lone females or sisterhoods discovered by multiple males - whether in coalitions or ungrouped - provoke immediate and intense competition among the males. Even brothers or those with slightly stronger bonds find themselves driven to aggression when the potential of securing females arises, and coalitions quickly un-group to prepare for battle. Unwithstand bites from females or present them with critter gifts. Instead, they impress females by driving out rival males. Females observe silently as males engage in tense standoffs, sizing each other up and launching into vicious and brutal fights. These clashes are not only to secure rights to the females, but also serve as performative - proving to the females in the area that the victor is the strongest. These battles last until one opponent either dies or forfeits (should one forfeit, they must be allowed to flee), leaving the victor free to attempt to claim the females. When more than 2 males are present, the conflict escalates into a series of battles, with winners having to fight other winners. Each confrontation narrows the field until a single dominant male emerges victorious, claiming the right to court the females and form a pack. These fights may only be 1v1 at any point, but multiple battles may occur at the same time. In rare scenarios, such as where 2 bonded males in a coalition encounter a sisterhood with more than 1 female, the less dominant male may be permitted to try and court one of the females to form his own pack. This only occurs at the mercy of the more dominant male, who chooses not to drive his subordinate away after besting him in battle - especially if the male submits and forfeits before a battle starts. Instead, the ultimate winner opts to isolate the female(s) he desires and move off with them, leaving one behind for his subordinate to claim. While this divides the sisterhood, it provides a more balanced distribution of females and ensures the coalition remains somewhat intact, even if they do not group together again. However, this all hinges on the preference of the females. If the subordinate male is allowed to remain in the POI after all other males are driven out, the females may choose him over the dominant male. While most females prefer the ultimate winner due to his demonstrated strength and dominance, this dynamic poses a risk for the victor - especially when sisterhoods are unwilling to separate. Thus, all females may align with the subordinate male, ultimately undermining the dominant male's efforts despite his victories - illustrating that granting this mercy can lead to a substantial loss for the winner. Should the winning male not wish to gift the obsolete male any females to form a pack with, or should the females not wish to go with the loser, the two especially bonded brothers exchange a final :friendly_call:  call to one another—a stark farewell that acknowledges their bond forged in their shared childhood.
