# Torvosaurus – Parenthood

Once eggs hatch, all females participate in raising them - even those that do not have cubs of their own. Cubs are lavished with affection and nuzzling from a young age. Male cubs are tenderly indulged by all adult females in the group. They are usually fed first by adults, and babied for far longer than their female counterparts. Males are sharply corrected if they attempt to hunt, with adults preferring to kill the prey for them and present them with the corpse. Female cubs are encouraged towards independence from a young age - carefully guided towards critters and even small prey - and praised as they bring back kills. Adult females never tolerate playfighting from cubs and always intervene. They allow them to tussle amongst themselves - after correcting them when they go too far - but will not engage in these fights with them themselves - a privilege they reserve only for other adult females in their group. This is in stark contrast to the father of the group, who happily endures his offspring's roughhousing. Adults utilise a number of different calls and body language to communicate with their cubs in urgent situations. Crouching and staring in the direction of prey without sound is a clear indicator to cubs to gather near their father, who will usher them to a safe place to hide while the females go out to hunt. The father always acts as this form of 'babysitter' when females go out to hunt, standing guard as a stoic presence as cubs play and target him, nipping at his legs and tails. Though he enjoys this play, he is careful to quiet them if they get too raucous, mindful not to alert any potential prey. Adults frequently have to round up cubs that wander too far, especially as males age and become more restless. Fathers - who usually patrol the POI for most of the time - are the ones most responsible for keeping cubs in line like this, often returning them to their mothers.
