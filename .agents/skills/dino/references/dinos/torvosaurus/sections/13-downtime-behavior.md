# Torvosaurus – Downtime Behavior

Torvosaurus primarily rest at night, typically finding deep foliage or dens to construct temporary nests in to sleep, especially when cubs are present. Females will typically take over the care of offspring at this time, permitting the male a break. He will often go on walks through the territory, patrolling to ensure no males enter as well as to have time to himself. At night, Torvosaurus reserve their energy, focusing on being ready for the next day. Often, females will lay close together, cleaning one another and softly purring at each other. Females - especially sisters - bond deeply and closely with one another, either in a sisterhood or a pack. They begin a tight knit and close bond often from cubhood, and frequently disperse from their natal packs together with their sisters. They show deep affection to each other, with nuzzling and grooming commonplace, as well as resting close to one another during downtimes. Males, on the whole, are largely stoic. They may form loose bonds with their brothers or even other lone males they temporarily group with, but this relationship dissipates as soon as the potential of females occurs - with brothers turning against each other in order to secure females for themselves. Males that are in a pack with females show great affection and care towards them, happy to pass by and offer them affection during their patrols, and joining in with cuddle piles at rest times. Females respond to this enthusiastically, calling affectionately to him and leaning in to groom him. Males are also fiercely protective of their females and cubs, and are the first ones to leap to their defence when danger is spotted. Females sometimes engage in playful sparring with one another at rest times. This sparring is always consensual between a pair, and never goes below half health. They carefully lay beside each other afterwards, grooming and chuffing to solidify their bonds. Males never playfight with other males in this way when in a coalition. In addition, they do not show any hint of aggression towards their females - and so do not engage in play fights with them. In the event a male is ever aggressive towards his females, this behavior provokes an immediate and vicious response from all adult females in the pack. Such an act could even cause the females to rebel against him entirely, and cast him out in favor of a new male. Torvosaurus communicate with each other in a variety of ways, but usually through chuffing. The amount of chuffs issued signifies the importance of the call, with multiple chuffs in quick succession usually indicating that they wish for others to follow them - especially when issued towards offspring. Males are always summoned by females after a successful kill with :broadcast_call: loud broadcasts. No females will begin to eat until the male has started - and should the male take too long to arrive, a female will leave the corpse in order to collect him. Only after the male begins eating will females join him. Cubs are not permitted to feast on the corpse until all adults have eaten - but some mothers take pity on their cubs and tear off a chunk of flesh to deposit for their young, tiding them over until the adults are full.
