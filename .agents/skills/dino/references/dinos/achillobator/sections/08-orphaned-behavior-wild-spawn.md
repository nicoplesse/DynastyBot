# Achillobator – Orphaned Behavior (Wild Spawn)

Achillobator pups who are orphaned or hatch without parents grow and develop more quickly than their parented counterparts. Through necessity, they are forced to develop essential survival and hunting skills more quickly, and are often adept at taking down critters by as early as Juvenile. What they experience during their fear period at the end of the Juvenile stage is crucial for their future development. Alarming or frightening situations can provoke lifelong fears for orphaned individuals - lacking the necessary reassurance to overcome these situations that comes with nurturing parents. However, if lucky enough to be adopted, Achillobator readily adapt and settle easily with their new adoptive parents. Orphaned or abandoned individuals who grow to adulthood may form stronger bonds with Latenivenatrix than their own species. Achillobator who grow up alone take longer to form lifelong bonds, and typically show shy and avoidant behavior, if they are not outright snappish. Achillobator are nocturnal, often traveling far distances under the cooler and more comfortable cloak of night. They may travel during the day, especially when food items or other resources are scarce. Achillobator are often incredibly quiet when traveling, and may even take advantage of how quietly they are able to move to ambush potential prey items. Achillobator may be observed rubbing themselves up against trees in order to mark their territories. They also rub their cheeks and sides against objects to deliver messages to other Achillobator, including their partner. These signals may indicate food, mark dens for repeat use, or places where food or trinkets have been hidden. Achillobator pairs can be observed grooming and feeding one another in quiet moments, and often sleep very close together during rest periods, nuzzling their faces alongside one another. Achillobator sometimes exhibit a form of “shyness” towards other species, avoiding being in their line of sight for extended periods where possible. This extends to other Achillobator, with pairs and individuals preferring to give others a large berth, except when courting. Achillobator utilize strong legs for jumping, leaping, and pouncing, and are able to latch onto the sides of large prey.
