# Achillobator – Latenivenatrix

Achillobator benefit from the presence of Latenivenatrix, a smaller raptor that are able to act as companions to solitary or paired Achillobator. Both Achillobator and Latenivenatrix defend each other fiercely, and work together, as directed by Achillobator, to hunt larger prey, and flush small prey into dens and trap them. Bonds between Achillobator and Latenivenatrix companions form quickly, with Latens taking the time to preen Achillobator, bring them small items and trinkets, and food gifts. Achillobator often view Latenivenatrix as their own offspring, and adopt them openly, even with none of their own offspring present. They may deposit them in their nests the same as their Achillobator siblings. Achillobator, equally, are adopted by Latenivenatrix. Those adopted by Latenivenatrix often “sing” more often, exhibit more curiosity, and often believe themselves to be smaller than they are. Achillobator may nest within Latenivenatrix creches, and may defend them alongside Latenivenatrix. Achillobator and Latenivenatrix often nest simultaneously for this reason. As a result, when offspring are old enough, pups and chicks may disperse together to form new groups. Achillobator only hunt with Latenivenatrix at night. However, they may defend a nearby and bonded Latenivenatrix at any time. When Latenivenatrix companions are in a territory challenge with Deinonychus, Achillobator do not assist in these matters. As a larger species than Latenivenatrix, they become an easier target for the small raptors. Similarly, when with Latenivenatrix companions, Achillobator do not hunt Deinonychus. When hunting together, Achillobator are often eerily silent, with sharp focus on their prey. Mated pairs develop their own unique body language cues in order to encourage their partner forward or relay other strategies in hunts. Achillobator do not have the endurance for long distance hunts, and often prefer to chase for short distances, stalking prey until they are close and able to quickly close the distance. Often, Achillobator use ambush tactics and use their intelligence to take advantage of potential opportunities - such as waiting for parents to leave their nests, so that they may hunt the offspring within. Achillobator use their wickedly sharp sickle claws to carve deep wounds into their targets, and take their time to weaken prey through bleeding them out. Achillobator can be brutish while hunting, and rush to force themselves between prey and their paths out of the area, often attempting to back them into a corner where they cannot run from. Achillobator watch their targets for weaknesses and quickly take advantage of them, whether it is encouraging their mate into action or encouraging one of their Latenivenatrix to rush forward. Achillobator avoid chasing high-speed prey for extended periods, and prefer to hunt larger targets that cannot move as quickly. Achillobator have been observed leaping into the air to attempt to catch birds that fly overhead.
