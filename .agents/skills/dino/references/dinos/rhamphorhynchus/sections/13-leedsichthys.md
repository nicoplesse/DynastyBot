# Rhamphorhynchus – Leedsichthys

Most Rhamphorhynchus can hardly contain their joy when encountering these large fish. A sighting often causes a Rhamphorhynchus to react with calls of excitement, flying above, or diving into the waters to observe Leedsichthys up close. Rhamphorhynchus are quick to begin preening these large fish, digging between their scales and gills for parasites or krill remains. Their enjoyment of these species can result in a Rhamphorhynchus returning with various gifts for the Leedsichthys, watching on with curiosity and enthusiasm when their offerings are interacted with. Rhamphorhynchus are similarly friendly with carnivores, but do not seek the same mutualistic relationship with them as they do with herbivores, not wanting to risk being hunted themselves. When following a herbivore herd, Rhamphorhynchus go quiet and quickly flee from the herd upon seeing a carnivore. While they do not attempt to interfere with the hunt of any non-sauropod, up to 4 Rhamphorhynchus who are latched to a sauropod companion (Amargasaurus, Ampelosaurus, Apatosaurus, or Argentinosaurus) can remain latched, and may buff them during the engagement with “Shared Rhythm” and “Plotting Whispers”. However, if a Rhamphorhynchus is bitten or runs out of stamina, it leaps off with a fearful call, quickly fleeing from the area and finding a place to hide and recoup. Rhamphorhynchus will share food sources with other Rhamphorhynchus, but do not share with any other scavengers, meeting them with aggressive calls. Unfortunately, they are unable to do much for the defense of a claimed corpse; their thin, needle-like teeth glancing off the thicker hides of other carnivores. Instead, Rhamphorhynchus may attempt to quickly pluck an extra piece of flesh from the corpse and fly away with it, ensuring the scavenger gets less of a meal.
