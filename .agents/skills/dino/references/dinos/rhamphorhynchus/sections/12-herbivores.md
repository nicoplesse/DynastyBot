# Rhamphorhynchus – Herbivores

Rhamphorhynchus may be observed forming mutualistic relationships with other species of herbivores. While Rhamphorhynchus prioritize sauropods such as Amargasaurus, Ampelosaurus, Apatosaurus, and Argentinosaurus, they may also temporarily follow and nest alongside larger herbivore species that may tolerate them, such as: Eotriceratops, Barsboldia, Iguanodon, Parasaurolophus, and Pachyrhinosaurus. If a non-sauropod is temporarily followed, Rhamphorhynchus colonies will always abandon the herbivore and flock to sauropods when discovered. Rhamphorhynchus take part in grooming their herbivore herds during rest periods and may present them with curious items they find. Any item that is eaten or taken by the herbivore results in great dismay from the Rhamphorhynchus. Rhamphorhynchus will avoid Struthiomimus to the best of their ability, but are not required to entirely avoid herds where Struthiomimus are present. When spending time with Apatosaurus, Rhamphorhynchus rely on the massive sauropods for protection from Struthiomimus. Apatosaurus do not tolerate aggression towards Rhamphorhynchus and quickly correct any untoward behaviors towards them with a short charge and a crack of their tail. Rhamphorhynchus form “bonds” with sauropods, aided by these giant species assisting with carrying their offspring during migrations, and providing a rich resource of ticks and other parasites. As a result, Rhamphorhynchus colonies may often follow the same sauropod or sauropod herd throughout their entire lives, and across multiple generations. Some Rhamphorhynchus develop preferences for a particular individual, often for the sauropod whom they roosted on in their early life. If a herbivore shakes while Rhamphorhynchus are latched onto it, they will quickly get the signal and leap off, calling sadly. If aggressed, they do not attempt to latch to the same herbivore again.
