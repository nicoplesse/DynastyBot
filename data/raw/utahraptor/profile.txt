Utahraptor
 "Utah"
Utahraptor are highly social raptors that thrive in family groups. Known for their keen eyesight and pack coordination hunting strategy, Utahraptor are formidable predators of their size tier, capable of taking on a variety of prey.

✦ Offspring Behavior (Nested)
At birth, baby Utahraptors, referred to as chicks, are weak and fragile. They remain hidden in the nest, cared for by trusted packmates who provide protection during their vulnerable early weeks. Unattended chicks are blind to danger outside the den, so the entire family fiercely guards them.
The clutch a chick hatches into determines their roles within the family pack. Utahraptor family packs do not permit every rank to nest - only offspring to the Alpha pair (Beta), siblings to one of the Alphas (Delta) and grandchildren (Delta) are permitted to nest. Omegas are forbidden from nesting, and any pair ranked as Omega caught breeding are exiled from the pack immediately.
Offspring born from a clutch sired by both Alpha parents become betas. Betas who breed will produce offspring who rank as deltas, and offspring of delta become omega rank.
Once taking their first mouthfuls of food, chicks are big enough to begin exploring their immediate area. Chirping at their clutchmates, they crawl over each other and investigate the nest they hatched into.
As chicks, there is no defining behavior difference between which rank chicks are born into. The realization of rank only comes into play during adolescence where chicks are led on patrols and hunts by different family members who share their skills with the younger generation of their rank.
As chicks transition into the juvenile stage, they grow stronger and more restless, prompting their parents to introduce them to various objects around the den. Curious and exploratory, juveniles use their teeth to investigate their surroundings, engage in rough play with siblings, and occasionally nibble on their parents' tails.
Utahraptor chicks mimic the movements and sounds of their parents and family to strengthen social bonds. They chirp softly to imitate adult calls, practicing their future pack communication skills.
During supervised moments outside the nest, chicks explore under the watchful eye of their pack. They occasionally stray outside the realm of the territory, but are quickly herded back with gentle nips or growls from adults.
When it rains, chicks display highly playful behaviors such as pouncing on puddles or playing with dripping water, and shaking their feathers in the rain. This natural engagement helps them become accustomed to weather changes and terrain.
Within their family pack, chicks are chaperoned around by older offspring to the Alpha pair. Their role as Beta permits them to oversee skill development of the younger family members and start forming bonds early. These relationships last a lifetime and forge alliances that help establish one’s position within the family, and provides support when disagreements between packmates require support.
Once offspring reach adolescence, their curiosity and fast metabolism is no longer suited to staying at the den . They follow their older siblings out the nesting area, mimicking their behavior and learning the ins and outs of survival.
If the territory is challenged for, chicks will hide quietly under shelter, nervously watching the engagement. If their pack forfeits or dies in the challenge, babies will flee, skittish and flighty until they are reunited with a familiar face.
Offspring that end up a different color palette from their bloodline will find themselves exiled from the pack by the entire family upon maturity. Though confused and upset by this sudden rejection, they have no choice but to leave and try to survive on their own.
 
Once all the offspring are subadult, the Alphas invite chicks to join them on hunts, taking turns with each youngling to teach them hunting strategies and group co-ordination. Oftentimes, the caregiver who helped raise them attends these hunts, keeping a keen eye on their chosen sibling and helping them learn how to feed the family.
Young adults and packmates are shown critter burrows and water sources as well as where the borders of their territory end by older, more experienced members of the pack. Keeping each member fit by patrolling and swapping individuals out in regular hunting parties is a way Alphas ensure their territory is well defended.

✦ Orphaned Behavior (Wild Spawn)
Orphaned Utahraptors, being weak and small, face extremely low survival chances. As easy prey to most predators, they typically seek refuge in small dens where they remain hidden until they are strong enough to defend themselves.
Their soft, fluffy plumage provides some warmth, but without a caregiver, they must fend for themselves.
They attempt to survive by scavenging from abandoned corpses or hunting small critters.
Orphans do not group up with other orphans, and instinctively avoid other Utahraptor until they are physically mature, and wish to form a pack of their own.
 
Bild
Utahraptors are cautious towards their own kind and do not share resources or space.
Family packs are committed to securing and defending their nesting area against rival packs of ungrouped individuals. The area which they have claimed to nest in is referred to as the pack territory: a chosen location guarded by an entire bloodline. Although Utahraptor in a pack may venture beyond their chosen territory borders in search of prey, doing so is always a calculated risk.
Only preferred POIs can be claimed as territory, which is only defended from other Utahraptor. Each POI is listed as either a compact territory, or a grand territory. Compact Territories are limited to 5 Utahraptor per side in an engagement, whereas Grand Territories have no engagement limits.
Smaller packs gravitate towards compact territories that are more manageable for their numbers. With fewer mouths to feed compared to larger packs, the area holds enough resources to sustain them. Smaller in size, these territories are easier to patrol and manage, offering a safe haven from overpowering bloodlines who crave grand territories to support their larger numbers.
Once outside their territory, Utahraptor are mindful of their surroundings and move quietly from place to place, aware that they could be encroaching upon enemy pack's territories.
When pushed to venture beyond their preferred POI, family packs will travel together closely, following the lead of their Alpha pair. Utahraptor only leave their preferred POI when starvation threatens their pack and territory is abandoned in a desperate attempt to find food. Once their bellies are filled, Utahraptor quickly retreat into familiar regions, eager to return to their territory.
Utahraptor always posture and growl when an unfamiliar individual approaches. Therefore, when an individual is separated from their family and returns months later, their arrival back to the family territory is done with caution. The returning individual shakes out their feathers and calls softly, standing some distance away to wait for the recognition of family through their scent. When recognized, both will welcome each other happily, chirping and grooming to establish bonds.
Utahraptor are keen preeners - using self-grooming as a measure of self-soothing, as well as general hygiene upkeep. Individuals may groom one another as a sign of affection and an attempt to bond, especially after a squabble.
✦ Downtime Behavior
Utahraptor packs may have their own sleeping and waking schedules, depending on their biomes and what prey is available at what times.
During these rest periods, Utahraptor often share space with their packmates, especially those they are closely bonded with such as their mate, siblings, and offspring. Some Utahraptor may even opt to sleep curled beside one another, or in the same nest.
Utahraptor also preen one another extensively during these rest periods.
Utahraptor maintain their nests during this time, and ensure they remain clean for future offspring, as well as for being a place to sleep. The discovery of bugs or other obscenities in one’s nest may result in the complete removal of the nest and it being moved to a different location.

✦ Family packs
Utahraptor packs, bred into life by the Alpha pair, are a group of closely bonded related members in a grouping referred to as  "family packs". These large groups are easily recognized by their shared feather color scheme, indicating their kinship.
Utahraptors form close bonds with their siblings and immediate family members. These bonds develop over time as individuals prove their loyalty by protecting one another in combat, sharing food, and helping raise the young.
Bonds and friendships are crucial, as they may grant some members more leniency than others. Those with weaker bonds are more likely to be pushed out when the pack outgrows their numbers.
Family packs are highly structured, with clearly defined roles.

Pack Structure & Roles
Utahraptor pack roles are filled through birth rights, and do not change except when the Alphas die or step down.

  Alphas
As the leaders of the pack, the Alphas are the longest-bonded pair and are regarded as the strongest and wisest members. Their role is not challenged, but may be willingly stepped down from when they reach Elder status, to become former Alphas. Alphas are responsible for selecting hunting groups, accepting mates into the pack, and determining nesting sites. Their authority commands great respect within the pack.
Alphas are the primary breeding pair that form the entire family pack. All members of the family pack are their offspring, grandchildren, siblings and unrelated mates to their bloodline.
Same-sex pairs are permitted within the pack, but cannot become Alphas due to their inability to reproduce.
The Alpha pair determines what the color scheme for the pack will be. These colors are visually seen in both skins from the Alpha pair. Examples can be seen below.

Former Alphas 
Alphas that reach the Elder stage or choose to step down become Former Alphas. They are looked to for experience and guidance, but take a step back within the pack, and remain as well-respected and loved pack members.
If a new Alpha takes position, and is not a child of the Former Alpha, the Former Alphas will disperse from the pack, seeking to retire elsewhere. However, if the new Alpha is a child of the former alpha, they permit their elderly parents to remain in the family pack - keeping them close for support and guidance in their new role.
Former Alphas that retire from their position no longer nest. Instead, they give the opportunity to their child to pave their own way and prioritize the new generation under their new leadership.

  Betas 
Betas are direct offspring of the Alphas, stepping into leadership roles as needed. Well-respected and influential, they help maintain order and assist their parents in governing the pack. 
Betas can are only be children nested by both individuals in the current Alpha pair role. If a Beta-ranked child is related to only one Alpha, they will always become Omega when the Alpha takes a new mate.

Maintaining order: From a young age, the offspring of the Alpha pair learn through instinctive mimicry, closely copying their parents' behaviors. Betas play a key role in maintaining pack hunting styles, passing down essential knowledge and behaviors to the rest of the pack. As the most valued members of the bloodline, they receive the most extensive upbringing, making their understanding of pack life deeply trusted. Lower-ranking individuals never challenge them, instead showing respect through body shakes and unwavering obedience.
Hunting: Betas also play a crucial role in orchestrating the pack's strategic hunts.
When assigned into a hunting party Betas instinctively fall into leadership positions, flanking the group and working as a team to confuse and exhaust prey. When paired with other ranks, Betas always engage prey first, with other ranks supporting their lead.

Deltas
Deltas are offspring nested by Beta rank (grandchildren to the Alpha pair), but can also be siblings to one of the Alpha pair.
Scouts & territory defense: This role aids the pack in serving as scouts and sentries, and the first line of defence. They patrol the territory borders by perching on tall ledges and tracing the edges of the territory. Dried branches and roots are relocated from piles and placed on the borders, signifying to other Utahraptor in the area that the land they are entering is home to others.
When danger approaches - especially if an unknown Utahraptor enters their territory - the Deltas are the first to alert the rest of the pack, and initiate a territory challenge. The Delta rank, as a consequence, ends up the most battle worn - but also as highly skilled fighters.
Their experience and loyalty is respected, with the Alpha pair allowing them to place their nests high above the rest of the pack. These ideal vantage points offer the best protection for young and establish their role as keen sentries for the family.
﻿
Omegas 
Forming subordinate roles, the other related individuals (children of deltas, great grandchildren and offspring to only one of the Alpha pair) fill the rest of the pack's bloodline.
Submissive & low ranking: Omegas are the last on the pecking order. As further distanced relatives from the Alpha pair, they are permitted to remain in the pack and fill subordinate roles, often serving as designated bait to bluff prey attacks in hunts, or support during territory defence and patrols. Omegas fulfil the role of small game hunters, bringing back scraps of meat or small critters for hungry packmates who remain in the territory. When separated from Beta or Delta ranks, Omega are nervous, with no clear leader emerging in the leader's absence.

Unrelateds
These are individuals that are mates to Betas, Deltas and Omegas. They are accepted in the pack due to their pairbonds and are permitted to hunt and participate in pack life. Sometimes, unrelateds may find themselves widowed if their mate dies. If their children are still alive, they can remain within the family but are forbidden to take a new mate, unless courting into the bloodline again.
When an Unrelated's mate is offline, they may still continue to act as the role of their mate. Additionally, the rest of the pack respects them and treats them as the rank of their mate.
 

✦  Pack Rank Changing:
These ranks change only when the Alpha pair shifts from their position as family lead.
When both Alphas either die or retire as elders, the Beta they choose takes over as the new Alpha, along with their mate if they are bonded. 
If there's no clear choice when this time comes, or both Alphas have died, Betas may challenge each other for leadership. These challenges are often peaceful - with the pack voting on the new leadership based on who has the most allies in the pack to support them.
If there is no clear winner, the two Betas with the most allies compete to prove who is most capable to lead the family. Their mates, ranking as unrelated, watch nervously from the sidelines. Their relation to the bloodline does not permit them to fight with their mate. This challenge is fought until the loser reaches deathscars - an embarrassing proof that one is not fit to lead the large family.

✦ When a new Alpha pair takes over:
The Beta who won will assume their position as new Alpha. If they are bonded, their unrelated mate is promoted into the Alpha rank with them. 
If they have no mate when they become Alpha, whomever they court will automatically be promoted to Alpha rank and the pair begins their new reign together.
Any children related to both male and female in the new Alpha pair are promoted to Beta rank.
Previous offspring born to a deceased partner become Omega. Offspring must be related to both the Alphas to become Betas.
Siblings and grandchildren to the new Alpha become Deltas. 
Any of their children become Omegas.
Generations further than that are exiled.
Parents to the new Alphas remain as former Alphas, ranking just below their new Alpha child proudly.

✦  Voluntary Disbanding:
In situations where family members disband to nest with another bloodline or form their own, all relations are severed with the pack they were born into and they have no desire to return.
 
✦ Solitary Behavior
A solo Utahraptor, shaped by the harsh life of an orphan or wandering between hunting grounds in search of a mate - or simply exploring - is a quiet and observant hunter. Their keen eyesight helps them scout herds, spot other carnivores, and identify potential mates. Lone Utahraptors will avoid others of their own kind, steering clear of rivals to avoid conflict. Only when interested in courtship will a solitary Utahraptor linger along the edges of an occupied territory.
As solitary hunters without the support of a family, they may choose to target smaller prey, often the vulnerable offspring of herbivores.
Utahraptor are social creatures and their ultimate goal is to find a mate and form their own pack, or get welcomed into another.
Some individuals prefer to remain solitary for the entirety of their life. They can be seen trailing behind herds in the distance, keen to take advantage of opportunities that may come up.

✦ Aggression towards family members:
During their juvenile phases of life, Utahraptor chicks playfight to practice hunting skills on each other and adults.
Once reaching adolescence, their behavior is regulated by higher ranking packmates. An aggressive call or a nip on the back forces these rambunctious chicks to submit.
As they mature, Utahraptor playfights become more risky. What begins as harmless sparring can quickly escalate into real injuries if boundaries aren't respected. A misplaced bite or poorly judged swipe can turn a friendly scuffle into a bleeding wound.
While playfighting remains common among adults as essential hunting practice, it is never permitted to drop a packmate below half health. If a bout begins to go too far, the attacker is expected to immediately disengage, retreating with a visible display of submission, such as shaking out their feathers, lowering their head, and stepping aside. In return, the wounded raptor will instinctively distance themselves from their aggressor, a silent acknowledgment that the line was crossed.
Social rank plays a key role in how these interactions unfold:
Alphas sometimes engage in playfights, but lower ranking packmates never initiate these spars against them but happily take part.
If an Alpha observes an individual engaging in playfights that goes beyond half health more than once, they will begin to exile this packmate for over-aggression.
Other ranks may playfight among each other, but lower ranks are expected to submit first when sparring with higher ranks.
Even within the fiercest pack, Utahraptor packs are deeply rooted in mutual respect and the preservation of group cohesion.

✦ Exile from the Pack
The Alpha rank has exclusive rights to exile packmates. If caught in any of the following situations, an individual, or both individuals in the Alpha rank will readily force out the offenders.
Former Alphas are never exiled from the pack and take no part in pushing out packmates.
However, Former Alphas - understanding that the family is becoming too distantly related to them - will willingly disperse from the pack when a new Alpha takes over that is not their child.
New alphas push out excess relations to focus on their core bloodline, exiling members who are not their former alpha parents, siblings and offspring.
Family members in the Omega rank are never permitted to breed. When caught trying to nest, they are exiled along with their mate and offspring immediately, as they pose a threat to the new bloodline.
Offspring to any rank under the Alpha pair must be presented to at least one of the alphas and be accepted into the pack. Packmates that are caught with offspring not yet presented to an Alpha will be exiled immediately along with their offspring, regardless of age. 
While these nests may occur, being caught with offspring when an Alpha was not present at the time of the nest results in exile.
If only one Alpha is present, the offspring must be reintroduced again to the other Alpha when they are next online.
Siblings, offspring, mates or new grandchildren with unfavorable feather patterns or colors are exiled once they reach sub adulthood.
Melanistic individuals integrate well into dark-feathered family packs and Albinos are only permitted if the pack’s new colors are light in coloration.

✦  Exile of Unrelateds:
Should an unrelated's mate die, and they have no offspring within the pack to support them, the Alphas will exile them from the pack.
If the Alphas wish to exile an unrelated, they risk the mate and/or offspring leaving with the exiled member.
When an unrelated's mate dies, they are not permitted to bring another unrelated into the pack. They must disperse, or court a relative in the pack.

✦ Defending their territory (POI) from other Utahraptor:
When an unfamiliar Utahraptor crosses the border into a pack's territory, the entire pack will growl menacingly to intimidate and chase off rivals from approaching any further.
The approaching Utahraptor group must aggressive call :threaten_call: 3 times across any of its groupmates to show their desire to challenge for the territory. 
If no aggressive calls are given by the approaching Utahraptor, the defending Utahraptor group must aggressive call :threaten_call: 3 times total across any of their members to warn that they will attack, allowing the approaching Utahraptor time to leave the territory.
If the warning is ignored or the challenge is accepted with an aggressive call :threaten_call: by the intruder, then:
In Compact Territories, up to 5 chosen individuals will leap forward to battle up to 5 members of the other pack.
In Grand Territories, the entire family pack will charge forward, aiming to eliminate the rivals. Oftentimes one pack will forfeit when faced by the sheer pack size of their rival - preferring to relocate than compete so severely outnumbered.
Utahraptor territorial challenges are a vicious battle. These fights are extremely aggressive, often brutal, and can result in death of the entire challenging pack if neither side forfeits their claim. Victory is determined by which family pack manages to eliminate their rivals, or drive them out the POI. 
Forfeiting the POI is signaled the fear call :danger_call:﻿ played  repeatedly while fleeing the POI. 
If one member of the pack forfeits, the entire pack must forfeit also, and must withdraw from the POI.
The victorious pack must allow the fleeing pack to leave, but may escort the losers out without attacking if a proper forfeit is given, to ensure they leave the POI.
Utahraptor are not cannibals, but are not wasteful, and will eat from a Utahraptor body if one is available.

Utahraptor are territorial, but are only territorial to their own kind. Other species are not chased out, and Utahraptor may contest when challenged for the right to remain in the POI.
✦ Predator Reaction
When traveling as a pack, Utahraptor tend to quieten down and slip past other carnivores and herbivores unseen and unheard. Only the pack leader will bark orders to their subordinates. This strategy is to trick potential prey into thinking the pack is small and therefore not a threat.
If Utahraptor have young in their territory and a predatory species enters the POI, young are lured away and hidden in thick foliage.
Adult pack members, cautious, stand closeby to observe a potential encounter. A maximum of 5 Utahraptor may engage the threat, while the rest of the pack watch from a distance away (at least 10 bodylengths).
✦ Interspecies Tolerance
Utahraptor do not tolerate and have a deep hatred for Dryosaurus - this small feisty herbivore has a habit of invading nesting spots and attacking young. All Utahraptor in a family pack, of any age, will aggressively engage with Dryosaurus, regardless of hunger or claimed corpses nearby.
Utahraptor do not tolerate scavengers.

Utahraptor are excellent and co-ordinated hunters, with a hunting party organized by the Alphas to carefully balance skills and ability. Each member has strengths in the family pack, and young are encouraged into the hunting party to teach them the pack’s hunting strategy.  Family packs prefer to hunt within their territory where possible, but packs that desperately need food can move further out in search of prey.
Solitary individuals are seemingly seen before they are heard. Quiet by nature when alone, they curiously follow herds of sauropods, using the herd to alert them of any dangerous predators that may enter the area and wait for easy hunting opportunities in calves during breeding seasons.
Utahraptor may not start a hunt against other species if other Utahraptor are present in the POI. They must be chased out before a hunt can be started on other species.
Utahraptor prefer to hunt up to Large tier where possible, and will not hunt Apex tier if there are viable Large tier or smaller prey options available in the POI.
Only 1 hunting party per family pack may engage in a hunt at a time.
Utahraptors are skilled social hunters when hunting other species, and family packs maintain close bonds, with the offspring of the alpha pair holding the highest rank. These individuals, considered Betas, lead the hunts, while Omegas typically fill the remaining roles in the pack.
Utahraptors employ endurance hunting tactics, first circling their target and using growls and snapping jaws to frighten and intimidate. The Betas take turns running through prey groups to separate and confuse the animals, aiming for a weaker target. Some packs prefer to hunt adults over young, as it provides more sustenance for a larger Utahraptor family. Younger and smaller packs, lacking the confidence, often simply target the easiest meal while they are still establishing themselves in the area.
The hunting party divides its efforts between those who bite the prey and those who bluff, using their numbers to confuse and exhaust the target. When prey is successfully killed, the higher ranks and their mates eat first, filling their bellies while standing guard over their kill.
This feeding order is not challenged once learned, and respected by all ranks. Individuals may correct others if they forget their place with growls and well timed nips.

When interested in courting another individual, Utahraptor have a few options available to them.
One Utahraptor may opt to temporarily disperse from their natal pack to roam to the territories of other packs. They will not allow any of their siblings to come with them, as more than one Utahraptor entering the territory of a different pack risks an aggressive response.
If preferring to stay within the proximity of their natal pack, Utahraptor will roam at the edges of the territory, sniffing and searching for dispersals seeking a mate.
If without a natal pack, Utahraptor will roam in search of other solitary individuals to court with, or travel to pack territories.
✦ Courting in a Pack Territory
Dispersal Utahraptor will skirt around the edges of the territory of a pack they are interested in courting into, or courting an individual from. Rubbing themselves against and scratching trees, they attempt to create a subtle trail for individuals interested in courtship to follow.
They will rarely, if ever, make too much noise, not wanting to run the risk of being found by the dominant pair, or other individuals uninterested in courting them.
These Utahraptor may also find gifts to present to potential mates.
These dispersal Utahraptor will only ever be tolerated after courtship has been completed with a member of the pack that owns the territory, meaning that if they are caught by any Utahraptor that is not interested in courting them, they will be chased out, following the rules of a normal territory challenge.
During the rainy season, solitary dispersal Utahraptor attempting to court are tolerated by packs. These individuals are regarded cautiously by the pack and permitted to linger around the edge of the territory, but may be chased out if they venture too far in.
When approached by a Utahraptor coming from further in the pack territory, the dispersal Utahraptor will immediately crouch, cooing softly and presenting their gift, if they have one.
If approached by more than one Utahraptor from inside the pack territory, the dispersal interested in courtship will instead flee, unwilling to risk any potential aggression.
Multiple members of a pack interested in courtship do not approach a dispersal at the same time and must intiate the contact alone.
If multiple dispersals take interest in the same individual, the individual will only display interest in their potential mate. The dispersal that is ignored will quickly recieve the message and move on.
The pack Utahraptor will approach slowly, sniffing often, staring down the dispersal Utahraptor. If the dispersal Utahraptor stands too quickly, presents an unsuitable gift, or is otherwise offensive towards the pack Utahraptor, they risk being chased out, and the rest of the pack being alerted.
Once the pack Utahraptor has gotten used to the other’s presence, they will shake off, cooing and trilling at the dispersal. At this point, the bonding period begins.
 
✦ Dispersal Courtship
If two Utahraptor find each other, courtship is very similar to that if it would occur in a pack territory.
One individual will crouch and act submissive, while the other sniffs and investigates them; if they determine that the individual is unsatisfactory, they will immediately separate from one another, showing no aggression and not chasing one another down.
If multiple dispersals take interest in the same individual, the individual will only display interest in their potential mate. The dispersal that is ignored will quickly recieve the message and move on.
✦ Bonding Period
What occurs in the bonding period varies from individual to individual, but most commonly consists of the following:
Frequent preening; sniffing one another; extended periods of eye contact;
Finding suitable, comfortable places to lay alongside one another, often laying their heads on one another and rubbing their heads together;
Hunting critters and other small prey items together;
Utahraptor in the bonding period may hunt up to small tier together, and may opt to defend one another from threats, based on personal preference.
Building a mock nest and tending to it together;
Games of chase; brief sparring matches that never go below half-health; etc.
Gift-giving; frequently shiny items, food items, or trophies from kills;
If a pack member is nearby, the pack Utahraptor courting may attempt to lead the other individual away from their potential mate, in order to avoid courtship being interrupted.
The bonding period consists of at least thirty minutes of these activities. This must occur before any introductions to a dominant pair, or dispersing together. 
If discovered by members of the pack, the dispersal Utahraptor will be chased out. It is up to the pack Utahraptor if they follow, or if they return to their pack after the unsuccessful courtship. 
If still interested, the dispersal Utahraptor may return to the same territory after being chased out, if their potential mate has chosen to remain in the territory. They will wait for the rest of the pack to leave before attempting again.
Pack Utahraptor who have been caught mid courtship may be more carefully watched by other members of the pack– especially if they are an Omega, who is not permitted to nest.
The activities done in the bonding period will often follow the pair through their lifetime together, if the courtship ends up being successful.
Once the bonding period is complete, the newly formed pair may either approach the dominant pair of the territory they have courted in (if an individual belongs to the pack), disperse to form their own pack, or disperse from the territory to court into the dispersal Utah’s existing pack.
 
✦ Newly Formed Pairs
If starting their own pack, the process is relatively simple. The pair will move away to find a territory to claim, and usually start with nesting - doing so to become the Alphas in a brand new family pack and beginning their own bloodline.
Individuals who both belong to a family pack can opt to start their own with their new partner at any time. If they choose to leave their old pack and form a new one, they have no obligation to say goodbye, but may individually approach their old family members with their new mate to bid farewell.
However, many individuals choose to remain in their family pack, bringing their new partner back as an unrelated. The choice of which family to return to is down to the new couple - and may end up with them opting to go with the second choice if the first family does not accept their new mate.
If they choose to return home to a family pack, both individuals will look for a suitable gift to gain favor. Food is always the most guaranteed to please, and unsuitable gifts may ruin an unrelated's chances of being accepted into the family.
When returning to the dominant pair with their new mate, all pack members will come to inspect the new addition, circling and sniffing them. The family member will stay very close to their mate, who must remain crouched and submissive.
The new pair drop their gifts for the Alpha by their feet, and resume crouching and cooing beside each other as they are inspected.
The Alpha has the only say in this choice, and will always approach the partner before deciding whether or not they are a good fit for the pack. Each Alpha pair has their own set of rules and standards to decide whether an individual will be accepted, and the decision is at their discretion.
If the Alpha's mate is not available to oversee the introduction, their mate will introduce the new pair to them and help soothe the stress of seeing an unfamiliar Utahraptor in the pack. If both alphas are unavailable, new mates are promptly chased out the territory by the whole family.
Should the alpha accept the new member, he or she will be greeted with affectionate calls and shaking of feathers, a clear sign and indication of their acceptance. The new partner joins the family pack as an Unrelated.
✦ Nesting
This new bond results in a single egg in opposite sex pairs. The first clutch often takes longer to come into fruition, but the pair will still build a nest together each season to prove their dedication to each other. 
Seasons of unhatched eggs and deceased offspring can drive wedges between partners, perhaps signifying a difference in their combined genetics that make producing offspring together difficult. This can put strain on their relationship - but family members are quick to help soothe tensions.
Once in a lifelong pair together, nesting happens whenever the pair opts to do so. They will work together to build a nest somewhere in the pack's territory, spending much time lovingly constructing it and filling it with soft, downy feathers and resources to protect and keep their future young warm.
✦ Omega Sneaky Nesting
Omegas do not commonly attempt to nest, as it risks their safe position in a pack. However, certain circumstances - such as wishing to start a pack or intense broodiness - can influence them into wishing to nest. Doing so must be done discreetly, as if they are caught, they are immediately exiled from their pack.
When both individuals in an Omega pair are interested in nesting together, they slip away from the pack, patrolling through their pack's territory in search for the perfect hidden haven to nest.
Without the presence of their pack or their pack’s alphas, Omegas will choose not to nest, as they feel most comfortable in the territory while their pack’s leaders are around to protect it.
Once a suitable location is found, the Omega related to the bloodline places the nest where their eggs will be laid. Both raptors work together, carefully decorating the nest with dense foliage and shrubbery to hide their vulnerable young from sight.
As they take turns brooding upon their eggs, and possibly their freshly hatching chicks, the pair takes care of one another instinctively. While one remains with the nest, the other returns to their pack, attempting to ensure their kin do not discover their secret. They socialize and bond with their packmates as usual, before eventually sneaking away to deliver food to their waiting mate and young.
Offspring born to an Omega pair remain ungrouped in-game, as they are too distant from the pack’s bloodline and are not considered part of the wider pack.
Offspring are still required to fill out a bloodline log as usual to prove relations, but must change their bloodline upon aging out of their parents’ care.
Without the direct support of a pack, parenting is much more of a challenge for these highly social raptors. The pair becomes hypervigilant and anxious, constantly scanning the horizon for any sign of predators or, worse, their packmates. They preen and quietly coo to one another often, attempting to soothe their mate’s nerves.
Terrified of their aging chicks drawing attention with their growing restlessness, the pair are quick to correct any overly noisy behavior with huffed growls and gentle nips.
Disruptive, disobedient, or loud chicks are pushed from the nest and territory, finding themselves abandoned. The anxious parents, should the chick not understand, will aggress the offspring away.
Otherwise, once chicks hatch, parents do not abandon their offspring.
 
If Discovered by Pack Members
When the nest is at risk of being found, parents rush to hide their offspring, nudging them into the secluded nest - or any other foliage nearby.
If caught, the packmates who discover the sneaky nest rush to alert the rest of the pack to the area, collecting one or both Alphas in the process.
During this short period, some Omega pairs enter a state of panic and choose to flee the POI immediately after detection, willingly dispersing rather than being forced into exile.
At first sight of their Alphas, the Omega and their mate become submissive, crouching low and avoiding eye contact, attempting to redeem themselves through affectionate calls and shakes. Alphas remain unfazed by this appeasement behavior, and conduct a brief, intense inspection of the pair and their chicks.
Aggression follows quickly after, with Alphas stepping forward to begin a series of threatening calls. Witnessing pack members quickly join in with snarls of their own, and the whole pack works together to push the Omegas out of the territory.
Exile is always met with acceptance in these circumstances, and Omegas quickly signal their understanding with forfeiting calls, gathering their chicks and leaving the territory as quickly as possible.
Once alone, the pair and their chicks are often left disoriented and afraid, unused to being left without a clear leader. It is not uncommon for the Omega pair to show great distress following their exile, especially for the related.
After a brief period of mourning, pairs eventually accept their circumstances, and begin to seek out a new territory to raise their young in, inevitably beginning their own pack.
At times, the resulting stress may result in a pair rejecting and abandoning their offspring, choosing to continue exile as a bonded pair instead.
Offspring abandoned due to this struggle to survive, relying heavily on siblings  - if they have any - for comfort and safety. Individuals grow to become skittish, forming strong attachments to Utahraptors they meet later in life.
 
If Undiscovered - Offspring Maturity
Once one of their offspring reaches Sub-Adult, the pair leads the mature offspring to the border of the pack’s territory, bowing low (:question_emote:) and pushing them away with their bodies.
If their offspring fail to understand their gentle attempts at removal, they begin to hiss and snarl, issuing aggressive calls :threaten_call: their way in order to swiftly remove them from the territory.
The offspring, though hurt and confused by the sudden fierce rejection, always accept the decision, leaving to try and survive on their own. If they have siblings who have not yet aged out of their parents’ care, they may attempt to linger on the edge of the neighboring territory, waiting to rejoin their dispersing kin and possibly form a new bloodline together.
When all offspring have reached maturity and have left the territory, the Omega pair return to their unknowing family and reintegrate back into pack life.
Sometimes, the Omega pair chooses to disperse alongside their aging young in order to form a new bloodline together. In this situation, they all slip out of the territory upon the maturity of their eldest offspring, leaving to find a new territory to call their home. 
Once the newly formed pack creates and/or updates bloodline logs for their new bloodline, they may then group together in-game.
Mature offspring of Omega pairs go on to display quieter, more subdued behavior than others of their kind. They may show a natural affinity towards a more stealthy hunting strategy, becoming adept at remaining hidden and laying in wait.
✦ Parenthood
Once eggs hatch, the parents begin by caring for their offspring by themselves - but all members of the pack participate in child rearing, and help one another with the raising of young.
Alphas support nesting efforts within their pack by organizing members into rotating hunting groups to bring back food for those caring for young. 
When fewer family members are present, both parents alternate between hunting and caring for the eggs or offspring. They incubate the eggs by carefully covering them to keep them warm without crushing them, mimicking modern bird behavior.
Some packmates, specifically elder individuals, help those nesting by bringing roots, flowers and reeds to warm the chicks. They also serve as primary caretakers of young when other adults - particularly parents - are out hunting, or defending territory.
Nesting is particularly stressful for the entire pack, especially for the Alpha Pair. The Alphas must coordinate hunting parties to ensure a steady food supply for their growing family while also defending their territory from potential thieves. Raising chicks is mainly the job of parents, elders and omegas.
Caregivers regularly bring food and various objects to be used as toys back to the nesting spot, which are eagerly explored by chicks and fought over jealousy.
Offspring are kept in line by the entirety of the pack. Those that wander too far are quickly corrected, and at least one adult always has eyes on the young, acting as a babysitter.
Raising young is far easier with more pack members, and becomes less stressful for the entire pack when there are more hands on deck to assist in providing for offspring.
✦ Offspring Maturity
Once adult skins come in, the Alphas once again assess young. Utahraptor born into the pack with different skins or colorations are quickly exiled from the pack by Alphas. 
This can be upsetting for the parents - but Alphas have been known to even exile their own offspring - those would-be Betas - should they not match the family bloodline skin or coloration.
Parents have two choices: they can either watch their offspring be exiled and accept the Alpha's decision, or they may opt to leave with them, breaking away to create their own family pack as the new Alphas, with their offspring becoming new Betas. This decision is a huge one and results in them severing ties with the rest of their family - so is kept as a last resort by parents that are especially attached to a particular child.
✦ Adoption - Same sex pairs & related members only
Same-sex pairs can be accepted into a family pack, depending on the skins and individual chosen as a potential mate.
Just as opposite-sex pairs do, same-sex pairs prove their dedication to each other each season. They participate in nest building together and reaffirming their bond.
While they cannot reproduce offspring together, same-sex pairs are trusted packmates, and particularly broody pairs will linger near the nesting site to help raise offspring in the family.
Sometimes when a clutch is overwhelming for parents - especially when there's more than 2 eggs - new parents will nuzzle one of their smaller chicks over to the nest of a same-sex pair they trust; willingly allowing a single child to be raised by them instead.
Shaking their feathers passively, the broody same-sex pair happily accept the chick given to them, and devote their attention and time to raising the chick as their own.  The chick will mature with knowledge of its biological parents, but develops a closer bond towards its caregivers who spent more time and energy raising it than its parents did.
When raising the chick given to them, or participating in the seasonal nesting rituals, same-sex pairs take turns bringing meat chunks for expectant parents in the pack and standing guard over the nesting site. It's often these dedicated packmates are the first to ward away rivals in the territory, fiercely protecting the pack’s newest generation.
When the related partner dies, the unrelated mate is forced to leave. While the initial jab is painful and mourned deeply, oftentimes a sibling to their deceased mate may take the opportunity to find them at the edge of the territory, hoping to catch their interest and form a lifelong bond with a known provider. This is the perfect situation for a widow who was a trusted ally to the bloodline.
(Offspring raised by other packmates must still be listed under their biological parents in the bloodline post. A note can be made that they were raised by a same-sex pair/or other, but ultimately, their rank is decided at birth. When pack ranks change, so too will their role - regardless of who raised them, they are still biological offspring to their parents.)

Once two raptors commit, whether within an established family pack or forging their own path, they remain steadfast. Profile - Quick View
The Utahraptor profile is intentionally complex and not aimed for new players.
Utahraptor are Medium Cathemeral Terrestrial Carnivores.
Utahraptor form packs of unlimited sub/adults and unlimited offspring in a grouping of related individuals and unrelated mates. 
A maximum of 20 Sub/Adults can group in game on one server at a time. 2 of these slots must be reserved for the Alpha pair at all times.
Utahraptor live in strong family packs, where individuals look the same through inherited skins. They have different hierarchies, including alphas, betas, deltas, and omegas.
Utahraptor exile relatives from their packs if they do not share the same color scheme as the alpha pair, or are too distantly related to the alpha pair.
Utahraptor are tolerant to other species, and aggressive towards rival Utahraptor. When solitary, they are tolerant towards other Utahraptor.
Utahraptor packs claim a large territory in their preferred POIs, which they only defend from other Utahraptor. Claimable POIs for territories are separated into compact territories (which are ideal for small packs, and have a lower engagement limit for territory challenges) and grand territories (which are ideal for larger packs, with no engagement limit during territory challenges).
Utahraptor can have up to 5 in an engagement and hunting party.
When fighting other Utahraptor for territory, they can have 5 if challenging for a compact territory, or the entire pack if challenging for a grand territory.
Utahraptor may hunt up to Apex tier prey if no other prey items are available in the POI. Utahraptor may not scavenge.
They uniquely travel alone to the edges of other Utahraptor territories in order to attempt to court. Utahraptors go through a bonding period, which lasts at least 30 minutes and consists of several different types of bonding activities.
Utahraptor are required to log their relations in the ⁠🩸・bloodline-logs channel.
