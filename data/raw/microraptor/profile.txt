Microraptor
    "Micro"
Microraptor are tiny dromaeosaurids that utilize their wings to glide between trees and cliffs. Intelligent and ever cunning, Microraptor are capable of mimicking the calls of other species, which they use to their advantages in hunting and courtship.

Growing Up
✦ Offspring Behavior (Nested)
Microraptor offspring, known as chicks, hatch out of their eggs burdened by their oversized wings. Damp, heavy, and entirely too large for their small body, any movement is quickly found to be far too tiring, resulting in long periods of rest.
Offspring are assisted by their parents with constant preening and grooming, an action chicks appreciate heavily, as signified through their affectionate trills in between naps.
Alongside keeping warm, a chick's top priority is ensuring they stay well fed. As soon as they sense a parent approaching, chicks scurry to the front of the nest and squall loudly for attention, pushing and shoving against their sibling in an effort to be fed first.
As chicks age, they begin to notice a difference in attention provided towards themselves and their siblings. If a chick realizes itself to be favoured, and does not feel like sharing their parent’s affection, they may begin acting differently towards their sibling, shoving it about, and vocalizing in dissatisfaction, encouraging their parents to outcast it.
Chicks may make this known by frequently bullying their sibling in front of their parents. Crying, screeching and aggressively wailing at their sibling, accompanied with shoving and pecks are common tactics.
As chicks reach Juvenile, their bodies soon catch up with their wings, allowing for more movement. They begin stretching their wings, jumping and hopping around the perimeter of the nest, steadily increasing their strength in preparation for gliding.
Chicks begin inquisitively playing with the various trinkets their parents gather for them, and often compete against their sibling to hoard particularly favoured items. This action may ignite scraps, though fights are never fatal.
Once prompted by their parents, adolescent Microraptors will be pushed to leave the nest and begin gliding to other trees and cliffs nearby. They carefully aim for sturdy tree trunks, wide branches, and sturdy rocks, climbing to elevated heights to then practise gliding back to their nests.
After considerable practice, chicks are deemed experienced enough to explore the world outside the nesting location. It’s at this point that parents lead offspring across various POIs, steadily introducing new items - and, most importantly, encouraging their offspring to begin mimicking other species they come across.
As a Microraptor reaches Sub-Adult, offspring are steadily ushered into complete independence. If within a scurry, offspring generally choose to remain, though rarely interact with their parents aside from occasionally passing affectionate calls.
Offspring that reach maturity within a pair grouping are made to disperse, first encouraged with gentle shoves, and then escalation to aggressive displays if the message is not made clear enough the first time.
✦ Orphaned Behavior (Wild Spawn)
Orphaned chicks often hatch on the forest floor, without the usual nest that provides warmth and safety during early stages of life. Chicks instinctively crawl towards safety, nestling between foliage or debris in an attempt to regulate their body temperature.
Once a spot has been secured, the chick remains relatively stationary until Juvenile, practicing only minor movements during the few hours they are awake each day. These small bouts of exercise eventually build up, and as their need for continuous sleep decreases, chicks stray further and further away from their temporary nest, seeking out food, and eating near anything that they can grab with their beaks.
These chicks are instinctively silent, lacking the parental figure needed to reinforce the idea that being loud is a rewarding behaviour.
Due to having no positive association to outside noise, orphaned chicks treat unusual sounds as a potential threat. They are naturally skittish, and as a result, often sprint to the nearest dense bush or plentiful cover available to hide when they feel unfamiliar with a situation.
Upon reaching adolescence, the orphaned chick is driven by compulsion to climb up nearby trees for safety and for better scouting opportunities. They cautiously glide from tree to tree, continuously seeking objects of interest, such as food, trinkets, other valuables, to find comfort in.
The life of an orphaned Microraptor is not an easy one. Once the chick has reached Sub-Adult, they have typically gained enough confidence and mastered their basic survival skills to leave the grounds that raised them, and seek new lands to thrive in.
Many orphaned Microraptors end up socially behind as adults, but offer unique habits that may be otherwise intriguing to others of their kind.

General Behavior

Microraptor are generally curious, but equally fearful creatures, perfectly capable of distinguishing when encounters may result in injury, and quick to disengage when that risk is present.
These tiny dromeosaurids spend most of their time hopping and gliding from tree to tree or clinging to the edges of cliffs, observing their surroundings from a safe height.
Microraptor are cautious, especially towards species they have never seen before. At the first sign of aggression towards them, they flee, typically accompanied with fearful cries.
Microraptor are most commonly known for their mimicry of other species.
When mimicking the call of a creature, they often call right back to the creature, very quickly memorizing the sound, and then repeating it in quick succession to familiarize themselves with the noise.
Microraptor enjoy mimicking the calls of creatures in their surrounding area. When confronted by a species, and tolerated, Microraptor may take part in "conversations", in which they repeatedly parrot back calls made at them.
Microraptor may go as far as to mimic the movement and actions of other species, though are quick to stop if agitation follows.
Microraptor, for as small as they are, have an excellent memory, often recalling not only calls they’ve heard, but past experiences they have had with certain species.
Microraptor have a general disdain for water, though are still regarded as clean creatures. Landing on sandy clearings and banks, Microraptor will use their small noses to toss sand around in order to take dust baths.
Microraptor interact with any species that tolerates them, though do not linger long and do not form bond with any species other than Apex Carnivores.

✦ Downtime behaviour
Microraptor sleeps in short bursts - this is always high up in a tree or cliff. Caves and small tunnels are far too constricting and do not offer the creature the same freedom.
Microraptor with carnivore companions who migrate often will cling to their sides, their heads falling forwards or to one side as they take the time to rest during travel.
In scurries, all Microraptor take time to rest in unison. The group’s chatter fades into near silence as individuals drift to sleep.
Some Microraptor may choose to remain alert while the group rests, quick to rouse the scurry if predators approach.
Microraptor do not function in this way with their carnivore companions and are not sentries. They only react or alert to predators they must flee from.

Social Interaction
✦ Scurry
Scurries are the most volatile of groupings for Microraptor, yet can also be one of the safest options.
Scurries form naturally and often on a whim when lone Microraptor are not interested in searching for carnivore companions.
Microraptor interested in grouping with others attempt to catch the attention of another with small chirps, landing hesitantly near them. If not aggressed out of their personal space immediately, the Microraptor shakes off the initial stress, and allows the other to approach cautiously, inspecting them with interest.
If willing to accept the other, they both shake to loosen the initial tension and demonstrate friendliness. After this point, they may be accepted into the group.
Any Microraptor may accept another into the scurry. Other members of the scurry who are nearby may also take part in the greeting and inspection.
Should members of the scurry disagree with the choices of another, they will disperse from the scurry. Sometimes they may take more interest in the ungrouped individual, and chase after them in order to form a scurry of their own.
Scurries can deny other Microraptor for any reason, which can lead to a constantly changing rotation of members.
However, scurries with familial ties are less likely to have such experiences, and find it easier to maintain a consistent grouping.
Within a scurry, there is no clear leader, and migrating can seem rather hectic, especially if there are several new individuals intent on proving themselves.
Scurries often travel to the courtship grounds together, even if all members are not particularly interested in courting.
New objects may be inspected by several members of a scurry at one time - however individual methods vary, often with the object being relocated frequently in a desperate attempt to get a better view.
This action may spark disagreements if others feel they have not had adequate time to observe the object, inspiring short bursts of constant picking up and moving of the item around between scurry members.
Vocal outbursts soon follow with prolonged bickering - though irritation never leads to biting.
Many objects cannot be carried for long, as most are too heavy for the feeble Microraptor.
Disagreements do not last long, as Microraptor eventually grow bored and move on to other activities.
✦ Pairs
Microraptor in pairs are able to enjoy the company of each other as well as that of Apex Carnivores.
Pairs may consist of any relation between two Microraptor, guiding the direction of the interactions.
Siblings are more prone to bickering over the smallest of issues - scraps of food, which side of a companion they're latched onto, or where to cross between POIs.
Two uncourted individuals may only travel together briefly, benefiting from the company momentarily, but eventually going separate ways - or deciding to visit the courtship grounds together to make the bond official.
Courted pairs are typically much more agreeable with each other and bicker far less, happily investigating objects together and exchanging mimicked calls to strengthen their bond.
Pairs of Microraptor are more likely to remain with the same carnivore companion for longer than a solitary individual would.
✦ Solitary Behavior
Though Microraptor have the general tolerance to associate with others, many individuals choose solitary lifestyles where they are less likely to be bothered by persistent noise and bickering.
This action is common in orphaned individuals, but equally, those who grew up with an unusual number of siblings may prefer the tranquility of it in contrast to their upbringing.
Solitary individuals find increased happiness in following other species about, straying from their claimed carnivore to investigate other options and more frequently switching between choices on a whim.
Despite this, even the most solitary Microraptor seeks occasional stimuli from others of their own kind. As such, despite often having no interest in courtship, solitary individuals may choose to gather at the courting tree within Big Tree Overlook, simply to marvel and gawk at others.
Both female and male Microraptors actively choose to linger within the audience, rather than partake in courtship attempts down below.
However, persistent visits may sometimes spark a reluctant interest in some otherwise solitary Microraptors, transitioning them into forming a pair when particularly impressed by a display.
✦ Albino & Melanistic Behavior
Mutated Microraptor face no challenges in interacting with others of their kind, freely courting and grouping with others.
However, these individuals are more often found in regions with denser foliage to avoid the effects of the sun.

Aggressive Interaction

Outside the below circumstances, Microraptor are generally tolerant to those outside their grouping, so long as personal space is respected.

✦ Claimed Carnivores
Pairs of Microraptor and solitary individuals may claim carnivore companions, fiercely driving out rival Microraptors from approaching the same group.
This applies to the entire group of carnivores, not just a sole individual.
Groups of 2+ Microraptors are generally ignored by pairs, with the understanding that large clusters of their own kind do not claim companions.
Microraptor become incessantly loud to grab the attention of nearby rivals once spotted. If the rivals are interested in attempting to claim the companions for themselves, they land within two body lengths of the claimed carnivore and face off with the rival, spreading their wings wide to provoke the opposing party.
If the opposing party wishes to accept, they land nearby and mimic the action - at which point, a territory challenge is issued towards the opposing pair from either side.
All challenge calls, including the accepting call from the opposing party, must be issued from the ground in order to make it clear to both parties that a challenge is taking place.
Broadcasts and aggressive calls must be done with the natural sound of the Microraptor - mimicked calls may not be used when issuing challenges.
Microraptor who are uninterested, already have claimed carnivores of their own, or have lost the challenge, move on quickly, and do not linger in the area out of spite.

✦ Nesting Territory
When nesting, pairs of Microraptor claim individual trees or cliff ledges. Otherwise, members in a scurry claim only the general location around their nest.
Microraptor only issue territory challenges to other Microraptor if their claimed space is landed on, but may begin to aggress sooner in an attempt to warn others that this is their territory.
In a scurry, these challenges are still issued to signify claims. Nesting pairs, even within a scurry, do not tolerate other Microraptor close to their nest despite previous relations.
Females in a harem will extend this same behaviour towards each other.
Challenges are only accepted if another pair desires to use the same location for nesting.
If a losing pair of Microraptor have live offspring within their nest, the parents will carry them to safety, forfeiting frequently between carrying to request the winning pair provide them with leniency.
The winning pair will permit the losers to gather their chicks, but will not tolerate further aggression, and may continue to attack if provoked, potentially resulting in death.

Cross-Species Interaction

Depending on the grouping type, Microraptor react differently to some carnivores.
However, all Microraptor flee from Hatzegopteryx, Quetzalcoatlus, Megalania, Kelenken, Thalassodromeus and Tropeognathus.

✦ Carnivore Companions
Solitary or pairs of Microraptor are often tolerated by larger carnivores. These scavengers in low numbers do not draw attention to themselves, inhibit the carnivores, or place burden on the surrounding resources.
Scurries are not tolerated, as too many Microraptor may draw unwanted attention to the carnivore.
Microraptor only attempt to create bonds with Apex Carnivores.
When attempting to bond with a larger carnivore, Microraptor does not immediately latch onto them.
Upon finding a new individual they would like to bond with, Microraptor follow closely in order to acclimate the carnivore to their presence; making soft, curious chirps to hesitantly test the tolerance of the individual. Though they may attempt to remain subtle, the sound of their own wings often give them away.
Over time, Microraptor close the distance, though it will not immediately latch on if this action is tolerated.
Instead tolerance is regarded with some hesitancy, outweighing their interest in bonding. Microraptor are aware that even just one miscalculated move may result in serious injury, or death.
After half a day's cycle has passed without aggression displayed, Microraptor begin to feel confident in having earned a carnivore’s trust enough to latch on.
Should this be accepted, Microraptor chirps happily and resumes clinging to their side.
If the carnivore begins to shake them off or aggresses them, Microraptor will promptly remove themself from the carnivore and silently move on to experiment with a different individual.
If attempting to bond with a group of carnivores, Microraptor does not have to repeat this process with each individual. If there are both tolerant and intolerant individuals, the intolerant individuals will be avoided, with Microraptor only interacting with the tolerant.

Once they are past this stage, a new relationship is formed, though this is primarily one-sided towards the Microraptor.
Once permitted to latch onto their companions, Microraptor - if they have not already done so - begin to pick up on the calls of the carnivore.

Microraptor may not use any buff calls or attempt to assist their companions in any way during engagements and may not assist them during hunts.
Microraptor may not use mimicked vocal calls during challenges to help their companion issue, accept, or forfeit challenges.
Microraptor latched onto their companions grow distressed when the carnivore begins to approach the edge of deep water, unwilling to be submerged and waterlog their feathers.
If the Microraptor has been bonded long enough, the tiny dromaeosaurid will cry out to persuade its companion to cross in shallower areas or areas where the crossing is narrower.
Crossing large bodies of water may cause them extra distress - the unhappy Microraptor often makes their displeasure known while having to glide across.
Microraptor who have experienced a companion species nesting previously may opt to time their nesting similarly - their own offspring able to disperse with the offspring of their companion, allowing offspring better success in surviving on their own.

Microraptor born beside a parent’s companion species may grow to prefer this lifestyle, and rarely seek out scurries.
Should a carnivore companion die in an engagement or of old age, Microraptor who have spent a considerable amount of time with the carnivore may attempt to rouse the corpse if it is not already claimed by another species.
Microraptor may even use its mimicked calls or “Cheer Up” near the corpse in an attempt to receive a response from their dead companion. Nips and futile shoving at the body may occur in frustration before the Microraptor eventually gives up, forced to move on.
If the body is already claimed, Microraptor seeks safety nearby and may cry out briefly, before moving on quickly.
In some cases, Microraptor may even turn to their previous companion’s killer, desperately working to form a new bond.

✦ Scurries encountering other species
Microraptor are known for their hyperaggression in retaliation to either themselves or their groupmates being hunted.
When a member of a scurry is attacked, Microraptor will climb up high, swoop, and dive at their aggressor. The rest of the scurry - often witnessing this behavior - join in until the entire scurry is swarming the attacker.
Learned vocal ability calls are not used against the attacker, their noisy defence signaled by their own broadcasts and aggressive calls during this time.
Once the aggressor flees or forfeits the hunt, or is able to kill a Microraptor, the scurry will stop attacking and return to perch in high places, opting to watch their attacker’s retreat momentarily before resuming as normal. The goal is to harass the hunter until they give up, never going as far as to continue pursuing once they have left the vicinity.
If intruders appear to be ignoring them, Microraptor grow relentless, scaling their intimidation display by introducing the mimicked calls of other species in retaliation.
Scurries still flee from feared species and do not retaliate against Apex Carnivores, instead calling out in distress before moving out of reach.
✦ Nesting Site Defence
When any species intrudes on a nesting site, both scurries and pairs react in the same way. This includes species Microraptor fear.
Apex Carnivores may be excluded in this at the discretion of the Microraptor.
Microraptor consider a nesting site claimed once they have courted and placed a nest in their desired location.
Any species that intrudes on a claimed tree or cliff edge is met immediately with loud, screeching protests, with members swooping overhead repeatedly and aggressing the intruder. Small pecks may even be aimed at the intruder in irritation.
Microraptor, towards other species, may begin to harass and aggress another species if they are within one body length of their claimed area.
In scurries - even if the Microraptor has not claimed the tree or cliff edge for themselves - join in on the mobbing behavior. While assisting in defending their nesting site, Microraptor do not aggress other Microraptor from their claimed site.
Pairs of Microraptor exhibit the same behavior towards species outside their claimed carnivore companions, if they have them.
Microraptor are unable to issue territory or nesting challenges towards other species, as their small size does little to intimidate others. Instead, the tiny carnivore relies on harassment to deter the intruder away.
If the species or individual returns within a body length, Microraptor may continue to harass.
✦ Deinonychus & Latenivenatrix
Microraptor have an innate hatred for these species, and seek to raid their nests should they come across them, should they be at the correct hunting hunger.
However, any form of physical retaliation from these groups prompt Microraptor to flee.

Hunting Strategy

Up to 2 Microraptor may work together during a hunt.
Microraptor may opt to hunt attended or unattended juvenile or below offspring of Deinonychus, Latenivenatrix, Psittacosaurus, and Struthiomimus.
Microraptor may stalk and observe these species from a distance, tucking themselves high up into trees or cliff ledges while waiting for the opportune moment to strike.
While Microraptor are relatively quiet hunters, they are extremely advantageous when it comes to using their unique ability to mimic the calls of other creatures.
When working within a pair, oftentimes one Microraptor will attempt to lure adults away by mimicking calls of their own kind, serving as the perfect distraction, and allowing their partner to infiltrate the nest without issue.
Equally, Microraptor may observe and utilize the calls of other species in an attempt to distract their prey into moving away from their nests.
Microraptor within a scurry do not intervene or attempt to assist with calls when 2 individuals are already engaged. Instead, individuals watch silently, observing and collecting information that may prove beneficial for their next hunt.
When hunting Rhamphorhynchus, tactics vastly change. Microraptor become relentless, racing in active pursuit, attempting to dive and bite at the tiny carnivore before it can flee into water.
Microraptor may be attracted to the sound of herbivores, seeking out any Rhamphorhynchus that are nearby them.
Even Rhamphorhynchus that are latched onto herbivores may be attacked.
Microraptor do not consistently hunt the same prey and will move on after a successful hunt, not willing to risk a second encounter when their presence has already been alerted to.
If Lucky Feather activates during a hunt, this is considered an individual forfeit, and the Microraptor must flee.

Courtship & Parenthood
✦ Courtship
Microraptor gather in the south of Big Tree Overlook to court, collecting at the giant tree that looks over The Heights.
Female Microraptor take positions within the branches and large roots that surround the area, silently observing the clearing below.
Male Microraptor claim the clearing below as their stage, enticing onlookers by spreading their wings wide and vocalizing towards the viewing party. They compete fiercely with each other to maintain attention. When simple shoving and aggressive vocals do not work, males take part in non physical - yet highly attractive - challenges against each other, known as a showdown.
Male Microraptor fortunate enough to have no competition within the clearing continue their standard displays as normal.
Once a female Microraptor has made their choice in a male, she swoops down from her branch and approaches him, who continues to eagerly display his wings for her to inspect. If pleased, she will perform a short courtship dance towards him (using the ability “Cheer Up”), with the male returning the gesture if he likes her.
Female Microraptors seeking a same-sex partner partake in passive courting within the audience. Bringing forth gifts and food, they approach spectating females and present the gift, mimicking a male’s display in spreading their wings.
Male Microraptors seeking a same-sex partner purposefully partake in showdowns with other males, often surprising their rival when their fierce demeanour is replaced with affection and admiration.
Once Microraptors have accepted a mate, they depart the courting ground together, spending the oncoming days learning the preferences and habits of their partner.
Some Microraptor females are content to share the same male, forming a temporary harem group with the male and his female mates. This only occurs in scurries.
✦ Showdowns
When two male Microraptor have lost patience in sharing the spotlight with each other, they perform an escalating performance of mimicry to both intimidate their rival, and further capture the audience’s attention.
Facing one another, both males continue posturing by spreading their wings wide and lifting their heads to the sky. The challenge begins with one of the males vocalizing a mimicked call, always beginning with the meekest they can recall, akin to that of a small-tier species.
The opposing Microraptor produces a similar call of their own in response, adhering to the tier set by the first male. If successfully met, the first male responds back, either to another call within the same tier, or escalating their mimicry to a larger tier if they have no further calls in the same tier, and the cycle continues.
A male that rushes the process, either by starting with their loudest call, or not allowing for the calls to build-up appropriately, instantly lose the crowd’s favour, seen as inexperienced and immature by spectating females.
The showdown continues for as long as necessary, until one male has no choice but to escalate to an apex-tier call. If one male is able to produce an apex-tier call, and the other isn’t, then the first male is deemed the winner of the showdown.
If both males are able to produce a replication of an apex-tier call, it is entirely up to a female’s preference in whose performance she had been most captivated by.
Female Microraptor judge by different aspects within a showdown, but commonly attractive traits are:
The male able to replicate the most calls before producing an apex-tier call.
The male able to produce the most unique calls, particularly from species harder to come by.
After each vocalization, males posture as they await their rival’s response.
A male will never replicate the same call twice in a single showdown.
If at any point, a male wishes to forfeit the showdown, he will do so by fear calling and retreating a short distance, allowing for the opposing male to bask in his victory.
✦ Nesting
Microraptor nest in trees and atop cliffs, with higher seen as better and safer from predators. These sites are protected from other intruding Microraptors, in the form of a territory challenge.
Harem groups place their nests relatively close to one another, but females sharing the same male will not tolerate the other female too close to her own nest.
Nests are heavily decorated with items of interest, providing their future offspring with as much entertainment as possible during their youth.
Clutches are small, and typically consist of only 2 eggs, where one is deemed as the main egg, and the other as a spare - in case the first does not hatch.
Nesting Microraptor instinctively take on differentiating roles based on their gender.
Male Microraptor are typically happier to stray further from the nest, and as such, take on the role of feeding himself, his pair and his offspring.
If a male has a harem, he splits his time between the different females nesting with him and their nests.
Female Microraptor take on a more observation role, staying within the general area, incubating the eggs, and only straying to collect interesting items and trinkets to further decorate the nest with.
✦ Parenthood
Once eggs have hatched, parents quickly get to work drying their offspring off, preening their feathers and rewarding the chicks with attention and affection at every milestone.
Parents are at their most pleased when only one of their two eggs hatch, successfully providing them with a single chick that they can dote on.
In instances where the second egg hatches alongside the first, both chicks are cared for regardless, though the chick deemed as the main is prioritized, including in regards to feeding, cleaning and gifting.
In rare instances, the main baby may display signs of displeasure with having to share their parent’s attention with a sibling. In some cases, but not all, the parents may choose to outcast the spare baby to provide their main baby with the best possible chances.
Though cruel, parents will never go as far as directly killing their offspring. Instead, parents gently shove the spare baby out the nest, encouraging it to glide, or rather tumble, to nearby foliage, away from the nesting location.
At this point, the baby will either be left completely alone, as though it were an orphan, or still cared for infrequently, fed with anything the main baby rejects.
This is an opportune moment for same sex pairs to adopt an orphaned baby, and is an ideal scenario for these rejected chicks.
While both parents leave frequently, offspring will rarely be left alone in the nest, unless both parents have had to leave to protect the territory from rival Microraptors.
However, chicks born to a father that currently has a harem may find themselves alone every so often, as their father is forced to help care for multiple nests at once.
Once chicks reach adolescence, both parents partake in encouraging further independence and flight ability. Perching on a tree, they will glide across one surface to another, waiting expectantly for their offspring to mimic them. Efforts are highly praised upon, and parents may spend several hours a day improving their chick’s mobility and confidence through games of chase.
When parents feel chicks have progressed enough, they will abandon the nest, and begin slowly introducing offspring to the outside world. Still hesitant to see their offspring on the ground, many parents encourage offspring to continue moving between trees as frequently as possible.
Parents showcase their offspring new pois, items, and even species, demonstrating appropriate reactions to each, and most importantly, the art of mimicry.
✦ Offspring Maturity
Once offspring reach sub-adult, parent’s reactions vary depending on if they are a pair, or a part of a scurry.
If within a scurry, parents signify their lack of interest by shooing their offspring away, encouraging offspring to interact with others members of the group. Their previous affection largely disappears, though they may acknowledge kinship with an occasional affectionate call in passing.
If a pair, parents will encourage their offspring to disperse, beginning with gentle shoves, and then increased aggressive display if the decision is not respected.
✦ Adoption
Same-sex paired Microraptor within scurries may adopt, often swooping in and stealing a rejected chick when they feel the urge to raise offspring.
Otherwise, Microraptor do not adopt, and continuous unwanted advances into personal space are met with aggression.

Elderly

Aging Microraptor begin to notice their inability to glide and fly for as long as they once were able to.
Their ability to latch onto surfaces weakens, and they are more often seen tumbling to the ground, either through missing their mark, or losing significant grip strength.
Some Microraptor shift their preferences in carnivore companion, preferring the territorial Tyrannosaurus or male Tyrannotitan that allows them to remain stationary.
A Microraptor’s feathers may dull with age.

✦ Death
With their deteriorating ability to latch onto surfaces, most Microraptor find escape from predators difficult and succumb to terrestrial hunters - or are simply unable to move out of the way of passing creatures.
Microraptor who can sense their waning time often seek out soft, mossy beds on the forest floor or dense underbrush to rest in, curling up to preserve any of their lasting warmth.
Lifelong bonded pairs will often pass together, though when one remains alive, they will often call out in distress and attempt to rouse their passing partner.

Microraptor do not taunt hunters who have failed a hunt against them.

Profile - Quick View
Microraptor are Tiny Cathermal Terrestrial Carnivores.
Microraptor can group up to 10 sub/adults and unlimited offspring. Microraptor do not adopt as a rule, but same sex pairs within a scurry may adopt rejected chicks.
Microraptor are tolerant.
Microraptor can have up to 2 in a hunt, and 10 in a defence.
Microraptor may hunt attended or unattended Deinonychus, Latenivenatrix, Psittacosaurus, and Struthiomimus offspring of juvenile and below, and Rhamphorhynchus.
Microraptor may scavenge.
Microraptors are curious creatures with a unique gift in mimicking other species, a skill they use in both hunting and courtship.
Many Microraptors have vastly different preferences in the company they choose to keep, whereas more socially confident individuals choose to collect in large groups, known as scurries, those who prefer quieter lives remain with a pair, or completely solitary.
Microraptors, much like another familiar tiny-tier, find great company in other species, though lean heavily on the carnivorous variety. Oftentimes, this bond is one-sided, providing the Microraptor with far more advantages than it is able to return.
