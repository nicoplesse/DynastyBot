# Quetzalcoatlus – Offspring Behavior (Nested)

Quetzalcoatlus offspring, known as chicks, typically hatch alone without parent supervision, with the only company that they have being their siblings. It is common for siblings to scrabble with one another for space and their parent’s attention. The largest and strongest tends to be the most successful at getting itself fed, so siblings are highly competitive with one another. Since Quetzalcoatlus nests are placed in high places, some squabbles may result in smaller Quetzalcoatlus chicks to be shoved from the nest, resulting in less competition for the larger chicks and less mouths to feed for the parents. The corpse of the fallen chick may also be fed from at times. Squabbles amongst chicks will only ever result in a chick dying if the chicks are desperately hungry, 25% hunger or lower. Otherwise, these fights will rarely go below half-health, as overly violent chicks risk being shoved out. Both parents will be present in rearing offspring - but even then, they only watch as their children peck and shove one another. Quetzalcoatlus parents are not affectionate and eagerly await the day where they do not have to attend to their squawking mischief-makers. Chicks will remain in the nest or on the cliffside they hatched on until Juvenile. At this point, their parents will teach them how to fly by encouraging them to jump or by fully pushing them off the cliff. Chicks take to flying quickly, and find it exhilarating. They will fly around each other, learning how to avoid colliding with other Quetzalcoatlus and terrain, and learning how to land safely. Some chicks take more time to learn, and some go through one or two rough landings before getting the hang of it. Chicks that try to run to their mothers for comfort, will instead get clacked at. Once the chicks can fly, parent Quetzalcoatlus will begin teaching their young how to hunt, sending them off on critters so that they don’t have to worry about feeding them. Chicks seeking their parents approval will often bring scraps of their kills to the parents. Some chicks will attempt to steal each other’s kills in order to receive parental approval. The father may leave the nest as soon as the chicks reach adolescence, with the mother staying behind longer. This is often to seek other females or hunting opportunities further afield. Fathers will often only remain at the nest if continuously tolerated by the female. If a Quetzalcoatlus chick survives to adolescence, they will begin to venture away from the nest, exploring the nearby land; pecking at bugs or sneaking bites of meat from their mother’s kills. A mother will tolerate this from the strongest or oldest chick but from none others. As the Quetzalcoatlus chick continues to grow, a mother Quetzalcoatlus will eventually grow restless and bored, moving on and away from her previous chicks to stretch her wings and maybe find another mate. Some sub-adults can chase after their mothers if there is space, but most will not.
