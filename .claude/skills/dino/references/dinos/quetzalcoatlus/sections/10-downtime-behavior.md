# Quetzalcoatlus – Downtime Behavior

Quetzalcoatlus are often seen roosting communally, and most often in the early morning or at night, when they must wait for the sun to generate rising thermals to make it easier for flight. Quetzalcoatlus may also be seen roosting immediately after a filling meal, preening and sunning themselves to rid of parasites. These roostings most often occur on tall trees or cliffsides. Resting periods are brief and common, as a lack of sleep makes coordination especially when in flight difficult. Most Quetzalcoatlus are selfish creatures, preferring to feed themselves over the weak or young. Males may attempt to impress females at times with displays of “generosity” bringing them food or items that they think are interesting. As perpetuated in their youth, Quetzalcoatlus will always squabble with other Quetzalcoatlus, especially over prime portions of a corpse or the pride of a kill. These bickering matches tend to be through pecking, clacking their beaks, and more often than not result in the group splitting afterwards. As sub-adults, Quetzalcoatlus may group with any surviving siblings, but will abandon them at any opportunity of a mate (unrelated opposite sex individual). Close or bonded Quetzalcoatlus will preen each other’s necks and sunbathe together, ensuring that every perch they land on always has room for the other. Males will often squabble with each other, especially over prey or a particularly attractive female or hunting opportunity. They may group with other males in order to hunt. Females are most likely to form longer-lasting relationships with other females; particularly with daughters who prove themselves to be the strongest of their clutch, but these relationships largely remain temporary. At some point, they will separate, relying on pure chance and the wind to bring them together again. Quetzalcoatlus may fly together for a while if a particularly exciting hunting opportunity arises that would be best to be taken on by a committee of Quetzalcoatlus. Quetzalcoatlus will clack their beaks at each other in irritation, especially if their personal space is intruded or they feel as if the other’s behavior is unsatisfactory. Simple pet peeves, or disappointing hunts, may result in a committee splitting up. Quetzalcoatlus will not be aggressive towards other ungrouped Sub-Adult/Adult Quetzalcoatlus, unless protecting a kill. While up to 3 Quetzalcoatlus may group at a time, larger flocks of Quetzalcoatlus may calmly co-exist with each other, watching over herds and hunts. Quetzalcoatlus will clack their beaks and broadcast loudly if aggressing another carnivore, herbivore, or occasionally another Quetzalcoatlus (while defending a carcass) – they will flare their wings to display their power and even do mock attacks on trees, rocks, or the ground to display the power of their beak. If squabbling with another Quetzalcoatlus over food or in front of a female, they will peck, squawk, and beat one another with their wings, until the other party flees or is killed. A Quetzalcoatlus who has defeated another in this way has no issue feeding from the corpse, no matter relationships with the now dead individual. Quetzalcoatlus will follow all kinds of herbivore herds, looking for unattended young or the old or sickly to take advantage of. If solo and repeatedly aggressed away by a Large or higher creature, they will leave in order to pursue an easier hunt. If grouped, Quetzalcoatlus will distance themselves, unless aggressed by an Apex herbivore or carnivore, in which case they will fly away. In the event that a Quetzalcoatlus is stalking an albino large or apex that is a part of a herd or group, they will not respond to the aggression, staying still and watching. Quetzalcoatlus will also follow groups of Medium carnivores in order to steal their hunts. Quetzalcoatlus will always terrorize Compsognathus groups if seen. This can be a display of power for males, or a feeding opportunity for females. Quetzalcoatlus will avoid Hatzegopteryx if solo, showing nervousness and anxiety. However, if there are more Quetzalcoatlus than Hatzegopteryx, or unattended Hatzegopteryx offspring, Quetzalcoatlus are more likely to begin a hunt. They tend to avoid these larger winged creatures due to their size and ferocity, and will choose the easy way out rather than starting a fight. At times, orphaned Hatzegopteryx will imprint on Quetzalcoatlus. Female same sex pairs are more likely to adopt them as strange Quetzalcoatlus chicks, but will otherwise eat them. If a Hatzegeroptyx chick is adopted by a female pair of Quetzalcoatlus, they will be treated as any other Quetzalcoatlus chick until reaching Sub-Adult when the differences become too obvious to ignore. Quetzalcoatlus will bully Rhamphorhynchus by stealing their fish, even if unable to eat them. These situations rarely if ever escalate into violence, as the Quetz will give up the fish to the Rhamphorhynchus when the incessant squawking becomes too irritating.
