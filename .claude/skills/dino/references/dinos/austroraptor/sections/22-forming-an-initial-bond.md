# Austroraptor – Forming an Initial Bond

Austroraptor seeking their lifelong bond will migrate to Volcano Islands or Red Island Pond in order to court. At the courtship grounds, males greet new individuals via bowing their heads, lifting their tails, and extending their wings, switching from foot to foot. This acts as indication that they are male, an interest in courting the other individual, and as a beginning to the performance, all in one motion. If the male greets another male, he will return the posture, and both will shake off before moving off to find females. To accept the male’s advances, a female will issue soft trilling calls towards him. This will begin the rest of the courtship process. To reject a male, a female will turn her back and move away. This is a respected motion by both parties, and neither will try to court the other again. Acceptance starts the male’s performance - in which he rushes into the water and begins to broadcast, swimming as fast as possible, diving in and out of the water, and stretching out his wings. Once a female is satisfied with the performance, she will move into the water and approach the male, trilling excitedly. A female who is bored of a male’s performance, or not interested, will move away to watch a different performance. The male, when approached, will trill excitedly in return. The pair will then nuzzle one another, and dive into the water together, beginning their first shared swim, and their first fishing trip together as a new pair. Same Sex Female Pairs: Females interested in other females will pace the beaches, and will approach other females to introduce themselves. They will do so with soft calls and gentle movements, and females who are interested in courting will mimic with soft coos of their own. The initiating female will softly trill and coax the other towards the water, looking back to ensure the other is following. If the female is still interested, she will follow. If not, she will sit down as a show of gentle refusal. The females will swim into the water together, calling to one another happily. The pair will only break apart in effort to find fish for one another. Once this is done, they are considered lifelong bonded. If a female pair decides they would like to nest, they may return to the courtship grounds to find a suitable male. This follows typical opposite sex courtship, and only one female in the pair will court a male. Once courtship is complete, the other female will quickly rush to block the male from following her mate, making intense eye contact. Males quickly pick up on this signal and back off, and may return to the courtship grounds to court a different female. Same Sex Male Pairs: Males interested in other males will greet each other as normal. If both males take interest, a courting display is done by both males as they simultaneously jump into the water with excitement. This courtship display is filled with calls, dashing and diving about in the water, and often circling each other. Once satisfied with their performance, they will breaking apart, finding fish for one another. After exchanging fish with excited trills, they are considered lifelong bonded. Male pairs do not form temporary bonds with a female, but may still travel to the nesting grounds and make a nest in the chance that a chick sneaks in or is dropped into their nest when they are away. Lifelong Pairbonds: Once a bond has been established, a pair does not have to return to the courting grounds in order to nest. Instead, one of the individuals in the pair will indicate interest in courtship by intensely preening the other. If the other is interested as well, this behavior will be returned. Once returned, the pair will set off on a fishing trip. Once both individuals in the pair have caught a fish, they will present it to each other. They will both eat the fish that the other has caught, and trill to one another happily.
