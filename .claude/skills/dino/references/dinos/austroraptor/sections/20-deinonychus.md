# Austroraptor – Deinonychus

Deinonychus, easily grabbed by Austroraptor, are entertaining prey. Austroraptor attempt to grab and run to the water with a Deinonychus in their mouth, quickly submerging the smaller raptor and shaking it. Should the smaller raptor get free and begin to swim to shore, Austroraptor will nip and taunt before grabbing hold of it once more to prevent its escape, inevitably killing it. Austroraptor are cautious creatures and will only begin a hunt if they feel they can overwhelm their prey. Before a hunt, Austroraptor carefully assess their chosen prey, quietly chattering to each other or remaining silent. Once they begin a hunt, Austroraptor surround their prey and call loudly in an attempt to disorient and overwhelm. They move in quickly with harsh bites and kicks to wear down their target. Austroraptor mouths are particularly toxic; and when they land a bite, this bite often comes with unpleasant side effects for their victim. Primarily, Austroraptor will aim to drown their prey if they are able to pick it up. Pairs will take turns picking up the prey in their mouths and taking them back into the water. The opposite is true for Leedsichthys pups, which they will carry to the shore instead. If unable to do so, they will pick up their prey and smash it into the ground and against rocks; this is especially done with Anodontosaurus, Desmatosuchus, and Peloroplites that are young enough to be carried.
