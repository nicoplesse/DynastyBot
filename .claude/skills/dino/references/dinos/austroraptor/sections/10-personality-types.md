# Austroraptor – Personality Types

Austroraptor exhibit different personalities naturally throughout their childhood and into their adulthood. Some individuals may show more dominant behaviors, and others more submissive behaviors; with a few individuals having behaviors that vary dependent on the situation. All Austroraptor have unique personalities, but these are particularly pertinent examples that develop and remain throughout the entirety of their lives, from hatchling to elder. Dominant Personality Type: Austroraptor with a more dominant personality are frequently seen broadcasting, posturing, and flaring their feathers dramatically. They are much more physically expressive than other Austroraptor, and use eye contact and body language to take control of situations. In order to show their dominance towards other Austroraptor, Austroraptor with a dominant personality straighten themselves out, slowly stepping forward as they make intense eye contact with the other individual. Austroraptor with this personality type additionally rush to be the first to feed from corpses, even pushing aside their own offspring. Dominant Austroraptor may rush to claim prime perching, fishing, sleeping, and nesting locations, screeching joyfully to declare their ownership of these spots. Dominant Austroraptor are significantly more eager to prove themselves to other Austroraptor and their mate via hunting larger prey, and by defending their mate from threats. Dominant Austroraptor flare their wings, head feathers, and tails often to make themselves more physically imposing, whether it is towards another Austroraptor or a threat. At times, Austroraptor may even point its head directly upwards as method to intimidate. Dominant Austroraptor may also bite and peck at individuals who intrude too closely into their personal space. These bites and pecks are nothing more than a one-time correction, with other Austroraptor immediately giving them their personal space. Submissive Personality Type: Austroraptor with a more submissive personality are much quieter than their dominant counterparts, preferring to remain to the outskirts of social interactions and close to their mates. In order to indicate submission, Austroraptor crouch in front of, turn away from, or look away from dominant individuals, openly avoiding eye contact. Submissive Austroraptor are more relaxed and peaceful, preferring to primarily fish and at times scavenge rather than take part in riskier hunts. Austroraptor with this personality type prefer to keep their feathers smooth and inoffensive, remaining small in comparison to other Austroraptor. They rarely puff up at all. Submissive Austroraptor will also carefully preen other individuals to build bonds and trust, unwilling to take part in riskier behaviors in order to show their abilities.
