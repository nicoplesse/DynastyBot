# Austroraptor – Standoffs

Austroraptor rarely, if ever, escalate to physical violence with other Austroraptor. However, more dominant individuals may wish to reserve the right to a nesting area, fishing spot, or their personal space, and will do this via posturing– standing upright and making intense eye contact. While submissive individuals will crouch, look away, and immediately move away, other dominant individuals may return the gesture, returning the eye contact instead. At this point, both individuals will begin to flare their feathers and call loudly (broadcasting), in effort to drive the other Austroraptor away. If this effort still does not work, they may begin to bite in each other’s direction. At this point, if the individual does not have a mate, they must flee. If the individual does have a mate, they will rush forward and also begin posturing, in order to pose a bigger deterrence. Mates may also opt to rush their mate away from the standoff, particularly those with submissive personalities. This is always respected by their mate, even if begrudgingly. Fights will only break out after both sides have had their mate join the standoff, and neither side has backed off after 30 seconds. If this occurs, both sides must aggressively call twice. Both pairs then may fight each other until one individual in the engagement reaches half health. At this point, a forfeit is declared by repeated fear calls and rushing away. The winners will not chase after this forfeit is issued. After a standoff has completed, the same pairs may not issue another standoff against each other for 30 minutes. Austroraptor are tolerant towards other species, though opt to keep a safe distance from other carnivores unless interested in a hunt. If approached by a group of carnivores, Austroraptor continue to maintain distance. If the carnivores are unable to be hunted by the Austroraptor present, then Austroraptor will flee if aggressed. Austroraptor do not tolerate scavengers. Austroraptor may be observed following herds and carnivore packs long distances, “claiming” them in order to hunt their offspring if they fall behind. They keep a solid distance between them and the herbivores, and hide if aggressed if not actively hunting offspring. Austroraptor pairs work together to get the attention of larger species in order to push logs over. Austroraptor will not accept territory challenges from other species when not at their courtship or nesting grounds. Austroraptor may defend their right to stay in their courtship or nesting grounds, with 4 individuals forming a temporary group to do so.
