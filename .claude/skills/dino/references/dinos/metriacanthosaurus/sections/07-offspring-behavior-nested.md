# Metriacanthosaurus – Offspring Behavior (Nested)

Metricanthosaurus offspring, called polliwogs, are quiet and unassuming from the moment they hatch. Within moments, these hatchlings are able to walk and are mostly self-sufficient, not requiring heavy parental guidance. Hungry once they hatch, polliwogs begin to eat - their first meal being regurgitated mushrooms from their mother. Polliwogs instinctively tumble from the nest the moment they hear their parents footsteps begin to move away; guided by instinct to trail after them. At Juvenile, polliwogs are more aware of their surroundings and regard their environment with light curiosity. Sniffing and inspecting new objects around them, some polliwogs may become distracted and temporarily lose their parents. This is especially dangerous for the smallest siblings, who come to learn quick where they stand with their siblings. At this age, polliwogs begin to establish a hierarchy amongst themselves. Larger polliwogs shove and nip at their siblings to push them off food. A hungry polliwog will take advantage of a smaller sibling who lags behind, attempting to turn it into a meal. The rate of success depends entirely on if the parents intervene. Polliwogs who are corrected do stop this behavior - only attempting again if it feels its parents cannot see. Successful polliwogs may be rewarded with the meal - but equally, may be shoved off the kill by their own parents who eat it for themselves! Properly fed offspring learn quickly that parents are intolerant of them while eating, discovering that the hierarchy extends past their siblings with their parents at the top. Only once the parents have finished eating do polliwogs creep towards the meal, picking off small shreds of meat and gathering parts in their claws, attempting to feed themselves subtly. Herds and carnivore packs are often observed at a distance with extreme curiosity, sometimes causing a distracted polliwog to fall behind. When their parents take interest in hunting, polliwogs distance themselves from their parents to observe them from a safe spot and mimic their actions on the nearest opponent - typically their siblings. At Adolescent, a polliwog’s scales begin to change. Vibrant coloring becomes unmistakable, and serves as the first sign that the young Metricanthosaurus has become poisonous. This is due to toxin provided by the variety of mushrooms their parents have led them to. Polliwogs with the brightest colors may have additionally been fed other Metricanthosaurus. Exhibiting increased independence and no longer as eager to follow their parents, they begin to wander away for periods of time, seeking tiny and small creatures in an attempt to hunt. To assist with this burst of independence, parents no longer tolerate them eating from their meals, nipping at polliwogs if they attempt to feed. With the disinterest and lack of care their parents offer them, some polliwogs even disperse at this age. By Sub-Adult, young Metricanthosaurus risks cannibalization remaining within its parents’ vicinity. They quietly slink off, unwilling to attract the attention of their parents.
