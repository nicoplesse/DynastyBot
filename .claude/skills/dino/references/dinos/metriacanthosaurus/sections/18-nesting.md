# Metriacanthosaurus – Nesting

After courting, Metriacanthosaurus seek out secluded places along coastlands, swamps, or in caves to build their nests. If in an existing pairbond, Metriacanthosaurus do not travel to the courtship grounds again and instead, the female leads her pair to a suitable spot to build her nest. This is the only indication her partner will receive when interested in nesting. Females place seemingly excessive amounts of foliage on and around the nest in order to protect and shield their young. The male attempts to assist with the nest building process, often bringing reeds or branches for the female to arrange, or mushrooms to offer the female. In preparation for hatchlings, female Metriacanthosaurus consume an excessive amount of mushrooms to regurgitate later. Once the nest is complete, the female remains on top of the nest until their eggs begin to hatch. Once polliwogs emerge from their eggs, the female leaves the nest, only calling once or twice to each hatchling before leaving her nesting site.
