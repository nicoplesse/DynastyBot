# Metriacanthosaurus – Other Species

Metriacanthosaurus are tolerant of all other species and rarely show any sort of aggression towards other creatures. Metriacanthosaurus have a high tolerance for Rhamphorhynchus and allow these scavengers to feed from their kills. Metriacanthosaurus also find these little creatures useful for cleaning their scales and allow them in their space to do so An especially noisy Rhamphorhynchus may be grumbled at - or in some cases, hissed away. Metriacanthosaurus do not tolerate other scavengers. 2 sub/adult Metriacanthosaurus may participate in a hunt and hunt up to Large tier. Adolescent Metriacanthosaurus do not participate in their parents hunts. If their parents are not hunting, adolescents may begin a hunt of their own on small or tiny tiers. Metriacanthosaurus always target other Metriacanthosaurus (if smaller than them, or Albino) in line of sight before any other species. Metriacanthosaurus then target Struthiomimus, Citipati, Utahraptor, and Kelenken. Unattended Adolescent and below Therizinosaurus are selected if these other options are unavailable. Metriacanthosaurus take interest in any feathered creatures, typically attempting to hunt these species over other prey if available and within reach. Metriacanthosaurus are quiet observers when interested in hunting and will stare at other species from a distance, remaining motionless even if noticed. Metriacanthosaurus are patient, silent stalkers. Lowering their bodies to the ground once spotting potential prey, they pick through foliage and terrain to conceal themselves as their target’s back is turned. If the prey turns and unintentionally faces Metriacanthosaurus’ direction, Metriacanthosaurus will freeze, only continuing their approach once their prey turns away again. Metriacanthosaurus begins the hunt once they feel they are close enough to burst from their cover and surprise their prey - or if their prey unintentionally comes close to the Metriacanthosaurus. Metriacanthosaurus in a pair are coordinated hunters and sometimes stalk prey from different angles in an attempt to catch their prey off guard from multiple directions. A hunt always begins silently, increasing in noise from the Metriacanthosaurus as the engagement progresses. Metriacanthosaurus are not endurance hunters and will not chase prey through more than two POIs to catch it - much preferring to slowly stalk until they find the right moment to pounce. If spotting Citipati or Struthiomimus beginning to nest, Metriacanthosaurus may linger around the area until eggs begin to hatch. Rushing forward, they attempt to scare the parents away in hopes that the offspring imprint on Metriacanthosaurus, who then lure them from the nest with soft chatter, before selecting one to kill. Metriacanthosaurus sometimes remain hidden, making low chuffs from their hiding places if they spot offspring, mimicking the sound of small critters in the hopes of an overly curious young approaching their hiding spot.
