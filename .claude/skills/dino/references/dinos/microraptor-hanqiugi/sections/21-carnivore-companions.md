# Microraptor (Hanqiugi) – Carnivore Companions

Solitary or pairs of Microraptor are often tolerated by larger carnivores. These scavengers in low numbers do not draw attention to themselves, inhibit the carnivores, or place burden on the surrounding resources. Scurries are not tolerated, as too many Microraptor may draw unwanted attention to the carnivore. Microraptor only attempt to create bonds with Apex Carnivores. When attempting to bond with a larger carnivore, Microraptor does not immediately latch onto them. Upon finding a new individual they would like to bond with, Microraptor follow closely in order to acclimate the carnivore to their presence; making soft, curious chirps to hesitantly test the tolerance of the individual. Though they may attempt to remain subtle, the sound of their own wings often give them away. Over time, Microraptor close the distance, though it will not immediately latch on if this action is tolerated. Instead tolerance is regarded with some hesitancy, outweighing their interest in bonding. Microraptor are aware that even just one miscalculated move may result in serious injury, or death. After half a day's cycle has passed without aggression displayed, Microraptor begin to feel confident in having earned a carnivore’s trust enough to latch on. Should this be accepted, Microraptor chirps happily and resumes clinging to their side. If the carnivore begins to shake them off or aggresses them, Microraptor will promptly remove themself from the carnivore and silently move on to experiment with a different individual. If attempting to bond with a group of carnivores, Microraptor does not have to repeat this process with each individual. If there are both tolerant and intolerant individuals, the intolerant individuals will be avoided, with Microraptor only interacting with the tolerant. Once they are past this stage, a new relationship is formed, though this is primarily one-sided towards the Microraptor. Once permitted to latch onto their companions, Microraptor - if they have not already done so - begin to pick up on the calls of the carnivore. These calls are mimicked back at them upon being learnt. While unsure truly of what these calls mean, Microraptor test for a response and are visibly pleased when the carnivore acknowledges their efforts. Microraptor may stay bonded with their chosen companion for as long as they wish. Younger or underfed individuals often seek out different companions if they feel unsatisfied with their current. When spotting a species that Microraptor fear, individuals do not remain on their companion and instead flee and hide in nearby trees or shrubbery until the threat has passed. When their carnivore companions enter an engagement, Microraptor immediately unlatch and seek safety out of reach. They remain silent until the engagement is entirely over. Only when the engagement is finished will Microraptor latch back onto their carnivore. “Cheer Up” may then be used. Microraptor may use any of its own passive buffs on their carnivore companions prior to an engagement beginning, but may not use vocal calls as an aid before an engagement. Microraptor may not use any buff calls or attempt to assist their companions in any way during engagements and may not assist them during hunts. Microraptor may not use mimicked vocal calls during challenges to help their companion issue, accept, or forfeit challenges. Microraptor latched onto their companions grow distressed when the carnivore begins to approach the edge of deep water, unwilling to be submerged and waterlog their feathers. If the Microraptor has been bonded long enough, the tiny dromaeosaurid will cry out to persuade its companion to cross in shallower areas or areas where the crossing is narrower. Crossing large bodies of water may cause them extra distress - the unhappy Microraptor often makes their displeasure known while having to glide across. Microraptor who have experienced a companion species nesting previously may opt to time their nesting similarly - their own offspring able to disperse with the offspring of their companion, allowing offspring better success in surviving on their own. Microraptor born beside a parent’s companion species may grow to prefer this lifestyle, and rarely seek out scurries. Should a carnivore companion die in an engagement or of old age, Microraptor who have spent a considerable amount of time with the carnivore may attempt to rouse the corpse if it is not already claimed by another species. Microraptor may even use its mimicked calls or “Cheer Up” near the corpse in an attempt to receive a response from their dead companion. Nips and futile shoving at the body may occur in frustration before the Microraptor eventually gives up, forced to move on. If the body is already claimed, Microraptor seeks safety nearby and may cry out briefly, before moving on quickly. In some cases, Microraptor may even turn to their previous companion’s killer, desperately working to form a new bond.
