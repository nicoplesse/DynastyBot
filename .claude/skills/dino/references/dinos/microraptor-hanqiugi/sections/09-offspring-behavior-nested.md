# Microraptor (Hanqiugi) – Offspring Behavior (Nested)

Microraptor offspring, known as chicks, hatch out of their eggs burdened by their oversized wings. Damp, heavy, and entirely too large for their small body, any movement is quickly found to be far too tiring, resulting in long periods of rest. Offspring are assisted by their parents with constant preening and grooming, an action chicks appreciate heavily, as signified through their affectionate trills in between naps. Alongside keeping warm, a chick's top priority is ensuring they stay well fed. As soon as they sense a parent approaching, chicks scurry to the front of the nest and squall loudly for attention, pushing and shoving against their sibling in an effort to be fed first. As chicks age, they begin to notice a difference in attention provided towards themselves and their siblings. If a chick realizes itself to be favoured, and does not feel like sharing their parent’s affection, they may begin acting differently towards their sibling, shoving it about, and vocalizing in dissatisfaction, encouraging their parents to outcast it. Chicks may make this known by frequently bullying their sibling in front of their parents. Crying, screeching and aggressively wailing at their sibling, accompanied with shoving and pecks are common tactics. As chicks reach Juvenile, their bodies soon catch up with their wings, allowing for more movement. They begin stretching their wings, jumping and hopping around the perimeter of the nest, steadily increasing their strength in preparation for gliding. Chicks begin inquisitively playing with the various trinkets their parents gather for them, and often compete against their sibling to hoard particularly favoured items. This action may ignite scraps, though fights are never fatal. Once prompted by their parents, adolescent Microraptors will be pushed to leave the nest and begin gliding to other trees and cliffs nearby. They carefully aim for sturdy tree trunks, wide branches, and sturdy rocks, climbing to elevated heights to then practise gliding back to their nests. After considerable practice, chicks are deemed experienced enough to explore the world outside the nesting location. It’s at this point that parents lead offspring across various POIs, steadily introducing new items - and, most importantly, encouraging their offspring to begin mimicking other species they come across. As a Microraptor reaches Sub-Adult, offspring are steadily ushered into complete independence. If within a scurry, offspring generally choose to remain, though rarely interact with their parents aside from occasionally passing affectionate calls. Offspring that reach maturity within a pair grouping are made to disperse, first encouraged with gentle shoves, and then escalation to aggressive displays if the message is not made clear enough the first time.
