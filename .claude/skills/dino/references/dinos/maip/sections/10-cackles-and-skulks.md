# Maip – Cackles and Skulks

While Maip are not keen on others besides their mate or offspring, they occasionally interact with other Maip. Maip groupings - while usually consisting of a pair of unrelated individuals bonded together with one of their offspring - may also consist of a pair or trio of siblings, grouping after dispersal from their parents. These three, if not seeking to mate, will bond for life very closely. Sibling groups - or Cackles - also end up splitting when one or more of the group members seeks a mate. However, encountering their sibling again will result in excited calls and grooming between the two, indicating their long term memories and recognition. Mated pairs of Maip - known as a Skulk -  become very close and show affection gestures towards one another, even when not raising pups. Play is also common, but females tend to take it too far, prompting panicked shrieks from their mates. Despite this, females back down, not wanting to distress their mate, and are quick to comfort them. When a skulk encounters previously fledged pups from previous nests, they often recognize each other, and may spend time grooming and socializing before separating once more. These moments are brief, but affectionate.
