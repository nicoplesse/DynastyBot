# Maip – Parenthood

Once eggs hatch, both parents rush to the nest and begin a parade of calls -  ranging from coos to cackles - and listen acutely for a response from their pups. Should an elder offspring be present, this offspring joins in the chorus to welcome in their new family members. If their pup is born into the world and silent, the parents will consider the pup sickly, and opt to cannibalize them immediately for the safety of the other pups. However, they never do this if this pup is their only pup in a clutch. Instead, this pup is doted on more so than if it were born in a full brood, hoping to ensure the solo pup's survival. Maip parents (and elder offspring) often leave the nest at the same time, allowing the parents to hunt larger game, but also to avoid bringing attention to the brood. This can be dangerous for pups, but is usually beneficial for the adults. Once pups reach mid-juvenile and nightfall arrives, the nesting site is left behind, and offspring are coaxed out to join their parents in exploring the area around them. Parents happily engage in play with their offspring, enjoying roughhousing and teaching them different calls, and how to hunt. This can be seen with one parent acting as "prey," while the other encourages the brood to stalk, giggle, and pounce. This is important in the formative years, as these skills will be needed in adulthood. At adolescence, parents expect their offspring to accompany them on hunts - though only 2 pups are permitted to assist at the discretion of the parents, and must back off at half health. However, they prefer to have pups watch nearby, careful so that they don't disrupt the hunt for their parents. Keeping pups away from active hunts is observed more often in first time parents, rather than experienced pairs.
