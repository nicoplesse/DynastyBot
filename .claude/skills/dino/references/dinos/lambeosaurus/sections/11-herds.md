# Lambeosaurus – Herds

Lambeosaurus do not have dedicated leaders in groups, and often follow the first individual to set into motion, mimicking their posture and pace. This results in herds moving together as one mass, with fawns kept tucked within the center. Those who straggle or stray from the rest of the herd often panic at being left behind and rush to meet the rest. Lambeosaurus are very social within their herds, and will happily greet any new individual to join the herd, issuing affectionate bleats and shaking at them in greeting. Does tend to exhibit a more dominant personality, with bucks being more shy and submissive. Does are fiercely protective of those they bond with and their own offspring. Lambeosaurus do not mind intrusions to their personal space by other species accepted into their herd, as well as by other Lambeosaurus. In fact, close proximity often soothes a panicked or frightened Lambeosaurus, with individuals bunching closely together when danger appears, or during inactive periods at night. There are always one or two Lambeosaurus, typically males, who watch over the herds as sentries for potential predators both in the daytime and at night. These individuals may be substituted with Struthiomimus or Citipati, depending on the herd's level of calm.
