# Lambeosaurus – Adoption

Lambeosaurus eagerly adopt any Lambeosaurus offspring they find, even if they do not have a suitable mate to share parenthood with, both outside and within their herd. They do not adopt other species, but may tolerate imprinting species such as Citipati. They will not defend an imprinted baby. Adopted Lambeosaurus offspring are treated as biological offspring for all intents and purposes, and are protected fiercely by their adoptive parents. As Lambeosaurus age, they begin to desaturate their colors, and show less interest in courtship and nesting. Elderly Lambeosaurus also tend to exhibit less anxiety as they age, typically due to losing their hearing. Deaf Lambeosaurus tend to fall behind the rest of their herd, resulting in more easily being picked off by predators. Elderly Lambeosaurus are regarded as valued members of the herd, and are typically pushed to the middle of a fleeing herd when it is time to flee. However, no Lambeosaurus will stay behind to defend them if they are unable to keep up.
