# Lambeosaurus – Courtship

During resting periods, Lambeosaurus does are the first to initiate courtship. This is done by the careful and quiet construction of a nest in deep foliage, preferably nearby a water source, and nestled within thick coverage. Building a nest is a careful and meticulous process. Does bring twigs, roots, and reeds to their nests to ensure the nest is comfortable, safe and warm for her future fawns. She takes particular care to fill it with mashed and regurgitated shoots, roots, and berries. Even small decorative items are brought to the nest - both to lure potential males as mates, and to serve as objects of curiosity and play to fawns. Once she is satisfied with her nest, the doe will begin to perform and prance in front of her nest. She will raise onto her back legs, sing and kick her legs in attempt to draw the attention of bucks nearby. She may even unleash series of spinning kicks, lowering her head and kicking her back legs into the air. These performances will only occur during the day. Interested bucks will approach the female and her nest with a gift, typically a food item of some sort. If more than one buck approaches the female, or she is satisfied with the male’s gift, she will pick up the item and eat it, or place it in her nest. She will then affectionately call too and nuzzle the buck, inviting him close to the nest as they wait for the arrival and hatching of their offspring. Does with lifelong bonds will initiate the same process, but will only select her lifelong bonded male.
