# Lambeosaurus – Parenthood

Once fawns hatch, parents make distance from their offspring, leaving them alone in the nest until they are able to move around on their own. They do not sleep nearby the nests, not wanting to risk the safety of their offspring. Parents split bringing the offspring food in the nighttime period after they hatch, but otherwise keep their distance and sleep within the safety of the herd, which has typically moved away from the nests in order to provide the fawns with higher security. Fawns first begin to walk and move from the nest where they rest to their parents, who watch and do not rush to their aid even if they stumble. This brief period without support is essential to the independence and success of their offspring, especially in regards to fleeing and keeping up with their herd. Parents are extremely protective of their offspring, and dote heavily on them, bringing them food whenever they call. They teach their fawns necessary survival tactics from very early on, and will use their bodies to block their fawns from predators and danger. Both mothers and fathers are extremely involved in the rearing of offspring, with fathers typically exhibiting a bit more anxiety over their fawns than mothers. Parents work together on teaching fawns how to swim early on in their lives, and reinforce fleeing and hiding behaviors until they are adults.
