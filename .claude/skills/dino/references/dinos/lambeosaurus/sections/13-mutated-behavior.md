# Lambeosaurus – Mutated Behavior

Albino and Melanistic Lambeosaurus spend much more of their lives in water, using the water to regulate their body temperatures and in some cases avoid sunburn. FREEMARTIN: Freemartins are individuals who were born with a male or female twin in the same clutch, who struggle with infertility and exhibit some behaviors of the opposite sex. This is an optional affliction, but it is common that both twins are freemartins. Freemartin individuals must still display the behavioral greetings aligned with their biological sex. Freemartin Lambeosaurus do not struggle with courtship. Female freemartins have no eggs, and male freemartins only father up to 1 egg. Lambeosaurus exhibit very little real aggression towards each other; rather, focusing on quiet displays of dominance. Does approach one another, getting very close to one another. They will maintain an extended period of eye contact, huffing sharp breaths to indicate their frustration. The first to falter and back off is considered the less dominant individual. These conflicts never turn to loud aggressive calls or blows. Bucks exhibit dominance by performing showy kicks, tail attacks, and punches with their forelegs. Those who move away from a performing male are considered more submissive than the other. However, if a blow lands by accident, both bucks are quick to shake off the stress and separate, with no clear winner. Lambeosaurus are a social and friendly species, even going as far as to share resources with any others that approach. They do not truly “resource guard” food and water sources from any other species, and will even move away once full to avoid conflict. Lambeosaurus tend to give larger ceratopsians such as Styracosaurus, Albertaceratops, Pachyrhinosaurus, and Eotriceratops a wider berth, not wanting to face the sharp ends of their horns. Regardless, they are always tolerant of these herbivores. Large sauropods such as Ampelosaurus, Apatosaurus, and Argentinosaurus tend to frighten most Lambeosaurus due to sheer size alone - but no Lambeosaurus flee from them, only giving them a wide berth. Sentry species, such as Struthiomimus, Camptosaurus, and Citipati, are fondly welcomed into herds. Citipati are not tolerated to pick up fragile Lambeosaurus offspring, but are allowed to watch over the herd while they feed and sleep. Attempts to pick up fawns are quickly responded to with loud, aggressive brays and even kicks from new mothers. Lurdusaurus are viewed as friends similar to Camptosaurus, so are greeted excitedly. However, Lurdusaurus are nowhere near as friendly as Camptosaurus, and typically respond with grumpy and even aggressive calls and grunts. This often results in panic from offending Lambeosaurus, whom quickly flee back to their herd. Other hadrosaurs (& Iguanodon) are happily grouped with and warmly tolerated, and are quickly considered to be a part of their herds.
