# Eurhinosaurus – Courtship & Parenthood

Once ready to court, shoals from all over the world travel to the nesting river of between the POIs of Dark Woods and Young Grove, or Cedrus Forest as shown in the map below. Shoals mix together and group into one larger breeding group, this group is called a school. Eurhinosaurus are passive to all members of their species within these POIs and will travel further away to seek food if resources are scarce, before returning to their chosen nesting area. Both males and females shed into bright courting skins which signifies their desire to court and nest. Females prioritise blue skins, while males prioritise green skins. Males of this species begin the courtship process by beaching themselves on the river edge, vocalising and flapping their fins on the sand or mud. They move up and down the small section of beach and show off their strength and ability to stay out of water for a short period of time. Females who are interested will watch from the water's edge, approaching, splashing, and calling out softly with approval. When the courtship display ends, the male returns to the water and approaches a female nearby that has been calling to him. They circle together and brush their bodies against one another to form a close bond. The female leads him to a section of the riverbed, and builds her nest to lay eggs and allow the male to fertilise them. Females stay near their nest to protect their eggs once laid, but males are promiscuous and will return to the beach to display themselves once more, hoping to attract another female. Once males have finished courting and fertilising eggs, they leave the nesting grounds. A small group of bonded males will usually leave together. Males take no responsibility in child rearing, and return to the ocean long before any offspring hatch. Mothers stay within the nesting grounds with their young until their oldest offspring becomes adolescent. Then, the mothers leave the rivers and swim back to the open ocean with other adult females.
