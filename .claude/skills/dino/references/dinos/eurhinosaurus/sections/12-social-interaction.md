# Eurhinosaurus – Social Interaction

Shoals form strong, lasting bonds with their members, often spending the majority of their lives together. These connections are vital due to their cooperative hunting strategies and mutual reliance for comfort, protection, and survival. With no maximum capacity for shoals, smaller groups may form within a shoal, containing a few closely bonded members usually of the same gender. When two separate shoals encounter each other, they may briefly interact in a friendly manner before moving on. Groups are rarely in conflict, but if two shoals occupy the same area and deplete resources, it can lead to increased tension and potentially aggressive behavior. For this reason, shoals rarely spend more than a few minutes within the same POI as one another. If a shoal is occupying a POI with a reduced number of resources, another shoal may be challenged if they enter the same area. Fights are rare, and challenges involve group members lining up in a defensive position to show the newcomers that they are not welcome to stay. Eurhinosaurus never show aggression to other adults within their nesting grounds.
