# Eurhinosaurus – Offspring Behavior

Eurhinosaurus young, known as fry hatch from their egg completely mobile and talkative. Often locking sights with their mother, they stay particularly close to their mother's tail, feeding from shells and small fish within their nesting areas. When approaching juvenile young fry adjust to the chaos of other young Eurhinosaurus within the nesting areas. Juveniles from all nests often take up games of tag together with slight nips and pushing into each others sides. These games of tag promote key survival instincts and the friendship bonds they need for their survival in the future. Mothers will monitor these games of tag to make sure they never cause too much injury. When the oldest fry in a mother's clutch becomes the age of adolescent, the mother will abandon her offspring and leave the nesting grounds to return back into the oceans. She will seek out her previous shoal members and groups of adult females will leave together. Closely bonded females will sometimes wait at the river’s mouth for her companion to leave her nest. Mothers do not wait for their youngest offspring to mature, they leave all the offspring in their nesting ground, which turns into a creche of youngsters looking out for one another while they continue to grow. At adolescent, siblings or other similarly aged young start to form their own groups, known as shoals and form close friendship bonds with those of the same sex. They swim out with their newly formed shoal, or sometimes choose to branch out on their own. These youngsters will only ever return to the river nesting ground when wishing to nest themselves.
