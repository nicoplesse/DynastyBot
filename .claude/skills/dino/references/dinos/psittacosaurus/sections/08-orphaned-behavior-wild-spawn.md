# Psittacosaurus – Orphaned Behavior (Wild Spawn)

With no parent to feed or protect them, orphaned hatchlings remain utterly silent, suppressing all sound instinctively to avoid drawing the attention of predators. Driven by desperation, hatchlings tirelessly crawl toward any nearby food source, expending every ounce of energy to stave off starvation. At this stage they are indiscriminate feeders, consuming whatever nourishment they can access. When not looking for food, Psittacosaurus seek nearby shelter and remain hidden, waiting for their eyesight to develop enough to better navigate their surroundings. Upon reaching Juvenile, improved vision and greater limb control allow them to move more confidently. They continue feeding whenever possible, and instinctively seek the safety that comes from being part of a group. If no passel is available, Psittacosaurus orphans readily attach themselves to ceratopsian herds, favouring Eotriceratops in particular. Should a herd or passel be unavailable, orphans continue a solitary existence, prioritising foraging and survival above all else. If a passel is eventually found, orphans integrate quickly and continue development as though they had been hatched within the group. As orphaned Psittacosaurus reach Adolescent, they begin testing their growing strength. Digging becomes a frequent activity, as they instinctively prepare escape tunnels to flee from predators. Lacking other Psittacosaurus to groom and maintain their tails, the venom glands of orphans often become permanently blocked. As a result, their quills sharpen through repeated digging, transforming their tails into a highly effective defensive weapon - one that focuses on bleed over inflicting venom to their target. Those that make it to Sub Adult remain remarkably adaptable and confident, capable of integrating into a passel when they find one without many social misunderstandings. Psittacosaurus are a friendly and cooperative ceratopsian species. Living in a world dominated by much larger creatures, their primary objective is survival, and they value protection and safety above all else. From adolescence onward, Psittacosaurus are highly vocal. They actively communicate with one another and other ceratopsian species, using calls both to reinforce social bonds and to alert the passel or herd to potential danger. Skilled diggers, Psittacosaurus leave behind small mounds of disturbed soil and tangled, displaced roots, marking where they have burrowed beneath dense root systems and broken back through the surface. Grooming plays a vital social role within the passel. Psittacosaurus regularly groom one another's tail venom glands, ensuring continued venom production and readiness for defence. Individuals with permanently impacted glands still participate in these rituals, though grooming no longer triggers venom production for them. Those unable to produce venom compensate by brushing their tails against hard surfaces such as boulders, sharpening their quills for defence. When it rains, Psittacosaurus raise their tails to the sky, allowing water to clean their quills. To keep their beaks sharp, Psittacosaurus frequently forage along coastlines and shores for shellfish. Cracking shells not only maintains beak health but also provides a rich and satisfying food source. After consuming it - due to the rich nature of shellfish - Psittacosaurus seek a sheltered area to rest and digest their food. During rest periods and foraging, at least two Psittacosaurus act as sentinels. Sentinels scan the area on their hind legs, looking for nearby allies or threats. Often, these sentinels hop on top of nearby rocks or high places in order to have the best vantage point. On rare occasions, a member of the passel may attempt to consume noxious meat. This results in short term illness and lethargy, prompting the individual to seek shade and rest. Observing this outcome teaches others within the passel to avoid such food sources. When travelling within large mixed-species ceratopsian herds, Psittacosaurus leap toward and cling on to nearby groupmates for added security. The side of a much larger ceratopsian serves as a great shield and is the ultimate preference for Psittacosaurus to ride with. If particularly muddy nuts or roots are discovered while foraging, Psittacosaurus often carry them to the water's edge to wash them clean before eating.
