# Psittacosaurus – Solitary Behavior

When separated from a passel, Psittacosaurus become notably more vocal. They call confidently and persistently, allowing the wind to carry their melodic cries in the hope that other Psittacosaurus will respond. Solitary individuals also employ tactile signals, scraping and brushing their tails against rocks, boulders, trees, and fallen logs to produce sound and scent cues - meant to attract others of their kind. While alone, Psittacosaurus remain alert and cautious, frequently pausing to scan their surroundings before calling again. If a response is heard, solitary Psittacosaurus gradually move toward the sound, calling intermittently until contact is made - or the attempt is abandoned. Prolonged solitude increases the urgency of these behaviors, as Psittacosaurus strongly prefer the protection and stability of a passel over a solitary existence. Within a passel, the Quillguard reserves the right to discipline other Psittacosaurus when necessary. Discipline is typically enacted in response to overzealous behaviour, the consumption of poisonous items like noxious meat, or actions that risk drawing predator attention to the group. When disciplining a member, the Quillguard approaches the offending Psittacosaurus and issues a series of sharp, aggressive calls. If the individual fails to retreat or correct their behavior immediately, the Quillguard may deliver a brief nip as a final warning. Physical discipline is rare and never prolonged. In exceptional cases of widespread unrest or unresolved leadership tension, a Quill-Clash may occur. During these events, multiple members rake their quills against one another and engage in rapid burrowing and resurfacing displays. Quill-Clashes are loud, chaotic, and highly demonstrative, serving to release tension and reassert social order rather than cause serious harm.
