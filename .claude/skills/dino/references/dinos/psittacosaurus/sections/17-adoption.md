# Psittacosaurus – Adoption

Should a passel encounter a lone Psittacosaurus, they will always accept the individual and attempt to raise them as one of their own. Both jacks and jills may adopt, with or without a pairbond. Adopted offspring are given the same care, training, and protection as biological young. However, depending on the stage at which they were adopted, they may never develop functional venom glands if early tail grooming was missed. A Psittacosaurus is considered an Elder within the passel only after demonstrating extensive knowledge of established migration routes and dependable food and water sources. Elders have also proven their genetic success, having raised at least 3 successful clutches. Elders do not waste their energy frivolously. Shaped by experience, they understand when to burrow, when to flee, and when to conserve their limited stamina rather than expend it unnecessarily. Due to their knowledge and experience, Elders are the most likely candidates to assume the role of Quillguard, guiding the passel through familiar territories and seasonal movements. While no longer primary caregivers, Elders actively supervise younger members, gently nudging lagging offspring forward and ensuring no individual falls behind during travel. Age gradually takes its toll on their bodies. Elder Psittacosaurus rely less on tail-based defence, as their quills become brittle and more prone to breakage. With age, their once-vivid patterns and colors often fade, serving as a visible marker of seniority within the passel.
