# Psittacosaurus – Offspring Behavior (Nested)

Newly hatched Psittacosaurus, known as pups, are completely defenceless. As hatchlings, they stay within the confines of the nest, entirely dependent on their parents for survival. Their eyesight is foggy and underdeveloped, allowing them to perceive little more than vague shapes and movement. Scent is their strongest sense at this age, and they instinctively huddle closer to their siblings and adult caretakers. Upon reaching the Juvenile stage, their senses sharpen significantly. They tumble to their feet, and eagerly begin trailing after the adults and siblings in their group. If danger approaches, young Psittacosaurus respond by rushing to a nearby shaded area and laying motionless in an attempt to remain unnoticed by potential threats. While Juvenile Psittacosaurus can walk well and manage short sprints, they lack the stamina for longer runs. They take frequent rest stops, and nap in bursts after a long day's travel. Growing confidence fuels their exploration and play. They begin playing with each other, chasing after their own wiggling tails or those of their siblings within the passel. Tail grooming is a vital part of juvenile development. Adults regularly raise and shake their tails, encouraging young to mimic the behavior. By brushing tails together, they clear out the clogged pores and stimulate the venom glands, allowing venom production to begin properly. Psittacosaurus are exceptional foragers, with digging being one of their most specialised skills. Juveniles begin honing this ability to dig by foraging for food, never straying too far from the nest. As Psittacosaurus grow into Adolescent, their reflexes and muscles fully develop. They push their bodies to new limits, running for extended periods of time and foraging to find anything that piques their interest. With the ability to burrow effectively now within their grasp, adult caretakers take the lead in instructing adolescents on the quickest and easiest methods to tunnel underground - a vital defence mechanism when faced with predators. Adolescent Psittacosaurus begin mimicking the adult alert calls within the passel. They stand on their hind legs, scanning for nearby threats, and call out in alarm if something catches their attention. Affectionate behavior increases during this stage. Adolescents frequently pounce onto their parents' backs, nuzzling them and helping them with grooming. Upon reaching Sub Adult, Psittacosaurus are considered fully independent members of the passel. Parental care ends, and adults shift their focus away from their once-dependent offspring. Although considered independent, they do not engage in courtship until reaching full maturity as Adults. Offspring rarely disperse from their birth passel, often remaining with their family for life. Dispersal typically only occurs when suitable courting opportunities are unavailable.
