# Psittacosaurus – Parenthood

Psittacosaurus parents are highly attentive, doting on their offspring to ensure all their needs are met. As hatchling Psittacosaurus are born nearly blind and entirely dependent, parents continuously ferry food back to the nest, ensuring steady growth during this vulnerable stage. Jacks routinely visit each hatchling, softly brushing them with their snouts or tails to reinforce the scent of the passel. This strengthens the vital bonds throughout the passel. If faced with a predator, parents distract threats away from the nesting site. They wave their tails at predators, baiting them away from the hatchlings. Once their young reach Juvenile, improved vision and mobility allows their parents to guide them on short trips around the nesting area. Tail grooming becomes a priority at this stage. Parents carefully groom their pups' tails to ensure proper development of their venom glands - sometimes to an annoyed and wiggly pup's displeasure! Burrowing is a cornerstone of Psittacosaurus survival. Parents bring juveniles to soft terrain, encouraging them to dig and refine their technique through repetition. Vocal communication increases as parents use calls frequently, teaching offspring how to recognise alarms, all clear signals, and social chirps. Upon reaching Adolescence, offspring are capable of burrowing independently. Adults demonstrate proper digging techniques before observing as adolescents mimic them, with the Quillguard keeping a close watch to ensure competency. Adults may occasionally introduce unfamiliar or unpleasant items - such as bones or noxious meat - into the area. These controlled exposures, known as Scent Drills, train adolescents to recognise dangerous scents and respond appropriately by burrowing or seeking shelter. By the Sub Adult stage, offspring are considered fully independent within the passel and no longer require direct parental care or lessons.
