# Yutyrannus – Parenthood

Mothers are the primary caretakers of kits. They carefully, affectionately, and regularly clean the kits in order to ensure that they do not get sick, and keep them tucked close to their curled bodies in the hatchling stage in order to ensure that they stay warm as they develop their plumage and as their eyes and ears finish developing. Fathers ensure that the entirety of the grouping is fed, taking turns to bring chunks back to their nests and mollies, or bringing mollies to a successful kill. Mothers gorge themselves and take a chunk for their offspring, to practice with feeding solid food and to ensure she has plenty to regurgitate for them when they are hungry. Once juvenile, kits are no longer blind and fragile, and have the beginnings of their feathers. They call frequently, testing their voices, and mothers always respond, especially to a kit in need, in pain, or afraid. Mothers use their voices to soothe their kits, with constant chirps, purrs, and coos to let the kits know where she is. Mothers allow their kits to meet other kits in the colony at this time, but keep a constant eye on them, and scold kits who wander out of the den or start too many playfights. Toms happily initiate games of play or dance with their kits, and take them on trips out into the surrounding area - if they are not harshly scolded for it by the nesting females! At about halfway through Juvenile, kits are brought by their parents out into the world to finally explore. In the dry season, these trips remain contained within a POI at night. However, in the rainy season, families travel greater distances through multiple POIs. Mollies always prioritize going to locations where they can easily hide the kits during periods of rest, or while the rest of the group hunts. Kits beyond this point are raised communally, but always prefer to stick close to their mother or a tom they are particularly bonded with. At adolescent, kits are taught appropriate hunting behaviors on critters or other small prey - or, if resources are limited, by practising on their other parent. Fathers often take the lead on this behavior, especially for young tom kits. Toms teach their male kits how to perform, sing, and dance for females by performing mock dances with one another and their kits. Mollies spend a great amount of time with their kits socializing, and may even have young mollies assist with the nurturing of other, younger kits within the colony.
