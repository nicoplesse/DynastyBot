# Yutyrannus – Offspring Behavior (Nested)

Kits hatch nearly entirely helpless, fragile, blind, and deaf. It is essential for kits to be cleaned carefully by their mothers at this time, as going without cleaning risks a kit developing Mange- a skin and feather disease that often results in kits having a lesser feather amount, and even pulling out their own feathers as they age. Mange is optional, and might not be accepted in every nest. Parents may opt to not allow offspring to have mange in their nesting post. Mothers are extremely attentive to freshly hatched kits, carefully cleaning each one and regurgitating food (placing it in the nest) when needed. She spends most of her time curled around her kits, cooing and calling at them, even if they cannot hear her. By the time that they reach juvenile, kits are no longer blind and deaf, and are able to take their first wobbly steps– frequently rushing, stumbling, and falling into their mother’s soft plumage. Learning to walk is a slow process at first for many kits, but is softly encouraged by their affectionate mother, who nuzzles them when they fall and encourages them to move. However, once kits are on the move, and confident with regular reassurance, they begin to move quickly, darting about in the den and frequently colliding into their siblings and other objects. Kits begin to play with one another at this stage, nipping at the air, pouncing, swiping their claws in the air and chasing one another. This play behavior is also shown towards their mothers– who coo back happily, but never nip or claw in return, wanting to avoid hurting her kits. Kits at this time may also be introduced to other kits within the colony– whom they quickly form fast bonds with. At about halfway through juvenile, kits are brought out into the world in order to begin migrating in the rainy season, or to begin exposure to it in the dry season. Kits take on these new adventures eagerly, running about and curiously sniffing all new things– from tiny lizards, to big rocks, and even other dinosaurs if their parents do not reign them in first. Adolescent kits begin to show more disparity between genders, with males drifting towards their fathers and other toms and females staying close to their mothers. Young toms begin to express interest in dancing and performance, and practice with one another to begin honing the performance that they take with them the rest of their lives. Their performances are often taught to them by their fathers, and often take inspiration from those performances. Young mollies often assist with the nurturing of other kits within the colony or clowder, and do so eagerly. They will also continue to practice play behaviors that will assist them with courtship in their adulthood. Kits are also taught to hunt at this stage, and work together to hunt large critters or other small prey items. Once they have successfully killed something, they will yowl, begging for their mother to come look and praise them for their success. As kits near Sub-Adult, they become confident and independent individuals. They will split off on their own accord if there is no room for them within the group, or, if dry season is nearing, leave with their parents in order to find a suitable place to hunker down to avoid the heat.
