# Tropeognathus – Hunting Strategy

Tropeognathus are highly opportunistic, and do not like to waste their efforts on a hunt if there are ample scavenging opportunities nearby. However - Tropeognathus do hunt - but do so only at night, when their targets are more likely to be sleeping or resting. When a target is spotted, a group of up to 4 Tropeognathus work together to take down their prey - often taking it in turns to launch forward and aim nasty pecks at their prey, before darting out of the way for a group member to land the next bite. Tropeognathus may use aerial abilities to deliver attacks to their prey, whether terrestrial or aerial. If the target is near a cliff edge, Tropeognathus may attempt to knock them off with a well placed flap of their wings. Tropeognathus tend to work together to circle around their prey, clacking their beaks to unnerve and disorient them. This sharp, rapid sound creates a sense of panic and confusion, enabling individuals to dive in and deliver swift nips to their backs or flanks, aiming to wear them down over time. When targeting rival pterosaurs like Thalassodromeus, Tropeognathus employ a daring aerial maneuver: they soar above their target, then dive and execute a barrel roll, ramming into the opponent with their robust wings and heavy body. This powerful strike often sends rivals spiraling out of the sky, leaving them easy prey. Tropeognathus never hunt Hatzegopteryx or Quetzalcoatlus unless these large pterosaurs are solo, and Tropeognathus has 3 or 4 adult members in its hunting party.
