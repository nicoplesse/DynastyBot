# Tropeognathus – General Behavior

Tropeognathus group in large numbers along coastal regions in Gondwa. They are adept at both fishing and flying - often spotted diving in and out of the water with the ability to fly even when drenched. Tropeognathus are highly opportunistic, opting to divebomb a claimed corpse when they spot one, regardless of whatever predator has it. This is often the main cause of their own demise, as many carnivores do not take kindly to these rude pterosaurs. Tropeognathus spend approximately half of their lives in water, half of their lives in the sky, and on land for all other times. They are very active pterosaurs, and enjoy being on the move - often getting restless if grounded for too long. Tropeognathus take great interest in following around terrestrial and semi-aquatic species. They are not fussy on whether they follow herbivores or carnivores - enjoying pursuing whatever creature is nearby that might provide them entertainment or food. Tropeognathus are nowhere near as vocal as other pterosaurs - namely Rhamphorhynchus and Thalassodromeus. They are quieter by nature, reserving loud vocalizations for more extreme - or exciting - situations. Tropeognathus seem to thrive in any aquatic environment. They can be frequently observed dipping in and out of the ocean and freshwater and splashing around in the waves on coastal regions. Rain, in particular, seems to perk up their moods, prompting more energetic aerial displays and contented chirping between individuals. Tropeognathus bathe and swim more frequently in the Dry Season, disliking the drier conditions. They will, however, bask for short periods of time, extending their wings to dry them out in the sun. Tropeognathus sleep for short bursts throughout night and day. They prefer to sleep somewhere surrounded by water - ideally a rock, small island, or coastal cliffside - and take it in turns when in a group so that someone is always watching over the others. Tropeognathus especially enjoy picking up shells and crustaceans from either the ocean floor or along the coast. They take these objects high into the sky before dropping them, letting them smash on the ground below to crack them open for a tasty meal. Albino Tropeognathus are typically weaker individuals in comparison to others of their kind. They fly for much shorter periods, and try to stay in shaded areas during the day to help protect their sensitive eyes and skin. They - essentially - are forced into a more nocturnal lifestyle.
