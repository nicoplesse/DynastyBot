# Tropeognathus – Elderly & End of Life (Optional)

Male Tropeognathus in particular begin to lose their brighter pigmentation as they enter their elderly stages, signifying a decline in fertility and overall health. As a result, they may appear less attractive to females, and struggle to fight for a dominant spot at their communal courting grounds. Both sexes tire more quickly - with less time spent flying or swimming, and more time indulging leisurely naps - often while floating on the water. The desire to court and reproduce doesn't fade with age - with both males and females continuing to return to Ripple Beach each season, eager to find partners. Males, sadly, tend to find less success each year that passes, and females slowly begin producing less eggs with each clutch.
