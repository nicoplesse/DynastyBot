# Barsboldia – Downtime Behavior

Barsboldia sleep for short periods, with individuals taking it in turns to nap to ensure that at least one Barsboldia keeps watch. Bulls notoriously take the longest naps - sometimes prompting a nudge from an impatient cow when wanting to move the herd on. Barsboldia often rest in shade or in water, but prefer clean water to dirty water. They shake often in the water, at times using the mud at the bottom of the shallow pools to cool their legs and underbellies. Solitary Barsboldia Lone Barsboldia are very capable of surviving alone, but do eventually end up craving companionship. This craving can be satisfied with at least one companion - something which can be achieved by having a friendly Struthiomimus or Bonapartenykus to migrate alongside them, if no other Barsboldia can be found. Albino & Melanistic Barsboldia Albino Barsboldia are more sensitive to heat and sunlight than their non-Albino counterparts. From their youth, they are spoiled and doted on upon by their mothers, as they are weaker, slower, and more fragile. Albino Barsboldia are more likely to become sunburned and suffer from heatstroke, move slower due to weak legs and limbs, and - more often than not - are born completely blind. Blind albino Barsboldia always have blue eyes and must be guided by a Barsboldia bonded to them. Albino Barsboldia are skipped over during courting. This is due to limited fertility and their visible fragility. Often, an albino Barsboldia's only luck with being accepted in a herd is remaining under the care of their mother, or grouped with their siblings, as they can be rejected from joining non-related groups. At times, mothers of albino Barsboldia opt to leave with their albino offspring when they reach Sub/Adult, preferring to stay by their side permanently until one of them dies. Mothers of albino calves are extremely protective of them, to the point where they may panic if their albino calf goes out of their line of sight. This extended care continues even as the albino matures into adulthood. Melanistic Barsboldia are treated like any other Barsboldia, and suffer no hardship with courting or nesting. Hyponatremia Barsboldia (of Sub-Adult and above) that do not consume salt rocks on a semi-regular basis become afflicted with hyponatremia. This behavior is a roleplay behavior, and is not a built in in-game mechanic. This illness is low sodium in the blood, and presents with a slower gait, and frequent head shakes. Barsboldia frequently feel nauseous and lose their appetite. Starvation - while extreme - can occur if the individual does not consume salt rocks to remove the affliction. Their energy levels drop, and Barsboldia with this affliction take much longer nap periods. When standing, they are plagued by bouts of dizziness, and are prone to stumbling into herdmates, objects, and even over their own feet. In rare cases, Barsboldia will go blind - with their eyes turning blue - from prolonged instances of this condition. The only cure is to consume salt rocks again to restabilize the sodium levels in their blood, slowly reversing the damage. In Riparia, a lack of salt rocks may be substituted with one or more sips of salt water. Barsboldia have a strong need for companionship, and form very close bonds with other Barsboldia. The strongest of these bonds tend to be with their family members, but deep and meaningful friendships also form between individuals that spend very large amounts of time together. New friendships and bonds take time to warm up to one another. However, bonds can be formed between both same sex and opposite sexes, and are not exclusive  to individuals of the same sex. Herds Groupings remain fluid, with members changing at regular intervals when it suits. All Barsboldia travel in single file (though calves travel side by side with their mothers), with one following after the other, starting with the leader. Herds are lead by an elder cow, or, if one is unavailable, a lead bull, who is the eldest and most dominant bull of the group. Herds always prefer to follow elder cows if one is available - perhaps linking back to their innate and strong bonds with their mothers - but will defer to a bull instead if no such cows are in the herd. Elder cows naturally assume the position at the front of the herd. Her position is never challenged, and is welcomed by the rest of the group. She holds a lot of respect among her herdmates. If a herd is already led by a lead bull and an elder cow joins, the bull naturally steps aside, allowing the elder cow to walk in front of him and steer the direction of the group. Without an elder cow or bull, Barsboldia will follow the most experienced individual. Massive Migrations At times, small breeding or familial groups of Barsboldia move together in mass migrations, with multiple separate herds moving side by side (while in separate groups in game). While most groups tend to keep to themselves, there may be some intermingling, especially between calves. These groups do not show any aggression to one another while migrating, but this can change during nesting and consuming resources. These massive migrations may at times be led by groups of elder cows. Younger females in the group bring their calves to be shown to them, or otherwise may like to hover around the outside of this grouping, especially if a mother, aunt, or older sister is present. Barsboldia travel in single file, with calves walking side by side with their mothers. Photo by QueenBella Barsboldia are not naturally aggressive toward one another in daily life, and prefer coexistence within their strong familial bonds. However, disputes do arise - particularly over resources, bull leadership, and mates. Bulls are most commonly involved in aggressive interactions, especially when a herd lacks a clear leader or enters the nesting season. These challenges follow a structured set of behaviors, designed to test strength between males. Leadership Challenges Lead bulls only arise when there are no elder cows to take the helm. If a lead bull is present but an elder cow joins the herd, he willingly allows her to take the lead. This position is either inherited by age and respect - an elder bull will almost always claim this, if present within the herd - or earned through competing in a leadership challenge. Bulls that desire a leadership position must first signal their intent by approaching the current lead bull, and begin bugling :broadcast_call: directly at him. This loud, broadcasting noise can be quite deafening, and is a signature noise Barsboldia bulls are known for. If no lead bull is present, but there are multiple potential bulls, a determined bull will select another bull to "challenge" for the right to lead the herd. If the other bull accepts the challenge, he will respond by slamming his tail into the ground and aggressive calling :threaten_call: at the challenger. Both males then rise up onto their hind legs. Bulls lunge at each other, utilizing fierce kicks with their front legs, shoving, and slamming their tails against each other. The first bull to reach half health loses the challenge, making the other bull the new lead. Cow Authority Elder cows do not challenge one another for dominance. Their leadership is never contested, and younger Barsboldia automatically defer to them. If there is more than one elder cow present in the group, one naturally gets accepted as the lead through mutual agreement from the rest of the group. Disputes Over Resources While generally tolerant, Barsboldia defend prime food resources, nesting locations, and Salt Rocks from other species and other herds of Barsboldia. Barsboldia initially try to use their bodies to block access to the resource - or nesting site - with the majority of adults moving into a clump in an attempt to cut off the intruder from reaching the defended resource. If the intruder does not take the hint - or starts a resource challenge - 2 Barsboldia step forward to defend, with the others stepping aside and bellowing in support of their herdmates. Grouped Bonapartenykus and Struthiomimus are always permitted access to resources and nesting sites. Barsboldia are tolerant with other species, providing their personal space is respected and resources are not contested. The only species permitted close proximity to both their nests, calves and resources, are Bonapartenykus and Struthiomimus, both of which Barsboldia accept into the herd as their peaceful companions. Unfamiliar species that threaten a Barsboldia's personal space are met with escalating warnings. At first, a sharp, guttural aggressive call :threaten_call: is issued, soon followed by a threatening tail slam if the species does not take the hint. If the intrusion persists, Barsboldia swipe the offender away with their tail.
