# Barsboldia – Other Species Adoption

Barsboldia mothers that suffer the loss of a calf enter a deep mourning period, which ultimately culminates in them attempting to adopt the next orphan under adolescent they encounter - regardless of species. These adoptions are instinctual and emotional, and driven by the mother's grief and need to nurture. While survival for other species' adoptees can be difficult - especially if the offspring is a carnivorous species - mothers show no discrimination in their care, treating them exactly like Barsboldia calves. Bulls may attempt to try and drive out these other species (especially carnivorous ones), but many allow the cows some time with the orphan, acknowledging the cow's need to mourn. By the time the orphan reaches Sub-Adult, if it has not already left of its own accord, the bull will chase it away, regardless of the adoptive mother's cries. Predator Reaction: Aggressive/Defensive Barsboldia are generally unbothered herbivores once they reach adulthood. Even apex carnivores do not provoke much of a reaction from these species, though they make sure to keep an eye on them at all times. This changes dramatically when they are directly threatened - such as a hunt starting, or predators getting too close to calves (within 2 body lengths). Barsboldia begin issuing deep warning calls, and slam their tails against the ground. If the predators do not respect the space, they may get charged by up to 2 adults until they retreat. Mothers and bulls act as the primary defenders, but any member of the herd will move to defend a grouped calf. While 2 adults actively engage, the rest of the herd move to shield the other offspring from harm. Should a predator approach a nesting site, with a cow actively resting on top of eggs or beside her hatchlings, bulls and non-nesting cows (up to a maximum of 2 adults) may charge to drive predators (and other species) away from the nesting site, ensuring they remain at least 5 body lengths away. Once a predator forfeits, they are not pursued once they leave 5 body lengths away. Mothers may defend her nesting site offspring and override engagement limits. However, she prioritizes having the two defenders take over, moving herself and her offspring away from predators. Other Barsboldia in the herd step away from the engagement, not wishing to receive accidental damage from standing too close. They are allowed to watch, but must be 10 or more body lengths away, and do not assist the other Barsboldia. When part of a mass migration, with multiple herds migrating together, Barsboldia does not call for help across herds. Each herd handles threats independently of one another. Barsboldia may use cliffsides to its advantage in order to defend itself from a hunter, but does not run to a cliff when spotting a predator - they may only move towards one once an engagement has started. Barsboldia does not climb onto boulders and other higher terrain or hide in dens when being hunted. Barsboldia may flee at any time if they wish during an engagement. If solo, Barsboldia may choose to start moving away when Apex carnivores are spotted. Particularly stubborn individuals may still engage if needed. Barsboldia prefer to keep moving during hunts, using their great size to control space and intimidate predators. By abruptly charging at their hunters, they strive to cause momentary panic, swinging their tails down on those too slow to dart aside. They may chase for a short period if given the opportunity, but do not go out of their way to attack and kill forfeited individuals. Calves keep pace with their protector's movements, using them as a shield against incoming attacks. Barsboldia adolescent and below do not chase nor charge, lacking the confidence and size to make an impact, but will follow an adult that chooses to do so. Tyrannosaurus One exception for Barsboldia is adult Tyrannosaurus. When these species attack - or are spotted before they can - Barsboldia opt to leave. Engagements are fled from, but Barsboldia may defend themselves as they seek to escape. The only exception to this is when Barsboldia have calves or adoptees of Adolescent or below. Barsboldia may opt to defend as normal in these circumstances, with their strong parental instincts overriding their fear. Without offspring/adoptees under Adolescent, Barsboldia receives no defenders from Tyrannosaurus. With offspring/adoptees under Adolescent, up to 2 adults may defend, as in a normal engagement. Barsboldia court within their own herd, and form harem-style bonds, where a single bull courts a small group of cows within the herd. Bulls do not force cows into their harem. Instead, they must earn the favor of each cow individually - and usually each nesting season.
