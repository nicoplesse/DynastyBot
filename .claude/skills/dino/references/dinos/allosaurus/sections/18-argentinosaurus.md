# Allosaurus – Argentinosaurus

Allosaurus, while unable to take on the sheer size of Argentinosaurus, often find themselves entranced by their similarity to other sauropods. They remain rooted in place, their eyes fixated on what they could only imagine to be a meal that would’ve sustained them for many seasons. Those braver may approach the sauropod slowly, staring up at the creature. Should the Argentinosaurus approach or attempt to interact, Allosaurus will break through their haze and retreat, aware that they are incapable of taking down such a creature. Allosaurus hunt only during the night time, and find most success in stalking and ambushing their prey. They often seek out prey together, stalking quietly throughout the night, hoping to avoid creating too much sound and alerting a potential target. Allosaurus must challenge out other ungrouped Allosaurus that are knowingly in the POI, whether solitary or grouped, in order to hunt. Allosaurus must hunt Adolescent and below Apatosaurus or Amargasaurus before all other species, whether they are attended or not. These young sauropods are an easy meal, and favoured for that reason. Solitary Allosaurus do not have to target protected or Adolescent Apatosaurus if they do not wish to. Allosaurus will then target stegosaurids if these sauropod young are unavailable, typically attempting to catch these species by surprise before they can react. Even solitary, Allosaurus will attempt this, and are usually nimble enough to avoid the sharp tails swinging in their direction. If these options are not available, then pairs and coalitions will opt to target any Large or Apex herbivores within LOS before attempting other options. Grouped Allosaurus may hunt Apex carnivores. If Adolescent and below Apatosaurus or Amargasaurs are not available, solitary Allosaurus may hunt prey options of Large tier and below. Allosaurus do not hunt solitary adult Quetzalcoatlus. Adolescent Allosaurus may only hunt up to small tier. When potential prey is located, Allosaurus grow silent, crouching low to the ground, and tucking themselves behind foliage and rocks. Most Allosaurus will look for a creature that is alone or sleeping, hoping to make an easy kill. If unable to ambush, or simply finding preference in not doing so, Allosaurus may take the opportunity to rush their prey instead, attempting to inflict as many wounds as possible before the prey has had a chance to react. Each Allosaurus develops a different method of hunting most suitable for their needs, but above all else, Allosaurus typically prefer to bleed out their prey rather than relying on blunt force. Once a kill has been made, Allosaurus feast collectively, rarely bickering between themselves in regards to feeding order. If there is a trophy, Allosaurus take turns inspecting and playing with it, before eventually leaving it behind, or allowing someone within the group to claim it as their own. Allosaurus may tolerate Small tier and below scavengers, at their own discretion. Solitary Adult Quetzalcoatlus are always tolerated.
