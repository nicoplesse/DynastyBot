# Allosaurus – Orphaned Behavior (Wild Spawn)

Orphaned Allosaurus, upon having opened their eyes at juvenile, suddenly find themselves void of a parental figure. Without the gentle nudging of a parent, they rise to their feet on their own esteem, and begin helplessly searching nearby foliage for signs of their family. Unsteady on their feet - and far more rushed in their first steps than parented individuals - cubs often end up significantly more battered and bruised as a result. They frequently slip off small ledges, crash into objects and trip over their own feet, slowly growing quieter after each unheard cry. With no kin to familiarise themselves with, comfort for an orphaned Allosaurus often lies in the smell of the environment around where they first hatched. This association ends up channeling into a preference towards those POIs later in life. At times, orphans may be lucky enough to be discovered by a tolerant female, securing themselves a relatively normal upbringing afterwards. For others, once realization has settled in, cubs cease searching for their lost family, and begin focusing purely on survival. Orphaned Allosaurus begin hunting critters from a younger age, and take this time to practise skills like stalking and ambushing an unsuspecting target. Sometimes, unattended babies of other species may be mistaken for critters, receiving the exact same treatment and hunted as a result. Threats are fled from as soon as they are heard. At other times, these orphans may choose to observe active hunts from cover, assessing techniques and strategies, and documenting them for their own attempts. In adolescence, cubs expand their hunting capabilities to include small tiers, having grown more comfortable with hunting alone. As orphaned Allosaurus continue to age, they maintain their solitary nature. Fiercely independent, orphans end up extremely hesitant to accept the company of other Allosaurus, and may go out of their way to present as unappealing to suitors. Even when acquainted with others, orphaned Allosaurus typically only enjoy the presence of their mate and bonded offspring. Only in rare cases do they tolerate being in a group of unrelated Allosaurus. Allosaurus is a powerful theropod that roams through arid and semi-arid regions, favoring biomes with shallow waters and suitable foliage to conceal themselves both day and night. Allosaurus is most active at night, leaving claw marks along trees and rocks as they pass through their territories. As a nocturnal creature, Allosaurus spend a significant amount of the daytime sleeping in underbrush and caves as they wait for nightfall. Reclusive spots are most ideal, as Allosaurus avoids sleeping out in the open. Allosaurus is a generally tolerant carnivore and does not often show aggression towards other species if disinterested in a hunt. Allosaurus favors the dry season, when marshes become humid and warm. Due to their affinity for drier regions, Allosaurus is hardy and able to go long periods of time without drinking water. They often drink large quantities during dusk and dawn, before beginning their travels. During the rainy season, Allosaurus grow disgruntled by the shift in temperature and the near-constant downpour, often seeking out caves or overhangs that offer relief from the rain. Allosaurus has large claws and powerful arms, prone to inspecting their surroundings with their front limbs and grasping onto any kills they’ve secured. Alongside their hardy nature, Allosaurus is able to digest the flesh and meat of Metriacanthosaurus, and are not warded off by the bright scales of this carnivore. Allosaurus relies mostly on sight and scent to get around, and are more prone to deafness at a young age, though this does not usually inhibit their ability to survive. Allosaurus is remarkably resilient, known to heal from deep wounds and broken bones quickly with appropriate rest and care.
