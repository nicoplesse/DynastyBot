# Allosaurus – Offspring Behavior (Nested)

Allosaurus offspring, known as cubs, are born altricial, feeble, and fragile, carefully tended to by their doting mothers from the moment they hatch. They are blind and deaf, only able to identify their mother and siblings through scent. However, with steady feedings and encouraging nudges from their mother, cubs soon open their eyes and begin to move around on wobbly legs, showcasing mobility by the time they are mid-hatchling. Cubs quickly began to run and trot, often careening straight into nearby objects - whether it be their mother or a wall. This often results in bumped snouts and frequent cries from the cubs, which their mother is quick to soothe. Once cubs are steady on their feet, mothers ferry them out into the world under the darkness of night for their first visit to fresh water. Cubs eagerly explore a few body lengths around their mother during this trip, investigating and scratching their claws against trees, branches on the grounds, and rocks. The sound and feeling of their claws scratching on rock often frightens young cubs - where they quickly barrel back to their mother to be soothed once more. Once cubs have proven that they are able to keep up with their mother, they are introduced to their father, if he is a part of the group. While tolerant, he remains rather aloof with his offspring, permitting their mother to do much of the childrearing as he watches from a distance. Cubs often play games of chase, practice stalking, ambushing, and partake in non-physical playfights between themselves. During these playfights, cubs never land their attacks on siblings – with the game only ever being a fierce, often loud, posturing match. Cubs always return to their mother first when frightened, hungry, or otherwise in need of comfort. By the time that cubs are adolescent, they have massively increased in size, strength, confidence, and capability. Mothers begin to train their cubs to hunt critters, encouraging them to hunt larger targets as they continue to develop their skills. After taking down their first few critters, cubs are encouraged to fend for themselves. Leaner and quicker than their adult counterparts, adolescents unite with their siblings to bring down small tier prey and unattended babies of other species. Parents do not help in these hunts at all, and are often elsewhere within the POI, hunting for themselves or ensuring the area stays clear of threat. If at any point, the adolescents deem the hunt too dangerous, or are hunted themselves, cubs are quick to forfeit and flee, broadcasting loudly to alert their parents if the hunted choose to continue pursuing them. In order to be protected, the cubs must have already forfeited the engagement on their side through heart calling (:heart_call:) the other party. Cubs must make this obvious to the parents by affectionate or fear calling again once the parents arrive to witness the ongoing engagement. Adolescents are notoriously bad at relaxing, and frequently get bored without stimulation for long periods of time. When not hunting, cubs taunt and pester their siblings instead, using them as props to practise biting and clawing, no longer restricting themselves to purely vocal confrontations. These scuffles quickly turn aggressive and disruptive, so much so that parents travel across the POI simply to scold them for being too loud. As cubs mature, they will notice parents focusing their attention onto them once more, particularly during their brief check-ins. Adolescents catch on quickly on what behaviour results in the most affectionate, and which behaviors are reprimanded, working efficiently to receive continuous praise whenever in their presence. By Sub-Adult, it becomes clear who the favored cub is. Siblings do not hold resentment once this is revealed, instead focusing their energy on bonding with their other rejected siblings - often forming a dispersal group that leaves together. At this stage, offspring have matured into most of their adult mass, and no longer maintain the small, slender bodies built for targeting small species. Their hunting focus changes, conforming to the species their parents and other adult Allosaurus prefer.
