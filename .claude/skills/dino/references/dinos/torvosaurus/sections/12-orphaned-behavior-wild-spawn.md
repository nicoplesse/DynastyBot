# Torvosaurus – Orphaned Behavior (Wild Spawn)

Female orphaned Torvosaurus are much better equipped at surviving than their male counterparts. They quickly learn to remain in the shadows and use dense overgrowth for concealment, reducing the likelihood of encounters with predators. This quiet and cautious behavior contrasts sharply with others raised in a stable environment, making them adept at avoiding danger. Female orphans readily adapt to challenges and pick up hunting quickly. Males, unfortunately, rarely survive. Without a mother or other females to provide food and no one to teach them how to hunt, male orphans spend most of their time trying to seek out other Torvosaurus, loudly crying for attention and food. Lacking the guidance of a parent or the cooperation of a group, these males struggle to hunt effectively and instead rely on scavenging or attempting to intimidate smaller dinosaurs into surrendering their kills. Sometimes they can mistake Daspletosaurus and even Tyrannosaurus for other Torvosaurus and end up becoming an easy meal for these other, larger theropods. Orphaned offspring may be adopted by other Torvosaurus, but only by sisterhoods. If a male is present, he aggressively chases away (or even kills) unrelated offspring, so solitary females or groups of bonded females are the only potential salvation for orphans. Torvosaurus are a cathemeral species, but hunt exclusively during the day. They spend most of the day and night lounging around and taking naps, enjoying a slower pace of life. This is especially noticeable after a pack has just eaten their fill - with members nuzzling against each other to enjoy a satiated nap. These carnivores can be found in many different biomes across Gondwa - but are most commonly seen in a plains or arid environment, basking in whatever snippet of sunshine they can find. Torvosaurus typically prefer the Dry Season for its hotter temperatures, and love rolling and shaking around in dust baths and warm sands. The Rainy Season can prompt more grumpy behavior in Torvosaurus - particularly when raining or snowing, as they do not like the cold. Torvosaurus are sedentary, meaning they prefer to stay in one POI for the majority of their time, and do not travel often like nomadic species. Additionally, they lack any territoriality towards other carnivores or herbivores - permitting them in their line of sight as potential future hunting opportunities. The one exception are other Torvosaurus packs, detailed under Aggression Interaction. When Torvosaurus do travel to a new POI, they prefer to do so during the night. Lone males or male-only coalitions, however, are nomadic, traveling frequently in search of food opportunities, and the potential to form packs of their own with females. Torvosaurus have strong behavioral dimorphism. Males greet others with a raised head (⤴️ emote), while females greet others with a bowed head (⤵️ emote). They each have largely different roles and behaviors in a group, detailed in Social Interaction.
