# Torvosaurus – Pack Takeovers

Solitary males or coalitions may happen upon a pack that is already formed, and wish to challenge the male for the right to his females. Coalitions must dissolve before a takeover challenge can start, with the more submissive of the pair opting to willingly submit to allow the dominant male the chance to fight. This submission preserves the chance that the subordinate may later secure a female if the challenge is successful. Regardless of the number of females in the pack, only 1 male may issue the challenge at a time. The challenge begins with the rival male approaching the pack's male, signaling his intent with his head held high and a bold stance. The pack's male, seeing this, will approach him also with his head raised, matching his stance with an equally confident one of his own. The posturing escalates into :threaten_call: aggressively calling at each other, before finally a fight ensues. The battle is brutal and fierce - and one that can result in death if either male is particularly insistent on winning. The fight continues until a male either succumbs to his injuries or forfeits - leaving the winner the right to the females. If the original male wins, the challenger (and his subordinate, if he watched) will be driven out of the POI. Should the newcomer win the pack, a number of changes occur: The original male is driven out of the POI if he is still alive. If the victorious male has a subordinate, and the pack includes multiple females, the new male seeks to select a female for his subordinate. A female may volunteer herself forward for the position, but otherwise is nudged in the direction of the waiting male. If no females consent to this, the subordinate male is driven out of the POI by the entire pack - alone. Afterwards, the male is subject to inspections by his new females. They circle him and inspect as they would any other male, but do not subject him to the biting or critter gifting in order to impress them - as he bested the original male, he is already proven. Females may or may not accept him at first - a position which is largely dependent on differing personalities, and whether or not they have cubs present. Females with cubs present will never accept the new male until her cubs are grown or gone. Harmony in the pack cannot be achieved until all females have accepted the male - so sadly, males that takeover a pack must work quickly to either drive out or kill cubs in the pack. Adolescent female cubs are spared from this, permitted to stay as potential future breeding partners. Mothers may or may not opt to defend her cubs from this attack. Other females do not assist the mother in this task, and males must always back down if the female bests him - biding his time until her back is turned to try again. Some females, however, opt to turn a blind eye - especially if they have particularly demanding cubs. Regardless of their protective instincts, all females must leave their cubs unattended at times to hunt, creating openings for the male to act. New males are not killed by females, and males always back down when the female starts attacking. However, they may attempt to cull the cubs at a later time. If the pack has adult daughters present, the new male must make a choice of which females to drive out of the pack. This can either be the mothers or the adult daughters. Only a maximum of 3 adult  females may remain in the new pack. Torvosaurus have little tolerance or social interactions with other species of dinosaurs. Other species are seen as a threat - or potential food source.
