# Torvosaurus – Thalassodromeus

However - one exception is for Thalassodromeus. These pterosaurs are tolerated for their scouting ability. Thalassodromeus can form bonds with particular Torvosaurus packs or groups, and will be permitted to scavenge from all Torvosaurus kills. In exchange, Thalassodromeus will alert Torvosaurus to the presence of others in the area, helping their bonded Torvosaurus to maintain their territory borders. Thalassodromeus do not alert Torvosaurus to potential prey - only rival Torvosaurus. Doing so may spook prey and ruin a hunt for Torvosaurus. Torvosaurus that forge these bonds with Thalassodromeus will no longer hunt them, and will permit them to come quite close to them at rest times. They may aggress unknown Thalassodromeus, but will show considerable tolerance for those that prove themselves useful. Torvosaurus only allows Thalassodromeus to scavenge and all other scavengers are chased away. Only 3 female Torvosaurus hunt within the pack. Solo males and coalitions will only hunt as a last resort, and prefer to scavenge or contest for bodies. If a pack has less than 3 females, the male may step in to fill out the hunting party temporarily. As soon as a third female is found, he will no longer participate in hunts. This includes mobbing situations. Torvosaurus must first make sure they have driven out other Torvosaurus from the POI (if applicable) before they can hunt other species, or they must hunt them first. Amargasaurus is the priority target for all Torvosaurus for hunts and must be targeted first, after Torvosaurus.  If there are no Amargasaurus or mobbing opportunities, Torvosaurus must next target ceratopsians. If no ceratopsians are available, Torvosaurus may target whatever it prefers. Prey tends to be stalked for a short while before a hunt begins. One member of the hunting party may be responsible for flushing prey towards other members, which remain concealed. Hunts are very coordinated between members. They take it in turns to go in and attack their target, all from different angles, to maximise terror and confusion for their prey. Once a kill is made, females will broadcast loudly to call the male over - who will (usually) have played no part in the hunt whatsoever. If the male does not come in a timely manner to eat his fill, one of the females will go to find him and bring him back to the kill. The females and the male eat first, with offspring being allowed to eat only after the adults are full. Mothers may take pity on her offspring, and take a chunk off of the kill to give to them before resuming eating. Mobbing: If Apatosaurus or Argentinosaurus are in the POI, other Torvosaurus groups will be permitted into the POI to aid the original group in a mob hunt. In this hunt, up to 6 Torvosaurus may participate in a hunt against one of these sauropods. This must be done in separate groups, but they may work together to bring it down. If a pack has enough females in it already to begin a mob, they can do so without forming an alliance with another pack. If successful, they will share the kill together. Afterwards, one group must be driven out by the other.
