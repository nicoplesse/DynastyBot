# Megalania (Arid) – Offspring Behavior (Nested)

Baby Megalania hatch fully independent from their parents, armed with sharp claws and a strong instinct for survival. From the moment they scurry free from their eggs, they have little need for parental care, and are immediately capable of hunting small critters and prey to feed themselves. Offspring may group with their mother and each other until they reach Sub-Adult. While Megalania are not nurturing or maternal in the traditional sense, mothers remain in the general vicinity of the nesting area while her offspring are young, loosely monitoring to ensure predators do not raid the nest. Offspring do not form any particular bonds with their mother - and almost never encounter their father - but sometimes develop a loose friendship with their clutchmates. Baby Megalania, like adults, are incredibly driven by movement. A flash of motion prompts them to chase, sinking their tiny fangs into the object if they can catch it - but often promptly letting go. Offspring do not vocalize often, but explore the world carefully, "tasting" objects and new experiences with a flick of their tongue. They often stand up on their hind legs, turning their heads around to get a better view of interesting objects. Social interaction begins early, though how it manifests depends heavily on variant: Temperate variant offspring are more tolerant of each other. While they maintain independence, they share space with their siblings peacefully. Temperate juveniles initiate mock hunts on one another, using short sprints and nips to engage in rudimentary play. Arid variant offspring are more aloof. While they tolerate a sibling nearby, they avoid unnecessary contact, and are more likely to respond to mock aggression with stillness and warning hisses. Arid offspring explore independently, preferring sunny rocks to bask on alone. Cannibalism from other adults is a constant background risk - even from their mother. Though she largely ignores her young, any offspring of Adolescent or below that is significantly injured - or especially bold during feeding - may be treated as prey. Offspring learn to keep a respectful distance when their mother is feeding, or otherwise irritable. The nest site is abandoned when offspring reach midway through Juvenile, and the mother may start to travel to another POI. Offspring are expected to keep up - though at a reasonable distance behind - and scurry after their mother, hoping to benefit from her kills. By adolescence, Megalania show more independence from their mother and siblings, though do stay within the general area, eager to scavenge from each other's kills where possible. At Sub-Adult, Megalania disperse entirely, leaving the group from both their siblings and mother.
