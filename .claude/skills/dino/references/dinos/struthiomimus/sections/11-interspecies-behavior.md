# Struthiomimus – Interspecies Behavior

Male Struthiomimus may display aggression toward smaller creatures such as Dryosaurus and Psittacosaurus, particularly during the breeding season. While both males and females are aggressive toward small or lone carnivores, especially baby carnivores, females are generally more tolerant of the presence of small herbivores even when they are alone. Males, however, may show increased aggression toward smaller herbivores during breeding times, especially when defending their territory or harem. Male Struthiomimus defend a radius of 5 body lengths around their territory or harem against Dryosaurus and Psittacosaurus. This should be treated as a territory challenge. Struthiomimus flocks may sometimes choose to settle with larger herbivores, such as Deinocheirus, becoming more sedentary in nature. By staying with these large creatures, the Struthiomimus benefit from their protection and social company, and may even adopt new behaviors like taking baths in shallow waters if they feel safe enough. Solitary Struthiomimus, on the other hand, may be seen following herds of other herbivores, provided those herbivores tolerate their presence. This behavior offers them safety in numbers and better opportunities for foraging. One unique interspecies relationship male Struthiomimus have is with Therizinosaurus. Male Struthiomimus find Therizinosaurus irresistable, and attempt to court any females they are lucky enough to come across. These behaviors usually amuse female Therizinosaurus - though males may not find it quite as entertaining - and  can result in the Struthiomimus grouping up with the female, acting as a pseudo-mate, and receiving her protection and affection.
