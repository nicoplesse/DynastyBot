# Pachycephalosaurus – Personality Type

Pachycephalosaurus, despite their quick growth, mature extremely slowly. Even when the physical size of an adult, a yearling will only fully mature much later. It's during this age that their personalities begin to fully emerge as they navigate the shifting hierarchy of Pachycephalosaurus social structures. A yearling's personality fits within 2 categories: a leader or a follower, which typically shifts throughout their life, even after fully maturing or progressing into their elder years. Leaders are pushy, loud and entitled. They are outgoing, social and assert themselves at the top of the social hierarchy through games of play and consistent chattering with the group. They are followed by those who take a liking to them, and have learnt through engaging with others that some are better off leading the band while others are suited as supportive followers. Pachycephalosaurus with this personality type are the first to investigate a new thing or accept a new female member into the band. Whether it be an object or a species, the leaders will assess the possible danger first. They do this by circling the new thing, watching carefully and waiting for it to move. If it's deemed safe, leaders will call out to their band to come see. Some followers might not heed the invitation and will watch from a safe distance. Leaders, being the more pushy individuals, take the first pick of food, drink, and resting spots and often demand space when entering an area. They seemingly expect followers to make way and will bully others to assert themselves. Followers are peaceful and cooperative. These individuals are perhaps not as social or outgoing and happily exist at the bottom of the pecking order. Their personality is better fit as companions to outgoing Pachycephalosaurus. They spend their time resting beside other followers, finding food and following those they take a liking to. When cornered or approached by an unfamiliar or untrusted face, followers will sit and rumble :danger_call:  to ward away any advances. This odd posturing display will always be respected by the more outgoing individual. Over time, a shy Pachycephalosaurus can learn to be more trusting and may grow beyond their rank.
