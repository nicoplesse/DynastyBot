# Pachycephalosaurus – Solitary Behavior

Solitary Pachycephalosaurus females instinctively crave the companionship of their own kind and even if not particularly friendly, will always prefer to exist within a band than live on their own. For males, however, solitude is a forced reality. Once their adult colors begin to show, the band rejects them, driven off by the residing buck or pressured by the females to leave. From that moment on, his life becomes a quiet pursuit. He lingers on the outskirts of the group, far enough to avoid confrontation but always near enough to watch, wait, and follow. His goal is singular: to challenge the buck and replace him as the bands adult male. Occasionally, he crosses paths with yearlings - too young yet to show their true color dimorphism. Unaware of their developing maturity, he mistakes them for females. When that happens, he lowers his posture and issues a soft, friendly call. If they accept, they're welcomed as band members. They remain unaware of the mistaken identity until time reveals the truth. It's only when the yearling's adult markings emerge that the once-accepting buck now sees a rival, and quiet companionship is replaced with instinctive rejection.
