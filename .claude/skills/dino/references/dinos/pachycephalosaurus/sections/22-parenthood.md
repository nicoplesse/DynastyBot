# Pachycephalosaurus – Parenthood

When offspring, known as kids, hatch, they rumble and call to their mother. She immediately relocates them to a more defendable spot if her chosen nest location is compromised. Her young follow her closely, recognizing their own vulnerability when unable to leap as high as their mother. Following her, they are led away from other nesting nannies and back towards the nest for food when hungry. Once reaching adolescence, nannies pool their offspring together and co-parent young as a group. The buck has no desire or patience to deal with offspring. He does not contribute at all to raising young. His priority lies in chasing away rival males and winning the respect of females. If a buck finds himself in a situation where the mother of a kid has died, he will chase the kid relentlessly, attempting to drive it out the band. If no female adopts the orphan, the kid is exiled (removing them from the group). Only young that are larger in size avoid harsh treatment from adult bucks.Yearlings are tolerated by males and treated as adult members of the group. Yearling males are always mistaken for being female and will be accepted into a Band with any buck nearby.
