# Pachycephalosaurus – Downtime Behavior

At night they retreat to safety - ideally a raised structure built of rock still warm from the morning sun. Using these elevated areas for protection, Pachycephalosaurus sleep either together or separately, with followers and leaders resting in their own little cliques. The buck of the group never has allies in a band and sleeps separately from his females, usually in quick bursts atop the tallest rock for a better view of the immediate area. His position in the band is challenged relentlessly, and night time is the only time he can rest unchallenged. When not facing off rivals, his sleep is disturbed by every rustle in the trees or crunch of twigs. His keen senses alert his females of incoming danger, should the group be approached. Rival males or solitary females tend to linger on the outskirts of these band groupings. At night, females, yearlings and orphaned kids approach quietly and clamber up to where the band sleeps, hoping for the safety of the group in this time of rest. Rival males, knowledgeable of the risks when approaching a band off guard, rest on nearby cliffs and wait till day before approaching. If no Pachycephalosaurus are in the area, they are known to rest nearby other herbivore species, perhaps to use these larger animals as temporary warmth and protection while they rest.
