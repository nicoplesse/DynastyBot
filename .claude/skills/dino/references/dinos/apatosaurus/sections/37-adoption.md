# Apatosaurus – Adoption

Apatosaurus same sex pairs are the most likely candidates that show interest in adopting orphaned calves. Despite this, calves are never entertained by these adults for adoption until they are at least Adolescent; otherwise, they are gently ushered away into thick foliage if the orphan attempts to follow the herd. Opposite-sex pairs and even singular individuals may also decide to adopt orphans, depending on personal preference. If an orphan below Adolescent is encountered outside of the usual nesting grounds, adults interested in adopting the orphan may stay nearby to the thicker foliage area, keen to welcome the calf when it grows to Adolescent. Older age brings a sense of frailty to Apatosaurus, with elders frequently suffering from brittle bones that are more prone to breaking after repeated migrations. Elders often struggle to maintain the pace of the herd, frequently lagging behind during long journeys. Despite this, they make persistent efforts to keep up, driven by strong social bonds and familiarity with their pod. Pods do not abandon their elderly. Movement may subtly slow to accommodate them - controlled by the matriarch, who more often than not is the elder individual in question -with family members remaining nearby and adjusting their positioning to ensure these individuals are not left behind. Older Apatosaurus favour longer rest periods, often settling more frequently when the herd pauses between migrations, and sleeping for longer periods. With old age comes a calmer temperament. Temper tantrums and shows of aggression are deeply subdued, and elder individuals become more tolerant in general.
