# Apatosaurus – Bull Challenges

Challenges begin with parallel pacing, where both bulls move alongside one another, keeping close proximity while attempting to intimidate the other into backing down. This escalates into further posturing displays, with bulls letting out long, rumbly warning growls, and the pacing turning into both bulls circling each other and maintaining eye contact. If neither have backed down by this point, the challenge turns physical: bulls shove, hit and whip each other with their heads, necks and tails in a vicious - and very loud - battle between the sauropods. This fight continues until one of the males reaches half health and is forced to withdraw. The loser may no longer challenge to court that particular cow again until the next time, but may try his luck in courting other cows - or nest with her if she still shows willing. Other bulls, however, may step in to challenge the winner if they show interest as well. Cows hold the ultimate decision. Though they notably prefer stronger and more aggressive males, cows may sometimes opt to nest with a loser bull. This decision is always respected, but may result in a temper tantrum display from the rejected bull. Females uninterested in the advances of a particular bull simply move away from him, turning their backs in a clear signal that his advances are not welcomed. Bulls only court one cow at a time. If accepted, the female lowers her head and nuzzles against the bull, who returns her affections, often resulting in the pair entwining their necks for a period.
