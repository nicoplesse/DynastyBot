# Apatosaurus – Other herbivores

Apatosaurus are broadly tolerant of most other herbivorous species, but this tolerance is conditional and heavily dependent on space and resource availability. Small tier herbivores are largely ignored - but species that move too erratically or linger too closely are soon met with warning bellows and irritable flicks of their tails. Medium tier herbivores are tolerated so long as they do not interfere with food resources, or get in the way during migrations. Apatosaurus are not particularly careful where they step, and think nothing of treading on species smaller than them that they do not group with. Should these smaller species be considered to be consuming too many resources, Apatosaurus begin barging in the way and utilising their bodies to block the other herbivores from berry bushes. Large tier and other Apex tier herbivores are treated with firm neutrality. Apatosaurus refuse to yield space, and stand their ground if other large species attempt to crowd them or compete for food sources. Apatosaurus have little patience for disorder and overly panicky species. As such, the sudden bolting of species such as Lambeosaurus and Parasaurolophus when faced with predators seems to agitate the sauropod, who may show bad tempered and irritable behavior when meeting these species the next time. During the Dry Season, Apatosaurus' tolerance runs thin with all other species. They become highly food aggressive, and begin chasing away all non-groupables from water, feeding spots, and wallowing pits.
