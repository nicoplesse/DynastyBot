# Apatosaurus – Courtship

Courtship in Apatosaurus is initiated by females, with cows signalling their readiness during rest periods in between migrations. These signals are very obvious: cows move in slow, deliberate pacing patterns with their head raised skyward, holding the posture for extended periods as they wander through open spaces. This display is sustained and purposeful, aiming to draw the attention of nearby bulls and signify their interest in nesting. Even cows in lifelong pairbonds behave in this way - though focus their attention on performing beside their mate. Other bulls never attempt to court a cow in a lifelong pairbond. Bulls respond by entering musth - a heightened sense of aggression and restlessness around cows. They separate from their birth pods to respond to these courtship advances. Interested bulls begin behaving erratically. They charge frequently, whipping their tails and even stomping - a behavior that can easily injure others nearby, who quickly move to give space.
