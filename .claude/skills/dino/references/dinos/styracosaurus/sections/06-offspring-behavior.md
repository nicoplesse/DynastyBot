# Styracosaurus – Offspring Behavior

Starting from birth, Styracosaurus babies, known as fawns, are quite clumsy - but it doesn't take long for them to stand up and start exploring their surroundings. Calves stay close to their mothers while they become familiar with them and their siblings. At juvenile, fawns begin to explore away from their mother's side, choosing to play with calves from other nests, and even other ceratopsians they may be herding with. Fawns simulate fights by clashing their crests together, beginning without aggression at first - but young stags can sometimes get carried away, requiring intervention from their mothers. When Styracosaurus reach adolescence, they begin to become more unruly, moving further and further away from their mother, which often causes them to get lost among the other adults and calves within the herd. This stage is where sons begin turning their attention to the adult stags in the herd, following behind them to imitate and copy adult bullfights. Daughters are typically more social than their brothers, and spend time socializing with the rest of the herd, copying along in common behaviors with their mothers. At sub-adult, Styracosaurus are considered adults, and are allowed to participate in courtship, bull fighting, herd defense and protection. Males tend to become more distant socially, whereas females remain with closely forged bonds. Siblings do often develop bonds with each other that can last a lifetime. Once they reach maturity, Styracosaurus become fully part of the herd. Daughters will attempt to join the harems of other unrelated males, and sons will attempt to form their own harems.
