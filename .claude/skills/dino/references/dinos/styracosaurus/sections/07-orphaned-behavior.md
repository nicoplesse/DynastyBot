# Styracosaurus – Orphaned Behavior

Orphans that hatch without a parent (or lose their parent) immediately run towards sheltered areas. Dense foliage or rocky outlooks help shield them from predators, and they skittishly dart between spots of cover as they grow. If an orphan has siblings, or spots other fellow orphans, they will always band together for both comfort and safety. An orphan's main objective is to find another adult female of their species to take them in. This can sometimes prove difficult, but Styracosaurus will continuously search until they either find one, or become adults themselves. In the absence of adult members of their species, Styracosaurus orphans will follow any ceratopsian they encounter, mixing with the rest of the fawns in the hope of not being detected as "strangers" and obtaining protection from their new "family." This does not go well with less tolerant ceratopsians, such as Eotriceratops and Pentaceratops.
