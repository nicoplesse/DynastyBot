# Styracosaurus – Social Interaction

Styracosaurus herds have established hierarchies. These are made up of several males with their own harems, lead by the most dominant male and his harem. The dominant stag is the male that has the largest harem. This position cannot be fought for, only won through proving himself and claiming the most cows. This stag is respected by the rest of the herd, and carries the weight of leading the group. Does without a harem tend to gravitate towards him, impressed by his experience and leadership. As a result, the dominant male has the largest harem. Styracosaurus have a natural inclination for rough play - and this is showcased most clearly between males. Using their impressive frills and horns, they engage in sparring matches, competing with each other to win the favor of unclaimed females, as well as practising their skills. While males can choose several does to join their harem, does only belong to one harem at a time, and dedicate themselves to the stag leading it. These females form part of the male's harem, and become very sociable over time with other females in the harem. Within each harem, the oldest and most experienced female asserts herself as the 'dominant' female, responsible for keeping the other cows in the harem in line. Does show great affection to their stag and biological relations both within the harem and the wider herd, but tend to be standoffish to newcomers. When a stag integrates a new doe into the harem, existing does give the new female a cold welcome - even going so far as to block her from food and turning their backs on her as she approaches. This "trial period" can last for a variable amount of time, with the dominant female deciding when this temporary period of shunning ends and she is allowed to further integrate with the rest of the does. Stags are respectful of each other's does in their harems, and do not attempt to steal does from others. Stags caught attempting to do so are often expelled from the herd. Males that demonstrate good behavior and responsibility are more accepted by the females in their harem. If the females in a harem particularly trust a male, they may entrust their calves into his care while they go to eat.
