# Styracosaurus – Predator Reaction

Styracosaurus flee from pairs of Apex carnivores, only defending themselves if cornered or out of stamina. Styracosaurus are a stubborn species, but also relatively nervous in the presence of multiple carnivores - or a very large carnivore threat. The sight of any carnivore is enough to draw Styracosaurus to begin aggressively calling and waving their heads fiercely, hoping to drive away the predators before they attack. Daspletosaurus causes a much higher fear response in Styracosaurus - prompting the herd to pace, and emit more fear calls than aggressive calls. They do not chase down or initiate combat with Daspletosaurus - but will defend if hunted or attacked. If migrating near Daspletosaurus, they opt to go around these carnivores where possible. If these aggressive displays are not enough to deter carnivores away - Styracosaurus bulls may begin mock charges at the predators - stopping just in front of them, or darting out of the way at the last minute, careful not to land an attack accidently. This may only be done if the predator is within 10 body lengths to the herd. Predators within LOS but a reasonable distance away do not get charged unless they are threateningly close. Styracosaurus do not approach the predator to close the distance. If, after 2 mock charges, the carnivores do not leave 2 body lengths of space between themselves and the herd, Styracosaurus can immediately start an engagement against them. When an engagement starts, the majority of the herd band together in a defensive formation, building a formidable wall with babies and injured individuals put behind them. They only move as far as one body length forward to land a hit when the attacker comes close, before rejoining the formation. Up to 4 defenders/attackers participate outside of the defensive circle - typically always stags if available, including the dominant stag - becoming "active" engagers that charge around outside the circle, and chase down the threat to land hits. These defenders opt to use "hit and run" tactics, charging forward to land a blow before darting away before the predator can catch them, often in unison with other defenders. These defenders cannot swap out once they are set. They are always stags, and only does if there are not enough stags to make up the 4 defenders. If the predator forfeits, these 4 active defenders can chase down the predator until it leaves at least 10 body lengths of distance between itself and the herd. If there are 4 or less Styracosaurus in a herd, and all members of the group are active defenders, they may not chase predators who have forfeited for more than 5 bodylengths.
