# Spinosaurus (Freshwater) – Pairbond, Offspring and Adoptee defence

Spinosaurs pairs and individuals are fiercely protective over their offspring and adoptees. When their young is threatened - either approached by a predator or attacked outright - the parent(s) rush to their aid, issuing aggressive displays to distract the attacker and give their young time to flee by their side. For fully grown Suchomimus, this protection proves a nuisance - as Spinosaurus will step in to prevent the Suchomimus partaking in hunts or challenges. A problem which forces the Suchomimus to hunt further in land, away from the prying eyes of the Spinosaurus "parent". When their same-species offspring reach Sub-Adult, parents (or adoptive parent/parents) push their young out of the territory with loud calls (:danger_call:) and aggressive displays (:threaten_call: ). Sometimes, offspring are seemingly oblivious, requiring a few attempts to successfully get the point across. Adoptive young of a different species sometimes remain with Spinosaurus for longer. Lacking a large sail, Suchomimus young are considered somewhat stunted, and can remain with the Spinosaurus if they are tolerated. Tolerance to these sly Suchomimus vary between Spinosaurus individuals. Some grow tired of the constant demands of their young and eventually push them out, while others blindly feed and protect them for years.
