# Spinosaurus (Saltwater) – Halszkaraptor tolerance

Initially treated similarly to any other species at first, Halszkaraptor are quickly recognized by Spinosaurus as being too swift and elusive to reliably catch. After several repeated attempts to expel them - and the resulting failure - Spinosaurus begrudgingly ends up tolerating them. However, if the opportunity presents itself, especially through an ambush or if the tiny dromaeosaurid decides to stray too close, Spinosaurus will not hesitate to attempt a hunt. Spinosaurus primarily feeds on fish, supplementing its diet by taking advantage of abundant resources in rivers and large lakes. Spinosaurus uniquely hunt only in key hotspot areas. These areas are referred to as territory areas. When hunting, Spinosaurus become alarmingly passive and silent in water. A Spinosaurus not immediately chasing out or aggressing any other species encroaching upon its water source is an extremely dangerous one. Rather than aggressing, they silently slip towards their prey. All hunts must start from within Spinosaurus' claimable territory. This is within the water, and 10 body lengths from the waters edge. All hunts may continue beyond their territory. Spinosaurus hunt alone. If their prey fights back, their companion does not step in to defend them. Spinosaurus prefer to retreat into water (or out of it) as needed if hunting success seems low. Outside of territory areas, Spinosaurus feast on fish and occasionally chase small critters or scavenge from long forgotten corpses. They do not bother challenging others for territory or corpses outside of claimable territory, as these undesirable areas are considered low in resources and not worth the effort. Spinosaurus are not cannibalistic and never hunt their own kind. Additionally, Suchomimus receives the same treatment due to being considered stunted young.  However, Spinosaurus is not wasteful, and may still eat from a Spinosaurus or Suchomimus corpse. Concavenator is another low priority target when Spinosaurus are tolerating them in their water source.
