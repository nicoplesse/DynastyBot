# Spinosaurus (Saltwater) – Offspring Behavior (Nested)

Immediately after hatching from their eggs, young Spinosaurus are greeted by their mother, who lures them into the water with gentle calls. The initial waddle into the shallows puts these younglings at risk, so - standing protectively over them - their mother shifts her body to hide them from predator view during their short path into the water. Baby Spinosaurus seek comfort by hiding under their mother's tail or pressing closely against her side, frequently calling out to ensure she's still nearby. From as early as inside the egg, babies fine tune into their mother's sounds and scent, and feel reassured by her presence. Their attachment to her is intense and they display visible distress if separated, even for a brief moment. Hatchlings show little interest in their siblings, only acknowledging them with a quick sniff, or cuddling together during rest periods. They remain largely indifferent to each other in the early stages of their life. While they may not be particularly aggressive with their siblings, they tend to push ahead to stay close to their mother. Once swimming, young remain very close to their mother, who leads them from the nesting grounds with strong strokes of her tail. Their father remains aloof in the early stages, but swims nearby, occasionally calling to his mate and patrolling the water's edge. He periodically swims close to inspect his brood, but otherwise keeps a distance while his young are still hatchling. Juveniles grow quickly and instinctively hunt small fish, crabs and scraps of meat. They survive by staying close to their siblings and mother, who brings them food in her jaws. Young Spinosaurus frequently collect sea shells, lake sponge and lakeweed to chew on. The crunch of the shells against their jaws helps tooth grinding tendencies, and the softness of lake sponge and lakeweed soothes aching gums as new teeth grow through. Young chirp constantly, a sound sometimes mimicked by adults. When distressed, they wail in a distinctive fear call :fear_call:. This sparks an immediate reaction in their parents who rush to their aid, ready to defend their young from the perceived threat. As Spinosaurus mature into adolescence, they gradually begin to explore independently and stray further from their mother's side. At this stage, some offspring show a greater interest in their father, spending time swimming behind him, imitating his movements and vocalizations. Once reaching Sub-Adult and fully shedding from their mottled baby scales, both parents push them out of the territory. Freshly matured Spinosaurus are often eager to disperse of their own accord at this stage, but the sudden rejection from their parents sometimes provokes distress in sensitive individuals. Despite this, they disperse willingly and instinctively migrate to Triad Falls. This ancestral neutral ground harbors the perfect location for subadults to grow into full maturity and secure their first pairbond.
