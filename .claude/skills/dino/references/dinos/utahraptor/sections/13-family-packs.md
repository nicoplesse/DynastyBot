# Utahraptor – Family packs

Utahraptor packs, bred into life by the Alpha pair, are a group of closely bonded related members in a grouping referred to as  "family packs". These large groups are easily recognized by their shared feather color scheme, indicating their kinship. Utahraptors form close bonds with their siblings and immediate family members. These bonds develop over time as individuals prove their loyalty by protecting one another in combat, sharing food, and helping raise the young. Bonds and friendships are crucial, as they may grant some members more leniency than others. Those with weaker bonds are more likely to be pushed out when the pack outgrows their numbers. Family packs are highly structured, with clearly defined roles. Pack Structure & Roles Utahraptor pack roles are filled through birth rights, and do not change except when the Alphas die or step down. Alphas As the leaders of the pack, the Alphas are the longest-bonded pair and are regarded as the strongest and wisest members. Their role is not challenged, but may be willingly stepped down from when they reach Elder status, to become former Alphas. Alphas are responsible for selecting hunting groups, accepting mates into the pack, and determining nesting sites. Their authority commands great respect within the pack. Alphas are the primary breeding pair that form the entire family pack. All members of the family pack are their offspring, grandchildren, siblings and unrelated mates to their bloodline. Same-sex pairs are permitted within the pack, but cannot become Alphas due to their inability to reproduce. The Alpha pair determines what the color scheme for the pack will be. They are looked to for experience and guidance, but take a step back within the pack, and remain as well-respected and loved pack members. If a new Alpha takes position, and is not a child of the Former Alpha, the Former Alphas will disperse from the pack, seeking to retire elsewhere. However, if the new Alpha is a child of the former alpha, they permit their elderly parents to remain in the family pack - keeping them close for support and guidance in their new role. Former Alphas that retire from their position no longer nest. Instead, they give the opportunity to their child to pave their own way and prioritize the new generation under their new leadership. Betas Betas are direct offspring of the Alphas, stepping into leadership roles as needed. Well-respected and influential, they help maintain order and assist their parents in governing the pack. Betas can are only be children nested by both individuals in the current Alpha pair role. If a Beta-ranked child is related to only one Alpha, they will always become Omega when the Alpha takes a new mate. Maintaining order: From a young age, the offspring of the Alpha pair learn through instinctive mimicry, closely copying their parents' behaviors. Betas play a key role in maintaining pack hunting styles, passing down essential knowledge and behaviors to the rest of the pack. As the most valued members of the bloodline, they receive the most extensive upbringing, making their understanding of pack life deeply trusted. Lower-ranking individuals never challenge them, instead showing respect through body shakes and unwavering obedience. Hunting: Betas also play a crucial role in orchestrating the pack's strategic hunts. When assigned into a hunting party Betas instinctively fall into leadership positions, flanking the group and working as a team to confuse and exhaust prey. When paired with other ranks, Betas always engage prey first, with other ranks supporting their lead. Deltas Deltas are offspring nested by Beta rank (grandchildren to the Alpha pair), but can also be siblings to one of the Alpha pair. Scouts & territory defense: This role aids the pack in serving as scouts and sentries, and the first line of defence. They patrol the territory borders by perching on tall ledges and tracing the edges of the territory. Dried branches and roots are relocated from piles and placed on the borders, signifying to other Utahraptor in the area that the land they are entering is home to others. When danger approaches - especially if an unknown Utahraptor enters their territory - the Deltas are the first to alert the rest of the pack, and initiate a territory challenge. The Delta rank, as a consequence, ends up the most battle worn - but also as highly skilled fighters. Their experience and loyalty is respected, with the Alpha pair allowing them to place their nests high above the rest of the pack. These ideal vantage points offer the best protection for young and establish their role as keen sentries for the family. Omegas Forming subordinate roles, the other related individuals (children of deltas, great grandchildren and offspring to only one of the Alpha pair) fill the rest of the pack's bloodline. Submissive & low ranking: Omegas are the last on the pecking order. As further distanced relatives from the Alpha pair, they are permitted to remain in the pack and fill subordinate roles, often serving as designated bait to bluff prey attacks in hunts, or support during territory defence and patrols. Omegas fulfil the role of small game hunters, bringing back scraps of meat or small critters for hungry packmates who remain in the territory. When separated from Beta or Delta ranks, Omega are nervous, with no clear leader emerging in the leader's absence. Unrelateds These are individuals that are mates to Betas, Deltas and Omegas. They are accepted in the pack due to their pairbonds and are permitted to hunt and participate in pack life. Sometimes, unrelateds may find themselves widowed if their mate dies. If their children are still alive, they can remain within the family but are forbidden to take a new mate, unless courting into the bloodline again. When an Unrelated's mate is offline, they may still continue to act as the role of their mate. Additionally, the rest of the pack respects them and treats them as the rank of their mate.
