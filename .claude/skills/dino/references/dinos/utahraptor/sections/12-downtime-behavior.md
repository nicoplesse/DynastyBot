# Utahraptor – Downtime Behavior

Utahraptor packs may have their own sleeping and waking schedules, depending on their biomes and what prey is available at what times. During these rest periods, Utahraptor often share space with their packmates, especially those they are closely bonded with such as their mate, siblings, and offspring. Some Utahraptor may even opt to sleep curled beside one another, or in the same nest. Utahraptor also preen one another extensively during these rest periods. Utahraptor maintain their nests during this time, and ensure they remain clean for future offspring, as well as for being a place to sleep. The discovery of bugs or other obscenities in one’s nest may result in the complete removal of the nest and it being moved to a different location. Utahraptor social structure is built off of pack specific communication, with each pack developing its own unique pack language to communicate.
