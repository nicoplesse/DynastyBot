# Utahraptor – Omega Sneaky Nesting

Omegas do not commonly attempt to nest, as it risks their safe position in a pack. However, certain circumstances - such as wishing to start a pack or intense broodiness - can influence them into wishing to nest. Doing so must be done discreetly, as if they are caught, they are immediately exiled from their pack. When both individuals in an Omega pair are interested in nesting together, they slip away from the pack, patrolling through their pack's territory in search for the perfect hidden haven to nest. Without the presence of their pack or their pack’s alphas, Omegas will choose not to nest, as they feel most comfortable in the territory while their pack’s leaders are around to protect it. Once a suitable location is found, the Omega related to the bloodline places the nest where their eggs will be laid. Both raptors work together, carefully decorating the nest with dense foliage and shrubbery to hide their vulnerable young from sight. As they take turns brooding upon their eggs, and possibly their freshly hatching chicks, the pair takes care of one another instinctively. While one remains with the nest, the other returns to their pack, attempting to ensure their kin do not discover their secret. They socialize and bond with their packmates as usual, before eventually sneaking away to deliver food to their waiting mate and young. Offspring born to an Omega pair remain ungrouped in-game, as they are too distant from the pack’s bloodline and are not considered part of the wider pack. Offspring are still required to fill out a bloodline log as usual to prove relations, but must change their bloodline upon aging out of their parents’ care. Without the direct support of a pack, parenting is much more of a challenge for these highly social raptors. The pair becomes hypervigilant and anxious, constantly scanning the horizon for any sign of predators or, worse, their packmates. They preen and quietly coo to one another often, attempting to soothe their mate’s nerves. Terrified of their aging chicks drawing attention with their growing restlessness, the pair are quick to correct any overly noisy behavior with huffed growls and gentle nips. Disruptive, disobedient, or loud chicks are pushed from the nest and territory, finding themselves abandoned. The anxious parents, should the chick not understand, will aggress the offspring away. Otherwise, once chicks hatch, parents do not abandon their offspring. If Discovered by Pack Members When the nest is at risk of being found, parents rush to hide their offspring, nudging them into the secluded nest - or any other foliage nearby. If caught, the packmates who discover the sneaky nest rush to alert the rest of the pack to the area, collecting one or both Alphas in the process. During this short period, some Omega pairs enter a state of panic and choose to flee the POI immediately after detection, willingly dispersing rather than being forced into exile. At first sight of their Alphas, the Omega and their mate become submissive, crouching low and avoiding eye contact, attempting to redeem themselves through affectionate calls and shakes. Alphas remain unfazed by this appeasement behavior, and conduct a brief, intense inspection of the pair and their chicks. Aggression follows quickly after, with Alphas stepping forward to begin a series of threatening calls. Witnessing pack members quickly join in with snarls of their own, and the whole pack works together to push the Omegas out of the territory. Exile is always met with acceptance in these circumstances, and Omegas quickly signal their understanding with forfeiting calls, gathering their chicks and leaving the territory as quickly as possible. Once alone, the pair and their chicks are often left disoriented and afraid, unused to being left without a clear leader. It is not uncommon for the Omega pair to show great distress following their exile, especially for the related. After a brief period of mourning, pairs eventually accept their circumstances, and begin to seek out a new territory to raise their young in, inevitably beginning their own pack. At times, the resulting stress may result in a pair rejecting and abandoning their offspring, choosing to continue exile as a bonded pair instead. Offspring abandoned due to this struggle to survive, relying heavily on siblings  - if they have any - for comfort and safety. Individuals grow to become skittish, forming strong attachments to Utahraptors they meet later in life. If Undiscovered - Offspring Maturity Once one of their offspring reaches Sub-Adult, the pair leads the mature offspring to the border of the pack’s territory, bowing low (:question_emote:) and pushing them away with their bodies. If their offspring fail to understand their gentle attempts at removal, they begin to hiss and snarl, issuing aggressive calls :threaten_call: their way in order to swiftly remove them from the territory. The offspring, though hurt and confused by the sudden fierce rejection, always accept the decision, leaving to try and survive on their own. If they have siblings who have not yet aged out of their parents’ care, they may attempt to linger on the edge of the neighboring territory, waiting to rejoin their dispersing kin and possibly form a new bloodline together. When all offspring have reached maturity and have left the territory, the Omega pair return to their unknowing family and reintegrate back into pack life. Sometimes, the Omega pair chooses to disperse alongside their aging young in order to form a new bloodline together. In this situation, they all slip out of the territory upon the maturity of their eldest offspring, leaving to find a new territory to call their home. Once the newly formed pack creates and/or updates bloodline logs for their new bloodline, they may then group together in-game. Mature offspring of Omega pairs go on to display quieter, more subdued behavior than others of their kind. They may show a natural affinity towards a more stealthy hunting strategy, becoming adept at remaining hidden and laying in wait.
