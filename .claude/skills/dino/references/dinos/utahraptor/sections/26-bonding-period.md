# Utahraptor – Bonding Period

What occurs in the bonding period varies from individual to individual, but most commonly consists of the following: Frequent preening; sniffing one another; extended periods of eye contact; Finding suitable, comfortable places to lay alongside one another, often laying their heads on one another and rubbing their heads together; Hunting critters and other small prey items together; Utahraptor in the bonding period may hunt up to small tier together, and may opt to defend one another from threats, based on personal preference. Building a mock nest and tending to it together; Games of chase; brief sparring matches that never go below half-health; etc. Gift-giving; frequently shiny items, food items, or trophies from kills; If a pack member is nearby, the pack Utahraptor courting may attempt to lead the other individual away from their potential mate, in order to avoid courtship being interrupted. The bonding period consists of at least thirty minutes of these activities. This must occur before any introductions to a dominant pair, or dispersing together. If discovered by members of the pack, the dispersal Utahraptor will be chased out. It is up to the pack Utahraptor if they follow, or if they return to their pack after the unsuccessful courtship. If still interested, the dispersal Utahraptor may return to the same territory after being chased out, if their potential mate has chosen to remain in the territory. They will wait for the rest of the pack to leave before attempting again. Pack Utahraptor who have been caught mid courtship may be more carefully watched by other members of the pack– especially if they are an Omega, who is not permitted to nest. The activities done in the bonding period will often follow the pair through their lifetime together, if the courtship ends up being successful. Once the bonding period is complete, the newly formed pair may either approach the dominant pair of the territory they have courted in (if an individual belongs to the pack), disperse to form their own pack, or disperse from the territory to court into the dispersal Utah’s existing pack.
