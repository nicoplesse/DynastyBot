# Utahraptor – Courting in a Pack Territory

Dispersal Utahraptor will skirt around the edges of the territory of a pack they are interested in courting into, or courting an individual from. Rubbing themselves against and scratching trees, they attempt to create a subtle trail for individuals interested in courtship to follow. They will rarely, if ever, make too much noise, not wanting to run the risk of being found by the dominant pair, or other individuals uninterested in courting them. These Utahraptor may also find gifts to present to potential mates. These dispersal Utahraptor will only ever be tolerated after courtship has been completed with a member of the pack that owns the territory, meaning that if they are caught by any Utahraptor that is not interested in courting them, they will be chased out, following the rules of a normal territory challenge. During the rainy season, solitary dispersal Utahraptor attempting to court are tolerated by packs. These individuals are regarded cautiously by the pack and permitted to linger around the edge of the territory, but may be chased out if they venture too far in. When approached by a Utahraptor coming from further in the pack territory, the dispersal Utahraptor will immediately crouch, cooing softly and presenting their gift, if they have one. If approached by more than one Utahraptor from inside the pack territory, the dispersal interested in courtship will instead flee, unwilling to risk any potential aggression. Multiple members of a pack interested in courtship do not approach a dispersal at the same time and must intiate the contact alone. If multiple dispersals take interest in the same individual, the individual will only display interest in their potential mate. The dispersal that is ignored will quickly recieve the message and move on. The pack Utahraptor will approach slowly, sniffing often, staring down the dispersal Utahraptor. If the dispersal Utahraptor stands too quickly, presents an unsuitable gift, or is otherwise offensive towards the pack Utahraptor, they risk being chased out, and the rest of the pack being alerted. Once the pack Utahraptor has gotten used to the other’s presence, they will shake off, cooing and trilling at the dispersal. At this point, the bonding period begins.
