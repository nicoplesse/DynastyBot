# Latenivenatrix – Courtship

Courtship is performed at night, before the group's communal nest territory is placed for the day's rest. It begins with males placing down a nest that they construct carefully, decorating it with elaborate objects such as branches, bones, feathers, and other items. The more intricate the design the better, with beautifully built nests serving as the most attractive to potential partners. Once placed, males climb atop their constructions, and begin performing their own unique dance. These dances vary between individuals, but often incorporate jumps, soft calls, twists and spins - all to match the individual's own rhythm. One male placing a nest and dancing in this way usually triggers a domino effect in the rest of the rookery, with others rushing to follow suit, ensuring a rookery produces offspring at a similar time. Females seeking a female partner will also construct a nest and perform a dance. Any males that approach her are ignored, with the performing female looking only for female partners. Interested partners will settle at the foot of the nest of the performer and coo softly and affectionately to them. If the performer is uninterested in any of their audience, they turn their backs and ignore them. With no potential partners - or only unsuitable ones - the nest is abandoned entirely. A male is only able to court up to two females at a time, to be able to successfully provide for both and all of his chicks. Males may seek a male partner and settle together after one has performed to woo the other. Once in a pairbond, they may perform for another partner to join them as a trio - and perform together in the same nest. Female pairs seeking a third female will perform the same. Already established pairs or trios still perform the nest construction and courtship dance - though this is entirely performative, and either occurs to signify a desire to nest with their partner(s) - or simply enthusiasm to join in with the rest of the rookery. When creating a nesting post, if the trio consists of 2 males and 1 female, the males will post separate nests, splitting the eggs. The total egg limit cannot be exceeded unless multipliers are used. Trios of 1 male and 2 females, if both females wish to nest, will be done in two separate nesting posts by the male, each listing one of the females. Each female has their individual egg count.
