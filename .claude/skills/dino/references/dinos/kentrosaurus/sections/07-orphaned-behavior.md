# Kentrosaurus – Orphaned Behavior

Crias face significant challenges when hatching alone, but can still make it to adulthood if they are able to avoid predation and other threats. Their instinctual shyness in the first phase of their life is actually beneficial in this regard, driving them to hide from potential threats. As they become more curious and confident in the juvenile phase, they begin searching for others of their kind - or other stegosaurids that they could group with and receive care from. Once adopted by another stegosaurid, crias quickly adapt to the group’s dynamic and settle with ease. They mimic their new “family,” learning their behaviors and preferences, and often form bonds with tolerant individuals - especially Miragaia. Those that do not find others until adulthood are often more cautious as adults, lacking some of the confidence that their peers develop from being cared for when young.
