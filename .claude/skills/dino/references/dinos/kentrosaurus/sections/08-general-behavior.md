# Kentrosaurus – General Behavior

Kentrosaurus - like their cousins Stegosaurus and Miragaia - are a cathemeral species, but showcase active behavior both day and night, with only short naps taken intermittently at both times. During the cooler early morning of dawn, and the cooler period as the sun goes down at dusk, Kentrosaurus are at their most energetic - often engaging in playful sparring, running in open spaces, and shaking their bodies against trees and vegetation. In the hotter midday hours, Kentrosaurus opt to seek shade under trees or rocky overhangs, preferring to rest to avoid overheating - especially in the Dry Season. Kentrosaurus are not territorial, and remain nomadic for all of their lives. However, they are flexible - and will opt to follow the other stegosaurids when in a stegosaurid herd. This means they will confine themselves to a Stegosaurus' territory when grouped with them, but remain nomadic when alone or just with Miragaia. Kentrosaurus are naturally cautious, but not as aggressive as Stegosaurus. Their innate reaction to anything that spooks them is to spit - a behavior that serves as a defensive move by temporarily surprising a would-be predator, as well as something that that signifies to others of their kind. During the Dry Season, Kentrosaurus focus on conserving water and staying close to reliable sources, such as rivers or shaded pools. They feel alarmed if too far from a water source at these times, and quickly prefer to move on if a water source drops to low, undrinkable qualities. In the Rainy Season, Kentrosaurus seem far happier individuals - taking advantage of abundant foliage, and roaming further from known water sources, realizing that water is far more accessible in this period. Kentrosaurus seem to have a fondness for water. They are confident when wading into rivers and shallow pools, using their tails to stir up cooler water over their bodies. During the Dry Season, they frequently bathe and roll in mud to cool down and protect their skin from the sun. Unlike Stegosaurus, they are comfortable entering deeper waters though they are slow swimmers, allowing them to cross rivers when needed. Kentrosaurus particularly enjoy eating nuts and roots, crushing them with their beaks. They forage for food on the ground most often, leaving berries typically for young or other stegosaurids. Kentrosaurus rub against trees frequently, brushing their backs and sides against them to clean and sharpen their spines. This behavior is communal, with several individuals sometimes taking turns on the same tree, leaving distinct markings behind.
