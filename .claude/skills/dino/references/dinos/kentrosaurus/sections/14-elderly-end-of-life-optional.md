# Kentrosaurus – Elderly & End of Life (Optional)

Elder Kentrosaurus begin to slow down as they age, often choosing to remain at the back of the bristle or stegosaurid herd during migrations. They patrol far less, and have slower reaction times - making their reliability as a sentry species for a stegosaurid herd wane over time. Joint stiffness and muscle fatigue becomes more common, limiting their ability to use their tails with the precision and strength they once had. As a result, elders are less likely to engage in defensive displays, relying on younger herd members for protection and intimidation. As their mobility diminishes, elder Kentrosaurus spend longer periods resting and grazing in shaded or protected areas. They prefer areas with plenty of cover where they can remain undisturbed. Despite their reduced mobility, elder Kentrosaurus remain deeply protective of the bristle's younger members. They act as calming presences, using their tails to nudge crias or juveniles away from danger or toward safer grazing spots. Those elders that do manage to still produce young are far more likely to accept a grouped Miragaia's offer of parental care for their young.
