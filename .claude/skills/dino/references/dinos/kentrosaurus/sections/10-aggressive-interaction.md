# Kentrosaurus – Aggressive Interaction

Disagreements - usually over prime resting spots, during courtship, or just over who gets to eat first - are initiated with spits, aimed directly at each other's faces. Kentrosaurus are also excessively dramatic - and are prone to loud, angry bellows and tail swings to show their displeasure. All scuffles within a bristle begin with spits, and typically end there before anything more physical occurs. Kentrosaurus that encounter another Kentrosaurus bristle - and do not have room for others to join them - become aggressive if neither are willing to leave the POI. First encounters begin with both bristles approaching each other and inspecting members over. In some cases, a bristle might have too many of one gender - and if this is the case, especially for both sides, both groups are amicable to one another, splintering off to form new bristles with more even gender ratios. For those that have little interest in this, and wish to push the other out of the POI or steal their place in the Stegosaurid herd instead - the confrontation escalates to a Challenge. Challenges to remain in the POI: Challenges to remain in a POI is only possible if both bristle groupings are not part of a Stegosaurid herd - or both bristles are accompanied by their own stegosaurid herd. When not in a stegosaurid herd, the entirety of the Kentrosaurus bristle will face off against other Kentrosaurus. This challenge always begins with spitting back and forth, before aggression rapidly escalates into fierce tail smacks against each other's bodies, with the intention to cause their rivals to bleed out and forfeit. This challenge is fought until an individual on one of the sides reaches deathscars - which then forfeits the challenge for their entire bristle, and forces them to abandon their rights to the area and leave the POI. If in a stegosaurid herd that is being challenged by another stegosaurid herd, Kentrosaurus will not join the fight, standing on the sidelines nervously and leaving the combat to the Stegosaurus or Miragaia in their herd. Challenges to take a place in the Stegosaurid Herd: When Kentrosaurus approach a stegosaurid herd to challenge a rival Kentrosaurus out and take their place, there are three options: 1) If the Kentrosaurus bristle has room, the new Kentrosaurus are absorbed into the bristle grouped in the stegosaurid herd. 2) If a bristle is full but needs to balance its gender ratio, and the new Kentrosaurus helps achieve this, members of the more numerous gender will compete in battles with each other to weed out the weakest member. The individual who loses the most battles (or simply forfeits) leaves to make room for the newcomer. If a Kentrosaurus bristle is entirely female for example, and the new approaching Kentrosaurus is a male, all females will fight until a clear loser is decided. The common loser of each challenge is ousted from the bristle, making space for the new male. Sometimes, if two bristles approach, they might opt to swap members and form new bristles without any fighting. Additionally, some Kentrosaurus may choose to leave willingly, giving up their spot without the need for bloodshed. 3) If the Kentrosaurus bristle does not have room, and a balanced gender ratio, the approaching Kentrosaurus must challenge for a position to replace someone in the bristle. They must single out another individual in the bristle to target as their sparring partner - and solidify the choice by spitting over that Kentrosaurus. The attacked Kentrosaurus will spit back, before the two move into the open and begin a challenge - landing fierce and aggressive hits with their tails. This battle continues until one individual reaches deathscars - thus forfeiting the engagement, and being forced to give up their place to the newcomer. If the bristle member is forced to give up their spot and has a close bond with another, or is a mother with offspring, the offspring will go with her - and the closely bonded individual may choose to leave with them.
