# Alioramus – Males

Male Alioramus have a more fluid role within a pack. Naturally competitive and prone to aggression, they frequently engage in sparring matches with other males, keen to assert dominance and test their strength. While these encounters can appear violence, they are often mutual and function as structured outlets for tension, rather than true aggression. Unlike females, males do not seek lifelong bonds with another, and are perfectly capable of living a solitary life if needed. However, most prefer to be grouped with others - if only for more chance of hunting success - and some go further, developing small bonds with others. Brothers often stick together, preferring familiarity, but male Alioramus are also open to forming small, casual friendships with unrelated males, creating loose-knit social groups within the pack. Brothers show the strongest male to male tolerance. Males raised together tend to spar more frequently, but utilize greater restraint, engaging in rough play that rarely escalates to serious injury. Male Alioramus are highly sensitive to changes in pack composition. The arrival - or loss - of females dramatically alters male behavior within the pack. Without females present, all male groups become increasingly restless and unpredictable, often forming aggressive cliques and engaging in bullying behaviors. Stress and excitement are expressed primarily through physicality. Increased pacing, higher rates of sparring and air snapping are common during periods of tension. Conversely, well balanced packs see males displaying more restraint. Sparring is less frequent and resolves more quickly - and males exhibit greater cooperation and tolerance. In rare cases, older or socially experienced males develop a stabilizing influence within a mixed sex pack. These individuals intercept fights between younger males, intervene in disputes, and show increased tolerance. However, even these stabilizing males struggle to maintain order in the absence of females, becoming restless and volatile over time.
