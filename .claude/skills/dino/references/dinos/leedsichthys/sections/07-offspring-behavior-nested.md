# Leedsichthys – Offspring Behavior (Nested)

Leedsichthys babies, known as pups, enter the world already capable of swimming and feeding independently. Despite this independence, they remain closely gathered around their nest and father. Immediately after hatching, pups wriggle free from the kelp nest constructed by their father, and slip into the water below. Newly hatched pups have an instinct to remain close to their siblings, forming a tightly packed school formation almost immediately. Rather than exploring alone, pups move together as one collective, with the movement of one individual triggering a mirror reaction from the rest of the group. If one pup flees, the others follow, even if they have not detected the threat themselves. Pups that suddenly find themselves without the safety of their siblings become distressed, and actively search for other young or their father. During their earliest growth, pups remain extremely close to their father. They gather beneath, behind, and alongside him, using him as both a landmark and protection from any approaching predators. Pups learn much of their early behavior by following both their father and one another. When their father begins to feed, the nursery group gathers around him and imitates his movements, gradually learning to identify more productive feeding waters. Young Leedsichthys frequently initiate sudden bursts of movement during travels, causing their siblings to follow in what appears to be a playful game. Pups often repeatedly chase one another and race across different terrains. Pups are extremely skittish towards unfamiliar species, but gradually lose this fear as they gain size. As they approach adolescence, the juvenile schooling behavior begins to lessen. Individuals start to swim more independently, no longer mirroring the movements of the group as a whole. By Sub-Adult, Leedsichthys disperse fully from their family and begin a solitary life. They may choose to continue to swim with familiar individuals for a while - or even indefinitely - while others drift away completely.
