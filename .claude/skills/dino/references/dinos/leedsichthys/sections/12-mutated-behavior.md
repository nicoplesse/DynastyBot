# Leedsichthys – Mutated Behavior

Albino Leedsichthys are incredibly sensitive to light, with direct sunlight causing extreme discomfort, and prolonged exposure leading to permanent blindness. As a result, Albino Leedsichthys live in near constant darkness, restricted to deep waters where sunlight barely filters through. These individuals rarely find the opportunity to travel communally with other Leedsichthys due to this. Melanistic Leedsichthys face no difficulties. Leedsichthys are not aggressive to one another, and any unwanted behaviors are handled by simply swimming away from the offender. Frequent negative interactions with the same Leedsichthys may cause individuals to make a conscious choice in avoiding them for longer periods of time. Leedsichthys rarely defend their personal space from other Leedsichthys when travelling communally, instead preferring to remain close together in order to deter predators and shelter young. Leedsichthys are inherently interested in the activities of other species, often pausing their migrations to observe when something new comes into view. Large and Apex aquatic species, like Deinosuchus, Sarcosuchus, Spinosaurus, are a common sight within the waters Leedsichthys inhabit. After initial  encounters, Leedsichthys eventually find their curiosity dimmed, preferring to place attention on species far more different than themselves. Though curious, Leedsichthys never go out of their way to continue pestering a species if they have displayed clear discomfort or aggression, preferring to avoid confrontation where possible.
