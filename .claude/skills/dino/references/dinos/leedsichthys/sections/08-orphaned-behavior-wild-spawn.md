# Leedsichthys – Orphaned Behavior (Wild Spawn)

Orphaned Leedsichthys retain the same powerful schooling instincts as nested pups, but without a father or siblings to follow, they are considerably more nervous and vulnerable. Lone pups are extremely skittish, rarely remaining in one place for long. They search the surrounding waters for other young Leedsichthys, frequently moving between feeding areas in the hopes of encountering a nursery group. When frightened, orphaned pups rush to seek safety among terrain or dense vegetation. Without a father to retreat towards, they are much more likely to flee immediately from unfamiliar species. When an orphan encounters a nursery group of Leedsichthys of a similar age and size, they immediately show interest in joining it. The orphan begins following the movements of the group from a short distance, mirroring changes in direction and gradually moving closer until they incorporate themselves amongst the other pups. Young Leedsichthys show little concern for whether or not a pup is related to them, and readily accept similarly-sized orphans into their group. Fathers, additionally, protect any young that merge with their nursery group. Orphans sometimes encounter one another naturally. Two or more similarly-aged orphans may immediately begin schooling together, forming their own nursery group and permitting other orphans to join them as they discover them. As orphans grow, their reliance on finding a family or siblings gradually diminishes. Hatchlings, juveniles and even young adolescents are firmly drawn towards others of their kind of a similar age, but larger Adolescents and above eventually accept and become content to travel alone. At Sub-Adult, both orphaned and nested Leedsichthys are indistinguishable from one another in terms of behavior, with both dispensing into a solitary lifestyle natural to that of adults. Leedsichthys are incredibly large, peaceful filter feeders that spend much of their lives slowly travelling through open waters, in search of productive feeding grounds with large amounts of krill. Adult Leedsichthys are predominantly solitary. They are perfectly comfortable travelling for long periods without the company of their own species. Despite this - they are not antisocial, and readily tolerate other Leedsichthys that choose to travel alongside them. Leedsichthys are highly migratory, and travel considerable distances in response to the availability of food. They rarely remain within unproductive waters for long, and readily abandon an area once available krill begins to decline. When travelling, Leedsichthys conserve their energy, maintaining a slow and steady pace as low energy species. Sudden bursts of speed are uncommon outside of play behavior, courting, or escaping/challenging a threat. Leedsichthys are naturally curious once they reach adulthood, particularly towards unfamiliar species and unusual disturbances. They investigate by slowly approaching or swimming alongside a subject - though keep enough distance to be able to move away comfortably. This curiosity is passive rather than intrusive. They do not persistently pursue species that move away from them. Their impressive size makes adult Leedsichthys relatively confident around smaller species. Others swimming close to them are generally ignored, provided they do not behave aggressively.
