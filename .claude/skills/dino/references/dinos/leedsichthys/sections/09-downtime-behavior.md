# Leedsichthys – Downtime Behavior

Leedsichthys sleep in short bursts, with no particular preference in where and when they choose to do so. During sleep, Leedsichthys’ muscles, particularly around their jaw, loosen to ensure they do not asphyxiate during rest. Pups eagerly take advantage of this behavior, and shelter within their father’s mouth during rest periods, hiding themselves away from opportunistic predators. Adult Leedsichthys are predominantly solitary. They do not form structured groups or social hierarchies, and spend most of their lives migrating independently across open waters. Individuals vary in their tolerance for companionship. Some are content to migrate alongside others for extended periods, while others actively prefer solitude, and avoid travelling with others of their kind. Leedsichthys that choose to travel together do not group in game, and do not defend one another in engagements. Companions may freely separate and go in different directions at any time. Leedsichthys do not attempt to prevent their companion from leaving, and separation causes little distress - even between individuals that have travelled together for long periods. Outside of courtship and parenthood, Leedsichthys have little need for social interaction. Encounters between individuals are usually peaceful, with neither attempting to establish dominance over the other.
