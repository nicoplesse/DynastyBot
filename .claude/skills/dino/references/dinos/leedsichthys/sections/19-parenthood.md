# Leedsichthys – Parenthood

Leedsichthys exhibit paternal care. Fathers assume full responsibility for their offspring, while females take no active role in rearing young. Females show little interest in their offspring after laying their eggs. A female that happens to encounter her own pups treats them much as she would any other young Leedsichthys. Some females display a mild curiosity towards a father travelling with pups, and follow the nursery group from a distance for a short period. This does not develop into maternal care,  and mothers do not assist in protecting or raising offspring. Once pups hatch, a father's primary responsibility is to keep his school of young together. Fathers move slowly enough for pups to follow, and monitors the dense group gathering around and behind him. Once all offspring have hatched and are ready to leave the nesting grounds, the male destroys his nest before they swim away together. Fathers regularly alter their course and circle back when their school of young become too scattered, encouraging stragglers to return to the safety of the greater group. Much of Leedsichthys fatherhood resembles crowd control as opposed to constant affection. Fathers spend considerable time simply keeping their numerous excitable pups together, migrating with him in the same direction. Despite this, fathers may show some affection towards their pups. They tolerate their young crowding closely against their bodies, and may gently brush against them, remaining patient as pups weave around them during play. Fathers defend their offspring from predators, and position their bodies between a threat and their young whenever possible. When threatened, fathers prioritise gathering pups and leading them away from danger, rather than pursuing a predator. Paternal bonds weaken as offspring grow more independent. Fathers become less concerned by adolescents straying from the school grouping, and make less effort to gather them.
