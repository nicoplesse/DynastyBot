# Deinosuchus (Ocean) – Aggressive Interaction

Deinosuchus are notoriously food aggressive, even towards their own groupmates. Brothers do not share a meal directly at the same time, with the more submissive male being made to wait until his brother is finished before he can eat from the corpse. Females act in just the same way - but expect food to be shared with them when paired with a mate. Males are forced to curb their food aggression in this situation, permitting his female mate to eat from a corpse at the same time. Males never tolerate another male in his claimed territory with one exception: a brother that they have not separated with since hatching. In this instance, both brothers will claim the same territory and remain amicable until a female enters the territory to be courted. Once a female arrives and shows interest in courtship, the more submissive brother will leave the territory to claim one of his own. This is often - where possible - a bordering territory to his sibling. The withdrawal prevents conflict, and ensures each male maintains access to future mates. Once brothers have split, they never tolerate each other again and cannot reform a pair with each other. Females, however, are always tolerated in a male's territory except when he is in a pair with another female. In such cases, the resident female may drive off the intruding female herself, if the male has not already done so. Challenging for territory When a male enters another male's territory and seeks to claim it for himself, he must confront the current owner in a dangerous contest. The rival male approaches slowly and deliberately, broadcasting his presence through loud, aggressive bellows and deep rumbles. If the resident male of the territory does not flee, both males must engage in a territory battle. These battles are vicious, with males tearing and ripping at one another with their powerful jaws. The first male to reach deathscars is forced to leave the area, leaving the other as the winner, and thus the new (or retained) owner of the territory. Driving out other Deinosuchus Once a male (or a pair of males) have claimed a territory, all other males are driven out on sight. Paired brothers will still work independently to drive out rivals, and usually patrol different parts of the territory to ensure they are vigilantly checked. All females are tolerated - unless the male in the territory is already paired with a female to raise young. In this case, either he or his mate may drive out the female. All Deinosuchus of adolescent and below - regardless of sex - are driven out by males. The only exception is young that are close to a paired female. Sadly, if a mother dies, her protection dies with her. Her offspring - even if biological offspring to the male - are no longer tolerated, and are forced to flee or be killed. Defending young Young are exclusively protected by their mothers. Males are responsible for patrolling the territory and driving out rival males (and females), while mothers defend their offspring. Offspring that stray too far from their mother may be aggressed by their father while in his territory. As offspring reach adolescence, mothers no longer defend them.
