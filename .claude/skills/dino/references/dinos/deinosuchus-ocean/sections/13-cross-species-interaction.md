# Deinosuchus (Ocean) – Cross-Species Interaction

Deinosuchus does not hunt healthy adult Deinocheirus, but may attempt to hunt unprotected Deinocheirus offspring, up to adolescent. Should an adult return to protect the offspring, Deinosuchus will forfeit the hunt. Unhealthy Deinocheirus have open wounds or are albino. Deinosuchus only ever attempts this on solitary individuals. Deinosuchus tolerate scavengers. They permit them to feed from their claimed corpses once they are full themselves. Deinosuchus enjoy hunting Sarcosuchus - and males drive them out of their territory when not hunting them, due to their similar niche and pressure they may put on prey and fish stocks within the territory. However, if there are 3 Sarcosuchus in the territory, and the male feels unable to drive them out, he must leave for a new territory. Female Deinosuchus behave similarly to males against Sarcosuchus when they have young within a male's territory, and assist in driving Sarcosuchus out when not hunting them. Scavenger species are recognized for their grooming abilities. When spotting these species, Deinosuchus slip out of the water and open their jaws, providing access for these nimble creatures to pick out pieces of flesh from between their teeth. They especially appreciate these creatures when shedding. They do not hunt Rhamphorhynchus or Compsognathus. All other species are ignored by Deinosuchus, unless hungry. If challenged by a territorial species, Deinosuchus may opt to defend for the right to remain in the POI, or move on to safer waters.
