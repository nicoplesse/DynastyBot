# Deinosuchus (Ocean) – Social Interaction

Deinosuchus aren't social creatures by nature, but form a more casual relationship and bond with their partner. When same sex females travel together, they often communicate in low growls and show light affection towards one another. Males, however, do not show affection to one another and simply form an uneasy truce - though this only ever happens with a brother. When a male and female pair, the male will dote on the female initially with affection, before backing away to give her space within his territory when eggs begin to hatch. Deinosuchus rely heavily on posture, movement and vibrations within the water to communicate with one another. When approaching other Deinosuchus, females open their jaws wide in a large yawn-like motion, signalling their sex to the stranger. Males, meanwhile, let out loud, rumbling hisses and thrash their heads from side to side - a much more aggressive display, and unmistakable. Females pay little attention to others and have a more tolerant nature - only reacting aggressively when her space is invaded, or when forming a temporary bond with a male to raise young. Males only tolerate the presence of females in their territory, and drive out any rival males. They only drive out females when paired with one and raising young. Males that hold territory for a long time may recognize other males in bordering territories. Over time, both males establish an invisible boundary between the two territories, preferring to hold an uneasy truce with the neighboring male, rather than continuous battles. Eye contact is kept brief between individuals, with long, deliberate stares being taken as aggression.
