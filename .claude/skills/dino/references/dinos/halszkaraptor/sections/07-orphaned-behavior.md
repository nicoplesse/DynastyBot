# Halszkaraptor – Orphaned Behavior

A newly hatched Halszkaraptor without parents instinctively stays within the safety of its hatching site, chirping to themselves every now and again. As the Hatchling reaches Juvenile, the dire realization that its parents are not coming begins to sink in. Hunger and loneliness drives them to roam the nearby area, searching for any sign of a parental figure. The orphaned Halszkaraptor will explore endlessly, looking for a species to imprint upon to provide food. When desperately hungry, Halzkaraptor orphans attempt to eat anything that looks like food, testing reeds and bushes and seeking out berries and roots. If the orphan is lucky enough to find another Halszkaraptor, it attaches to the other individual strongly. The orphan stays nearby its new parent at all times, observing and mimicking its every move. Orphans imprint on the first species they come across, even if this is a carnivore. The young Halzkaraptor will rush up to their “parent” and chirp softly, staying as close to their legs as possible, and following them everywhere. Due to an instinctual fear, Halszkaraptor do not imprint on crocodilian species such as Deinosuchus or Sarcosuchus.  They also do not imprint on raptor-like species, such as: Deinonychus, Latenivenatrix, Alioramus, Achillobator, Utahraptor. If the orphan is attacked by their “parent”, they flee a short distance and continue to follow, careful to stay just out of reach. The rejection of an imprinted parent is extremely distressing to the orphan, and they may become more vulnerable to death from exposure by eating less often and forgetting to avoid other predatory species. Persistent orphans sometimes carefully try to approach again, even if rejected multiple times, often when the “parent” is sleeping.  However, any orphan will eventually relent if continually aggressed. Halszkaraptor that imprint refuse to leave their adoptive parent until they reach Sub-Adult, even if their “parent” is intolerant to them. At this age, their adult instincts kick in and they seek out their own kind. These desperate attempts to be accepted by a “parent” can stretch into their adult life. Even when joining a raft, orphaned Halzkaraptor seek companionship desperately. With other Halzkaraptor, previous orphans can be particularly sensitive to rejection by others. They tend to form stronger friendships with others and can come across as clingy, often becoming particularly protective parents. While migrating on land, the raft moves in a single file line, a tactic used to minimize their physical footprint and reduce attraction from potential predators. Halszkaraptor are keenly aware of their place in the food chain and always avoids danger where possible. The joyful socialization behaviors seen in a raft are somewhat dimmed, and individuals lose their confidence when near predators. Halszkaraptor are fascinated by shiny and colorful objects. They often get distracted by bright flowers or shining pebbles, hoarding these items like treasures in their nests or resting places. When the raft is relaxed and not facing danger, Halszkaraptor sometimes gather together on the water's surface, before diving in unison to hunt fish with shimmering scales or for shiny objects. After successful catches, they celebrate together, performing a symphony of calls and songs, nuzzling together and dancing. When back on land, Halszkaraptor spend a considerable amount of time preening one another, and locating a sunny rock to bask on to help dry their plumage. If an individual sustains a wound from a predator - or accidental injury - they seek out a patch of thick mud. Sitting and shaking their feathers into the mud helps to coat injuries and prevent infection. Halszkaraptor that are particularly clean will also bathe in mud to rid themselves of feather lice. Halszkaraptor find delight in climbing to the top of waterfalls and leaping off into the water below in a chaotic, playful spree. A single Halszkaraptor performing a dance can trigger a chain reaction within the raft, with others quickly joining in until the whole group is a wave of joyful, erratic movement, strengthening their bonds. When a member of the raft finds lakeweed, other Halszkaraptor attempt to steal it, starting a rapid game of chase. The Halszkaraptor attempts to catch up to the member with the lakeweed, lightly snapping at their tail. Should the Halszkaraptor get bitten, they drop the lakeweed ready for the next member to take it. Feather Lice This is a roleplay behavior, and not an in-game feature. This is an optional affliction that affects Halzskaraptor under certain circumstances. Halzskaraptor that spend too much time on land can sometimes pick up tiny parasites, known as feather lice from the damp vegetation on the edge habitat of rivers and lakes. They become increasingly itchy, and may shuffle their feathers more often in an attempt to groom themselves. These small nuisances are left behind when larger dinosaurs swim across bodies of water, shying away from being submerged and waiting at the water’s edge for an unsuspecting victim. To rid themselves of parasites, Halzskaraptor bathe themselves in mud patches, or fully submerge themselves in deep water to dislodge the miniature hitchhikers. When joining a new raft, the newcomer will be carefully watched and many Halzskaraptor will choose to keep a safe distance until they have witnessed a thorough cleansing process by bathing in mud.
