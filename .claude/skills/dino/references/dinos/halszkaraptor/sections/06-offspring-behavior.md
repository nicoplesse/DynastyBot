# Halszkaraptor – Offspring Behavior

Upon hatching, Halszkaraptor imprint on the first dinosaur they see, ensuring they learn essential survival behaviors. Luckily for them, this is almost always their parents. Imprinting on a species other than their own can be fatal, as hatchlings tend to only show fear when attacked or if the rest of the raft is panicked. As their small size makes them an easy snack for many carnivores, Halszkaraptor have evolved to gain full mobility within moments of hatching. Hatchling Halszkaraptor are excitable and loud, chirping happily in their warm and secure nests. They are particularly fond of their parents and are loudest when they are nearby. Halszkaraptor hatchlings only call using the chatter call. Other calls are only made once they reach Juvenile and have more developed vocal chords. Hatchlings splash about in the shallows of nearby ponds, lakes and rivers. When they are comfortable, they practice diving to the depths of shallow areas to build up their ability to hold their breath for extended periods of time. Young Halzkaraptor dash about with bursts of energy, but tire extremely quickly and return to their nest to rest often. After diving, offspring often take a short nap in their nest to recover. Hatchlings are entirely dependent on their guardians - any Adult member of the raft - to provide food and they excitedly jump about when led to food, or are provided with morsels in their nest. As Halszkaraptor reach Juvenile, their confidence grows, prompting them to begin experimenting with more complex calls. They do this by mimicking the calls they hear from other members of their raft. Once one Juvenile uses a new call, others immediately copy, causing a joyful chorus of young voices all repeating the same notes and body movement. Their extremely small size means they require an almost constant supply of food. Juvenile Halszkaraptor start swimming with the Adults, searching for food along river banks and lake shores, never straying too far from the nest. Where possible, Hatchlings and Juveniles prefer feasting on fruits to fuel their rapid movements. Halszkaraptor offspring are particularly attracted to the sight of bright colours and may get distracted by them. Should the distraction be something nearby that they can pick up, they will take the colourful object to their nest. As Halszkaraptor reach adolescence, they start to explore further from the nest, without being accompanied by their imprinted guardian. It is at this time that rafts may choose to leave their nesting area behind and travel around their current POI, or potentially migrate if local resources are limited. Adult Halszkaraptor take the adolescents on extended swimming expeditions, teaching their young which species are safe to interact with. See Cross-Species Interaction for more detail. Sub-Adult Halszkaraptor are considered fully independent within the raft, but do not begin courting until they reach Adulthood. Halszkaraptor rarely disperse from their natal rafts, and often stay with them for life.
