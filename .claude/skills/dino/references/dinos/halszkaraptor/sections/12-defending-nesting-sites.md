# Halszkaraptor – Defending nesting sites

Up to Small tier carnivores are not permitted near to a nesting site. All members of a raft will work together to mob and drive away these carnivores. As their once-explosive speed fades with age, an elderly Halszkaraptor comes to rely on stealth and caution to avoid danger. In their final days, they may undertake quiet pilgrimages to locations from their past, with some even returning to the place they hatched to pass away. Slower movements in old age lead to increased risk of feather lice, so elderly members spend an increasing amount of their time meticulously grooming themselves and rolling in mud baths. When a member of the raft passes away, the others gather around the body in a quiet, somber circle to mourn their loss. Members will gather small bunches of reeds, branches, and roots to cover the body of their fallen raft member. They lay them haphazardly over the body, trying to cover up the scent and protect their raft from hungry predators. Halszkaraptor do not imprint on: Deinosuchus, Sarcosuchus, Deinonychus, Achillobator, Alioramus, Latenivenatrix, Utahraptor. If a hen is nesting with multiple drakes, she must split her egg amount between them, posting separate nest posts with the lowered egg counts for the amount of eggs fathered by each male.
