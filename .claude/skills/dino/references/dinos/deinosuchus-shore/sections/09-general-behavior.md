# Deinosuchus (Shore) – General Behavior

Deinosuchus are very lazy crocodilians, often basking on the shore and preferring not to expend too much of their energy. They adapt surprisingly well to brackish and saltwater environments, tolerating salinity levels that other  freshwater crocodilians cannot. Because of this, they are frequently found near estuaries and beaches. These powerful swimmers are also known to venture out into the open ocean, using strong tidal currents and their immense stamina to travel between islands or along the coastline in search of better territory, food sources, or mates. Females are sedentary by nature, lingering in various POIs until they find one with abundant hunting opportunities. They remain there until these resources begin to dwindle, before moving on in search of another fruitful location. They sometimes travel with one same sex related individual - this most often ends up being a sister, though sometimes mother and daughter pairs can be spotted together. Males, on the other hand, are fiercely territorial and defend a medium sized stretch of water from all other male Deinosuchus, and Sarcosuchus. Kaprosuchus are always tolerated within a male's territory. Males are always solitary, or rarely they may have a strong bond with a brother, and will claim and defend territory together. However, only one may engage at a time. Females are always permitted to pass through a male's territory, unless the male is already paired with a female. If he is already paired, the new female will be driven out by either sex. Deinosuchus spend a large portion of the day basking along the banks of rivers, lakes, or the beach. In order to properly regulate their body temperature, they require long exposure to the sun on their thick skin. They lay motionless with their mouths open while they do so - a behavior used to cool the brain, by letting heat evaporate from their open mouths and tongues. During particularly hot periods - particularly in the Dry Season - they may retreat to cooler waters, or move over to shaded areas to prevent themselves from overheating. Should prey be spotted whilst basking, Deinosuchus are quick to scurry back beneath the depths, enabling them to lay in wait for a hunting opportunity. At night, Deinosuchus returns almost exclusively to the water, submerging into deeper water when needing additional warmth. Deinosuchus need to shed their scales regularly, often sloughing off large pieces at a time. When coming into a shed period, they become more irritable, and stay in shallower waters, rubbing themselves against rocks and logs, and instinctively seeking out small, groomer species to assist them. They become highly active during periods of rain or storms. Potentially due to the scent of prey being more detectable - or perhaps the temperature shifts - they seem to exhibit signs of excitement: surfacing and breaching suddenly, snapping at raindrops, or patrolling more actively. Males tend to become much more vocal at night - rising to the surface to make loud vocalizations, and slapping at the water with their jaws. Deinosuchus is a strong apex predator, but its true abilities shine when it is in water, so they do not favor being on land for long stretches at a time. Traveling far inland is uncommon, and more likely to be exhibited by an ill or aging Deinosuchus. As such, they may not begin a hunt from on land. Deinosuchus do not enter small pools of water - such as the ponds at Wilderness Peak, Hot Springs, Impact Crater, Whistling Columns or Savanna Grasslands.
