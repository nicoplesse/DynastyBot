# Deinosuchus (Shore) – Elderly & End of Life (Optional)

As Deinosuchus age, they become slower and lazier, with fast dashes on land (or in water) kept to a minimum, replaced instead with leisurely ambles. After a life shaped by hunting and battles, Deinosuchus start to pick their challenges and hunting opportunities more carefully. Hunting spots are kept to proven and known areas, where success is more likely, and less troublesome prey is sought out. In particular, females lose interest in courtship, with egg production slowing considerably. Though they are not rendered fully infertile, they have significantly fewer clutches. Elderly Deinosuchus stick to more inland water sources, and spend much more time basking on land. As they age, they become increasingly prone to confusion, losing track of typical migration routes and favorable water sources - and even turn to wandering too far in land. Sadly, this often hastens the demise of these crocodilians, with especially disoriented individuals ending up in arid areas and far from a water source - and vulnerable to terrestrial predators.
