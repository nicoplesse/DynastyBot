# Achillobator – Offspring Behavior (Nested)

Achillobator hatchlings, known as pups, emerge from their eggs small, soft, and incredibly fragile. They trill and squawk softly for the attention of their parents, who eagerly give it, nuzzling their pups and issuing soft coos. Within a few minutes, pups are able to move around on their own, and quickly shuffle to find comfortable places in the nest before curling up to find a comfortable place to sleep. Buried in dense foliage or dens, pups are well-fed, well-maintained, and well-protected. These nesting sites are often surrounded in Latenivenatrix nests - and are as equally defended by them, as Latenivenatrix treat Achillobator similarly to their own offspring. As Achillobator pups reach Juvenile, they begin to show more interest in the things around them, and start to initiate play with other pups in their clutch, their parents, and other Latenivenatrix - if available! Pups might also nip at each other and their parents as a way of exploring; but are gently corrected and taught appropriate behaviors. The family will resume migrating at this time. During migrations, pups often jump onto the backs and sides of their parents, and even onto Latenivenatrix companions, if able! Pups also jump excitedly in greeting to new individuals, familiar individuals, their siblings, or at the sight of something they enjoy. When attended by their parents, Achillobator pups may squawk, bark, whine, and yelp for food, water, or attention. At times, they even do this when they are very tired while attempting to fight sleep. However, Achillobator are instinctively quiet when left unattended by their parents, and even when left alone with Latenivenatrix. Towards the end of their Juvenile stage, immediately before Adolescent, pups experience a brief fear stage, where things that they were accustomed to previously suddenly become frightening to them. With positive reinforcement and encouragement from their parents, Achillobator pups quickly regain their confidence, and may approach things with renewed vigor. If improperly introduced, or given reason to fear something without reassurance, pups openly avoid it in future, even far into adulthood. By the time pups are Adolescent, they show more readiness for independence, and may stray slightly farther from their parents. This is also the time where they begin to show interest in hunting for themselves -  but do not attempt to hunt things larger than them, and do not attempt to hunt with their parents or Latenivenatrix. They may hunt critters or anything smaller than them independently. They explore new curiosities by scratching and biting them, and may rub their sides and cheeks against trees and rocks. As they continue to develop into adolescence, individuals start to express more personality traits; varying from dominant and confident, to shy and skittish. Dominant pups are often more destructive, destroying nests and digging deeper marks into trees, rocks, and dens. They are louder and like to show off often. This may eventually be past the limit that Achillobator parents can bear, resulting in them being rushed out of the group and territory. Submissive pups will even openly avoid their parents, preferring the companionship of Latenivenatrix to their own family. Often, they disperse early. At Sub-Adult, Achillobator pups, now grown, disperse on their own, are gently chased away, or ignored by their parents.
