# Kelenken – Courtship & Parenthood

Courting is an elaborate affair whereby unpaired Kelenken will gather together in the middle of the night and put on a display to attract a mate. This behavior is actually one of the more vulnerable times in a Kelenken's life cycle, as it is when they are at their loudest and out in the open. Cobs begin their display by picking a spot in the open, and begin clacking their beak, lifting their head and flapping their wings. During this dance they release low rumbles and may move from side to side as well. After their first dance ends, the cob will crane his head to puff out his chest feathers, and then preens his chest and wings. The dance begins again, both the displaying and preening behaviors following one another in a cycle until either a mate is impressed, or he grows tired and ends his display. Pens (and sometimes cobs) watch the display from afar, and approach very slowly, clacking their beaks if they are interested. Impressed individuals eventually find the dance irresistible, and join in, lifting their heads and flapping their wings in the same dance, much to the cob's delight. Once mates have been secured, they sit beside each other, grooming and beak clacking contentedly; the beginning of a hopefully long and fruitful relationship together. Some pens may seek to impress other pens to form a lifelong pair bond with. In this event they will behave the same as cobs, performing a dance in a hope to entice impressed pens to their side. If it is a pen that is performing the courtship dance, cobs will never show interest. Once a pairbond has formed, courting does not need to be initiated again in order to form a nest. Both parents will work together to secure a nesting site, which they will viciously defend from other dinosaurs and Kelenken outside of their cast. Cobs and pens in a pairbond will take it in turns to bring objects back to their nest to decorate it. Anything from reeds, branches, pinecones to flowers may be used. The more elaborate the nest the better, and the deeper the pairbond will be. Same sex pairs may exhibit the same behavior and build a nest, even though they will never produce eggs together. Once they realise that their nest will not produce any viable eggs, they may notice other chicks within the cast, and steal one to raise it when the parents' backs are turned. The chick will happily transfer over to their new parents so long as they are doted on. Only one chick may be stolen at a time for a same sex Kelenken pair to raise. If a parent notices that a chick has been stolen by a same sex pair, they will usually turn a blind eye, providing they have at least one other offspring to care for. If the chick stolen was their only chick, they will attack the other pair, only backing down when either they or their mate is grievously injured (death scars). Orphaned chicks from outside the cast may also be adopted by same-sex Kelenken pairs in this way. Opposite sex Kelenken pairs will only adopt if an orphaned chick that is a similar age to their own, manages to sneak into the existing nest. Both parents will take it in turns to bring food back to the nest and care for their offspring. One parent almost always stays at the nest or with chicks, to defend them from others. At juvenile, chicks can start to leave the safety of the nest, and are usually accompanied by at least one watchful parent. When parents leave to hunt, chicks are ushered into the safety of dense undergrowth, or if a pair has a close friendship with another pair they may choose to group their offspring together for safety while a hunting party is out. When chicks reach Sub-Adult, they are able to leave the cast they were born in. They always prefer to remain in the cast with their parents if there is room for them. In some instances, a cast may have no room for newly aged-out offspring. Parents often choose to split off from their current cast to form a new cast with their young. This usually results in them being forced to leave the POI. Kelenken that lose a mate whilst having offspring will fall into a period of deep sadness and end up rejecting their chicks. The chicks become a constant reminder of what they have lost, and the remaining parent cannot bear the sight of them any longer. These chicks may then be adopted by pairs within the cast. Abandoned chicks without a cast to rely on are forced to strike out with any siblings they may have.
