# Kelenken – Offspring Behavior

Baby Kelenken, known as chicks, are born helpless as soon as they hatch from the egg. Recovering from their struggle out of the egg, chicks spend the entirety of their hatchling stage within the nest, huddled together against their siblings for warmth. Both parents return to the nest frequently with food for their chicks, causing chicks to squawk and clack their beaks loudly as they rally for attention and to be the first one fed. At all other times, up until juvenile, chicks remain silent and motionless within the nest, only occasionally clicking at their siblings. As they are born in nests on the ground, they instinctively keep themselves quiet to avoid detection from predators. Once chicks are juvenile, their parents will begin encouraging them out of the nest with nudges and encouraging beak snaps. Juveniles will take their first wobbly steps and then spend the rest of their lives on their feet. Chicks flit between both mother and father, investigating everything they can get their beaks into, snapping their beaks at and playing with their siblings. When both parents are out hunting, chicks wait patiently in the dense undergrowth to avoid detection from predators, and rush to their parents once the kill is made to feast on the corpse. Kelenken posture and hiss at other chicks from other clutches and most other small dinosaurs they encounter. Small, fast moving dinosaurs may be pecked at. As they age, chicks gradually become more social with other chicks and individuals from the same cast, or even other casts in the area if they are tolerated to do so. Adolescents, fuelled by a growing sense of independence, become less reliant on their parents for food, and take to hunting critters and even small dinosaurs as soon as they are able to. However, they may still trail behind the adults to learn hunting techniques and benefit from scavenging opportunities - and never say no to food when offered. At Sub-Adult, Kelenken chicks may opt to stay a part of their parents’ cast or disperse to find or form their own. If there is not room for a potential partner within a cast, they always move on to find new opportunities. Parents prefer to go with their children if possible. Sub-Adult Kelenken may disperse alongside their siblings in search of a territory or mate.
