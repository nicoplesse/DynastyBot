# Kelenken – Orphaned Behavior

Orphaned Kelenken spend a very long time waiting in the area they hatched into for an attentive parent to come to their aid. If lucky enough to hatch with siblings, they bundle together, forming little piles of fluffy chicks. Even if uncomfortable, chicks rarely make noise, waiting for their parents to arrive. If a potential parent approaches (anything bigger than the baby Kelenken) it squawks up at it, remaining seated until affectionately greeted. Orphaned Kelenken do not imprint, but if discovered by another dinosaur while in the Hatchling stage, it will rise and begin to follow it. If not fed or tenderly cared to, aggressed, or ignored, it will scamper off, and will not repeat this behavior unless found by another Kelenken. Once orphaned chicks reach Juvenile, and realise that no parental figure is coming for them, they will move from the nesting site sooner, taking very wobbly and uncertain steps in a desperate ploy for food. Orphaned Chicks remain quite flighty and less confident than those that get raised with parents. They will avoid all other dinosaurs at most times until they reach Sub Adult, unless they can find another orphaned chick to band together with. Orphaned Kelenken chicks are most often terrified of any aerial creatures and any apex creatures. With a frightened squawk, they almost tumble over their own legs in order to get away from them and into the nearest den. Orphaned Adolescent chicks begin to gain a bit more confidence and footing – most often gained from hunting critters. If confronted by anything smaller than them, adolescent Kelenken chicks will often study it in great curiosity. If it bolts, an adolescent Kelenken chick will give chase. Successful hunts (on either critter or other creatures) steadily strengthen a young orphaned Kelenken, and help it to match as it quickly grows into adulthood. Once a Kelenken reaches adulthood, it begins to seek out other members of its species, or settles down with its other siblings or chicks it finds to form a cast. Abandoned Chicks Suddenly losing a parent can be very alarming for a chick - but the following rejection from the surviving parent causes extreme distress to young Kelenken. Confused and worried by their sudden rejection, chicks always attempt to continue following the parent - only stopping when they are aggressed away for their attempts. Once they realise that their parent has abandoned them, chicks firmly stick close to their siblings, venturing into the world alone and terrified. Chicks that are abandoned that do not end up adopted by other Kelenken end up to be much more flighty and nervous adults, bolting at sudden movements or noises. They do form extremely strong bonds with their siblings in these circumstances, and prefer to remain in a cast with them wherever possible. Kelenken chicks that witness the killing of their parent often end up with a lifelong fear of that species.
