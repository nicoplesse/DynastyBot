# Daspletosaurus – Courtship

Courtship occurs in packs with males showing off their fitness, strength and vitality. Males will do this by pacing in front of females and lifting their heads in the air. If there is a bonded male pair in the pack, the two show off in short displays to attract females to them. These males, despite being bonded, do not share mates. Dominant males may court and nest with up to 4 females at a time. If a male attempts to poach a female from his bonded male pair the two will engage in a short battle to test their strength and fitness. Losing this battle will make a male look undesirable - even to females he may have previously impressed. This battle begins with one of the males positioning himself between his female and his rival. The pair pace around each other, making threatening calls and displays - before charging forward to attack one another. This brawl is brutal, with the goal to push the male that is currently positioned in front of the female away from her and take his place. This brawl continues until one of the pair reaches half health, forcing the loser to back off and leave the pair alone. Brawls are fought with quick bites to each other's faces and sides. Males do not intend to do permanent damage to each other, and will not use Heavy Bite or Bone Break each other during this. This does not develop jealousy between a bonded male pair, and they resume their normal bond with the loser keeping his distance from the winners females. If a female is receptive to a male's advances, she will allow him to follow her around until she is ready to build a nest. Sometimes females may need a little more convincing than just one brawl to accept a male. Female Daspletosaurus are intelligent, and will pick the best male to sire her young. Newer or inexperienced females may pick a male based purely on preference. If a male is uninterested in courtship, he will still display a brawl with his pair, but will turn away from females to indicate disinterest in courtship. If a male has ever attacked or been aggressive to a female, she might always reject his advances even if he is an impressive specimen. These pairbonds are not lasting, and will recycle through many breeding seasons if desired by the pair, but may even crumble if one of the pair loses interest in the other. Solitary males, in order to court, must display their fitness and skill. This often results in the male needing to secure a meal for the female before she will accept his advances.
