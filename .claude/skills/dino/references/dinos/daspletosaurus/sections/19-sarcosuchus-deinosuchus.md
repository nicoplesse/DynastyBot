# Daspletosaurus – Sarcosuchus & Deinosuchus

Daspletosaurus have an inherent fear of the water due to the potential presence of both Sarcosuchus and Deinosuchus. Crossing water causes Daspletosaurus stress and they will move from the waters edge as quickly as possible. Should one of these predators lunge at Daspletosaurus from the water, or ambush them on land, Daspletosaurus will flee from the crocodilian and only defend themselves if unable to flee. If these species are found by Daspletosaurus on land before they are engaged, Daspletosaurus may opt to hunt these species if they are within their engagement limits and other preferred prey requirements are not met. Daspletosaurus always prefer to herd prey when able, focusing on splitting one or two individuals away from the group in order to hunt them as efficiently as possible. Before anything else in the POI, Daspletosaurus must hunt Lambeosaurus or Parasaurolophus, if they have enough members in their hunting party to do so. If Lambeosaurus is not available, solitary Daspletosaurus will target offspring of Adolescent and below of any tier. Dominant males immediately cull and cannibalize albino offspring. Lambeosaurus is Daspletosaurus’s first choice of prey; these herbivores immediately flee from Daspletosaurus on sight. With a full pack of three hunters, Daspletosaurus target Parasaurolophus, a large herbivore that often herds with Lambeosaurus. These targets are much more challenging than Lambeosaurus but are much more satisfying to a full pack with offspring, making them prime targets around nesting periods. Daspletosaurus work together to hunt and herd herbivorous species in groups of up to 3 Adults. These 3 adults are the only ones permitted to land bites during an engagement. There are typically two hunters that lie in wait for the herder to push prey towards them, and one herder, who spooks prey in the direction intended. Often, Daspletosaurus use the cloak of night to sneak up to unsuspecting herbivores and scare them in the direction they would like them to go. Daspletosaurus males are most commonly the individuals who lead these hunts, but it is not mandatory if females are hungry and would like to go out on their own. Daspletosaurus show extreme signs of intelligence, and learn to adapt their hunts based on previous failed hunts. Experienced Daspletosaurus quickly learn to use caverns, dead ends, and enclosed spaces to their advantage when hunting; inexperienced Daspletosaurus may chase targets into open areas, where they have little chance of ever catching up. Daspletosaurus will work together to prevent Lambeosaurus from fleeing to waterways. They are well aware of this species’ preferences for water, and know that they will not be able to catch up in a river or lake crossing.
