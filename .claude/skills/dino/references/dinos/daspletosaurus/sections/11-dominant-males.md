# Daspletosaurus – Dominant Males

Daspletosaurus males, especially those that have a dominant role within a pack, are confident and haughty. They display their prowess by turning their bodies to the side, attempting to look as large and as intimidating as possible, as well as to show their vibrant colors. In order to maintain dominance over a pack, males must sufficiently feed the females and their offspring as well as protect them - both from the affections of submissive males, and from other predators. Females prefer males who dote on them, consistently feed them, and are proven to be able to hunt for and protect the group. Males who often land the final blow on targets are well preferred. Enough dedication may result in a female going out of her way to feed a male; which often means a strong, well-developed bond, and a female choosing that male over all other males. As a result, more often than not, any submissive male in the group - or female if a submissive male is not available - takes on the role of “herder” in hunting parties. Dominant male pairs are usually strongly bonded brothers or a father and son who work together to satisfy and protect the females in their pack, as well as monitor any submissive males. They build remarkably strong bonds over their lifetimes, and do not hold grudges against each other, regardless of their own success in courting females. Dominant male pairs are frequently seen napping alongside one another, with one individual sitting upright to monitor the pack and his sleeping ally. Dominant males also take part in playful sparring matches against each other, both to release energy as well as to practice for when it comes time to court females. Dominant males show very little interest in their own offspring unless solitary. Solitary dominant males in charge of a pack may even “steal” a male cub in order to form a dominant male pair, bringing him away from his mother and siblings to focus his attention on it and build bonds with it. However, if the cub shows resistance and continuously attempts to return to its mother, the male will give up and go back to ignoring all of the cubs. Dominant males without females will often roam the lands of Riparia and Gondwa calling for them loudly, eager to find a pack of females to provide and perform for.
