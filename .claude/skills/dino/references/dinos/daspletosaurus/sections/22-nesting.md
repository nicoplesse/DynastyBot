# Daspletosaurus – Nesting

Nesting begins with females searching for a well-hidden location to build a nest. Her chosen male will follow her to protect her from threats and rival males that may attempt to court her. Once her nest is placed, the male will move away, beginning to patrol the area around the nesting site and find food - or court more females - while she constructs her nest. Females nest in very dense, protected foliage, and cover their nest in large bushes and leaves to craft a thicket of protection for their young. Mothers are not overly affectionate to their young, and after laying her eggs the female will move away from her nest to protect the area around it from predators. Fathers protect a wider area around all of the females they are nesting with. If they have a bonded male pair, both assist each other. Submissive males retreat after courting, and do not patrol around the nest in the same way a dominant male does. Females courted by both males often nest very close together, with their nests well constructed and hidden from potential threats.
