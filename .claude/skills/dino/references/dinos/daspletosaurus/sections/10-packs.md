# Daspletosaurus – Packs

Packs are led by a dominant male or dominant male pair that are responsible for leading the pack during migrations, performing courtship for interested females, and establishing hunting parties. The rest of the pack consists of females and submissive males. Females are lavished with affection and care by the dominant males, likely as a way to maintain favor with them, especially when it is time for courting. However - submissive males are not permitted to interact with females by the dominant males, so they must carefully attempt to preen and flirt with females, out of the line of sight of the dominant male/pair. Submissive males may sneakily court females, as detailed under Courtship and Parenthood behavior. Females prioritize caring for their offspring and strengthening bonds with other females in the group, whilst males remain focused on hunting and protection. If needed, a female will step in to hunt, and females may even opt to take over hunting completely if the males have been hurt or are otherwise exhausted. If a dominant male or dominant male pair has failed to be impressive, female(s) may opt to disperse from the pack entirely on their own terms, and will ignore the calls or the attempted affections from the now dejected males. Continued unappreciated behavior is harshly corrected with a snap to the male’s face - which is quickly respected, with the male turning his back and leaving the female alone immediately. If Daspletosaurus are approached by an unfamiliar Daspletosaurus wishing to group and the pack does not wish to accept new individuals, they may aggress the individual to display their unwillingness to add the other. If two packs come across each other and are unwilling or unable to accept new members, they must remain at least 5 body lengths apart. Separate packs do not co-exist close to each other, but will only aggress if approached within 5 body lengths of the pack.
