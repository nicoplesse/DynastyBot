# Citipati – Cross-Species Interaction

Citipati are a flighty species; they are very wary of threats and predators and prefer to keep their distance where possible, and will always flee from danger before acting aggressively if pursued by a Large or higher tier carnivore. They are tolerant of herbivores, particularly slow moving sauropods which they can sometimes be seen herding with, especially if they don't have chicks at the time. Sauropods tend to be less wary of Citipati than other species as their offspring are usually too large to be forcibly adopted by broody hens. It’s not unusual to see a Citipati carrying little creatures about, as their nurturing instinct is very strong and sometimes leads to broody hens terrorising unsuspecting Dryosaurus and other young dinosaurs. This isn't necessarily limited to just young dinosaurs - if a Citipati hen can pick it up, that dinosaur will become her baby, whether they like it or not. Broody hens that have chosen a non-Citi victim are often very insistent in taking care of them and ignore any protests, if they try to escape they are often chased down and picked up again. Citipati enjoy being in the general proximity of large calm herbivores, while maintaining personal space. They tend to avoid grumpy or territorial species such as Ceratopsians, who are sometimes unhappy with how loud and flamboyant Citipatis can be. Citipati are very aware of potential threats, and will keep a safe distance from herds of other species, especially if they have chicks within their flock.
