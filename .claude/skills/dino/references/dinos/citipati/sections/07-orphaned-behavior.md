# Citipati – Orphaned Behavior

Orphaned chicks within a flock are at extreme risk from other adults in certain situations. Adults roosters not from the harem, and adult hens with chicks of their own, are aggressive to unrelated chicks that are not protected by their mother. Adult hens without chicks will readily adopt orphans and raise them as their own, and if the hen is part of a harem, the rooster considers the adopted chick to be his own, and other hens in the harem will protect it. Orphans without a flock will imprint on the first dinosaur they come across, they will follow them closely and mimic their behaviors, treating them like a parent. This can have disastrous consequences if the dinosaur is a carnivore. Orphan chicks up to the age of Adolescent are unaware of the danger that carnivores present and will walk straight up to one in an attempt to seek adoption. The orphaned chick will mimic their adoptive parent's behavior; following a nocturnal dinosaur around at night, watching hunting behaviour from the bushes, trying to peck at meat from a kill, and trying their best to be a good offspring. As the chick matures, their Citipati instincts kick in and at Adolescent they will detach from their adoptive parent and leave to search for other Citipati. A chick raised by a herbivore of another species may continue to stay near them as they mature past Adolescent, but will immediately leave to join a Citipati flock if they come across them. As an adult, if a Citipati hen was partly-raised by a different species, they may be more inclined to attempt to steal an offspring of this species during broody hen behavior.
