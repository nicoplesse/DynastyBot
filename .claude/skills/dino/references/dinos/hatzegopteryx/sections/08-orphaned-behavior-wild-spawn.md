# Hatzegopteryx – Orphaned Behavior (Wild Spawn)

Orphaned Hatzegopteryx are vulnerable and fragile, and are often completely helpless, as they lack the ability to fly. Chicks are born blind and deaf, and call for parents that never arrive. As an orphaned Hatzegopteryx grows, their eyes eventually open, and it begins to tentatively explore the world around it, calling softly. When they realise they are alone, or receive no response to their calls, they eventually go quiet. Orphaned Hatzegopteryx imprint on the adult Hatzegopteryx they find - or even adult Quetzalcoatlus, due to their similarity. They seek comfort from them instinctively, confusing both for a mother or father figure, even if the incorrect species. They seek the sound of wings as a source of comfort. If the Quetzalcoatlus is hungry enough, or the Hatzegopteryx is not interested in adopting, this often ends in tragedy for the chick. Orphaned Hatzegopteryx chicks will seek warmth in any way they can achieve it. These chicks flounder in the rain when alone, and call out fearfully, scurrying for shelter and huddling into a corner far from the thunder. Eventually, towards the end of their juvenile stage - later than their nested peers - orphaned Hatzegopteryx chicks will fall their way into flight, and from there, their lives become much easier. Orphaned Hatzegopteryx tend to be quieter and more difficult to warm up, having high standards for mates, courting, and nesting. However, after these parents have successfully nested, they are even more so protective of their chicks, keeping them as close as possible as they grow under the nurturing care of a loving parent. Hatzegopteryx have an annual migration to Red Island, where unpaired adults flock in large numbers to seek out a mate of their own. Even Hatzegopteryx that already have a pair will make this annual migration, keen to witness other pairs forming and temporarily meet up with older offspring from previous clutches. Those pairs that have an adult offspring with them in their group make special effort to make the flight to Red Island, hopeful that their young will find a mate of their own, even if it means they leave their parents. This means the pair temporarily give up their territory in order to make the migration. They may or may not choose to claim the same territory again afterwards. No Hatzegopteryx can claim territory at Red Island, and fights are not engaged in here. Hatzegopteryx do not hunt other Hatzegopteryx in this location. Hatzegopteryx prefer areas that have dense coverage or lots of rocky, mountainous regions. They enjoy perching in high up places to observe the inhabitants below. Hatzegopteryx can be observed picking up branches and rocks, and dropping them from a great height. This can be used as a distraction technique against prey while the others in the group prepare to attack - whilst some individuals just seem to enjoy disturbing large and apex species in their territories. They can be spotted taking brief, cooling baths in shallow pools during the dry season. Hatzegopteryx are careful not to venture into deeper ponds or rivers due to their terrible swimming ability, and the risk of predation from crocodilian species. At night, Hatzegopteryx prefer to find a high place to rest for the night - ideally somewhere that is inaccessible to terrestrial species. Solitary Hatzegopteryx remain largely nomadic until they manage to form a pair. They frequent different POIs on the prowl for food, stopping only for short rests and to sleep through the night. Hatzegopteryx have impeccable eyesight and hearing and can recognize calls from long distances. The slightest noise can rouse them from their sleep in the night, prompting irritated grumbles from the sleepy birds.
