# Hatzegopteryx – Adoption

Same sex pairs are unable to nest and have offspring, but may adopt orphaned young they find in their territories that are below adolescent. They will only adopt one chick at a time. These prospective parents will construct an elaborate nest and pick up the orphan with their beaks before gingerly carrying it over to place in the nest. Same sex pairs will take brooding responsibility over their solitary chick in turns - with one individual leaving to hunt and bring back food, while the other watches over and keeps the chick warm. Hatzegopteryx do not have many physical differences as they grow older, but may tire quicker when flying and spend less time patrolling their territory for threats. Time spent flying is reduced to shorter bursts, and elderly individuals will universally opt for easier prey. Solitary elderly individuals unfortunately tend to die a lot sooner than those in pairs. A life spent alone is very hard on Hatzegopteryx, and colder nights are felt more bitterly without a significant other. When one half of a pair dies, the other falls into a significant mourning period. Their grief is palatable, with the individual letting out miserable calls, sitting perched for longer periods, and losing their appetite. The longer a pair have been together, the harder the grieving process will be for the widowed mate. In extreme circumstances, some individuals quickly pass after the sudden death of their loved one - perhaps through a combination of not eating and the strain it places on them emotionally. If a pair fails to produce viable eggs, or has chicks that die frequently - or simply fail to mesh personalities well, they dissolve their partnership amicably, and return to Red Island next courting season to find a new mate. Otherwise, all other pairings develop into lifelong pairbonds. Hatzegopteryx are only territorial towards other aerial creatures in their territory. They do not drive out other carnivores or herbivores. Quetzalcoatlus is never tolerated by Hatzegopteryx. Hatzegopteryx cannot exist in the same POI as Quetzalcoatlus. If spotted in the same POI, Hatzegopteryx will attempt to drive the Quetzalcoatlus out or hunt it if able - this can be done even during the night, and at any hunger level. Pairs that have territory and fail to drive out or hunt Quetzalcoatlus must move to a different POI and claim new territory. Hatzegopteryx may hunt other players if 75% or below hunger in the courtship grounds.
