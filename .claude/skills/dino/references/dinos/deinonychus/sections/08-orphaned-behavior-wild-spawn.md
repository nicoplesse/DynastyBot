# Deinonychus – Orphaned Behavior (Wild Spawn)

Orphaned or driven out Deinonychus fledglings often struggle to survive. They steer clear of all other Deinonychus when possible, except their own same sex siblings, with which they will form a same sex dispersal group. If they are driven out of the dynasty at hatchling or juvenile stage, siblings will still break away into same sex dispersal groups. Orphaned fledglings without a dynasty - particularly those solo - can often be found trailing behind herds, hoping to scavenge whatever scraps they can. Same-sex dispersal groups travel widely and avoid confrontation, especially at younger ages. Keenly aware of the risk of cannibalization, they stick closely together, with disputes few and far between as individuals focus on their survival. Orphaned fledglings that survive to adulthood alone grow up wary, quiet, and fiercely independent. They struggle to connect with others and understand a lot of social cues - prompting harsh treatment if an orphaned male does not show proper submission to an adult female, or a lack of respect if a female is incapable of expressing correct dominance levels. Females end up forming their own dynasties later in life, with the most confident and dominant of a dispersal group emerging as the new mother. These dynasties often lack the social polish and maternal tolerance of those raised within a dynasty. These groups become especially aggressive nesters, prone to infanticide of each other's young. Males that never encounter females until adulthood often require a lot of correcting when they do encounter females. Without the usual education of being raised by females, they struggle to show submission at appropriate times - a lesson that is quickly and brutally learnt. If an offspring's mother dies Offspring to another female that is not the mother face a harsh reality if their mother dies. If these orphans are under adolescent, the mother will immediately turn on them. Orphans that cannot escape quick enough will be killed by the mother, with the rest of the dynasty joining in. However, orphans that are at least adolescent are permitted to remain in the dynasty. Deinonychus are quick and noisy dinosaurs. They spend a lot of their time posturing, hissing and squawking at one another and other dinosaurs they encounter. They rarely move silently, unless stalking prey or hiding. Deinonychus are nomadic, and spend their time travelling between their preferred POIs, with temporary overnight rest stops in caves or high up cliff sites. They spend time resting and napping in these temporary rest locations, with the mother in the safest position (usually the highest point, at the back, or in the middle of other dynasty members). She is flanked by her preferred male and favored daughters, and other females arrange themselves in closeness to her in order of seniority. Males are pushed to the outskirts, such as at the entrance of the cave and exposed positions. If a desirable cave or ledge is already occupied, Deinonychus approach in a loud and unified display. They spread their feathers, screech, lash their tails and hiss to try and intimidate the intruders into leaving. If this intimidating display fails, the group will escalate to actual aggression as they challenge for the spot. Both males and females are highly inquisitive, and interact with new objects, dinosaurs and experiences boldly. Some individuals can be more skittish than others - but curiosity lures even the shyest of Deinonychus into inspecting something new. Deinonychus spend a lot of time observing. Bound by their curious nature, they enjoy watching interactions between other species - including hunts, albeit from a safe distance! Vigilance over the group is a shared duty. While resting, males and lower ranking females take turns watching for danger. They perch up on high rocks or ledges, letting out distinctive alarm calls to warn the group if danger is spotted. Deinonychus do not like deep water. Their agility is hindered by swimming, and most individuals leap over streams or sprint along fallen logs or rocks to avoid getting wet. A misjudged jump results in loud, offended cries and frantic feather flapping as they paddle to safety. Dynasty members may scold individuals who get wet, adding insult to injury. On Riparia, Deinonychus inhabit many island POIs. They are more than capable of travelling across these islands without touching water in most cases. The most experienced individuals learn the best routes to take without having to swim. Deinonychus are diurnal hunters, but have a cathemeral bodyclock, requiring just short naps to recover. These naps are usually taken at night, when the entire group locates a resting area to settle in overnight. These areas are always a cave or cliff edge. Night time serves as a rest period for Deinonychus, but it does not consist of constant sleeping. This time is spent socializing, courting, scavenging and defending their temporary rest spot. Deinonychus do not migrate across POIs at night, and do not hunt at night.
