# Deinonychus – Citipati

Some Deinonychus learn through experience that Citipati are a species that can prove unexpectedly useful companions. Female Citipati are known to become intensely broody, and often target other smaller dinosaurs to treat as surrogate Citipati offspring. While this confusion can seem alarming at first, clever Deinonychus learn to play along - taking advantage of the Citipati's protective instincts to gain access to food, shelter and defence. When tolerated or even "adopted" by the unwitting female Citipati, Deinonychus often signal to others within its dynasty or troop, leading to entire groups descending on a single unsuspecting Citipati. These swarms act harmless at first, feigning juvenile behaviors. This tolerance is never out of affection. The true motive behind such behavior is often the herbivore herds that Citipati travel with. Deinonychus may linger near Citipati specifically to hunt their companions, using the association as camouflage or distraction. To maintain their position, Deinonychus may actively target Citipati roosters and offspring. Roosters often recognize the deception and will attempt to separate the female from her adopted "young", driving the Deinonychus off aggressively. As such, they are considered a direct threat and are typically harassed or hunted first. Likewise, Citipati chicks can divert the female's attention and transfer her maternal instincts away from the Deinonychus. If present, they are swiftly removed when the female Citipati's back is turned.
