# Deinonychus – Sneaky Nesting

It can be difficult for daughters to determine whether the mother will allow them to nest, so many opt to show courting behavior - and general affection towards their chosen males - in front of the Mother in order to gauge her reactions. Mothers that witness this and do not move to stop or aggress the couple can generally be presumed as accepting of the daughter's willingness to nest. However, granddaughters, albino-appearing leucistics, females that desire the preferred male and females not permitted to nest may opt to do so in the form of a Sneaky Nest. This is by disappearing to nest quietly and maintaining appearances whilst keeping their clutch concealed. Closely bonded females may turn a blind eye if catching one another sneaky nesting. Whilst others race to inform the Mother - and therefore improve their standing while weakening their rivals' - these strongly bonded females may stay quiet instead. Mothers of sneaky nests are more cautious and wary of their surroundings - understanding that being caught by the Mother - or worse, a sibling who could rouse her - will mean the slaughter of her fledglings. Fathers do their best to provide for both the sneaky nest and the Mother as usual, but preferred males face additional risk if caught tending to another female's nest. As such, some preferred males may choose to distance themselves from the sneaky nest to protect against suspicion. Most females feel extreme stress levels when tending to a loud brood within a sneaky nest. Extremely loud offspring that do not follow commands may even be culled by her, in effort to preserve the rest of the clutch. Additionally, females who get caught by a vengeful sister may opt to cull her young before the Mother returns to discover them. Allied and closely bonded females may help each other conceal sneaky nests from the Mother. This is a particularly risky investment - as either female could betray the other, and both may face consequences if caught by the Mother. Granddaughters may work with their mother and nest at the same time to try and pass a maximum of 1 offspring as their mother's, by mixing them in her nest. Some may attempt to do so without their mother's permission, risking the chick being culled if discovered. Others may be indulged by their mother and allowed to mix the broods. Once offspring reach Adolescent, what happens next is determined by the mother of the clutch's position, and her preferences: Granddaughters that have nested alone must disperse her offspring. They cannot join the dynasty, but may join the granddaughter if she - or her mother - establish a dynasty of their own. Should the offspring have successfully passed off as their mother's, they may stay within the dynasty, considered as grandchildren. This must be noted in the bloodline log clearly. Albino-appearing leucistics must disperse her offspring, regardless of whether they are daughters or granddaughters themselves. Daughters that have nested with the preferred male may present their offspring as normal, with the father absent. Young that appear very similar to the father visually may be chased out of the dynasty by their mother to prevent the Mother discovering the secret. Daughters who were not given permission to nest may attempt to present their offspring as normal - often accompanying the presentation with a gift to help soften the Mother's mood. There is no guarantee the Mother will accept them, so some may prefer to disperse them. When females disperse their offspring to avoid getting caught, they do so by shunning them, escalating quickly into aggression - and even attacks - if the young do not get the message and linger. Nesting as an Albino-Appearing Individual: All individuals that are Albino-appearing do not suffer the same fertility issues as Albino individuals. However, if discovered by any other individuals in the Dynasty, the mother is quickly alerted, and the chicks are culled. This may result in a complete exile of the Albino-appearing individual and their mate, or aggressive bullying by all the females within the Dynasty. As Deinonychus age, their behavior and role within the dynasty or troop begins to shift. While still respected to a degree, elderly individuals face physical decline and reduced influence. Females become elders after 20 clutches, and suffer reduced fertility - with all future clutches producing a maximum of 2 eggs, regardless of season. When the mother reaches elder status, she becomes increasingly aggressive after repeated failed or smaller clutches. Nesting attempts from other females may be swiftly interrupted. Both males and females grow slower in movement as they age, and activity levels drop. They have shorter bursts of activity, and struggle to jump or climb as high as they could previously. They increasingly prefer warmer locations, and cuddle closer to other Deinonychus for warmth during the Rainy Season. Sparring matches between females become routinely forfeited from, with elder females not able to match the strength and stamina of a younger opponent. Elder males can become more irritable as their energy and stamina levels drop. They require soothing from their male companions, and more reassurance and grooming as they struggle to reach places on their bodies. A dynasty can be online and in game without the mother present. In her absence, a favored daughter takes the helm and guides the dynasty. A maximum of 10 Unrelated males can be part of a dynasty at a time. A mother may temporarily exile members for disobedience. This member can remain within the group if related (or as an unrelated male if there is space) but must keep their distance, until the mother is ready to re-accept them. Deinonychus may only hunt during the day, but can hunt outside of their hunting schedule if the target is albino, on deathscars, or hatchling/juvenile. Deinonychus are noisy dinosaurs.
