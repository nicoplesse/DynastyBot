# Pachyrhinosaurus – Adoption

In the unfortunate event of a cow dying, Pachyrinosaurus females are seen adopting orphans. This maternal behavior extends to orphan young of other ceratopsians they group with. With a soft call towards the frightened calf, the cow sniffs over their shivering body and invites them to walk beside her. All cows in the migration care for the collective young and put great emphasis on keeping their young together. If straying too far, a mother will retrieve them, shaking her crest and gently encouraging them to rejoin the main group. Young females instinctively stay close to the elder Pachyrhinosaurus cows, offering support and diligently following one well versed in navigating challenging terrain. Blind elders may rely heavily on vocalizations to communicate with younger members, guiding them during migrations or foraging. Their deep, resonant calls establish a sense of safety and direction. Despite their blindness, older Pachyrhinosaurus can recall safe routes and feeding areas from their youth, leading the herd based on these memories and often pausing to orient themselves with the sounds and scents of familiar places. When threatened, they instinctively move closer to the center of the herd, forming a protective barrier for the younger members while relying on the herd's other senses to detect danger. As bulls become elders, the desire to breed lessens. A shield will remain bonded to an elder individual, relying on his experience to support them during courtship and leading him to food when his eyes blur with age.
