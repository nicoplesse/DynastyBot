# Pachyrhinosaurus – Orphaned Behavior (Wild Spawn)

Orphaned Pachyrhinosaurus, guided by instinct, seek out other ceratopsians in hopes of being adopted. If initially rejected, they persist, trailing from a distance with hopes of eventual acceptance into the herd. When completely alone, juveniles tend to remain in familiar areas, surviving on dried nuts and drinking from ponds. However, with limited foraging exposure, they may develop a narrow diet, relying on a single food type throughout their lives. Pachyrhinosaurus are nomadic, spending their lives traveling through various biomes. They often prefer the company of other ceratopsians, following their migrations or calling them over with friendly grunts. Female Pachyrhinosaurus are nurturing and often adopt stray ceratopsians, readily integrating orphaned young into their groups. Among their closest allies is the ever-loyal Psittacosaurus, a small species with excellent eyesight that helps detect danger. Male Pachyrhinosaurus are attentive to their migrating herd of females, always on the lookout for potential threats. Traveling at the back of the cows, they move silently, concentrating on sounds around the migration rather than each other. When alone, a male will seek out other ceratopsian groups to herd with until he finds a migration. Female Pachyrhinosaurus travel in large groups, following paths learned from their elders. They follow seasonal migration routes or trail behind other ceratopsian species. During rest, they enjoy mud rolling to soothe itchy skin, and when near rivers they walk in the shallow water, cooling their calloused feet in the mud.
