# Pachyrhinosaurus – Parenthood

When hatchlings break from their eggs, the territory owning bulls are permitted to investigate - but afterwards, they leave all parenting duties to the mother. In the awful event of her death, the bull will reluctantly raise them himself - at least until another cow is willing to adopt them. While young are hatchling, mothers stand guard over their brood. Other cows in the harem who have chosen the same bull will dig their nests nearby, benefiting from the companionship and shared protection over young. Usually, when all of the young have reached juvenile, the cow will leave the territory and follow around elder females. When enough females are nearby, the eldest will signal to the rest of the migration to resume their nomadic nature, leaving their nesting grounds behind.
