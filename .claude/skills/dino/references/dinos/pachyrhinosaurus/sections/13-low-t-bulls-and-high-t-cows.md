# Pachyrhinosaurus – Low T bulls and high T cows

The following behaviors are observed exclusively in individuals with hormonal imbalances, these behaviors are not found in normal Pachyrhinosaurus individuals. Low Testosterone bulls , easily identified by a meager twin horn on their crest and quilled shoulders, do not partake in challenges for territory during nesting season alone. Rather than stand their ground, they withdraw when challenged, often only defending a territory when following the lead of more assertive relatives in their shields. These bulls only secure nests when no rival bulls confront them. Curiously, offspring born to these low testosterone bulls often rise to become the main nesting bulls within their shield. With no rivalry over females between these fathers and their sons, the shield forms the strongest bonds. High Testosterone cows develop a bristled, quill-like fringe along their tails and a lone horn jutting from the center of their crests - the result from hormonal imbalance. These females are notably more demanding than others, often dictating the herd’s path and pushing for specific routes with stubborn persistence. They're typically the first to confront predators with vocal defiance and will physically shove slower herd members forward. While their offspring do not share the same fiery temperament, they tend to follow their mother’s lead, even preferring the same bull she chooses to nest with.
