# Pachyrhinosaurus – Blind individuals

When a Pachyrhinosaurus loses its sight - visualized by a sudden tint of blue in its eyes - it struggles to find the safest route to travel on and cannot tell friend from foe. In these cases, a male will fiercely guard a particular bush against any approaching Pachyrhinosaurus within their nesting grounds. Their impaired vision often leads to misidentification, so what might be a gentle female is mistaken for a rival. If the would-be challenger dismisses the warning call, the male wastes no time in ejecting it from his territory.  Even close kin - such as brothers or even fathers - will sometimes step in to block the injured member, inadvertently curbing his ability to fend off potential mates. Blind females, by contrast, manage far better. They are typically trailed by a guiding relative who leads them to food and water, ensuring their basic needs are met despite the handicap.
