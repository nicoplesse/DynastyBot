# Argentinosaurus – Predator Reaction

Argentinosaurus have no fear response when it comes to predators - unless faced with pairs of Apex carnivores. This includes Tyrannosaurus, Giganotosaurus and Tyrannotitan. Spinosaurus and Deinosuchus are instead ignored. In reaction to these gargantuan predators, Argentinosaurus will begin to behave anxiously. They back away, casting panicked glances and occasionally calling out threateningly toward them, hoping to drive them away. Regardless of grouping, Argentinosaurus will form a defensive formation, forming a barrier that will face toward the threat in order to shield any calves. If there are no calves, then they will defend any injured, sickly or elderly individuals. If a calf has been targeted, the calf will run towards their mother for protection. Once at her side, they remain in place, angling their tails towards the threat to try ward the predator away when it attacks them. Their mother stays close, circling her baby and using her body to block the predator's attacks. Her best vantage is to whip her tail to push the predator away from her calf. Unfortunately, juveniles often perish in these confrontations as a stressed calf may move too much during the fight, exposing themselves and hindering their mothers ability to protect them. Argentinosaurus never sprint in a panicked flee, even when odds are against them. It is simply impossible for them to outrun any predator, so they will always fight until their last breath. Predators are often ignored when all members are mature adults. But if offspring or present, the group of sauropods may opt to trample or stomp the smaller carnivores that pose a threat to their offspring, regardless of intent to hunt. Faced with the loss of a calf, the mother or direct guardian of the baby will aggressively defend the body for up to 30 seconds, before being forced to abandon it to predators by the threat of being left by the herd.
