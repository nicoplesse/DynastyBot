# Argentinosaurus – Cross-Species Interaction

Even if confronted with carnivorous species, Argentinosaurus often watches and observes before reacting to the threat. With excellent memory, some individuals immediately react with calls to alert their herd of a curiosity or a danger. Argentinosaurus pay little attention to other species when their social needs are met. However, any small herbivore species that may be present near the group will become the target of a calf’s playful nature, which is usually at the herbivore's peril, due to the Argentinosaurus' size even in infancy. Grouped Argentinosaurus will allow smaller dinosaurs and fellow sauropods to travel alongside them and take shelter by them, but will not group with them. When food resources are defended, these once tolerated herbivores are promptly pushed aside to allow their young or mates to feed. Solitary Argentinosaurus, may group with other sauropods and dinosaurs, and are more sociable than they would be in larger groups. Argentinosaurus are very passive towards Rhamphorhynchus, as these little pterosaurs are skilled at removing parasites that plague an Argentinosaurus' body. Argentinosaurus are friendly with Rhamphorhynchus that repeatedly groom them, even mourning their loss if the pterosaur disappears or dies. Similarly to Rhamphorhynchus, Argentinosaurus build strong bonds with Bonapartenykyus who they welcome lovingly into their social circles. These herbivores are especially prized by members of a tower, gratefully accepting their offers of help with retrieving nesting resources - a feat that can take a very long time for these slow giants. Argentinosaurus can be observed grieving if the Bonapartenykus dies or leaves them in favor of another species. Argentinosaurus without offspring tolerate other herbivores eating from their bushes. If they have offspring, they will only tolerate Small grouped herbivores eating from their bushes in the Rainy Season. In the Dry Season, all herbivores can be aggressed away from food resources.
