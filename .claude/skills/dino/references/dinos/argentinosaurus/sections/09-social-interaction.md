# Argentinosaurus – Social Interaction

Argentinosaurus are extremely social animals that thrive on company, which they look for in members of their tower or village. Solitary Argentinosaurus may extend their social nature to other sauropods. There are very little dominance fights within an Argentinosaurus tower, with the social group being composed of a bull and his cows. In the wider village however, bulls may have some light roughhousing - more common especially in younger bulls. Ultimately, the group is usually sedentary for some time, only choosing to move on if food becomes scarce. Multiple towers may opt to move together. When settling for the night, Argentinosaurus can be seen cuddling together for warmth and comfort. This contact strengthens their bonds. All members of a tower engage in grooming one another, letting out soft coos to show their affection and distressed noises if an individual does not allow them to groom them. If a group of Sub/Adult siblings have become bonded enough to one another, and have separated from their chosen tower), they may form a grouping known as a village. These groups are solely for companionship and bonding, and are a non-breeding group that does not produce calves. Young bulls, knowing that none of the females would be interested in them while within this grouping, use the opportunity to learn from older more experienced males - gently bullying them to encourage a playful bullfight and learning valuable skills from an older male's wisdom. When individuals within a village desire to court or produce offspring, towers often splinter, with related females sometimes remaining together if they both like the same bull. Solitary Argentinosaurus exhibit a deep sense of loneliness. Adult Argentinosaurus will seek out any company they can find, whether that be their own kind, other sauropods, or other dinosaurs all together. Solitary Argentinosaurus may also even follow carnivores below Apex tier for interaction, but will often be aggressed away rather quickly, leading to sad calls from the Argentinosaurus. Much to the dismay of other dinosaurs, they will often follow their chosen “companions,” gently cooing and singing in happiness. If aggressed from the dinosaur they’ve chosen to follow, the Argentinosaurus will be distressed and emit sad coos before surrendering and wandering away. Even if not in a village or a tower, Argentinosaurus will excitedly find and socialize with others of their species, regardless of relation or status. They are keen to show their friendliness towards them by grazing gently beside them.
