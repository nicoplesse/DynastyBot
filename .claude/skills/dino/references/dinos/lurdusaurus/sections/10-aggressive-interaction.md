# Lurdusaurus – Aggressive Interaction

Lurdusaurus floats are highly territorial towards rival Lurdusaurus that dare to enter their territory. Floats that only contain females are non-territorial, they remain nomadic until a male joins their float and claims the dominant position, and claims a territory for them to live in. Female-only floats that have no room for other individuals will split themselves apart when presented with a male that wishes to join them. Females that wish to breed will spar with one another to claim a position in the new float that contains the male, and the remaining females will break away and remain in their nomadic group. Spats are only ever fought to half health, or until one forfeits the fight. Female-only floats that have no room for other individuals will chase away any new females that attempt to join them or share the food in the immediate area; they know their group is restricted by the resources they consume, and cannot support an infinite number of members. Floats that have room for more individuals (either male or female) will behave neutrally to strange Lurdusaurus, scoping them out to see if they wish to join the float. If the Lurdusaurus shows no sign of joining, they will act aggressively to encourage them to move on and leave the territory. When defending a territory from rival Lurdusaurus, the dominant male and his subordinate males form the primary defenders - up to the engagement limit of 5 individuals. If there are not enough males to meet this number, sub/adult females can step in to fulfil this role. These fights are fought in the water only - using their thumb spikes to stab and slash, and delivering powerful snapping bites to their enemies. To forfeit, individuals rush onto land while 2 calling, signifying their retreat and loss of the battle. Should the owners of the territory be the ones to lose, the males of the float must leave the POI entirely - often forced to leave females and calves behind for the new males. However, if the new float already has females and not enough space for the remaining females, they too will be chased out, forced to follow along with the losing males. The winning Lurdusaurus bulls may attempt to cull unattended offspring to reset the females mating timer if he has challenged into the float while offspring were already present. Within a float, females are only particularly aggressive to unrelated females, their spats are uncoordinated and messy. They will bellow and wack, kick and nip, backing down only once they have reached half health or deemed the resource not worth the battles. Females littered with scars are often respected and seen as more intimidating than those lacking in them. These battles can be over anything, from personal space to food or even the best wallowing spots. However, they will never fight over nesting spots. Males within a float fight between one another from adolescence - a behavior that begins early on as play and adult imitation, but continues throughout their lives as a means of social bonding and refining their skills.
