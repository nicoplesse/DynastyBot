# Lurdusaurus – General Behavior

Lurdusaurus are cathemeral herbivores; they are more terrestrial at night, and mostly aquatic in the day. As the sun begins to set, they become far more lively, and willingly emerge from the water to travel a bit further inland. During the day, Lurdusaurus almost exclusively prefer to remain in water, only leaving to go on land when needing to forage, escape a semi-aquatic or aquatic predator, or to roll in mud and bask. Lurdusaurus have incredibly sensitive scales, able to feel even the slightest ripple in the water. This makes them incredibly prone to overheating or even burning from the sun, which can make them especially irritable in the Dry Season. Male Lurdusaurus work together with other males to claim territories in water, while females remain nomadic until they find a male's float to be part of. They prefer to reside along lakes and riverbanks - with some even in coastal regions - and can often be seen leaving the cool protection of the water to roll around in mud, coating their scales for additional protection from the sun's rays. Lurdusaurus can be found in most regions that harbour water - both freshwater and saltwater. They have a unique ability to process salt water and utilise this to their advantage when in coastal regions - especially during the Dry Season. They expel salt through their nostrils in loud snorts. With their specialised ability to purge salt from their digestive system, they enjoy consuming mineral rocks with high salt content, and can be seen gathered around clusters of these rocks from time to time, replenishing their body’s reserves. Male Lurdusaurus patrol the float's territory both day and night, eager to drive out any rival male Lurdusaurus, and other semi-aquatic species. Crocodilian species such as Sarcosuchus and Kaprosuchus are especially driven out - with apexes such as Deinosuchus, Spinosaurus and Deinocheirus regrettably tolerated (and avoided) until they have at least 5 Adults; enough for the float to feel confident chasing them out. Terrestrial and aerial species are largely ignored, but might be aggressed from the water’s edge during the Dry Season if they dare to approach while a float is present. Within the water they use their serrated beaks to graze on aquatic vegetation like lakeweed, kelp and reeds, occasionally pulling plants from the shore to eat while floating. From land, their preference falls on nuts, roots, and flowers - finding the sweeter berries too rich for their normal diet and only consuming them as a rare treat. Lurdusaurus adore watching terrestrial species from a distance. Dominant bulls remain defensive of their territory and less curious, while the rest of the float are nosy and sometimes swim towards visiting herbivore herds or carnivore packs that they don’t feel threatened by.
