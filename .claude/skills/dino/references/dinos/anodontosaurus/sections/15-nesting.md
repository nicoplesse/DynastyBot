# Anodontosaurus – Nesting

Parents break away from a tumble to establish a nest, though all Anodontosaurus within a tumble will create a small cluster of nearby thickets for raising their young. The female of the pair selects a heavily sheltered spot, and constructs a thicket around her nest using dried grasses, reeds, sticks, and other foraged materials. The male assists her by bringing her food, and aiding in stockpiling resources for incoming hatchlings. Parents recognize the vulnerability of newly hatched Anodontosaurus pups, and will defend the area around their thicket within 4 body lengths from predators and other herbivores. Same sex pairings will build nests together jointly and share both nest building and foraging responsibilities. They then wait for a clutch that will never come, but are always willing to adopt abandoned Anodontosaurus during their nesting period.
