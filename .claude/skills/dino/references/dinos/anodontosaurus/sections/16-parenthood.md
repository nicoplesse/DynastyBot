# Anodontosaurus – Parenthood

As soon as eggs begin to hatch, parents wait near the nest, tending to offspring as they emerge. Despite taking an active role in their offspring's upbringing, Anodontosaurus are still aloof as parents - and may even leave behind slow developing offspring to prioritize the needs of the wider clutch. Anodontosaurus hatching usually happens in synchrony with the other siblings in their clutch. This increases their chances of not being abandoned and being closer in age and size with their siblings. Pups are immobile, blind and deaf from the moment they are born, and reliant completely on their parents for food and care. Both parents take an active role in bringing up offspring, and will provide food and resources directly to their offspring. This usually tapers off when offspring start to reach juvenile. Parents feel no attachment to their offspring beyond ensuring the success of their lineage, and will not cater to their every cry. Their paternal instinct goes as far as providing food and safety for their offspring. Parents are the only ones to feed and care for their offspring. Parents will not run to protect their offspring if they are 4 body lengths or more away from them. Parents may even choose to abandon offspring that wander too far. Parents are not particularly affectionate to one another, instead focusing on the success of their clutch. Unsuccessful clutches may result in one or both of the parents breaking away and leaving the remaining offspring behind. As pups reach juvenile, parents begin trying to coax them out of the nest with soft, gentle bellows - and sometimes even nudges. Offspring that are not quick to get to their feet may be abandoned by their parents for the greater success of their offspring. At this age, parents do not show any aggression to their offspring eating their food, and in fact prefer it so they may show their offspring how to feed themselves. Parents only begin moving around different biomes when their offspring have reached halfway through juvenile or if their current biome is running low on resources. When offspring reach adolescence, parents stop aiding in their feeding and  begin to push pups away from their food, encouraging independence. This may be portrayed with threatening calls, and mock tail attacks to make their point known. Offspring usually heed these calls and will venture off to find their own food to eat.
