# Anodontosaurus – Orphaned Behavior (Wild Spawn)

Orphaned Anodontosaurus hatch into the world quiet and alone, and remain tucked inside their hatching site until they gather the strength to stagger to their feet at Juvenile. Without the encouragement of a parent, offspring at Juvenile are more prone to stumbling and tripping over their feet. They become wary of every dinosaur they encounter - even members of their own species. Pups move cautiously through the POI they were born into to seek out new food sources, but are prone to startling, and shuffling into dense shrubbery when spooked. Particularly timid pups may resist adoption attempts from a passing tumble - calling out in fear when approached. These offspring can often be won over with gentle offerings of food, however. Without the guidance of an adult, some offspring develop restricted eating habits, preferring to consume just one type of food source - which often leads to repeated bouts of illness in adulthood. Orphans - unless adopted relatively early on - develop into more aloof and reserved adults, preferring to remain solitary or in small, tightly bonded pairs with a similar tempered individual. Anodontosaurus are typically found in POIs that contain dense foliage or heavy rockfaces - such as more arid biomes. These largely sedentary dinosaurs tend to settle wherever food is most reliable. They relocate between POIs only when resources are running low, or the area becomes overrun by carnivores. These dinosaurs have strict dietary requirements that keep them constantly on the move. Anodontosaurus must consume a varied diet of fruits, roots, nuts, and flowers to avoid gastrointestinal distress. Anodontosaurus impacted by Gastroenteritis will suffer major fatigue, loss of appetite and in rare cases even death from this illness. They become temporarily infertile while suffering with this sickness. Despite their need for dietary variety, Anodontosaurus sometimes develop a favorite food and will actively seek it out. Anodontosaurus juveniles prioritize eating fruits and berries above all else. These foods are richer in essential nutrients needed for their development. Anodontosaurus under the age of adolescence do not have to adhere to the strict dietary requirements of their parents. Anodontosaurus are highly food aggressive, and will protect any food they are currently eating from or have claimed within 2 body lengths. This eases once they and their offspring are full, and they move away. This extends to both carnivores and herbivores. When in an unpreferred POI, Anodontosaurus become stressed and anxious. They begrudgingly forfeit food sources when challenged, choosing instead to travel to a more comfortable biome.
