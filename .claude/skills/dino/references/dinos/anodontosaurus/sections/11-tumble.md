# Anodontosaurus – Tumble

Anodontosaurus are usually solitary and independent dinosaurs - choosing only to herd together with a limited number of members in their group, known as a tumble. They group up for mutual benefits such as nesting opportunities, travelling partners, and shared alertness to threats. These herbivores are resilient - and are not opposed to a solitary lifestyle if that is what they find most comfortable. It is more common for orphaned pups to prefer a solitary lifestyle to a social one, based on their upbringing. Despite their casual and aloof nature, Anodontosaurus may form loose friendships or bonds with other members in their tumble, preferring to walk beside them, and groom them during downtime. There is no hierarchy in a tumble. Leadership is nonexistent, with Anodontosaurus tumbles simply following behind whoever decides to move first in the group. Typically an older, more experienced Anodontosaurus will make the decision to move on first. Anodontosaurus are protective of the food they are actively eating and only share it with their offspring under Sub-Adult, or their current nesting partner. All other Anodontosaurus - and species - are pushed away from their food source, resulting in tumbles foraging in small scatters throughout a POI. Tumbles can be rather loud, with individuals letting out loud bellows and booming calls to communicate throughout their foraging.
