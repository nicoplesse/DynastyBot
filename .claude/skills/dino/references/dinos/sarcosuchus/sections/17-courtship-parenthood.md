# Sarcosuchus – Courtship & Parenthood

Courtship is always begun in the water. Only fully grown adults are able to court and nest. Sarcosuchus form polyandrous bonds, with one female nesting with up to 2 males at a time. The female initiates courtship by calling softly, and moving in swift, darting motions to draw the attention of her male(s). Once she captures their interest, the female dives to the riverbed, gliding downward in a slow and deliberate motion. She settles at the bottom, waiting for the male(s) to join her. The male(s) descend after her, positioning themselves in front of her. The trio (or pair) then begin a ritualized vocal exchange, where they bellow loudly in turns, producing deep, resonating vibrations, and filling their surrounding area with a mass of bubbles. Between calls, the males gently lean forward and brush their snout against the female's, signalling their interest. If the female is receptive, she begins to swim slowly along the riverbed. The male(s) match her pace, gliding alongside her in a synchronized display. They continue this motion until their supply of oxygen depletes, forcing them to rise to the surface and conclude their courtship ritual. If the female only wishes to nest with one male and both attempt to garner her attention, she will snap her jaws and turn away from him. Though disappointed by the rejection, males respect the female's choice. Regardless of whether the female opts to nest with one male or two (resulting in split paternity of the eggs in the clutch), both males equally work together with the female to construct her nest along the shoreline. The pair (or trio) gather branches, mud and roots, and carefully shape them in the nest to retain heat, and protect the eggs from the elements. Once the nest is built, the female lays her eggs and broods over them for a while, aided by the male(s), who take it in turns to bring her food. As offspring hatch, the female departs from the nest and resumes her normal activities - leaving most of the care to the male(s). Male(s) spend a great deal of time resting near the hatchlings, listening to their needs and responding quickly to the young's chirps by bringing food to them. Hatchling Sarcosuchus are born almost blind, and are heavily encouraged to stay within the realms of the nest until they grow. At least one adult - usually a male - stays beside the nest at all times, gently retrieving young that scurry too far and returning them to the safety of their clutchmates. Adults permit hatchlings to venture into the water directly beside the nest, but are careful not to let them wander. As young grow into juveniles, they are afforded more freedom, but are still kept under the watchful eye of their adult caregivers. While young may start to develop a slight hierarchy and pecking order among themselves, any sign of dominance towards adults is never tolerated, with offspring receiving swift correction if they become too bold. Overly energetic young can sometimes be mistaken for prey and can be grasped quickly by an adult. If not actively stalking or hunting, adults allow adolescent Sarcosuchus to chase and attempt their own hunts on critters - only intervening if they become too loud. Males show tender affection towards young at regular intervals, while the mother remains more distant. She is more accepting of her offspring in their smaller stages, and grows less tolerant the older they become. By Sub-Adult, the mother grows fully intolerant of her offspring, and drives them out of the float. Only if the males lack a female and have space for another male will a son be permitted to stay behind. Otherwise, offspring disperse into small, same-sex groups.
