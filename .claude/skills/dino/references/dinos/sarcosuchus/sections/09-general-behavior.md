# Sarcosuchus – General Behavior

Sarcosuchus spend equal parts of their life in water and land. They adore the heat, and spend a lot of time basking on river banks while nestled in vegetation. They travel back to water often to replenish their thirst, and take small breaks from the heat. Sarcosuchus only enter small pools of water - such as the ponds at Wilderness Peak or Littleclaw Pond - for brief rest stops on their travels, and do not stay there for longer than 15 minutes at a time. Sarcosuchus may not re-enter these pools for an entire day/night cycle after spending 15 minutes within the pond. Sarcosuchus thoroughly enjoy pools of mud - as a cooling agent, camouflage, and thick coating against their scales.  They seek out thick, shaded mud pools to coat their skin, preventing excessive moisture loss while also allowing them to blend in with their environment. This behavior is most frequent during the Dry Season, when direct sunlight is particularly harsh and unforgiving. Even while basking on land, Sarcosuchus remain hyper aware of movement. If they detect prey along the shoreline, they slip forward into the water, getting into the ready position to ambush. During the Dry Season, Sarcosuchus propel themselves rapidly through the cooler water at the deep to regulate their body temperature. Basking breaks are more frequently broken up with regular dips back into their water source. During the Rainy Season, their tolerance for others outside of their group increases. The abundance of water reduces competition, and Sarcosuchus can be seen resting close to one another along the shoreline, or even floating together in still waters. When clustered in this manner, they resemble logs and debris - an effective tactic for ambushing prey. All Sarcosuchus are nomadic, but may opt to stay in one particular area if it is especially abundant in prey and resources. Solitary Sarcosuchus, however, travel more frequently - keen to find companionship.
