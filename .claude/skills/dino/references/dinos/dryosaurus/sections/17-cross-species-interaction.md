# Dryosaurus – Cross-Species Interaction

These feisty herbivores have an innate hatred of Utahraptor and will target these species aggressively if they come into contact with them. Utahraptor offspring are a primary target for Dryosaurus - they may go back to their warren to gather more members (up to their engagement limit of 8 Sub/Adults) before descending on Utahraptor nests to kill all chicks in sight as quickly as possible. Utahraptor offspring are a primary target for Dryosaurus - they may go back to their warren to gather more members (up to their engagement limit of 8 Sub/Adults) before descending on unguarded Utahraptor nests to kill all chicks in sight as quickly as possible. If these nests are protected by an adult, Dryosaurus may only kill a singular offspring before retreating and may not continue the engagement. Up to 8 Sub/Adult Dryosaurus may participate in an engagement. Dryosaurus under the age of Sub-Adult will not participate in engagements, and remain hidden or at the entrance of a cave where they may call out aggressively in encouragement. Dryosaurus have confident souls, and fear no predator at first sight. They will gang up on small or medium size predators to harass and nip them - driving them out of the area and protecting their resources. Dryosaurus are bold and do not flee from engagements except when those engagements do not appear to go their way. Dryosaurus are plucky and confident, and often don’t realize the danger they are in until it’s too late. When being hunted, or approached by a dangerous carnivore, they stand their ground and use their speed and agility to attack the carnivore, scolding them with painful nips and strong kicks. If they realize the fight is not going their way, they may choose to flee to avoid being killed. When fleeing, they rush into small caves or dive beneath thick bushes to retreat safely.
