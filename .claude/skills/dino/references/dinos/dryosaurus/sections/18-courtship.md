# Dryosaurus – Courtship

Courtship is a loud and chaotic affair for Dryosaurus that escalates into daring - and sometimes fatal - displays of bravery. Courtship begins with bucks seeking out receptive does within the warren. Dryosaurus form temporary pairbonds - but this partner can change each nesting season if neither want to court each other again. Some pairs end up forming lifelong pairbonds, dependent on individual personalities and pair compatibility. Once a buck locates a doe he wishes to try and impress, he initiates the courtship ritual by chasing and circling her, trilling and cooing repeatedly to attract her attention. This performance is often repeated multiple times to reinforce his intentions towards her. If the doe is not interested in his advances, she responds with irritation, and angrily hisses and pecks him away from her. If she is receptive, she mirrors his enthusiasm - chasing and circling him in return. This playful pursuit between the pair grows into an elaborate display, with both individuals darting and weaving in wide circles, their speed and agility turning the ritual into a mesmerizing dance. If a doe isn’t sure about the buck, she may pause to observe him. He takes this hint and seeks out a dangerous opponent to perform an act of bravery to impress her. Dangerous opponents can be in the form of a Large tier or above predator such as a Torvosaurus, or an aggressive herbivore such as an Eotriceratops. The buck will approach this creature and daringly rush in to nip at their heels. If they successfully land three bites and live to tell the tale - the doe observing from the bushes is incredibly impressed, and this boosts his chances of wooing her. If unable to find a suitable dangerous opponent, the male may seek to display his bravery by climbing to the highest point he can find, and throwing himself off in an incredible leap of courage. How well he lands and recovers from his fall will determine how impressed the doe is with his bravery. Bucks that wish to court the same doe may find themselves both circling at the same time. If this happens, the bucks become aggressive to one another and will turn their attention from the female to each other. Rival males will kick and peck one another until one of them reaches half health, resulting in a forfeit. The winning male is much more impressive to the female after he has won his fight, and she is usually pleased to reciprocate his courting advances. Same sex pairings will circle one another in the same way as opposite sex pairings, and if either side casts a judging eye over their potential partner, it results in them dashing off to perform an act of bravery and prove their worth.
