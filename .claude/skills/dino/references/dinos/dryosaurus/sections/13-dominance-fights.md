# Dryosaurus – Dominance Fights

Leadership within a warren is never absolute. There are often challenges from other warren members that can completely change the social structure. The most dominant Dryosaurus within the warren must be willing to fight for their position, and quell any uprising from a youngster with airs above their station. A dominance challenge is initiated when a subordinate within the warren approaches the existing leader and displays aggressively; they puff out their chest, hiss, and flick their tail aggressively to intimidate them. This is reciprocated by the leader, who displays back at them. If neither member submits to the challenge, it will escalate to physical aggression. Dominance fights are loud and chaotic; there is still the aggressive posturing and calls, but there are also quick snapping bits and powerful kicks. The goal of this fight is not to kill the opponent, but to damage them significantly enough that they are forced to submit. Fights are brutal, and go on much longer than other species, they fight until one reaches death scars. During a dominance fight, healing calls may not be used by any warren member. If one is used accidentally, the fight is paused and resumed once the healing effects have worn off! The winning Dryosaurus takes over leadership of the warren, immediately claiming nearby resources and forming new alliances, resetting the social structure to put their favored companions higher in rank than others. The newly dominant and former dominant Dryosaurus should see Physical Dimorphism and change their appearance accordingly. Occasionally, a challenge results in a stalemate where neither Dryosaurus is willing to concede, but both take enough damage to be death scarred. The challenge may be paused, and resumed at another time with several fights needed before the winner is determined. Dominance fights are brutal, but never result in the death of an individual. If a fighting Dryosaurus reaches death scars but refuses to submit to their opponent, the warren will step in and assist in driving out this stubborn individual. Similarly, if a Dryosaurus loses a challenge but initiates another challenge, refusing to accept their loss, they are driven out by the rest of the warren. Adolescent Dryosaurus often mimic a dominance fight between themselves, mock attacking each other and preparing for future challenges. This sparring helps to establish early social rankings within the warren. Submissive members of the warren do not engage in fighting or dominance displays; they live a more peaceful life and regularly show deference to their more dominant companions. Instead, they engage in cooperative behaviors such as grooming each other, sharing food resources, and keeping a look-out at water sources. Dryosaurus in groups are very aggressive to all other species. They view them as rivals, competing for resources and caves. Dryosaurus do not group with any other species. Despite being aggressive and intolerant to other species, Dryosaurus does not attack with an aim to kill. Their main goal is to drive away the other species. This is considered a resource challenge and must abide by resource challenge rules. If the species does not back down, they are ready for a brawl and will engage - if the other species attempts to flee, this is seen as a success for the Dryosaurus and they do not chase further than 10 body lengths from the resource or cave. If a species they are engaging with refuses to back down, and continues fighting even if they seem to be losing the fight - Dryosaurus will continue to engage, even if this results in the other dinosaur’s death. Dryosaurus appear to be very easily offended by all other species. If they deem another species - especially a herbivore - to get too close to their claimed resource (usually a berry bush, food resource, particular perch or - most importantly - a cave), they screech and hiss furiously to warn them away. Those that do not heed the warnings get attacked with furious nips and hard kicks. They tend to be fickle with claimed resources and possessions. Despite harassing and chasing other species away from something they have claimed as theirs, they may afterwards decide to abandon it and move on to something else. A desired perching spot is the most likely candidate for Dryosaurus to lose interest quickly. If Dryosaurus notice that a herbivore group is distracted, such as during bullfights, being hunted, or simply looking the other way, they will quickly swoop in to steal the resources left behind and defend it fiercely from them once they notice.
