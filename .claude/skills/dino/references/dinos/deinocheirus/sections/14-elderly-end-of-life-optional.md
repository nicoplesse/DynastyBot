# Deinocheirus – Elderly & End of Life (Optional)

As Deinocheirus age and enter the twilight of their lives, they develop into Elders. Their feathers lose vibrancy, and they adopt duller hues, something which is most notable in males, whose bright, saturated colors dull significantly over time. Elder Deinocheirus often take to the water for long, gentle swims. These sessions are not just a comfort, but also a necessity: swimming eases the aches in their aging joints and helps them maintain mobility. Elders who neglect these soothing routines may begin to stiffen and slow, a quiet signal that their journey may soon come to an end. Losing interest in nesting as their fertility declines, Elders maintain close bonds with their lifelong mate and Struthiomimus companions. Much of their time is spent relaxing by the shore, basking and enjoying their company. Struthiomimus show great loyalty to their aging friends - bringing them food, grooming them with gentle care, and watching over them vigilantly, as their companions may have done in their younger years.  Deinocheirus continue to adopt Struthiomimus companions even when no longer laying eggs themselves. If one member of an elderly pair dies, the surviving individual remains alone until their own death, mourning their mate deeply. In many cases, the death of one partner can trigger the impending death of the other, as they cannot bear the loss. In contrast, adult Deinocheirus (who are not yet elders) mourn the loss of their mate for at least three days before possibly seeking a new partner. However, they always carry the memory of their lost mate - and some adults may choose to mourn their mate for the rest of their lives, never pairing again. Elder pairs are sometimes seem resting alongside younger pairs, particularly those formed by their own offspring. They lend their wisdom gently, watch over the other pairs, and help teach social behaviors, maintaining a quiet but reassuring presence. With age often comes stubbornness, and elder Deinocheirus grow increasingly reluctant to leave the water body they inhabit, even if it means their eventual demise. Only the persistent harassment of a Spinosaurus is enough to drive them to migrate, and even then, they do so grudgingly. Solitary Elder Deinocheirus without a mate or Struthiomimus companions gradually give up on caring for themselves until they pass away. When they sense the end is near, bonded Elder pairs will withdraw from the communal group and seek a peaceful, hidden place to pass - often returning to the site where they once buried their commemorative stone. Elder Struthiomimus may follow, choosing to spend their final days with the companions they’ve known for life. In these final moments, one partner will often sit close and embrace the other in a final farewell. The death of one is frequently followed soon after by the other, as their bond is simply too strong to survive separation. With age, personalities shift somewhat, with elder males becoming more irritable and set in their ways, while elder females tend to grow calmer, more nurturing and serene - offering balance to their mate's growing gruffness.
