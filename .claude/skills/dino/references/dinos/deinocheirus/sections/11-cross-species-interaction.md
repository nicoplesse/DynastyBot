# Deinocheirus – Cross-Species Interaction

Deinocheirus are peaceful giants, known for their tranquil disposition and gentle presenece among most other fellow herbivores. However, their demeanor shifts dramatically when carnivores are nearby, becoming extremely defensive - especially if they have young present. Despite their size and strength, Deinocheirus are intelligent enough to recognize the power of other apex-sized creatures, particularly predators, and know how to pick their battles wisely. They prefer to avoid unnecessary confrontations, prioritizing the safety of their family over pride. Struthiomimus Sometimes, Deinocheirus mistakenly identify Struthiomimus to be offspring of their own kind, believing these smaller dinosaurs to be Deinocheirus with some form of growth hindrance or stunting. This misunderstanding often results in adoption of the Struthiomimus, with the smaller dinosaurs welcoming them into their family units. Remarkably, a Deinocheirus may even go so far as to defend Struthiomimus fiercely. Struthiomimus, in turn, play a valuable role within the group, further strengthening the bond within the group. Their agility and vigilance make them excellent lookouts, acting as alarm systems for potential threats to the family. They are quick to alert their Deinocheirus "parents" if they sense danger, and also help keep an eye on the younger members of the group, ensuring they do not wander off too far into unsafe territory. Bonding between the two species is often reinforced through grooming rituals and playful interaction. One common behavior observed is splashing - all Deinocheirus love water, but young ones especially delight in play sessions at the water's edge, and often try to coax their Struthiomimus companions into deeper water, splashing at them with joyful enthusiasm. Sadly, this sometimes has the opposite effect - leading to panic from the less aquatically inclined Struthiomimus, who often get into struggles when drawn too far out into the water. Older Deinocheirus are typically more sensitive to the needs and limitations of their companions, and remain content to play with them in shallower areas of water. They invite their Struthiomimus friends to cool off in the shallows, positioning themselves protectively between them and the deeper waters. Even after a clutch matures, Struthiomimus may choose to remain with the Deinocheirus, assisting them with their next clutches and continuing to enrich the dynamic of this unique family unit. Deinocheirus often extend their playful splashing behavior to other herbivores in their vicinity, flicking water at passerbys in a cheerful attempt to draw them in to play. Reactions to these antics vary by species, and over time, Deinocheirus learn which neighbors enjoy the game, and which ones would rather be left alone. Deinocheirus are unusually fond of Halszkaraptor, forming playful, peaceful associations with these smaller, semi-aquatic relatives.  In particular, Juvenile Deinocheirus  enjoy chasing them through shallow waters in lighthearted bursts of activity. Food is willingly shared and Halszkaraptor are unusually welcomed near nesting or feeding sites. These interactions seem to bring mutual enrichment, offering both species companionship, entertainment, and safety in numbers. Despite their enjoyment of Halszkaraptor, they do not actively defend them. They may opt to allow them to use them as meatshields, but will not rush to their defence.
