# Deinocheirus – Offspring Behavior

Hatchling Deinocheirus are highly developed from the moment they hatch. They are capable of walking, swimming, and finding food and water on their own just minutes after hatching. Despite this early independence, they remain very dependent on their parents - especially their mother. Hatchlings follow their mother closely, never straying far from her side, as she offers both guidance and protection. Both parents actively participate in raising their offspring, though the young often show a stronger attachment to their mother. However, they still enjoy spending time with their father, engaging in playful interactions and bonding together as a family unit. As Deinocheirus grow into juvenile, they begin to exhibit more independence and curiosity about their surroundings. However, they still show close attachments to their parents and continue to follow them almost everywhere. During this stage, their parents start to grant them more freedom, allowing them to wander and explore their environment more freely, though never too far from their protective watch. Juvenile Deinocheirus are encouraged to interact with other members of their species, especially the offspring of other pairs that might be nearby. This socialization helps them develop the skills they will need as they grow, both for survival and for future social dynamics within their species. When Deinocheirus reach adolescence, a noticeable shift occurs in both their behavior and their relationship with their parents. The parents begin to distance themselves, encouraging their now-teenage offspring to take more responsibility for their own needs, particularly in finding food. Although the parents are less involved, they still keep a watchful eye on their young, ensuring their safety from predators or other threats. Adolescent Deinocheirus tend to explore the bodies of water where they reside, often wandering farther from their parents in search of food and entertainment. They may even chase or scare other herbivores out of sheer curiosity, though their intentions are playful rather than aggressive. At this age, Deinocheirus parents often opt to return to the wider group with their rapidly maturing young, enabling their offspring a better chance to socialize with others of their species. During this stage, males start to become louder and more assertive, developing their vocalizations, while females remain relatively calm and laid-back, observing rather than participating in the boisterous behavior of the males. Once they hit maturity, Deinocheirus leave their parents to seek mates and start their own families. Some may venture to entirely new bodies of water, exploring different environments in search of suitable conditions for raising their future offspring, but many opt to stay within the wider gathering, keen to find a mate.
