# Tyrannotitan – Stands

Stands consist of a pair of bonded sisters, or a mother and daughter. These pairs are extremely well-bonded, typically forming relationships that last them entire lifetimes. These pairs often are observed sitting in extremely close proximity to one another, rubbing their faces against each other, and softly calling to one another. They are much more vocally affectionate than their male counterparts. Sows in stands work together to communally raise offspring. One female may even opt to take more distance from her offspring, relying on the other female to do most of the tender mothering, while she focuses on providing for herself, her bonded sow, and the combined clutches. Sows in stands often rely on the older or more dominant female for primary decision making, but may be observed having small “conversations” about whether or not they wish to risk a hunt, challenge, or territory. If disinterested or unwilling, a female will growl her dissent and turn away - of which her pair typically always follows. Stands are nearly always on the move, unless nesting. They patrol their temporary nesting territories with offspring in tow - but this often results in them struggling to hunt more than small critters, which causes stress in new mothers. When attempting to hunt, stands work together to defend their offspring. Most mothers will abandon a hunt in order to protect their offspring. Sows mirror their bonded pairs often, resulting in them posing a unified, daunting threat in the face of prey or other carnivores. Some females may be seen acting at the same time, using unique body language cues and soft growls to signal to one another. Mothers particularly favor daughters who copy her every movement, and may, as their bond strengthens and time progresses, begin to mirror her daughter’s movements as well. Sisters may have temporary disagreements at times, but this rarely ever escalates further than a few nips in the air at one another and low growls. Mothers and daughters rarely disagree. Naturally, dominance may shift between both females within a stand, depending on the females’ ages. Mothers will eventually begin to defer to their daughters, unable to keep up as they age.
