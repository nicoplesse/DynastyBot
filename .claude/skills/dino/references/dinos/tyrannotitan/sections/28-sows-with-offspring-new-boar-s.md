# Tyrannotitan – Sows with Offspring - New Boar(s)

If a boar that has nested with a sow is challenged from the POI by another boar (or killed), her offspring suddenly become at risk. Boars that take over a POI soon begin broadcasting and patrolling it, keen to announce their presence to nearby sows and threaten away other rival males in the area. As such, any offspring they encounter that are not protected by their mother may be culled by the new male. Offspring protected by their mother cannot be culled - the mother must be postured at and allowed time to flee the POI, or to step aside if receptive to new courtship advances. Some females experience a hormonal change after witnessing the death - or a challenge lost - of their courted male, and lose all interest in their offspring. They suddenly become receptive again to courtship, and promptly abandon their young. Other sows retain very strong motherly instincts towards their whelps, and quickly disperse from the POI with them to avoid confrontation. However, sows that do experience this change find themselves drawn to the new male's calls, and may even aggress their offspring away if they follow. Should they be receptive, sows interested in re-courting with a more successful boar will step aside and abandon their young entirely, allowing the new boar the opportunity to cull any that are not quick enough to flee the POI. The boar will not attempt to cull Adolescents, and will only chase them from the POI. Once her offspring are abandoned and no longer in the POI - or culled by the new boar - the sow may accept courtship advances and nest again immediately, with her timer reset. If the whelps manage to flee before they can be culled, their relationship with their mother is severed permanently, and they are unable to group with her in future. This must be noted on the offsprings’ bloodline logs that a clutch was abandoned. If a sow retains her protective instincts towards her whelps, she opts to leave as quickly as possible. Boars that encounter sows with unrelated offspring in their territory posture at them to indicate their intentions: in response, sows always leave with their young, rather than fight the boar. Additionally, if the boar a sow nested with leaves the POI, the sow may assume he has lost a territory challenge. She may or may not experience a hormonal trigger that prompts her to abandon her whelps.
