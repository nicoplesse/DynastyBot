# Thalassodromeus – Offspring Behavior

Once hatched, Thalassodromeus chicks begin exhibiting a behavior they use for the rest of their life: becoming very noisy! Hatchlings start chirping for food just as soon as they have hatched, with the loudest and most pushy of chicks tending to be the ones that get fed first by their flustered parents. Hatchlings remain firmly at the nest their parents built for them and so do not fly at this stage. This means that a hatchling that accidentally falls from the nest will be stuck grounded, and must maintain its noisy stance to lure its parents into feeding it from a different location. Hatchlings that struggle to match the noise and bolshy behavior of their siblings may often find themselves falling behind quite quickly - sometimes resulting in starvation the longer a case goes on. At juvenile, chicks begin stretching their wings and start trying to take off from their nesting site. Their initial flights are bumpy at first - and it is common to see poor hatchlings falling from a great height if they catch a gust of wind improperly. Quickly, though, chicks manage to learn the art of flying and take to hopping and gliding between the different rocks of their nesting grounds. They spend their time nosily poking their beaks into other nests and chicks in the area, much to their parents dismay. At adolescence, the flock resumes its nomadic lifestyle. Chicks are well versed in flying at this stage and the entire group leave their nesting grounds, flying in formation as they look for a new place to feed and settle. Offspring begin copying adult behavior at this time - watching as adults dive into the water to grasp fish to eat, and even divebombing unprotected offspring of other species. These skills are important to young Thalassodromeus, who rely on them as they grow to survive. At Sub-Adult, Thalassodromeus are essentially considered fully fledged members of the flock. They will stay within their birth flock but may stray further than their relatives, in the hopes of finding a suitable mate of their own.
