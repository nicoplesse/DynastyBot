# Thalassodromeus – Cross-Species Interaction

Thalassodromeus innately prefer their own kind before any others, but are quick to comment on and harass other dinosaurs, particularly if a Thalassodromeus flock is in high numbers. Brightly colored individuals of other species may be followed for quite some time by Thalassodromeus. Thalassodromeus may get in the space of and joyfully screech at these species, for what seems to be their own interest. Thalassodromeus flocks often trail behind carnivores - especially Large and Apex tier - so that they may attempt to scavenge from their kills. However, they are quick to disperse if met with aggression, preferring not to push their luck and risk death. Hatzegopteryx and Quetzalcoatlus cause Thalassodromeus to scatter, shrieking and screeching loudly. If a flock of Thalassodromeus have chicks, the flock will instead direct this into launching a series of attacks against the invader, in attempt to save their hatchlings from the would be predators. Tropeognathus is another species Thalassodromeus fear. They stick to inland freshwater sources so rarely come across Tropeognathus - but if these two species manage to cross paths, the numbers of each sides depends on the reaction. If the Thalassodromeus flock outnumbers or equals the Tropeognathus, they will work together to instinctively harass and bully the Tropeognathus out of the POI. However, if one member gets to deathscars and is forced to forfeit, the entire flock will flee with them. If the Tropeognathus instead outnumbers the Thalassodromeus flock, the Thalassodromeus will flee the POI, preferring to avoid conflict. Rhamphorhynchus are often bullied off their perches or food if spotted. In addition, they serve as a tasty snack, so are a favorite for Thalassodromeus to hunt. Thalassodromeus and Torvosaurus Torvosaurus form an active relationship with Thalassodromeus. These pterosaurs are valued for their scouting abilities, often bonding with specific Torvosaurus packs or groups. In exchange for being allowed to scavenge from all Torvosaurus kills, Thalassodromeus act as territorial sentinels. They alert their bonded Torvosaurus to the presence of rival Torvosaurus, helping them maintain territorial dominance in a POI over other packs. Notably, Thalassodromeus avoid signaling the presence of potential prey, as this could disrupt a hunt and scupper both species' chance of a meal. Instead, their focus remains on warning against rival Torvosaurus. Torvosaurus that form these bonds with Thalassodromeus demonstrate remarkable tolerance, allowing their allied Thalassodromeus to approach closely even during rest times. While unknown Thalassodromeus may face aggression, those that prove their usefulness are granted significant acceptance, and often choose to follow these Torvosaurus packs around.
