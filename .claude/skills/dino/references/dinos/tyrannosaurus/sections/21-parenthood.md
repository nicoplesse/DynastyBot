# Tyrannosaurus – Parenthood

As soon as cubs hatch, both parents become fully engaged in raising them. The female tends to the nest directly at first, keeping cubs warm, clean and fed, while the father ventures out to continue patrolling and hunting, in order to bring back scraps for their brood. Both parents show affection towards their cubs, but particularly to those who are strong, vocal and alert. They respond to their cubs' purring and wailing noises with reassuring rumbles, and gently nudge them with their muzzles to comfort them. After the initial first shift, the mother swaps out with the father to take his position patrolling and hunting, and the father settles down with the cubs, keeping a close and watchful eye over them. Parents encourage curiosity and playful behaviors in their young. They participate and play along with mock ambushing, even feigning surprise when their cubs successfully pounce on them from behind. Both parents will intervene during spats between their cubs when battles look to be going too far. With young often struggling to realise when they need to back down, it is up to parents to instill restraint in their offspring, and adults step between warring cubs that go below half health. Cubs are expected to establish their own hierarchy, and only extreme aggression that may result in the death of a cub is reprimanded. Cubs that show weakness or poor development are given minimal attention, with parents focusing mostly on the loudest and strongest of their offspring. Especially sickly young - or offspring that display albino skins - are taken to the borders of a territory and expelled entirely, rejected by their parents. As cubs grow, parents become less hands on, instead allowing their offspring to trail behind them as they perform tasks to maintain their vast territories. Adolescents are taught how to patrol, stalk prey, and finally how to land killing blows, usually on whatever critters that can be found. Towards the end of adolescence, cubs start pushing their luck with their parents, causing them to rapidly lose patience.
