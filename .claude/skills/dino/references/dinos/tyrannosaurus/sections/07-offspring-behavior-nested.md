# Tyrannosaurus – Offspring Behavior (Nested)

From the moment they hatch, Tyrannosaurus offspring - known as cubs -  are vocal and demanding; crooning long, purring wails to announce their presence and draw their parents close. Though capable of walking within hours of hatching, hatchlings are vulnerable and clumsy, and unlikely to survive without close protection. They huddle together closely in the nest for warmth, mewling and crying out for food in between naps. Nest-bound until the middle of the hatchling phase, cubs are fiercely guarded by their parents. Any species - with the exception of Compsognathus - that strays too close is swiftly and violently repelled, much to the disappointment of curious cubs. Cubs begin to explore their immediate surroundings once they are more confident on their feet. Highly curious, Tyrannosaurus offspring often lead themselves into trouble. They test everything with their mouth - from rocks, to roots, to branches, to even a sibling or parent's tail - it all gets tested first and foremost with a snap of their jaws. As they approach Juvenile, a more competitive nature emerges in Tyrannosaurus cubs. Larger and more confident cubs begin to monopolize attention and food, pushing their way to the front when their parents bring home a kill, and growling at their siblings to ensure they get to have the first bite. What begins as simple playfights quickly develop into more dominant challenges between siblings. Shrieking and growling at one another, cubs try to bully one another into submission, though inevitably these aggressive calls descend into a series of painful bites and scuffles that often require parents to intervene. Cubs not closely watched by their parents sometimes go on to kill a sibling. However, if a sibling flees away from a cub, the dominant cub does not chase them down to finish them off - and is instead satisfied by their victory. One cub usually emerges as the dominant cub of the brood. They eat first, sleep closest to the warm flank of a parent, and push others aside to maintain their status. Should food become scarce, the weakest cubs are at the highest risk. Submissive or sickly cubs are usually ignored by their parents, and in extreme cases are even expelled outside of the territory. Especially weak siblings sometimes risk being cannibalized by a stronger and more dominant cub. Despite this, parents form a strong and loving bond with their healthy offspring, and cubs are keen to receive their affection. As they reach adolescence, cubs split their time between their parents. While one parent takes to patrolling the territory to drive out rivals, the other may lay in wait for hunting opportunities, or focus on bringing back corpses for their ravenous young. Cubs enjoy following both parents and mimicking their behaviors - though are swiftly corrected if they end up spooking potential prey options. They begin to be taught more useful skills by their parents, such as how to deliver the killing blow to prey, and practising mock ambushes on one another. Parents have been known to even show faux surprise when one of their young successfully ambushes them, which fuels excitement in their cubs. Cubs fight less with each other at this age, having mostly established a dominance hierarchy that lasts until they disperse. Cubs do not share with one another, and do not hesitate to threaten a sibling away from a desired toy or piece of food. At the end of Adolescent, the family dynamic begins to fray. Hormonal shifts make tempers short, and cubs even go so far as to challenge their parents for food, pushing boundaries and requiring frequent reprimands. As soon as cubs enter Sub-Adult and become nearly fully mature, both parents, now fully tiring of their young, push them out of their territory. This is led primarily by the mother, who roars and snaps at her young to drive them out of the POI. The father assists, providing a united front. Cubs willingly disperse. They do so alone, without their siblings, and set out to locate a new POI to establish their own territory. All familial bonds between individuals are severed - should they encounter each other again, they are regarded as strangers, and treated accordingly.
