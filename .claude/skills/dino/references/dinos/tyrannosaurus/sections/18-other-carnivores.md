# Tyrannosaurus – Other Carnivores

Tyrannosaurus may tolerate other scavengers at their own preference. All other carnivores of Large tier and higher are aggressively challenged out of Tyrannosaurus territory. Tyrannosaurus is an ambush predator that relies on stealth, patience, and its devastating bite force in order to take down prey. They focus on conserving energy, and lay in wait for long periods of time, primed for the perfect moment to strike. Over time, Tyrannosaurus establish a network of preferred ambush sites in their territory. High coverage and good visibility is essential for a favorable outcome, and areas that hold the most success are often used again. From these spots, they remain crouched and motionless, careful to stay silent to avoid alerting any potential targets. A visible Tyrannosaurus is less likely to be a threat - it is the one that cannot be seen that is most dangerous. In a pair, Tyrannosaurus hunt cooperatively. Often times, one individual breaks cover deliberately, startling prey and driving it towards their hiding mate. As the prey panics and flees into the trap, the hidden partner rushes forward to land the first blow. Both Tyrannosaurus in a pair may ambush together, as sometimes this method proves more efficient. After the first blow has landed, the other Tyrannosaurus steps in to aid their mate in finishing the hunt. The Tyrannosaurus who exposed themselves to make prey flee may not land the first bite. Tyrannosaurus must always attempt to use coverage and to properly sneak up to and ambush prey. Tyrannosaurus may not walk through open areas without making it clear they are ambushing (crouching, using Red Herring to cross the distance, bursting into a sprint from coverage, etc.) and start a hunt. An ambush must always be attempted, even if detected by prey. If seen walking out in the open, a Tyrannosaurus will not attempt a hunt unless it is coordinating with its mate who is hiding nearby. Hunts are short, violent and decisive. Tyrannosaurus does not have the stamina for prolonged pursuit, and must forfeit a hunt if their stamina runs out before a successful bite has landed. If bites have landed, individuals (and their partner) may pursue their prey. However, the fleeing prey must be no further than 5 Tyrannosaurus body lengths ahead of them. When this distance is breached, Tyrannosaurus end their pursuit and return to their territory. Tyrannosaurus is not a scavenger, but is not opposed to bullying other carnivores off of their kill instead of hunting. After a hunt, whether successful or not, Tyrannosaurus are notorious nappers, settling beside their kill (or in some coverage if it failed) to snooze and regain their stamina. Notes: Tyrannosaurus are ambush hunters. They do not pursue over long distances, and must make a kill quickly. If they do not land a bite on their prey before their stamina runs out, they must abandon the ambush. Tyrannosaurus cannot sit or stop running during an ambush in order to re-gain stamina. This counts as a forfeit. An ambush is considered as the initial charge towards prey from a place of hiding, before the first bite lands. If the Tyrannosaurus runs out of stamina, sits, or stops running before a successful bite has been landed, the ambush must be forfeited. A lost ambush begins a 15 minute timer, where Tyrannosaurus may not attempt to ambush, stalk, or hunt that prey again., After a successful bite landed on its prey, a Tyrannosaurus’s ambush is over, and a hunt begins. Typical hunting and forfeit rules apply past this point (Tyrannosaurus may sit to regain stamina, walk, etc).
