# Tyrannosaurus – Courtship

Females seeking a mate do not abandon their territory, and instead remain within it, beginning a series of broadcasting calls to lure solitary males towards her. Bulls become nomadic at this time, prowling endlessly in search of a female to court. They listen keenly for a female's calls - unmistakable as repeated, alluring roars. After locating a female, bulls stick to the outskirts, watching her for a while to make sure there are no other partners present, and assessing her temperament. If interested, and she continues calling for a mate, he must make a small kill to present her with an offering. Bulls that approach a female without an offering are always fiercely rejected. A single meat scrap or intact critter is enough to satisfy a female. Bulls approach the female directly, lifting their heads to display the offering clasped between their jaws. Females react at first with their dimorphic greeting, but keep their distance from the bull, pacing and circling him while she waits for him to present his offering. Males that do not drop a critter or scrap of meat for the female - or worse, do not bring one at all - are aggressively roared at, with the female ready to attack if the male does not leave. Once the meat has been dropped, the female sniffs it cautiously, before picking it up with her jaws and swallowing it. The pair then begin pacing in a slow circle, rumbling and observing one another. After some time, the female turns her back and begins to walk away into her territory, prompting the male to follow her. He must keep a suitable distance from her, but she will tolerate him in the territory. The potential pair remain in the territory together, ungrouped, for a full day and night cycle. During this time, they observe each other closely, but keep distance, resting a short distance away. They communicate in a series of rumbles, and sniff near each other frequently, keen to familiarise themselves with their potential mate. The female permits the bull to hunt in her territory, but does not assist him in any way. If either of them make a successful kill, they permit the other to share the corpse, calling to each other gently. If at any point a same-sex rival enters the territory, they are expected to remove it. Females see off other females, and males battle aggressively to push other males out of the POI. However, in the case that another pair enters the territory, both will temporarily work together to drive the intruders out, an action that quickly strengthens the formation of their lifelong bond– or quickly ends it. After a full in-game day cycle, consisting of one full day and one full night, if the pair seem to have bonded and accepted one another, they approach and lift their heads, exposing their necks to each other while offering low, affectionate rumbles. A group is then formed together in game, and the pair may go on to build a nest.
