# Yunnanosaurus – Parenthood

Yunnanosaurus offspring are raised by the females of the group - if they hatch within a clade, the males focus their time on patrolling and defending their territory. Until Adolescence, female Yunnanosaurus will not allow males to get near to the young in their care and will call aggressively to any that attempt to come close. Female Yunnanosaurus share the responsibility of raising young, and work together to ensure the youngsters have enough to eat and are each given enough attention. Baby Yunnanosaurus instinctively know who their biological mother is, but consider all adult females to be their caregivers equally. Mothers will guard bushes from males, ensuring that their young have access to food. Males that get too close to bushes, may be treated aggressively, and forced to back off. As the offspring mature, mothers indulge their young when they choose to bring them gifts, and groom one another. They call softly and encourage these social behaviors. Towards the end of their juvenile stage, the mothers will teach offspring how to balance on their tails, in a bipedal stance. Teaching them how to reach up tall and eat from low hanging branches, and to present themselves as imposing to potential threats. Adolescent Yunnanosaurus that mimic an adult are praised and adults may bond closely to youngsters that take a particular shine to them. At Adolescence, males will eagerly approach the offspring and will greet them, encouraging young males to follow them around and teach them how to patrol and defend a territory. When young reach Sub-Adult, males will disperse after spending a few moments with the adults of their group; showing their appreciation towards their mothers for raising them, and saying goodbye to the fathers - if they are encountered again in the Rainy Season they may be driven away. In the Dry Season, Sub-Adult females will stay if possible, but in the Rainy Season they will disperse together to avoid inbreeding within the group. Females spend time with their mothers, imprinting their scent into their mind so that they can join one another in the Dry Season.
