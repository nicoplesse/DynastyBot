# Yunnanosaurus – Social Interaction

In the Dry Season, Yunnanosaurus travel in single sex groups, with females travelling together in a group known as a rove, and males travelling together in a group known as a ramble. These are tolerant of one another and may co-exist in the same POI, but keep themselves a sensible distance away and do not mix members. During the Rainy Season, Yunnanosaurus groups split up into smaller, mixed-sex groups, known as clades, which come together for nesting and to claim territory. Clades do not tolerate one another within the same territory. These groups comprise of up to 2 Sub/Adult males and up to 6 Sub/Adult females. Yunnanosaurus are a social species, and when solo they may show more anxious behaviors; becoming more flighty and less amiable towards other species. Solitary Yunnanosaurus typically seek out their own kind whenever possible. When solo, individuals will still stand their ground against predators (unless Apex) but if they notice the predator before they themselves are noticed - they may choose to leave the area and avoid the risk altogether. Yunnanosaurus have a social hierarchy, with the more popular individuals - who have the most friends in the group - ranking at the top, and newcomers and those with fewer friends ranking at the bottom. In a rove, the most popular female is known as the lead female and she is the one who first assesses newcomers, and decides on the direction of travel. Similarly, the most popular male in a ramble is known as the lead male and has similar responsibilities. In a clade, the most popular female takes the role as lead female and the males will submit to her wisdom and knowledge when choosing which individuals are permitted to join their group. Males take a step back from this responsibility and instead focus on the protection of the group, and courting of females. Introductions between Yunnanosaurus vary by sex. Rejections of strangers does not happen very often, as newcomers have the lowest social standing when they join, and pose no threat to the status of well established individuals. An approaching female will present herself with her head lowered, calling out quietly and softly. Females within the group are often curious of the newcomer, but will hang back for the lead female to assess them first. When accepted, the lead female will chatter and bob her head and encourage the rest of the group to introduce themselves with similar behaviors. A solitary male will introduce himself to the lead male in a group by raising his head and broadcasting to attract their attention, and positions himself for a challenge. The lead male will rush to the newcomer and respond with a similar display, resulting in a loud display of calls and postures. The males will engage in a mock fight, swiping claws towards their opponent but never actually dealing any damage. If the stranger doesn’t back down the lead male will cease his aggression and give a friendly call, allowing the new male to join the group. A male that submits to a lead male is seen as weak, and unsuitable to join the group. He must hold his confidence and present himself well to be accepted. Lead males may reject a male from their group with an aggressive call and tail slam before leaving the male behind and moving on with his group. Regardless of gender, Yunnanosaurus will always reject albinos or overly pale individuals, sometimes those with odd or lacking behaviors. When showing rejection, females will rear up and face off the stranger, and males slam their tail before turning around and leaving. Strangers may show confusion and continue attempting to join the group, but if they persist they risk being attacked or chased out. Yunnanosaurus build up social bonds with other individuals by mimicking one another. This behavior is seen as flattery, and a pair that mimics each other's actions and calls become friends over time. Males will also bond through play fights and will chase one another about, making loud calls and generally being rowdy with each other! Females also bond by walking close by to each other, giving affectionate gestures, grooming, gifting each other items and raising their young together. Male and female Yunnanosaurus do not bond with one another, but rather coexist with each other during breeding season only.
