# Ceratosaurus – Physical Dimorphism

♀️ Females have much smaller crests above their eyes which are duller in color. Their body is more uniform in pattern, patterns tend to be less defined and more faded in appearance. Suitable female subspecies: Nasicornis. ♂️ Males have much larger crests above their eyes that are brightly colored, and more complex, striking patterns on their body. Suitable male subspecies: Dentisulcatus or Magnicornis. Both sexes have varying colors, sometimes bright and sometimes darker, this can be influenced by the size of the pack that the individual is raised in. Those raised in smaller packs tend to stay closer to their natural colouring of warm and dark muted tones, while those raised in a larger pack tend to develop brighter, flashier colouring. This is in response to the greater competition for future mates. An individual with brighter colours is more likely to attract the attention of the opposite gender during courtship. Physical dimorphism for Ceratosaurus is not mandatory, but individuals differing from the typical dimorphism may face difficulties during courtship.
