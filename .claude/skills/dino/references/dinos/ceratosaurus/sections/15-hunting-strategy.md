# Ceratosaurus – Hunting Strategy

Only 4 adult Ceratosaurus may enter an engagement or hunt at any one time. Rather than hunting living prey, Ceratosaurus will always go for prey that has already been killed. They love a challenge, and will contest for the rights to the body. Contesting for a claimed body can be done at any time of day regardless of activity schedule. If a claimed body is present in the POI, Ceratosaurus must challenge for this body before initiating a hunt on other prey, if they are able to. Ceratosaurus can hunt up to Large tier if the pack numbers fewer than 4 adults. Ceratosaurus can hunt Apex tier only when there are 4 adults in the hunting party. Ceratosaurus, however, will prioritise Albertaceratops and Nasutoceratops, or Medium tier and below dinosaurs, if within LOS. Offspring are permitted to join hunts by running alongside adults, but are not allowed to engage in combat. Ceratosaurus prefer hunting in a pack where possible, and will stalk their prey if the opportunity presents itself. When a hunting party of up to 4 Sub/Adults, sometimes with accompanying non-engaged adolescents, comes across prey that hasn’t noticed them yet, they will remain quiet and crouch low to the ground while walking forward using their forest environment to their advantage to remain hidden while stalking. Once a hunting party is close enough to the prey, or risk being noticed, they rush forwards together and take turns attacking the prey. Ceratosaurus keep to their pack instinct and if an individual brings down prey alone, they will broadcast to summon the rest of the pack to share the feast. If the pack has offspring, the adults will carry back scraps of meat to the den-site for the whelps to enjoy. If Ceratosaurus comes across prey while alone, or comes across an Apex they wish to hunt but are under the required 4 adults to take on the much larger opponent; they will rush back towards their pack mates and excitedly head-bob, followed by running in the direction of the prey and looking back to encourage the rest of the pack to follow - before returning to stalk the prey together. Ceratosaurus is not cannibalistic and does not kill or eat corpses of their own species - except with orphaned offspring that are discovered outside of the den-site. The corpse of a killed stranger-whelp is not eaten, as they are not cannibalistic. If a kill is large and meat is plentiful, Ceratosaurus will tolerate scavengers.
