# Pycnonemosaurus – Parenthood

Mothers are very protective over their hatchling offspring, known as shoats. The excitable youngsters are born with strength and energy, and the mother keeps a close eye over them as they explore the nest. She will defend her nest from any other that approaches, while the offspring are hatchlings. At Juvenile, the mother encourages her shoats out into the open and calls to her group members so that they can be properly introduced. At this age, the young are slightly bigger and less likely to be trampled - her protective instincts start to subside and she accepts that the other drove members will defend her shoats if needed. Pycnonemosaurus mothers are protective, but do not coddle their young. Offspring are allowed the freedom to roam about, as long as they are within line of sight of a drove member. All drove members share responsibility of teaching shoats to hunt and how to survive. Mothers are directly responsible for their young, but Pycnonemosaurus take great interest in the small versions of themselves and guide shoats through their youth. On reaching adolescence, adults in the group start to include the offspring in their rough play - mock charging, shoving, and playfully biting their tails to encourage them to play and bond with drove members.
