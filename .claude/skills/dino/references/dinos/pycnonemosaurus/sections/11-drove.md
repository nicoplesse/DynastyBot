# Pycnonemosaurus – Drove

Pycnonemosaurus often form small groups of any relation. They are not necessarily picky when it comes to social interactions and form loose friendships with others. Just as easily, a Pycnonemosaurus may break away from the group to search for different friends. Pycnonemosaurus enjoy butting heads and pushing each other around. Such behaviors are extended to offspring and youngsters adore rough housing with their older group members. Pycnonemosaurus frequently engage in mock displays: both with others in their group, and other dinosaurs they encounter. This usually consists of loud vocalizations and head bobbing. Displaying to another Pycnonemosaurus (typically one of the same sex) and having them respond with mimicking displays serves to reinforce bonds between individuals and strengthen existing friendships. Pycnonemosaurus that ignore another’s displays may be seen as rude, and may be ignored by others for a short period of time, until they inevitably forget about it and resume friendly interactions. Pack structures are fairly equal, with one of the boars taking the respected dominant boar status; to monopolize breedings rights with the sows in the group. This boar tends to be the most dominant and physical in the group, he never backs down from a game of rough housing and almost always comes out on top. Dominant boars are also determined by a dominance fight, outlined in Aggressive Interaction. Other boars within the drove are not permitted to sire any offspring while they do not hold the dominant position. Dominant boars are respected by the other drove members, and others will always move away slightly so that he can eat from a corpse if he wishes to. Droves are not territorial, and if they encounter one another they spend a great deal of time posturing and displaying their swaying calls. If they are bold enough, they may step up and initiate a short game of playfighting with the other drove. After a short game, the two droves part ways to ensure that there are enough resources for each drove to sustain themselves. They split into different POIs where possible, to avoid disturbing the other’s hunting ground.
