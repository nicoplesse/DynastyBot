# Camptosaurus – Downtime Behavior

During the Dry Season, where hot weather is common, Camptosaurus prefer to flock to the beaches during the day, with a whole colony often collecting in the same general area. They create mound-like holes in the warm sand, and nestle into them, using their large tails to stir up sand onto their back and limbs. Camptosaurus enter a deep state of relaxation at this time, and grow disgruntled if their resting space becomes shaded, rising with grumbling snorts and repositioning themselves back into the sun’s rays. They pass through phases of sleep and wakefulness during this time. Mothers in particular often wake to look for their joeys, who can often be found investigating the ocean and its various treasures, or disturbing other sleeping Camptosaurus. Dominant males may lightly bully subordinate males during this time by attempting to steal their sleeping spot. If unsuccessful, these males do not escalate these situations into fights, saving their displeasure for the night time. During the Rainy Season, all Camptosaurus sleep within their home ranges, often built to protect them against the rain and cold. If a Camptosaurus is without a territory, they will find a bush or cave to sleep in temporarily. They are awake less often during these times, disgruntled by the weather, their moods soured. Moments awake are used to be on alert for distant dangers, muffled now by frequent storms. Joeys share this distaste, and often stay huddled with their mother in her nest. They chatter and bicker among themselves, restless and fidgety, much to their mother’s annoyance.
