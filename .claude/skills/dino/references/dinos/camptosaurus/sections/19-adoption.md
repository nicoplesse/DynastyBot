# Camptosaurus – Adoption

Female Camptosaurus may adopt, if they have similarly aged young with them. Adopted offspring are treated the same as biological offspring, receiving no additional attention. Orphaned joeys are inspected for signs of sickness or parasites that may endanger a female’s current young before all else. If the joey passes this brief inspection, and the female is open to adoption, she’ll call out affectionately to invite the joey in joining her current young. Male Camptosaurus, uninterested females and females without offspring will opt to ignore the orphaned joey after the inspection period. The orphaned joey may attempt to follow these Camptosaurus to learn from a distance, usually tolerated. Orphaned joeys will be treated as an intruder if they attempt to enter the home range of a Camptosaurus who has not adopted them, and do not receive protection from anyone if attacked for it. Elderly Camptosaurus can be identified by their dull, pale colouring and unkept appearance. Their scales are often damaged and flakey due to prolonged sun exposure, with some Camptosaurus bearing scars from previous encounters with predators. Players must change their skin to resemble aging once they’re reached elder status. Camptosaurus typically become elder after 10 clutches. As Camptosaurus age, their bodies grow slow, with walking, running and hopping becoming a tiring and painful task. They are slow to react to encounters and frequently experience bouts of forgetfulness, often seen pacing around the same area in a haze. They grow increasingly agitated and frustrated as a result of these changes, snapping at other colony members easier and displaying more aggressive reactions to having their personal space invaded. Camptosaurus lose the flee reaction, the task too strenuous for their body to continuously perform. Instead, elderly Camptosaurus freeze, becoming motionless, determined to blend in with their surroundings as their new means of survival. Elderly Camptosaurus abandon their home ranges at this stage, instead settling in areas close to food and water, to minimize the need for travel. They no longer maintain or challenge for home ranges at this point, and previously dominant males automatically fall to subordinate status. Elders are respected by all colony members, and they do not experience bullying or challenging behaviour. The only exception to this is a Camptosaurus protecting their home range. Colony residents pick up on these signs of aging quickly. Though solitary by nature, concern and grief may still be showcased between individuals, especially if the elderly Camptosaurus has been a part of a colony for a long time.
