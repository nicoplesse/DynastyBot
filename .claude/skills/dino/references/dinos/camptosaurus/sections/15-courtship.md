# Camptosaurus – Courtship

Camptosaurus courting is led by the males, but ultimately a female’s choice. The process lasts a whole night cycle, or until all interested females have been courted. The process begins with a male making himself known and heard at the community grounds, with bellowing broadcasts and dramatic sprints, he works to lure out the residing Camptosaurus into acting as an audience. This interaction causes a chain reaction within the colony, with other males rushing forward to begin their own displays to quickly catch the audience’s attention. Females watch from the sidelines quietly, moving around the clearing as they dictate which male they find most promising at any given time. Those unable to or uninterested in partaking like to spectate, but are careful to keep their distance as to avoid getting in the way of participating males. Subordinate males are very passive with their courting, and do not take part in typical courtship challenges like the dominant males do. Instead, their courtship consists of enticing a female by playing into their curious nature. A pretty flower, a chunk of crystal, a smooth clam, are all items that may be laid out in front of a female, encouraging her closer with soft calls and short sprints around the object. Few females find this sort of display acceptable, and subordinate males are typically ignored, especially if dominant males are present. Dominant males are typically the focus, with their loud and aggressive displays drawing in the most amount of attention from females. These males quickly turn to another male and begin a courtship challenge, aggressively calling at the male and stretching their bodies up in a threat display. Subordinate males never accept these challenges during courtship, and quickly crouch in submission, calling out fearfully, allowing for the dominant male to relish in his easily earned victory. They slip away to continue their passive courtship a distance away once the dominant male has diverted his attention. If this other male is also a dominant male, they will mimic the aggression and stretching, attempting to appear larger than the other, and showing off any scars they have accumulated. The two circle each other, calling loudly and repeating their threat displays. The goal here is to deter the other male away before it escalates to a physical fight. If neither male backs down after a period of display, the two rush in to meet in the middle. Rising to their hind legs, they work quickly in delivering brutal bites to their opponent’s neck and face, and shoving in an effort to knock the other off balance. Males do not turn their back or run away from each other during these fights, as this is seen as a sign of weakness, with this action causing them to instantly lose favour with the females. This challenge ends at half-health, with the losing male forfeiting by turning their back on the opponent and fear calling. They retreat a short distance away to shortly recover and mourn their loss. At this time, they may also take a moment to evaluate the courting males, purposefully picking out a subordinate male to regain favour quickly with the crowd. The winning Camptosaurus will gloat his victory, broadcasting after the fleeing males and stretching to show off his fresh scars to the audience. He too will take a moment while strutting to regain his breath, before finding his next opponent and beginning the process again. Once a female has settled on the male she likes the most, she will move towards him slowly, assessing his vigilance by how quickly he takes notice of her. Having gained his attention, she rises into a stretch, keeping her focus on the male. Though stretching is seen as aggression between Camptosaurus, in the context of courtship, the female is sizing the male up, looking for signs of confidence and intelligence in his response. At this stage, approaching non-dimorphic females are often mistaken for challenging males, leading to immediate aggression, or immediate fear from a subordinate male. This aggression will offend the female, and she will reject any further attempts from that male, opting to court someone else. A male interested in the female will replicate this action, stretching to show off his form. The female will circle the male a few times, inspecting his previous and new scars, assessing his pattern, and judging his age and experience. If she finds him acceptable, she will call affectionately at him, accepting him as a worthy mate. The male will escort the female to the edges of the community ground, where she will wait for him until he has completed courtship. Other females successfully courted with the same male will join her, forming loose friendships with each other during this short period of interaction.
