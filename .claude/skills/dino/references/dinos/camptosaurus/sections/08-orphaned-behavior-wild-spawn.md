# Camptosaurus – Orphaned Behavior (Wild Spawn)

Orphaned joeys rarely survive on their own, often succumbing to the cold, or getting picked off by scavengers before they’re able to effectively perceive and react to threats. They remain asleep throughout the hatchling period, taking longer to gain their sight, hearing and ability to stand as they reach Juvenile, their bodies placing greater focus on staying warm rather than developing. Orphans continue waiting aimlessly in the same spot they awoke for a parent to come to them, scanning the horizon for any sign of movement or activity, and ignoring hunger, thirst and sometimes sleep for as long as they can manage. Joeys who have waited a prolonged time with no sign of parents in sight eventually stand by themselves, urged on by hunger or thirst, having accepted that no one is coming. These joeys grow up cautious and fearful of all others, ready to bolt at any given moment, and take longer to seek out colony grounds. They no longer imprint after this stage. Other joeys who are more fortunate perk up at the first sight, or sound, of another creature nearby, confident they’d just located their missing parent. They rush to stand on unsteady legs, stumbling and falling as they make their way to them, calling out to draw attention to their location, and nuzzling up with the species as soon as they’re in reach. If tolerated, Camptosaurus will follow and learn from this species for a majority of their lifetime, mimicking their behavior and mannerisms, but maintain their instinctual curiosity towards the world around them. Rejection leads to fleeing, though the Camptosaurus may imprint on a number of species, intent on finding their parent. Upon reaching maturity at Sub-Adult, Camptosaurus became increasingly adverse and aware of others, their solitary and territorial instincts overwhelming prior connections. Joeys who previously imprinted on carnivores will flee upon this change, their fear overcoming any prior trust they’d placed in their parent figure. Meanwhile, those who grew with a herbivore parental figure are also incredibly prone to dispersal, though this process is usually slower, with the Camptosaurus taking several days to conform to their instincts. Camptosaurus may rarely choose to continue trailing after their herbivore even after reaching maturity. They begin a lifestyle of simply co-existing, no longer showing additional tolerance or affection to their parent or others, similar to how Camptosaurus act within a colony. They do not alert to threats, and exhibit increasingly aggressive behavior around their personal space - behaviors that may eventually cause the herbivore to no longer tolerate them. Camptosaurus who do disperse spend a majority of their day wandering coastlines, searching for signs of others. If, after several attempts, they have yet to come across a pre-existing colony, a Camptosaurus will instead choose to settle down and begin their own. Upon integrating themselves into a colony, both genders experience great difficulty in conforming, lacking the experience to understand the concept of home ranges or hierarchies. They are treated similarly to joeys, corrected by the whole colony as they grow to understand what’s expected of them. Camptosaurus are a docile species that spend a majority of their time alone, interacting with others only briefly to maintain loose friendships or for courting purposes. While not necessarily social, neighboring Camptosaurus do check on each other with short calls throughout the day. Though these calls may sound nonsensical to other species, Camptosaurus grow to recognize the differences in tone and length, supplying bits of information between themselves. Camptosaurus are susceptible to forming grudges against other Camptosaurus, and rarely, other species. These grudges can come about for any reason, but typically occur between rivalling males, or females who have felt wronged by a male in the past. They are incredibly curious, spending a great deal of time inspecting, studying and playing with new objects; particularly well-received items are often brought back to their home range, stored around their nest. Their curious nature extends to other species too, who they take great interest in watching from a distance. Species regarded as especially captivating may be followed for a short distance, with the Camptosaurus mimicking their behavior and calls to further entertain themselves. Aggression, however, quickly dulls a Camptosaurus curiosity. In addition, Camptosaurus have an excellent memory. They are able to recall dangerous or favored locations, distinct sounds and the faces of other Camptosaurus. Due to this, it can be common for an individual to develop a life-long grudge against locations or other Camptosaurus who have wronged them in the past. Unlike other species, Camptosaurus lacks the size and strength to stand against predators, easily overpowered when caught by surprise. Instead, equipped with quick reflexes and a strong resolve for self-preservation, Camptosaurus use their powerful back legs to ensure a nimble, silent escape. Camptosaurus have a natural love for warmth, spending large portions of their day sunbathing and playing in the sun. The love extends to their preferences in habitat, with coastal, beach areas being by far their favourite. Rain, on the other hand, is strongly disliked by Camptosaurus, with many retreating into cover at the first drop, eager to stay out the cold. In a similar fashion, many Camptosaurus refuse to court and nest in the Rainy Season, aware that having young means making extra unnecessary trips during rainy weather.
