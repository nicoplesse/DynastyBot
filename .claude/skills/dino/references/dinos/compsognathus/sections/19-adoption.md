# Compsognathus – Adoption

Both male and female Compsognathus may adopt unrelated chicks at any time, even when they are not nesting themselves Elderly Compsognathus are very rare, as most Compsognathus live short and fast paced lives. They hold a great deal of respect amidst swarms, and while they are not the quickest when hunting, are typically allowed to feed from the bodies first. Elderly Compsognathus only begin to lose coloration from their scales when they are nearing death; their scales fading to sickly white color (albino) within the 5 minutes of their impending death. Elderly Compsognathus will curl up amidst their swarm or alongside their chosen carnivore to pass away quietly, listening to the squeaks and high pitched calls of the friends and family that will succeed them. Following their death, fellow members of a swarm will give one last series of calls to celebrate their fallen friend, before respectfully consuming the corpse. None Compsognathus form strong bonds primarily with Tyrannosaurus, but may try to form a friendship with any larger carnivore that accepts them.
