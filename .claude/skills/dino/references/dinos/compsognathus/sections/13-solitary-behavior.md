# Compsognathus – Solitary Behavior

Solitary Compsognathus enjoy inciting play with other dinosaurs, screeching from a distance and darting away before their target can track the noise. While this is more often than not annoying to other dinosaurs, Compsognathus are blissfully unaware, repeating it over and over until eventually aggressed. Solitary Compsognathus struggle to survive on their own, and seek out a large carnivorous friend to follow, relying on it to provide scavenging opportunities for them - or call out for others of their own kind to form a swarm with. Solitary Compsognathus are very vocal and call out frequently for a companion. They keep a wide berth from any herbivores (unless it is a Citipati, who are known to “adopt” Compsognathus and protect them) and will curiously approach any carnivore. When on their own, Compsognathus relies mainly on bones, rotting corpses, and stealing chunks from other predators’ kills. Solitary Compsognathus enjoy finding high perches and comfortable places to sleep, out of the way of larger dinosaurs that may trample them accidentally.
