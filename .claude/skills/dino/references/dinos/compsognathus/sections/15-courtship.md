# Compsognathus – Courtship

During nesting seasons, the majority of females in the swarm become receptive at the same time, enabling them to be able to raise young together. As females come into season, they begin exhibiting a multitude of broody behaviours - each female will look for a nesting site where she will spend extended periods of time examining and tending to it. Females will gather pieces of foliage - such as roots, pinecones, or flowers - to place around her nest in a crude fashion, before sitting in it and calling out to males in the swarm. Dominant females tend to pick the most valuable nesting sites and will bully lower ranking females away from nesting sites they wish to claim. Males that are interested in courting and nesting, will approach the broody females slowly and call softly to them. The dominant male almost always chooses one of the dominant females to try and court. Females of any rank will reject lower ranking males if the dominant male has not yet chosen a female, in the hopes the male will choose her. If the approaching male is accepted by the female, she will call back affectionately, and the pair will lay eggs in the nest.
