# Kaiwhekea – Courtship & Parenthood

During courting season, both sexes become extremely vocal underwater. These loud calls are used to locate a possible mate and also to evaluate and select the best mate for nesting. This loud vocalization can be divided into two categories: long-distance, and local calls. Long-distance calls, being like a broadcast, are used to find a mate over some distance. Repeated local calls, one after the other, are considered more friendly and submissive, and employed to signal interest in a mate. Males will court multiple females during one breeding season, if able to. If two different males happen to end up in the same area during courtship, they will usually try to drive the other one away. Males bellow loudly, trying to scare off the other male. When all fails, they bite and spin into each other until one reaches deathscars. These fights rarely end up in a dead male, but deep scars and bite marks are left on both. Even if male Kaiwhekea are in bonded duos, these males still fight to maintain breeding rights with the females. Offspring Rearing When preparing to nest, females will head to a coastal area with shallow pools, constructing a simple structure to lay their eggs. After all young hatch, mothers leave the shallow ponds and head out into deeper water to wait for their young to grow strong enough to join them. Once young have made the stretch into deeper water at juvenile, they travel around the oceans nomadically, with males swimming on the outside of the bale. Male Kaiwhekea do not participate in taking care of the offspring, but protect their selected bale until offspring are adolescent. Once young has reached adolescent, males return to a solitary or duo lifestyle.
