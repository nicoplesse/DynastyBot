# Kaiwhekea – Social Interaction

Female Kaiwhekea are passive to their own kind, often forming loose groups up to 6 adults. They enjoy leaping out of the water with each other, and practising organised hunts, sometimes on one another. Though social, Kaiwhekea also enjoy their time alone, often sunbathing on beaches or swimming spread out from each other, careful not to get too close into one another's space. Male Kaiwhekea are less tolerant of each other, viewing the other as competition, but can occasionally form friendships and small duos with other males. These duos work together to drive away rival males during courtship and protect females. Female Kaiwhekea that spend a lot of time together in a bale sometimes end up forming same sex pairs. Though closely bonded with each other, they may still accept courtship advances from males, and raise offspring together with their female partner. Male Kaiwhekea do not stay with bales long-term, and slip away to find other females once their offspring reach adolescence.
