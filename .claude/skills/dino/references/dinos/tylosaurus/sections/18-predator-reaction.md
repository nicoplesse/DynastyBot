# Tylosaurus – Predator Reaction

Tylosaurus pay little mind to other carnivorous species, but will quickly move to defend members of their matriline should they be targetted. Up to 2 Tylosaurus may take part in defence - typically the mother of the matriline, and one other adult individual. The rest of the Tylosaurus in the matriline quickly rush calves to safety beneath them and away from the engagement. Sisterhoods and rovers may not defend one another. Pairs of Tylosaurus work together to bring down their prey, using their strong jaws to break the bones of their targets, as well as to clamp them and pull them deep beneath the water - drowning them. Tylosaurus can be observed “playing” with their prey - nosing them around in the water, blocking them from reaching land - before eventually dragging them beneath the waves and tearing into them. Leedsichthys adults, though a favoured meal, are treated with a more cautious approach, with Tylosaurus adults often relying on ambush, speed and numbers to overwhelm their target. However, Leedsichthys pups, whether attended or unattended, are treated with far less care.Tylosaurus are quick to clamp pups, slipping them away from their fathers and towards the coast, where the pup is dropped on the beach and left to suffocate. Different Tylosaurus matrilines may exhibit different and unique hunting behaviors, such as those detailed below: Beaching: Tylosaurus matrilines may stalk shores, looking for individuals drinking from or intending to swim across the water. To initiate the engagement, Tylosaurus will lunge out of the water and use their powerful jaws to bonebreak or clamp their target to pull them back into the water with them. This can be dangerous for Tylosaurus, especially if done against carnivorous prey. Tylosaurus will not crawl further than a bodylength from the water in pursuit of prey. Chasing: Tylosaurus matrilines may stalk and chase their prey for extended periods, with the intention of exhausting them. Herding: Tylosaurus matrilines may opt to selectively hunt Kaiwheka. Upon finding a group of Kaiwheka, Tylosaurus will position themselves in ways that frighten the Kaiwheka in the direction they would like them to go. The goal is to corner Kaiwheka against an underwater cliffside or into a tunnel or into a cave, where another Tylosaurus lies in wait to land deadly blows. Up to two Tylosaurus may take part in a hunt on another species. All other individuals move away from any ongoing engagement to ensure that there is no interference. Up to 1 adolescent Tylosaurus may also assist in hunts, but must individually forfeit when at half health and flee the engagement. Tylosaurus may third party engagements that enter the water or 1 body length of the water. Tylosaurus prefer to target the individual whom looks most injured or that it can clamp, dragging it away and deeper beneath the water to finish the engagement itself.
