# Tylosaurus – Shunning Behaviors

Within a matriline, it is very rare that any aggressive behavior is shown, if at all. If a Tylosaurus is aggressive towards any others in its matriline, bites offspring, or is otherwise acting sickly, the mother of the matriline may initiate shunning. The shunned individual is pushed outside of the boundaries of the group physically. If the shunned individual gets back into close proximity, they are then nipped at and aggressive called away. If they still do not pick up on the mother’s intent, or blatantly ignore her, the entire matriline may swarm and tear into the shunned individual. They will not feed from the body if they successfully kill the individual - instead tucking their offspring close and leaving the area. Tylosaurus only hunt Spinosaurus and Deinosuchus if desperate hunger (35%). Tylosaurus are a very intelligent species, and understand that Spinosaurus and Deinosuchus pose great risks to them. Tylosaurus may always accept a challenge from Spinosaurus or Deinosuchus for the right to remain within a territory. Otherwise, Deinosuchus and Spinosaurus are ignored. However, offspring of these species may be freely hunted. Tylosaurus only hunt Sub/Adult Apex terrestrial carnivores if they are actively swimming in water. However, they may hunt hatchling, juvenile, or adolescent Apex terrestrial carnivores at any time. Tylosaurus can also be observed killing Tropeognathus, Thalassodromeus, Rhamphornhychus, and Halzskaraptor when they dive for food or are in the water nearby them. Tylosaurus feed from the bodies of Tropeognathus and Thalassodromeus, but will not feed from Rhamphornhychus or Halszkaraptor bodies, instead abandoning them after playing with the corpses briefly. Tylosaurus additionally stalks herds of Lambeosaurus and Parasaurolophus when they are within line of sight of the water. Often, these hadrosaurs make great migrations across water, making them the perfect targets for a hungry Tylosaurus. Though these hadrosaurus usually migrate alongside Iguanodon, Iguanodon are less preferred as prey due to their sharp, piercing thumb spikes. Before anything else in LOS, Tylosaurus must first attempt a hunt on Kaiwheka or Eurhinosaurus. These smaller prey are perfect enrichment for Tylosaurus matrilines, and often are cornered by hunting pairs. Lambeosaurus and Parasaurolophus are the next targets. Only if none of these species are available to hunt do Tylosaurus turn to other prey. Lurdusaurus are also favored prey of Tylosaurus - often grabbed from their dams and herds and dragged deep into the sea, where they are less likely to escape, and struggle to defend themselves.
