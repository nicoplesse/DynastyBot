# Tylosaurus – Matrilines

Matrilines are the most common of Tylosaurus groupings, with a leading cow who has nested in or adopted all other members of the matriline. Adoptees may not court other individuals within their matriline. Matrilines require bloodline logs in order to be grouped. Adopted members must still log bloodline posts, and indicate that they have been adopted. At the head of all matrilines is the mother of the matriline, who leads the group in hunting, migrating, courtship and nesting. She is almost always one of the hunters, and the primary defender of the matriline. The rest of the group consists of the mother’s offspring and their descendants, who form extremely strong bonds with their mother and siblings, and remain in the matriline for the rest of their adult lives. Dispersal from a matriline after one has settled is extremely rare; a matriline only breaks apart if the mother of the matriline dies. In their mourning, bulls split with their brothers to form rovers, and daughters begin their own matrilines or sisterhoods. Relationships between individuals in a matriline are highly stable, with individuals rarely being apart from each other for more than a few hours. All members within a matriline are very strongly bonded, and can be observed constantly playing, vocalizing and harmonizing with one another. Mothers of a matriline pass down their knowledge, and daughters from there then pass down the knowledge to their offspring, and so on and so forth. Ancestral hunting strategies and migration paths are passed down through several generations in this way.
