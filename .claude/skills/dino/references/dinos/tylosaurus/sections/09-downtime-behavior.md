# Tylosaurus – Downtime Behavior

Tylosaurus sleep by only shutting down one half of their brain at a time, in order to allow themselves to rise for air when needed. Without this necessary sleep process, the Tylosaurus risks drowning, or sinking to the bottom of the ocean floor. Tylosaurus will float slowly alongside other members in their pod when sleeping, remaining very close together. This behavior is known as “logging.” For the deepest sleep, Tylosaurus will find kelp forests, nuzzling into the leaves and finding comfortable places with their offspring and loved ones.
