# Tylosaurus – Adoption

Mothers dote on adopted children the same as she would for her own offspring. They are raised with the same level of care and dedication as any other calves, nuzzled often and taught how to hunt and play. Tylosaurus cows, once they have had their 20th clutch, are no longer able to lay eggs. They show very limited interest in courtship and do not construct nests, but willingly assist others in their matriline in raising their own offspring, and happily adopt. Despite Tylosaurus cows not being able to nest after 20 clutches, it does not immediately make them an elder, and they continue to move as easily and hunt as easily as they did through the rest of their lives previously. When a Tylosaurus reaches an older age, they become slower, with their bodies beginning to ache. With these new found aches, Tylosaurus lose their strength and become less likely to participate as active hunters. Older Tylosaurus breach more for air, and have their vision worsen over time. These elders at times end up swimming into objects and other dinosaurs.
