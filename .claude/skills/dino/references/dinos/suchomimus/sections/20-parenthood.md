# Suchomimus – Parenthood

Once eggs begin to move, the female will move into the water where she waits for her offspring, issuing affectionate screeches to call for the little snappers as they tumble out of their eggs. Males, at this point, often come to greet his offspring. If having nested with multiple females, he must spread his attention between all and his offspring. This more often than not results in a female being the primary caretaker of snappers, but if able, he will assist as well. Suchomimus parents stay very close to their offspring, teaching them how to fish and catering to them when they refuse to. Every time a snapper fear calls, their parents quickly rush over to comfort and soothe them-- until they get sick of it, and begin to ignore the fear calls entirely. Suchomimus parents will always recognize their offspring by scent, and will often spend periods of time basking on shores with one another and grooming them. This often doesn't go well liked by the fiesty snappers-- who would much rather be set out into the world and exploring. If snappers begin to drift towards Spinosaurus, preferring them more than their Suchomimus parents-- Suchomimus will sadly watch them go from afar, calling out for them, but not stopping them. They mourn the loss briefly before moving on, turning their backs and finding different waters.
