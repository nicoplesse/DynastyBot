# Suchomimus – Orphaned Behavior (Wild Spawn)

As all snappers are relatively self-sufficient straight away, orphaned Suchomimus suffer no ill consequences in comparison to some other species with a greater reliance on parental care. However, individuals that reach adulthood without encountering others of their species may suffer socially at first. The lack of socialization leaves a relatively small  - but still significant - impact on them - resulting in adults that struggle to relate to others, and may be more vocal and dramatic at first. Luckily, they grow out of this behavior quite quickly, and readily adapt to social groups. They do not naturally seek out other adults as replacement caregivers, but still gravitate towards Spinosaurus. Suchomimus spend a considerable amount of time both in the water and patrolling the water's edge. They thrive in an aquatic environment and prefer lakes, rivers and swamps to nest in. Suchomimus are nomadic, and do not claim territory. They often swarm a particular area that is abundant with food and move on to another area once they have depleted the resources in it. This behavior can be considered somewhat sedentary - with Suchomimus only moving on when they feel the need to do so. Occasionally, if an area has a wealth of food available and plenty of courting and convenience opportunities (such as Spinosaurus nearby), they will spend a longer time there before moving on. Suchomimus enjoy the sun's rays and spend some time basking at the water's edge whenever the opportunity presents itself. Suchomimus is an incredibly opportunistic species. If a situation presents itself that will reward them without much effort, Suchomimus always prefer to take that route. They are notorious for attempting to bully other carnivores off their kills rather than spend energy hunting their own. Suchomimus are cathemeral, but take short, frequent naps throughout day and night. They often swim to an island or somewhere unreachable by most terrestrial dinosaurs to do this. As a highly calculating species, Suchomimus assess risks carefully before taking action. While they may be bold and pushy if a situation warrants it and benefits them, they avoid unnecessary conflict and rely on intimidation rather than brute force. They test boundaries repeatedly, retreating just out of reach if challenged.
