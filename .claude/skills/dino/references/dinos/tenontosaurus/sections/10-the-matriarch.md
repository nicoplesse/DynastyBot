# Tenontosaurus – The Matriarch

The Matriarch is the most dominant female within a group and acts as the leader. She determines where the territory is, and how the group behaves. The Matriarch allows more submissive does, known as subordinates, to remain in a glade, so long as they submit to her. She indicates that the bucks in their territory belong to her by investigating and showing dominance over the subordinates on a regular basis. Subordinates are subject to light bullying, and only permitted to eat last. A Matriarch has a great deal of responsibility within her glade. She takes care to brush her scent against each member regularly with gentle shakes, and organizes patrols of the territory. She is also the deciding factor in handling adversaries. Another responsibility of hers is ensuring that her territory can sustain the glade. She will aggressively expel members if the glade has grown too large, or opt to migrate/expand territory. The priority for expulsion is sons > subordinates > bucks. Matriarchs learn that they must migrate semi-regularly to keep the glade healthy and maintain diet variety. While glades are territorial, matriarchs will organize the movement of the glade to a new territory when their current one can no longer sustain them. If the matriarch encounters a stranger in her territory, the matriarch will stand up, posturing with her head held high before slamming her tail on the earth. She carefully smells and investigates the newcomer, becoming irritated if they do not submit. If the newcomer shows appropriate submission and she decides to accept them into the glade, the matriarch rubs her neck against them, covering them in her scent. Newcomers that do not show submission are rejected, and chased out of the territory with angry growls and tail smacks. Once the matriarch has accepted new subordinates or males, she rouses the rest of the glade to greet the new member of the group, before confidently guiding them along the perimeter of her territory, highlighting its bountiful resources. Experienced matriarchs possess a keen understanding of their glade's capabilities and limitations, and base her decisions on them. Younger matriarchs generally lack experience, occasionally becoming overly bold or timid in the face of danger, which can harm the glade in the process. Not yet equipped with the knowledge needed to properly protect her glade or create coordinated intimidation tactics, young matriarchs can make mistakes that can be costly for the glade. While young matriarchs generally strive to adapt to their responsibilities, shortcomings in care and coordination may arise if not learning is not quick. The matriarch primarily spends time near the bucks, vigilantly guarding them and ensuring they stay within her territory. In the event she notices an unrelated buck attempting to leave the glade’s territory, she will attempt to herd and nip at the male incessantly, guiding him back to the others. However, excessive force is never used, and ultimately some bucks will slip away despite this. The matriarch will actively cull fawns that are not hers if discovered within the territory.
