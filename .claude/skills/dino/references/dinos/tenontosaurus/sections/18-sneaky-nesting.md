# Tenontosaurus – Sneaky Nesting

Due to the limited nature of the matriarch's reproductive abilities, bucks that weren’t selected to nest after a long period of courtship may find other means to ultimately pass down their genetic lineage, and commonly stray towards more risky endeavors. ‘ Subordinates during this period may profit from the matriarch's sudden stolen focus in order to entice an overlooked buck. Waiting until the matriarch is distracted elsewhere, does looking to sneakily nest spend some time gathering valuable items to present as gifts to their chosen buck. Egg shaped items, such as pinecones and rocks, especially help to communicate a doe's interest towards a buck, and are prime gifts to be offered. If the buck is interested, the pair will silently slip away to a remote part of the territory and build a nest together to incubate the eggs. When offspring hatch, the buck ushers these additional hatchlings into the main nest with the matriarch. It is vital that this process makes little to no noise, and only happens as the glade sleeps - or that the matriarch is at least occupied elsewhere. If all goes well, the matriarch does not notice the additional young that join the nest, and accepts them as if they were her own fawns. Should any noise occur during this delicate process, the matriarch is alerted to the intrusion, and opts to cull the offspring immediately. Successful integration of fawns is only possible when the matriarch's fawns are juvenile or younger.  Subordinates can only nest with one male when sneaky nesting. Should the matriarch catch a subordinate attempting to sneakily court - or worse still, discover the nest with fawns still inside - the subordinate is driven from the glade, with all fawns culled if they are not quick enough to escape. Same-Sex Pairing Bucks may form a same sex pair. These naturally form over time, and most commonly come about between bucks that have spent a considerable amount of time together, bonding closely. Despite forming a same sex pair, bucks rarely deny a doe during courtship, and bucks within a pair are content to rear young together. Does do not form same sex pairings.
