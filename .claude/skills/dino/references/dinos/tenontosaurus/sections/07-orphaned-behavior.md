# Tenontosaurus – Orphaned Behavior

After hatching, fawns reach for the comforting warmth of their clutchmates, before looking around for protection from a guardian. Upon discovering their solitude, orphaned fawns begin to shriek in distress, and stagger to their feet to quickly search the area, desperate to try and find their family. Should nobody answer their calls, a fawn returns to its hatching place, isolated and alone. It continues calling for parents that do not come, but eventually these calls quieten down until the fawn falls silent. The absence of close-knit sibling bonds eventually causes an orphan to stray quietly from its birthplace, seeking companionship and food. If the hatchling finds another animal’s young, regardless of species, they quickly cling to the side of the other offspring, seeking to replace the absence of their siblings. However, if met with aggression by their newly chosen siblings, they will flee. Sadly, this can represent the end of a Tenontosaurus’ life if its newly chosen siblings happen to be of a carnivorous variety. Tenontosaurus that are lucky enough to find willing surrogate siblings eventually disperse from them once they reach Sub-Adult, keen to find others of their species. Those that are luckier still, and find other Tenontosaurus before this age, always switch to follow those instead.
