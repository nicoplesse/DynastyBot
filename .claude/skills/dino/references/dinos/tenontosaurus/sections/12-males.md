# Tenontosaurus – Males

Just like subordinates, male newcomers will lower their heads and avoid eye contact, careful to stay low to the ground, to showcase their submission to the matriarch. Males are deeply parental and have a gentle nature compared to their female counterparts. They care for fawns regardless of relation. Within kindles with other nomadic bucks, they happily accept and care for any orphaned Tenontosaurus they discover. They can be observed sometimes caring for similarly-sized babies of other species, but quickly stop caring for them once they notice the young are behaving oddly. If there are no young to care for, bucks stick together and wander throughout the territory with the matriarch following behind them. They may aid in territory maintenance as they spread their scent throughout, visiting patrolling does and occasionally distracting them with play. Bucks forge social connections within the glade. As the most gentle members of the glade, they address disagreements or internal conflicts that may arise - particularly those involving the Matriarch. They echo their loyalty to her at all times - unless she has proven to falter in her responsibilities to the glade. In a glade, a buck's intervention and support given to a doe during an argument is highly beneficial, and often leads to a resolution or compromise that favors the supported individual. Maintaining harmony is crucial for the males, as newcomers or disruptive individuals could threaten the established routine. The matriarch usually tolerates this behavior due to it benefiting her overall. However, if a buck shows too much interest in another doe, she may interrupt their interactions, not wanting her bucks to develop deep relationships with her subordinates. If a dispute is too aggressive or rowdy, bucks tend not to dare come close and instead watch from the sidelines, prioritizing their own safety. When a matriarch is overthrown, some bucks become deeply upset at the loss and attempt to follow her. This is swiftly intercepted by the new matriarch, who herds and nips at the males to attempt to keep them within her glade's territory. If they feel bonded to the old matriarch or are unhappy with the current glade, they sneak away during a rest period for the glade. Otherwise, some bucks are only upset momentarily if they find that the new matriarch is a better fit. When attempting to leave a glade and caught by a matriarch, bucks put up a fuss momentarily, but are spooked back to the rest of the glade easily. They dislike direct confrontation, and will only sneak away instead of fighting against the matriarch. They have a unique relationship with Bonapartenykus and act affectionately towards them, appreciating their helpful nest maintenance and grooming. Males may decide to defend Bonapartenykus that have integrated well into the glade, or ones they have formed an attachment to.
