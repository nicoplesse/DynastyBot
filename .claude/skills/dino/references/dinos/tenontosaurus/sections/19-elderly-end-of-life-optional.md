# Tenontosaurus – Elderly & End of Life (Optional)

As bucks reach the older stages of life, their colors slowly darken to a deep brown or black. Particularly old males may even appear melanistic, though reaching this age is rare. These males live a slow and more gentle life, though often find themselves being chosen as a courting partner much less. They continue caring for young but - due to their slower movements - typically end up dying if left to defend fawns from predators alone. If not picked off by predators, eventually their legs give way and they slowly drag themselves away from the glade to die, sometimes accompanied by another closely bonded buck. As does age, they colors and patterns tend to dull out. They acquire more grey and light features - though this doesn't affect their entire body in the same way age appears in bucks. Does, both subordinates and the matriarch, become more irritable with their advancing age, and frequently attempt to showcase their strength with riskier behaviors, aiming to maintain the appearance of youthful strength. Their decisions and coordination slowly falter, and thus cause a doe to lose respect within the glade until her inevitable expulsion. Upon expulsion, elder does linger outside of the territory for multiple days until leaving. These lone elder females are especially vulnerable to predators and move slowly, unable to properly defend themselves. They follow Barsboldia herds until eventually the strength in their legs wane, they are unable to keep up, or they are picked off by predators. Both bucks and does will mourn the loss of a buck - but only bucks mourn the loss of a doe.
