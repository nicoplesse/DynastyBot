# Albertaceratops – Parenthood

If their nesting cove is encroached by ungrouped herbivores or predators, the entire sett will defend their small territory around the cove and any food sources nearby.  Water is not defended or challenged for. These challenges are always initiated by the dominant boar and supported by the other boars. In smaller setts, even the dominant sow and other sows will stand to rise, forming the protective circle formation around their cove. The raising of the young is a communal effort by all the sows in the sett. Typically, smaller clutches are born, and the offspring are initially cold and fearful, hiding quietly until all their clutchmates have hatched. The collective sounds of nearby cubs prompt the young to venture out of the nest and explore the cove. The dominant female’s nest is often the first to be inspected, with the cubs curiously playing with her nest items and nearby young. Sows remain lying down while the young play, content in the boars’ protection and happily calling to cubs as they interact. When the time comes to move on, the dominant female stands and broadcasts a loud call to rally the cubs back to their mothers. Leaving the cove, the dominant sows walks ahead, accompanied by her dominant boar. The young stay close to their mothers during the early stages of life, only separating once they shed their juvenile skins and transition into adulthood. Cubs shedding into mutations (albinism & melanism) are promptly pushed out by their mothers and the dominant female. Once rejected, the dominant boar intently inspects the shunned young, cautiously sniffing them over to assess the issue before shunning them himself, solidifying their exile from the sett.
