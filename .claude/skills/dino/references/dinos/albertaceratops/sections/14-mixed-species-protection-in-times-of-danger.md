# Albertaceratops – Mixed Species Protection in times of danger

When traveling alongside Pachyrhinosaurus and Styracosaurus, Albertaceratops instinctively fall into a defensive formation when predators appear within 10 body lengths of themselves. They press shoulder to shoulder with their allies to form a defensive circle or line. Any individual separated from the herd is dangerously vulnerable. These stragglers act on pure instinct, driven by a single purpose: reach the herd. When a predator takes interest, the separated flee relentlessly toward the safety of the formation, ignoring all else. In times of danger, Pachyrhinosaurus, Albertaceratops, and Styracosaurus share a rare moment of a unified herd mindset. When threatened by predation, they respond as one, all movement and effort focused on regrouping and restoring the safety of the herds defensive formation. A targeted individual may attempt to outrun the threat, but if cornered or forced into close quarters, it will lash out with horn and bulk, fighting only long enough to break free and continue its retreat to the herd. Up to 4 adult individuals from any species may patrol beyond the defensive ring. These sentinels charge, chase, and aggressively drive predators away, pushing them back up to ten body lengths from the formation. The circle is never willingly broken and is only abandoned if a herd member falls. Psittacosaurus, while valiant sentries, do not partake in engagements and seek shelter behind the defensive formation.
