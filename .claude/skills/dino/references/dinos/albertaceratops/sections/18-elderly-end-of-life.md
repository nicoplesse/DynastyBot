# Albertaceratops – Elderly & End of Life

Elderly Albertaceratops, marked by scars and the wear of age, often show signs of a more difficult temperament. The dominant female and male typically reach this stage of life more quickly, as the stress of constant leadership duties wears on them, and the harsh environments these medium-sized herbivores endure shorten their patience. The dominant female, seasoned beyond her years, is always given the prime nesting spot within the cove. Her nest, adorned with various trinkets, stands as a testament to her experience. Her high rank within the herd provides her brood with enhanced protection, making their survival odds far greater than those of other females. The dominant boar proudly displays his age through his heavily scarred hide and short temper—traits that have solidified his place at the top for many years. Other boars who have spent considerable time by his side often hold great respect for him, occasionally competing over the privilege of defending the sett and patrolling the herd with him during quieter times. The loss of elder members in the sett is deeply felt, leaving a significant gap in the group's morale. Illness or weakness is rare in this species; instead, the most common cause of death is predation or engaging in conflicts their bodies can no longer withstand. Following the death of a dominant individual, the herd will often avoid the area where it occurred as a sign of respect and mourning.
