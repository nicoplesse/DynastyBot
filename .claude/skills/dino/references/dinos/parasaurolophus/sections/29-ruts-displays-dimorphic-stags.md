# Parasaurolophus – Ruts & Displays - Dimorphic Stags

All dimorphic stags must put on a courting display in order to court and nest for that season. The only exception to this is those that have established a lifelong pairbond. However, life bonded individuals may still wish to display for their chosen mate. Stags stand on their hind legs and begin to display in a clear, open area. They use loud calls, swing their heads, flick their tails, and shake to show off their size, muscles, and colors. If one stag enters another stag's space while displaying, this may quickly turn into a fight, known as a rut. This begins when one stag becomes displeased with the other encroaching on his own display "stage". The offended stag stomps his legs down in a gesture of challenge, aggressive calling to the other stag to force him to consider his actions. Stags that decide not to accept the challenge simply shake their head and step away from the displaying male in order to find a spot of their own. If the other stag accepts the challenge, he returns the aggressive call in response, before both males rise onto their back legs. Stags meet in the middle, attacking each other a maximum of 2 times each with their front legs, before backing up and calling/displaying once again. The two stags pace from side to side; taking only a few steps to size up the other stag while they continue to call and display. After a few moments of this, they stop and face each other once again before meeting in the middle to exchange blows. This continues until one forfeits by dropping on all fours, turning their back, heart calling, or once one drops to 50% health. Should a stag forfeit or lose in a rut, this does not lessen his standing with the herd or does - self preservation is also a desirable trait. Ultimately, it is a doe's choice in which stag she will nest with. Stags that are not chosen do not hold a doe's decision against her.
