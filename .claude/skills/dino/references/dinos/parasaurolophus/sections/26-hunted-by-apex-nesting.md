# Parasaurolophus – Hunted by Apex - Nesting

The only time Parasaurolophus will stand their ground is when at a nesting site, and there is just 1 Apex predator. In these circumstances, if Parasaurolophus have hatchlings in the nest, they do not flee and instead defend their young. Those who have an empty nest or are not nesting still flee in an act of self preservation. Parents only defend their own nests and hatchlings. Only 2 Parasaurolophus may defend one nest and engage with the apex at a time. In the case of harems, the father may move between his does' nests in order to help protect his offspring. They must stay within a body length of it in order to do so, but may charge down predators that provoke them (such as sitting). They must quickly return to the nest - the goal is to protect and drive the predators away. If Apex pairs approach to hunt, Parasaurolophus still flee, leaving hatchlings behind to hide in the undergrowth.
