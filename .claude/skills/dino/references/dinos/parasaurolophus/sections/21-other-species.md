# Parasaurolophus – Other Species

Parasaurolophus are more cautious around other species, but never exhibit aggression towards them - instead prioritising to move away from them altogether Stegosaurids, such as Kentrosaurus, Miragaia and Stegosaurus, are offered a wide berth. Experienced Parasaurolophus recognize the aggression in these species, and opt to stay out of their way. Ceratopsians and sauropods are regarded similarly - ceratopsians for their stubborn and aggressive natures, and sauropods for their large size, and pressure they place on nearby resources. Parasaurolophus are naturally alert, and highly attuned to signs of nearby predators. At the first sign of a threat, individuals rise onto their hind legs, appearing larger and improving their line of sight. This posture also serves as a visible warning to nearby herd mates - who often mirror it. The defensive stance prepares the herd for either a defensive circle or stampede. If a predator is spotted, some members begin fear calling, pushing the herd to become alert. If other herd members deem the predator to not be a threat, the fearful individual is comforted and given reassurance. Small tier carnivores are ignored in small numbers, but treated as threatening when they reach numbers of 4 or more. If a hunt begins, defenders do not run to aid the attacked herd member. The attacked individual must run to the herd for protection, within 30 seconds. When an Apex carnivore is spotted, Parasaurolophus begin to repeatedly fear call and flee, alerting the rest of the herd to the danger and triggering a stampede.
