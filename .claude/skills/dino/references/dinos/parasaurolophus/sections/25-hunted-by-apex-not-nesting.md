# Parasaurolophus – Hunted by Apex - Not Nesting

Unlike other predators, Parasaurolophus flee from Apex predators on sight. While fleeing, Parasaurolophus can defend themselves, but must prioritize escaping the engagement, as opposed to battling the predator. While fleeing, they may not attempt to purposely pace themselves in order to run out of stamina with another herd member, approach the Apex in any way, or intervene in an Apex’s chase of another Parasaurolophus. Up to 2 Parasaurolophus can defend themselves, but always look for a route to escape. Both individuals must be out of stamina or cornered to engage the predator - but do not turn around in order to defend each other. This does not apply if Parasaurolophus is already engaged with other predators. This does not apply if the Apex predator is Spinosaurus, Deinosuchus, or Tylosaurus. On sight of these species, Parasaurolophus move away - keeping their distance from them and the body of water they reside in. Parasaurolophus become more nervous and skittish, knowing that these Apexes are in the area and do not approach the body of water that they are residing in or were spotted in, instead opting to move to other water sources. However, Parasaurolophus stampede if these Apexes make any attempts to approach or hunt individuals within the herd. Parasaurolophus does not flee from Leedsichthys, as this aquatic creature does not hunt them. Some individuals may still react with surprise or anxious stares at the sudden appearance of the creature.
