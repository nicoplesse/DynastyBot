# Parasaurolophus – Hunted by Non Apex

Up to 2 adult individuals - almost always stags, unless there are not enough stags available in the herd - step forward to act as active defenders. Active defenders cannot be swapped out once the engagement starts, and cannot be accepted into any defensive formations for protection. An attacked individual must run to the herd for protection, and may receive defenders if they reach the herd within 30 seconds. Reaching the herd is considered within 2 body lengths of the herd. Should there already be a formed circle with defenders, the defenders may aid the herd member at risk of moving away from the circle. The attacked herd member outside of the circle is considered to be part of the "fleeing herd" if the circle has 8 members already. The fleeing herd can consist of one or more herd members. Any Parasaurolophus may use a tail attack in self-defense while being hunted without being counted as a defender If they turn to face the predator or kick them with their forelegs, they are an active defender. If the Parasaurolophus does not reach the herd in 30 seconds or turns to engage the predator, it is counted as a defender Active defenders move freely and chase within 3 body lengths around the herd. No minimum distance is required between the circle and active defenders. However, there are some exceptions: If a predator sits; If a predator stands still for more than 15 seconds; or, if a predator does not come back within reach of the active defenders after three aggressive calls and 10 seconds. The active defenders may then charge the attacker. This is a “mock charge” paired with kicks in attempt to intimidate the predator. If the predator(s) move to attack the fleeing herd, defenders may still run to them, remaining within 3 body lengths of a newly formed circle or attacked member. The primary goal of these defenders is to drive the predators away from the herd. If the predators forfeit, Parasaurolophus respect it and do not pursue, relaxing as they watch the predators flee the area. A maximum of 8 adults then work together to form a defensive circle, rushing to flush young and vulnerable offspring inside it and out of harm's way. Mothers opt to stand in the defensive circle if they get there in time, but otherwise these circles are comprised of the nearest adults at the time. Individuals stand back-to-back in a circle, stepping forward a maximum of 1 body length to land a hit if the predators get within their personal space, before retreating back into the formation. "Gutsy" and "Stampede" may be used non-defenders. “Rupturing Eardrums” is considered to be a damage call, not a buff call, and therefore may only be used by the active defenders during an engagement. The rest of the herd flees the area as quickly as possible, pushing the young that did not make it to the circle in time and the vulnerable forward to safety. The active defenders move to block the path for predators to pursue them. If the predators pursue the herd, a new defensive circle can form from the fleeing group, forcing the original circle to disengage. The active defenders do not change if they manage to keep pace with the predators and remain within line of sight of the fleeing herd. However, if they lose line of sight and cannot make it in time, new defenders may step forward as a new circle is formed. Mothers can always land a hit if a predator attempts to attack their offspring under Sub-Adult, if the fawn is within a body length of her. The mother is not counted as an active defender or part of a defensive circle unless she is in the circle. 1 Parasaurolophus may defend 1 adopted Lambeosaurus, Iguanodon or Barsboldia under Sub-Adult if they are treating it as an adopted offspring, and it is within a body length of them.
