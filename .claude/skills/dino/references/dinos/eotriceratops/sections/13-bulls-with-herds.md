# Eotriceratops – Bulls with herds

Bulls only group with matriarchies once they have been properly introduced to their own offspring. The matriarch presents the offspring to the bulls and allows them to investigate each one, establishing their scent and ensuring that the offspring is theirs. Once satisfied, the matriarch allows them to join the group. The matriarch is a strong component of a bull’s relationship with the matriarchy. She will approach him often to check in and show him affection. The bull himself will approach her at times and will often sleep close to her during rest stops in the day, trusting her to watch over him. Bulls will also bring items they notice females are fond of to them in order to build stronger relationships with the herd. Bulls are not present whatsoever with offspring when they are hatchling and Juvenile, but are permitted closer when the offspring are adolescent, and are a suitable size to interact with the fathers. Bulls at this point are allowed to fully roam within and around the herd, and investigate offspring. Any aggression shown by them towards the offspring, however, is quickly stopped by the matriarch. While the herd rests at night, bulls will patrol, and will sleep in shifts if there is 2 of them. This typically means that each bull will sleep for about fifteen minutes each night. If there is only one bull, he will sleep on and off, typically remaining seated in a position where he can watch over the entire herd. Only bonded bull pairs will claim a herd together; solitary bulls will not have another bull join them and will not allow ungrouped bulls close to their herds. Matriarchs will also not permit bulls who do not group with the herd from the beginning to join the herd. If a bull is bonded with another bull, and that bull joins late, he may not get within 5 bodylengths of the herd, and may not group, but may follow. The bulls will not protect each other during this time, and the ungrouped bull will not protect the herd. If one of the bonded bulls dies while grouped with the herd, the living bull may still continue to remain with the herd and join engagements with the matriarch. If the bull has sired no offspring in the herd, he may opt to abandon the herd instead.
