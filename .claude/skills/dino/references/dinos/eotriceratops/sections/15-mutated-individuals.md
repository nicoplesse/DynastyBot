# Eotriceratops – Mutated Individuals

Albino, leucistic, gynandromorph, and melanistic bulls are still treated as other bulls and may still group in bull groups, but are never successfully selected by a matriarchy for courtship. In the case that a mutated bull wins a bullfight against a typically patterned bull, either the other bull will be selected for courtship or neither will be, and the matriarchy will move on. If a bull group is made up of two strongly bonded mutated bulls, the matriarchy will move on to find other, more suitable bulls. Albino and leucistic bulls in bull groups are shunned and are not defended at night by other members of the group, and sleep separately (unless closely bonded with another bull). It is very rare for albino and leucistic bulls to form strong bonds with another. Strongly bonded bull pairs containing an albino or leucistic bull will be forced to split if the typically patterned bull is selected for courtship. Mutated cows struggle to be accepted by unrelated matriarchs, and are more likely to be tolerated within the matriarchy they were born into. Albino cows cannot become matriarchs as they are seen as too weak, and become babied. Melanistic cows may become matriarchs. Bulls never accept albino, leucistic, or gynandromorph cows for courtship, but may accept melanistics. This results in mutated cows adopting any and all calves that they find, as well as helping to raise those of the other cows in the matriarchy. While in herds, they are unable to adopt, unless a mother in the herd has died and calves are in need of a mother. Piebald individuals do not struggle with courtship or being accepted into a herd. Females rarely, if ever, show aggression to each other, only doing so if another female aggresses a calf. In these situations, the matriarch is quick to intervene, and it never escalates to more than loud calling and posturing.
