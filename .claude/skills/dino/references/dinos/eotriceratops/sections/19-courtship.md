# Eotriceratops – Courtship

When a matriarch determines that her matriarchy would like the nest, and a bull or group of bulls determines that they would like to prove themselves, Eotriceratops in Gondwa travel to Mud Flats in the north, or Ripple Beach in the south. In Riparia, they will travel to Coastland Swamp - communal courting grounds for all Eotriceratops. There, bulls will bullfight in front of matriarchs, and the most worthy become selected to father offspring and attend to a herd. A pair of bulls select one of the sandy islands to fight, leaving the shore closer to trees and coverage for the females to watch from. Only the matriarch initially enters the courting grounds, with the other cows resting and watching from a distance. The matriarch makes the selection for her entire matriarchy. All matriarchs look for a well experienced and controlled bull, who will be the least likely to charge and kill an unattended calf. This is assessed by their behaviors during the bullfight, outside of the brawling that occurs. Bullfights All Eotriceratops engaging in bullfights must leave their in game groups and fight solo. Two Eotriceratops bulls begin a bullfight by slowly circling each other, with low growls to declare their intent. This non-contact display serves as an assessment of size, strength, and confidence. What begins as low, rumbling vocalizations as the two bulls start the bullfight, turns to aggressive, loud bellows as the circle begins to shrink – bringing the bulls closer and closer to the proper brawl. Bulls who rush to close the circle too soon are seen as overly aggressive, and are rejected as matriarchs by potential mates. If neither bull submits during the initial circling phase, once they reach each other in the middle of the circle, they begin to brutally rake their horns into each other's sides, and slam their heads together, interlocking horns in a brutal brawl. These fights are intense and bloody, and continue until one or both bulls reach half health in the dry season, or until both bulls back off in the rainy season. If a bull does not back off of a rival and instead goes as far as to kill them, the matriarch will reject him for being over-aggressive. In the final stage of the bullfight, once the bulls have pulled back from brawling, the bulls continue to face one another, backing up in a direct straight line to make room for charging distance, bellowing, posturing, and stomping. Before charging, both bulls slam their forelegs into the earth and roar, rushing forward in sync and slamming into one another face to face. Bulls typically do not attack after this charge, the display is a show of strength and finality to the bullfight. Highly aggressive bulls - or bulls during the rainy season - take this opportunity to land a few more blows on their opponent before they both back away. The Matriarch's Choice The matriarch approaches the bulls once they back off from one another after the final charge. After taking a moment to consider both bull's behaviors during their battle, she finally turns to the bull she deems to be unsuitable, and lets out an aggressive call. If the bull returns her aggression, she moves forward to push him, using her frill to push him away from her and her prospective bull. Well-mannered bulls turn their backs and move off, laying a good distance away to heal again so that they can put up another fight with a different bull. If two matriarchs observe the same fight, the matriarch who approached the fight first will get to pick the winner. The matriarch then turns to her choice of bull and shakes her frill affectionately. If the bull intends to accept the matriarch, he shakes his frill in return. They then begin to circle each other in the same fashion as the bulls did to begin the bullfight, until they slowly meet in the middle – where they nuzzle and call affectionately to one another. If a bull is part of a bull pair, and has a strongly bonded bull relative (or friend) with him, this other bull approaches slowly, making calm and gentle movements to show the matriarch he is part of a duo. After inspecting him, she will turn to nuzzle him as well if accepting - or call aggressively if she decides to reject him. If rejected, the accepted bull must decide what to do next. He can either break away from his bull companion to join the females, or join him and leave the matriarch. If the bull chooses to instead reject the matriarch and leave with his fellow bull, he does so by simply turning his back on the matriarch, careful not to show aggression, as he and his companion leave to find other opportunities. If there is only one bull at a courtship ground and no bullfights are possible, the matriarch will wait 10 minutes before deciding if the bull is suitable enough for her and her group. If he fails to draw her attention, she gathers up her matriarchy to migrate again, intending to return another time. Greeting the Matriarchy In order to call her matriarchy to meet the bull, the matriarch broadcasts, announcing to the other cows in her group to approach. If two bulls have been accepted, females will have their pick of either bull in the pair. However, the matriarch will always select the bull that won the initial bullfight. All cows wanting to nest approach and shake their frills at the bull (or bulls) at this time. If a bull intends to reject a female, he will turn his back to her, ignore her, and move on to the next female whom approaches.
