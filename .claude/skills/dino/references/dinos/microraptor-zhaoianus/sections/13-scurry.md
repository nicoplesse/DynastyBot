# Microraptor (Zhaoianus) – Scurry

Scurries are the most volatile of groupings for Microraptor, yet can also be one of the safest options. Scurries form naturally and often on a whim when lone Microraptor are not interested in searching for carnivore companions. Microraptor interested in grouping with others attempt to catch the attention of another with small chirps, landing hesitantly near them. If not aggressed out of their personal space immediately, the Microraptor shakes off the initial stress, and allows the other to approach cautiously, inspecting them with interest. If willing to accept the other, they both shake to loosen the initial tension and demonstrate friendliness. After this point, they may be accepted into the group. Any Microraptor may accept another into the scurry. Other members of the scurry who are nearby may also take part in the greeting and inspection. Should members of the scurry disagree with the choices of another, they will disperse from the scurry. Sometimes they may take more interest in the ungrouped individual, and chase after them in order to form a scurry of their own. Scurries can deny other Microraptor for any reason, which can lead to a constantly changing rotation of members. However, scurries with familial ties are less likely to have such experiences, and find it easier to maintain a consistent grouping. Within a scurry, there is no clear leader, and migrating can seem rather hectic, especially if there are several new individuals intent on proving themselves. Scurries often travel to the courtship grounds together, even if all members are not particularly interested in courting. New objects may be inspected by several members of a scurry at one time - however individual methods vary, often with the object being relocated frequently in a desperate attempt to get a better view. This action may spark disagreements if others feel they have not had adequate time to observe the object, inspiring short bursts of constant picking up and moving of the item around between scurry members. Vocal outbursts soon follow with prolonged bickering - though irritation never leads to biting. Many objects cannot be carried for long, as most are too heavy for the feeble Microraptor. Disagreements do not last long, as Microraptor eventually grow bored and move on to other activities.
