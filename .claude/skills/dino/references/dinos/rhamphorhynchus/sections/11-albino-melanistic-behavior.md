# Rhamphorhynchus – Albino & Melanistic Behavior

Albino Rhamphorhynchus are most prone to having splay leg in their youth, and often hatch with varying forms of deafness and blindness. While still fed and protected by their parents, they have a low survival rate due to often experiencing a failure to thrive. Additionally, they experience struggles with courtship in adulthood. Melanistic Rhamphorhynchus are regarded with curiosity by other Rhamphorhynchus, as their sex is often indiscernible purely by sight. However, they are identified depending on the courtship behaviors they exhibit during courtship - and as a result, may still court successfully in the season where their colors correlate (i.e. a melanistic male may court in the rainy season). Rhamphorhynchus do not often exhibit aggression towards other Rhamphorhynchus; however, any incorrect behaviors are corrected by nips in their direction and loud, shrieking calls. Rhamphorhynchus do not compete for mates or resources amongst their own species, as any bonds made are temporary, and can be redone at the next courting opportunity.
