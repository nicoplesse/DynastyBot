# Rhamphorhynchus – Parenthood

Rhamphorhynchus parents focus on feeding their offspring during their hatchling stage, encouraging them to grow quickly while catering to their chicks’ fast metabolisms. One parent will stay behind to keep the chicks warm while the other rushes to the nearest water source to eat and return with food. This is kept in careful balance until the juvenile stage, where chicks are then able to pounce onto the sides and backs of their herbivore companions. Sauropods are much preferred as they do not mind the presence of Rhamphorhynchus chicks in the way that some other herbivores might. Rhamphorhynchus parents continue to provide for their chicks until they are Adolescent, teaching them how to fish for themselves as well as how to fly (typically during break periods when their chosen herd is not migrating). They will also teach chicks how to clean the herbivores of parasites. Once a chick is Adolescent, they are regarded as a fully fledged member of the colony, but will not be courted or nested with until they are at least Sub/Adult. Parents no longer feed them - unless their chick begs them for it!
