# Rhamphorhynchus – Courtship

Only individuals who display their correct coloring for the season may court successfully. Drakes flush their colors in the dry season and desaturate in the rainy season, while pens display their colors in the rainy season and desaturate in the dry season. Courtship is a simple and swift process. Pens are those who choose which drake they would like for the season, while drakes perform one of the following displays in order to entice them: Rushing: Two drakes take to competitively flying together over the top of the water, as close to the water as possible. Those who land in the water, or do not dive as low as the other, suffer a loss, and are not picked by pens unless they perform a different and more satisfactory display. Drakes will broadcast loudly to draw the attention of females, and as an attempt to intimidate his competition. Once the males have landed, both will stretch their wings out wide. Pens flock to and mimic the posture of the winning drake, indicating their interest in courtship. Lakeweed Dance: A drake may opt instead to pick a nice hunk of lakeweed in order to aid him in display. The choice of a proper weed is essential to the success of the male, so he takes time to carefully investigate potential options before making his pick. Once he has done so, he finds a high place to perch and broadcast until pens come to watch. With a suitable audience, the drake once again picks up his lakeweed, clasping it between his jaws while he performs a dance. To signify the completion of the dance, he will stretch out his wings. If a pen - or multiple pens - enjoyed the dance, she returns the motion. If not, she issues a loud, almost mocking call at him, before flying away. Gifting: Often the most popular form of courtship, drakes take some time to bring pens gifts such as interesting forage, fish, and pebbles. A pen will always reject a male who brings her lakeweed by quickly taking off and into the air. To indicate that a gift is being given with the intention of courtship, a male will stretch out his wings immediately after delivering it. If a pen is interested in him, she will copy the motion. Once a pair or harem has been established, they take to flying close together and calling loudly, circling and diving around one another. Pens who are interested in forming bonds with other pens may take the place of the male in courtship, and drakes who are interested in courting other drakes may join the other females.
