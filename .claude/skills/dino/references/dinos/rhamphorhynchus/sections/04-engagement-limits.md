# Rhamphorhynchus – Engagement Limits

Rhamphorhynchus have no engagement limit, but do not hunt. Rhamphorhynchus may only scavenge or hunt critters. If aggressed while scavenging, they must flee. Up to 4 Rhamphorhynchus may remain pounced on a sauropod companion (Amargasaurus, Ampelosaurus, Apatosaurus, or Argentinosaurus) when they are hunted, and may use Shared Rhythm and Plotting Whispers while doing so. However, if the Rhamphorhynchus is bitten or runs out of stamina, they must flee and disengage, and may not return to the engagement. Rhamphorhynchus may not use Rat Out to alert predators to the presence of herbivores, near sleeping creatures, or during an active engagement. Rhamphorhynchus may not use Plague Carrier or Infectious Touch while pounced on sauropods or other herbivore companions. Rhamph Sting: If Rhamphorhynchus pounce onto another herbivore or carnivore with the Plague Carrier and/or Infectious Touch ability, they will remain latched until out of stamina, attacked by, or shaken off by the target. This is referred to as “a Rhamph sting,” and results in the target being hungry and thirsty faster, as well as having itchy skin, feathers, and scales. Depending on the severity of the sting (how long the Rhamph was latched), the effects may stay up for a full in game day cycle. Rhamphorhynchus only “sting” others as a last resort, as following a successful landed sting, the Rhamphorhynchus begins to physically deteriorate, and dies within 30 minutes. Preferred Prey Fish, carrion, critters. Rhamphorhynchus may eat the body of another Rhamphorhynchus if given the opportunity. Rhamphorhynchus may eat Metriacanthosaurus bodies without suffering any averse side effects.
