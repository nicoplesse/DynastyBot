# Rhamphorhynchus – Downtime Behavior

Rhamphorhynchus do not sleep often, but when they do, will find high safe perches within caves to construct cozy nests on and curl up within. Often, entire swarms will flock to the same nests - even if it means stealing the nest from whoever had it before! Sleeping is a very vulnerable activity for Rhamphorhynchus; as a result, they are very light sleepers. In colder periods, Rhamphorhynchus are seen entering a state of torpor; standing in one place and becoming completely unresponsive to any outside activity. These periods are brief and often result in the Rhamphorhynchus waking back up with a flurry of flaps and calls. Within a colony, natural cliques may form, typically those of a “resident male” and his temporary harem. Males who do not have harems within a colony are considered “non resident males”, and typically socialize amongst themselves, while females are very easily accepted into social groups with their mothers and sisters. Due to their high metabolism, Rhamphorhynchus can only survive a short time without food. As a result, a frequent form of socialization between Rhamphorhynchus is “begging” - a hungry Rhamphorhynchus issuing short and sharp trills at a friend, family member or mate in order to demand food. More often than not, this individual is happy to assist or directly provide, responding with trills of its own before flying off to find a fish or suitable insects. If otherwise occupied or uninterested, they will not reply with these calls, and instead might fly away. This is the fastest way to form strong bonds between individuals, and males who respond to a female’s needs when she begs in this fashion are typically those that are first chosen for courtship. Rhamphorhynchus also bond through mutual preening, laying close to one another and picking through each other’s feathers. This is most popularly seen amongst groups of bonded females, but males exhibit this behavior as well in lesser amounts.
