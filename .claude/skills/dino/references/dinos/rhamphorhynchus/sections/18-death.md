# Rhamphorhynchus – Death

Rhamphorhynchus often die due to crash collisions with rocks, trees, or even sauropod friends. Only family members and close friends or mates mourn the loss, with shocked and fearful cries surrounding the body. Rhamphorhynchus may eat the corpses of a fallen ally. Rhamph Sting After a Rhamphorhynchus has stung another individual, a 30 minute timer begins before they die. Rhamphorhynchus in this stage of their life become much more irritable and confused. They are constantly hungry and thirsty, but forget to eat unless fed. This often results in an early death for most Rhamphorhynchus. Rhamphorhynchus are not outwardly aggressive towards others at this time, and cannot sting others again. They begin to forget how to fly, and run in circles on the ground, disoriented. Rhamphorhynchus also may forget to go up for air when swimming, and may die due to drowning. None. Rhamphorhynchus do not use “Rat Out” nearby active engagements, sleeping herbivores, or in order to alert carnivores to nearby herbivores. Rhamphorhynchus go silent at the sight of a carnivore when with herbivores, and will fly away if an engagement is at risk. However, up to 4 Rhamphorhynchus who are latched to a sauropod (Amargasaurus, Ampelosaurus, Apatosaurus, Argentinosaurus) may remain latched and use buffs such as Plotting Whispers and Shared Rhythm. Up to 4 Rhamphorhynchus may be latched across an entire herd and colony, not per individual. Rhamphorhynchus who are bitten or run out of stamina must immediately disengage. 3.13 Rhamphorhynchus do not use Plague Carrier or Infectious Touch while latched to sauropods or other tolerant herbivores.
