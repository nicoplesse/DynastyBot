# Rhamphorhynchus – Offspring Behavior (Nested)

Baby Rhamphorhynchus, known as chicks, are born tiny and fragile on high cliffsides across Gondwa, or nearby water sources. At birth they are unable to fly, but are quickly able to move around and call for their parents. They prefer to be as close to their mothers or fathers as possible, huddling in tight to absorb their body heat whenever they can. Some chicks - most commonly occurring in the last egg to hatch -  exhibit splay leg, a temporary condition that inhibits their movement and forces them to remain immobile in the nest until Juvenile. This results in a great deal of dismay and concern from parents. Chicks are capable of swimming as soon as they hatch, but are unable to dive. They will follow close behind their parents if possible, and exhibit fear otherwise. Hatchling Rhamphorhynchus are particularly demanding for food, constantly issuing small, trilling calls in order to indicate their hunger. Making sure that they are suitably fed is one of the most important aspects of a Rhamphorhynchus chick’s early life. They grow rapidly, and require near constant feeding, much to their parents' dismay. At Juvenile - if the colony is nesting with a nearby sauropod/herbivore herd - chicks are encouraged to pounce onto the backs and sides of the large herbivores, in order to keep up with migration. If the colony is not nesting with herbivores, colonies instead teach chicks how to climb down cliffsides, and travel primarily grounded until chicks are able to fly. However, this leaves chicks exposed to predators, and is not preferred. Chicks are curious and chatty, and often develop preferred individuals to pounce on. When the colony and herd is at rest, chicks enjoy running around on the ground, picking up items and presenting them to each other, their parents, and the herbivores their colony has chosen. By the time chicks are juvenile, they are able to dive and fish for themselves independently. Chicks are unable to fly until adolescence. At this point, they spring off the sides of their chosen herbivores and into the air, calling excitedly. They fly along with one another and their parents, performing small aerial displays of diving through the air and into water. If a chick hatched with splay leg has managed to pounce and latch onto an accepting herbivore for most of their early lives; they go on to make a full recovery by Adolescent. If not - they face struggles with movement throughout the rest of their lives, and will prefer to exclusively fly, swim, or travel latched to the sides of herbivores, limiting their movement on the ground. At Adolescent, they are fully self-sufficient, and will no longer be provided for by their parents. However, no Rhamphorhynchus is capable of nesting or being courted until Sub-Adult.
