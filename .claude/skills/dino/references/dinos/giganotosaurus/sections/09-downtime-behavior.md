# Giganotosaurus – Downtime Behavior

Giganotosaurus are lazy at night, preferring to take the path of least resistance when it comes to hunting (typically scavenging from AI corpses, eating bones, or hunting critters). They will not migrate unless threatened directly or challenged out of the POI. They will find deep shrubbery or soft sand to sleep on with their pair or offspring. Males may stay awake slightly longer than their females and offspring in attempt to watch over them, but often fall asleep shortly after them. During downtime is when Giganotosaurus is most likely to express a brief lapse of tenderness towards their mate or offspring, nuzzling them in order to keep them quiet and carefully cleaning them. Giganotosaurus may also take positions where they are able to watch over an entire area; however, they will not act on anything they see unless it is a rival that begins to approach them directly -- in which case, they will stand to defend their right to sleep in the POI. Giganotosaurus rarely, if ever, form particularly strong bonds with one another, and only do so with repeated grouping and nesting. Even then, if the other Giganotosaurus offends them in any way - or a better opportunity arises - they quickly disband and shun one another. Giganotosaurus that are interested in grouping with one another must express it in different ways, depending on their sex: Females that approach others to group must take it slowly, shaking out stress and yawning to indicate that she is no threat. Males boldly approach other males, examining them to ensure that they are a good fit to travel with before accepting. If a male approaches a female, it typically is for courtship, but if neither are interested in courting or nesting with the other, they may still form a temporary bond of convenience. All groups split in order to pursue courtship and nesting opportunities. Giganotosaurus are unbothered within their groupings, which typically are out of convenience, making it easier to hunt and defend oneself. Over time, if the same pair return to each other across multiple occasions, whether it be to nest or simply to travel together, they begin to show more affection and care for one another, rubbing their faces against each other, sleeping beside each other, and watching over one another while the other heals or rests. These bonds may prefer to stick together rather than split apart for courting, and are typically a bonded mother and daughter, sisters, brothers, or pairbond of opposite or same sex. Giganotosaurus may spar with one another in order to develop their fighting skills, or just out of enjoyment.  These fights are agreed upon by both parties and never go below half health.
