# Giganotosaurus – Parenthood

When kits begin to hatch, the father creates distance between himself and the nest. He hunts and patrols the area to ensure no other Apex carnivores pose a risk to his offspring, and that any prey is swiftly hunted to keep his family fed. The mother is the first to greet the kits as they tumble from their eggs and into the world, and does so with soft cooing rumbles. She keeps a very close eye on her kits while they recover from hatching, cleaning them carefully and sniffing them to familiarize herself with their scent. Once kits are up and on their feet, she will carefully observe kits playing, encouraging them to mock hunt their siblings. However, she does not tolerate a kit bringing one of their siblings to deathscars, and steps forward to chase them back to the nest with a harsh scolding. She will also not tolerate being “hunted” by her offspring. Mothers additionally do not over extend themselves to feed kits who are non responsive, constantly sleeping, or otherwise sickly. Giganotosaurus parents, while both assisting one another with the responsibility of feeding, defending, and teaching necessary skills to offspring, are in no way doting. When kits are first developing their strength, they may attempt to play bite and play with their parents. It is up to the parent whether or not this is tolerated. While they encourage their offspring with nudges and affectionate calls, especially in the early stages, as offspring grow they become less tolerant and expect offspring to keep up. If kits are unable to keep pace with their parents, they will be left behind. Parents do not allow kits to feed directly from a corpse, and tear chunks and drop it in front of them to eat. Parents will teach kits essential life skills through encouraging them to spar and play, and by initiating hunt on critters.
