# Giganotosaurus – Solitary Behavior

Giganotosaurus enjoy being solitary and spend their days traveling across Gondwa. Solitary Giganotosaurus often have a small set of preferred areas that they linger in, especially areas where running into other Giganotosaurus is rarer, in order to avoid being challenged. Solitary Giganotosaurus, especially males, practice their strength by shoving down trees and charging into rocks, which helps them practice their fighting skills in order to defend themselves and females. Solitary Giganotosaurus do not hunt Apatosaurus or Argentinosaurus. They understand their limits and instead temporarily seek out a partner to hunt with, even if the bond only lasts until the hunt is over, and the body has been eaten. Solitary Giganotosaurus avoid other paired members of their species, as paired individuals are highly aggressive, unwilling to pass down an opportunity or risk sharing resources. They avoid pairs if they see them if possible, and only defend themselves if attacked. Solitary Giganotosaurus enjoy bathing in small ponds, especially those at Hotsprings or Whistling Columns. Solitary Giganotosaurus enjoy the benefits of reduced food competition, lower risk of disease transmission and complete independence from any other Giganotosaurus.
