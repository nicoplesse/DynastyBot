# Giganotosaurus – Death

As their time draws near, Giganotosaurus often migrate to beaches or small bodies of water, in order to soothe their aching joints and bodies from years of migration. There, they lay down to sleep, and often pass peacefully there - if not hunted by an aquatic carnivore or something that stumbles across it on the beach. Giganotosaurus do not mourn other Giganotosaurus, unless it is a parent mourning their kits killed by a predator or another Giganotosaurus. They will mourn briefly, before moving on. In addition, individuals may hold resentment towards the Giganotosaurus or predator whom hunted their offspring, and will attempt to hunt them in return– if able. A Giganotosaurus will also mourn their bonded pair, often by sleeping through an entire day cycle, hiding in the shadows, and refusing to eat. None. Giganotosaurus males may not flush in order to bait females into approaching and then hunt them. Only solitary and ungrouped individuals may court one another. The grouping occurs after a successful courtship. Giganotosaurus may only cannibalize Albino or deathscarred adult Giganotosaurus. Otherwise, they mainly ignore each other, unless interested in grouping or courtship, or in a pair. Giganotosaurus bonds are temporary and usually do not last a lifetime, unless the pair successfully raise five litters together. If an existing group consists of individuals unable to court and nest with one another, they must ungroup and separate in order to do so. Solitary Giganotosaurus do not hunt Apatosaurus or Argentinosaurus, and require a second grouped individual to do so, unless the creature is albino, deathscarred, or adolescent and below.
