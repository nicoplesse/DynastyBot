# Iguanodon – Food/Water Aggression with Other Species

Iguanodon are stubborn and possessive of food and water resources, as well as their personal space. They do not tolerate any species invading this space except for the preferred species that they herd with. Any herbivore species that they do not group with - with the exception of Small and Tiny tier dinosaurs - will provoke Iguanodon to guard resources. Most small and tiny herbivores are ignored, with the exception of Pachycephalosaurus. Only Iguanodon herds with 3 or more sub/adult members engage in this behavior. Iguanodon will seek to push in front of their temporarily claimed bushes and block access to water by pacing along the water's edge if they spot other species nearby, offering low, rumbling bellows to threaten. Other species, especially ceratopsians and stegosaurids, are immediately shunned and prevented from coming close or sharing the same water and food. If the other group outnumbers the Iguanodon defending their resources, the herd will instead move on, uttering out irritated grumbles as they move to new resources. Sauropods, while treated with disdain, are too large and formidable to fight with. Instead of aggressing, Iguanodon shamefully move aside. The only exception to this is Amargasaurus. If the other species refuses to back down and continues to approach the resource, all members of the herd may engage, but they do not chase down the opponent - instead remaining stubbornly in place, refusing to move away, and landing hits each time the opponents are within striking distance. Calves hide behind their mothers during challenges such as these. They do not join in with the engagement, but call out in support from behind. If a member of the herd reaches severely low health (deathscars) during one of these resource battles, the rest of the herd will forfeit the challenge, and regretfully move on to a new location with more resources. When away from food and water, most other species are greeted with indifference.
