# Iguanodon – Herds

Iguanodon are highly social creatures, and seem to fare far better when in a herd of large numbers. While there is no set hierarchy in Iguanodon herds, bulls prefer to lead the rest of the herd from the front, bellowing out loud calls to call the herd to follow. Bulls are the more gregarious of the two sexes, happily greeting and welcoming strangers into the herd but normally don’t form strong bonds with individuals. They tend to become "everybody's friend" with their easygoing demeanour, and take great delight in games of play with both each other and calves. Bulls spread their attention across the entirety of the herd, rather than becoming close to a select few friends. They enjoy tests of endurance - bulls happily engage in games of tag, where the bull being chased will kick back at his opponent. Rarely do these playfights go very far, and both bulls will shake at each other afterwards to conclude the session. There is little rivalry between bulls, except during courtship - yet even this is short-lived, and quickly forgotten about afterwards. In contrast, cows are more selective with their social interactions. They tend to be very loving towards those they have formed bonds with, but stand-offish towards individuals they do not know. This can mean they take some time "inspecting" a newcomer to the herd before they accept them - though they are much more accommodating to strange calves than they are to adults. They do, however, make close friendships with some individuals. These friendships are deep and long lasting, with plenty of gift giving between friends, and opting to walk together or sleep beside one another at rest stops.
