# Iguanodon – Death

If they feel the end of their life is approaching, elderly Iguanodon will start to head towards a favored place to spend their last moments. Beloved companions, friends, partners and relatives will accompany them on their final journey. Spending as much time as possible rubbing their body against their friend, and shaking their heads in reassurance. At rest stops, the elder Iguanadon’s companions will search for flowers and morsels of food to bring them, providing comfort and friendship right to the end. Iguanodon can be seen swimming and rolling in lakes and ponds, and especially relish coastal regions and splashing in the sea. At night, bulls take it in turns (1-2 at a time) to slowly patrol around a sleeping herd. Bulls don't patrol if they are the only Iguanodon in a mixed species herd. Defenders may chase down and continue attacking predators that hunt the herd after they forfeit - but may not leave line of sight of the herd to do so. Iguanodon usually tolerate albino and melanistic individuals, but some personalities may not tolerate them at all. Iguanodon are stubborn and possessive of food and water resources, as well as their personal space. Iguanodon has no profile specific rules.
