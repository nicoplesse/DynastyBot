# Iguanodon – Adoption

Orphans, meanwhile, are readily accepted into the herd. Cows that are experienced mothers, whether they currently have their own calves or not, are the first ones to claim an orphaned calf for adoption - but even bulls will provide protection and affection to these lonely calves. After a life spent migrating from a young age, reaching an elder stage often brings on the onset of arthritis. This condition gradually limits their mobility and activity levels due to the pain and discomfort they feel in their joints. Elders are often the slowest in the group, only very rarely sprinting, and choosing to take safer paths even if they take longer to travel. As they age, their ability to keep up with the demands of migration diminishes. They struggle to maintain a good pace to keep up with their younger companions, or get to food first. Elder Iguanodon may end up breaking off into smaller herds that move at a slower pace, and take on more of a sedentary lifestyle, taking longer and longer rest periods between travelling. Should the herd migrate to Red Island, elders will not follow, and instead stay near the coast, hoping for their herd to return later. Predators may have more success hunting a herd that has elderly members. These older individuals sometimes lose their hearing, and may have slower or delayed reactions when the herd calls out a threat. Elders are unfortunately easy targets, especially if they are particularly old and cannot sprint as much or as far. Elder Iguanodon enjoy spending time near coastal regions; bathing in shallow water and enjoying the soothing feeling of the water lapping at their weary bodies. They may be reluctant to move on if the herd starts to leave the coastline, and younger herd members may have to spend some time coaxing the elder away from the relaxing beaches. With the majority of their life behind them, an elder Iguanadon’s coloration fades after so much time exposed to the weather. Their scales take on a weathered look; colors fade and patterns become less distinct. Even the brightest of bulls will notice their striking colors reducing to a shadow of what they once were. When the loss of an elder is felt, those that were closest to them can be observed spending periods of time mourning and staying close to their final resting place, before they move on.
