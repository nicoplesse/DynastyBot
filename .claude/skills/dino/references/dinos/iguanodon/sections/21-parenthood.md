# Iguanodon – Parenthood

Once babies - known as calves - hatch out of their eggs, they take a moment to adjust their eyes before scrambling to their feet. Mothers greet their calves warmly, offering loving brays and affectionate head shakes as they encourage them into standing. Cows usually develop a strong bond with their calves, but the strength of the bond differs between females. Some mothers - especially first time mothers - can be overly protective, and angrily call out to other herd members if she feels they have come too close to her precious infant. These cows usually relax over time and through subsequent clutches. Some cows treat motherhood as nothing more than an inconvenience, which can result in calves being left behind if they fail to keep up. They will, however, always defend their calf if attacked within one body length of her. Bulls prioritize caring for the cows they courted, before paying any attention to offspring. But they do endeavour to prove themselves as good, protective fathers as well. Calves are greeted with affection by their fathers, and may be encouraged to play with him in games of chase once they get a little bigger. Many cows will find fatherly behavior to be an attractive quality in a bull, and those shown to be protective and sociable with their calves are considered a higher quality partner for the next season of courtship.
