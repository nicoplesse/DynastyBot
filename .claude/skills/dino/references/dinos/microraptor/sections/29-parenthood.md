# Microraptor – Parenthood

Once eggs have hatched, parents quickly get to work drying their offspring off, preening their feathers and rewarding the chicks with attention and affection at every milestone. Parents are at their most pleased when only one of their two eggs hatch, successfully providing them with a single chick that they can dote on. In instances where the second egg hatches alongside the first, both chicks are cared for regardless, though the chick deemed as the main is prioritized, including in regards to feeding, cleaning and gifting. In rare instances, the main baby may display signs of displeasure with having to share their parent’s attention with a sibling. In some cases, but not all, the parents may choose to outcast the spare baby to provide their main baby with the best possible chances. Though cruel, parents will never go as far as directly killing their offspring. Instead, parents gently shove the spare baby out the nest, encouraging it to glide, or rather tumble, to nearby foliage, away from the nesting location. At this point, the baby will either be left completely alone, as though it were an orphan, or still cared for infrequently, fed with anything the main baby rejects. This is an opportune moment for same sex pairs to adopt an orphaned baby, and is an ideal scenario for these rejected chicks. While both parents leave frequently, offspring will rarely be left alone in the nest, unless both parents have had to leave to protect the territory from rival Microraptors. However, chicks born to a father that currently has a harem may find themselves alone every so often, as their father is forced to help care for multiple nests at once. Once chicks reach adolescence, both parents partake in encouraging further independence and flight ability. Perching on a tree, they will glide across one surface to another, waiting expectantly for their offspring to mimic them. Efforts are highly praised upon, and parents may spend several hours a day improving their chick’s mobility and confidence through games of chase. When parents feel chicks have progressed enough, they will abandon the nest, and begin slowly introducing offspring to the outside world. Still hesitant to see their offspring on the ground, many parents encourage offspring to continue moving between trees as frequently as possible. Parents showcase their offspring new pois, items, and even species, demonstrating appropriate reactions to each, and most importantly, the art of mimicry.
