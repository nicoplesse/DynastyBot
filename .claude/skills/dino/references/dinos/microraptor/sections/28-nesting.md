# Microraptor – Nesting

Microraptor nest in trees and atop cliffs, with higher seen as better and safer from predators. These sites are protected from other intruding Microraptors, in the form of a territory challenge. Harem groups place their nests relatively close to one another, but females sharing the same male will not tolerate the other female too close to her own nest. Nests are heavily decorated with items of interest, providing their future offspring with as much entertainment as possible during their youth. Clutches are small, and typically consist of only 2 eggs, where one is deemed as the main egg, and the other as a spare - in case the first does not hatch. Nesting Microraptor instinctively take on differentiating roles based on their gender. Male Microraptor are typically happier to stray further from the nest, and as such, take on the role of feeding himself, his pair and his offspring. If a male has a harem, he splits his time between the different females nesting with him and their nests. Female Microraptor take on a more observation role, staying within the general area, incubating the eggs, and only straying to collect interesting items and trinkets to further decorate the nest with.
