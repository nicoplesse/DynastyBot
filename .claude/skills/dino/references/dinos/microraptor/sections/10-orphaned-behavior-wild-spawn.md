# Microraptor – Orphaned Behavior (Wild Spawn)

Orphaned chicks often hatch on the forest floor, without the usual nest that provides warmth and safety during early stages of life. Chicks instinctively crawl towards safety, nestling between foliage or debris in an attempt to regulate their body temperature. Once a spot has been secured, the chick remains relatively stationary until Juvenile, practicing only minor movements during the few hours they are awake each day. These small bouts of exercise eventually build up, and as their need for continuous sleep decreases, chicks stray further and further away from their temporary nest, seeking out food, and eating near anything that they can grab with their beaks. These chicks are instinctively silent, lacking the parental figure needed to reinforce the idea that being loud is a rewarding behaviour. Due to having no positive association to outside noise, orphaned chicks treat unusual sounds as a potential threat. They are naturally skittish, and as a result, often sprint to the nearest dense bush or plentiful cover available to hide when they feel unfamiliar with a situation. Upon reaching adolescence, the orphaned chick is driven by compulsion to climb up nearby trees for safety and for better scouting opportunities. They cautiously glide from tree to tree, continuously seeking objects of interest, such as food, trinkets, other valuables, to find comfort in. The life of an orphaned Microraptor is not an easy one. Once the chick has reached Sub-Adult, they have typically gained enough confidence and mastered their basic survival skills to leave the grounds that raised them, and seek new lands to thrive in. Many orphaned Microraptors end up socially behind as adults, but offer unique habits that may be otherwise intriguing to others of their kind.
