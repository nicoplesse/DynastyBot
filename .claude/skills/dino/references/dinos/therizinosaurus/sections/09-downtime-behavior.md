# Therizinosaurus – Downtime Behavior

Males may stay awake for longer periods  to monitor and ensure offspring are fed. In these cases, females will watch over the group to ensure the males are safe and protected. Females will only patrol their territories during the day if she hears calls within the same POI, aware that too much activity during the day affects her vision. She will only issue challenges towards other species that are not Apex carnivores in the day if they enter her or her sect's sleeping area. Ungrouped Therizinosaurus can often be seen collecting in Hunter’s Thicket, where they will form tentative Tidings (groups of entirely males), Looms (pairs of females), and Sects (groups with one lead female known as a Dame). Therizinosaurus are very slow to form bonds with other Therizinosaurus - even between parent and child and between siblings. It takes a long time to earn an adult Therizinosaurus’s full respect and trust - and even longer to earn the respect and adoration of the Dame. Roosters are more likely to make immediate connections amongst each other, and with their children and siblings. The main two methods in which a Therizinosaurus forms bonds with another Therizinosaurus is via preening - using one’s claws to pick dirt, branches, and stray feathers from another’s coat - or via bringing gifts to one another. Therizinosaurus males enjoy bringing gifts to females in order to slowly build a relationship and connection with her. These gifts are highly treasured by females, so a male will spend a good portion of his life and time finding the perfect gifts for her. More often than not, females are the recipient of these behaviors from males. If a female chooses to groom or gift another female, it may not be received as well as if a gift was given by her male counterpart. Males are always happy to receive attention and gifts, no matter who it is from. It takes a very long time for a Therizinosaurus to make a lifelong pair-bond with another Therizinosaurus. They spend a lot of time travelling, patrolling, nesting, and gifting one another before deciding to settle down with another Therizinosaurus permanently. If lifelong-pairbonded, a Therizinosaurus will go nowhere without their mate, and will stay close to them whenever possible. Mated pairs will stay by each other until the end. If one half of a mated pair dies, the remaining individual will stand by the body patiently, waiting for them to rise. They will remain there until pushed out by a carnivore, and even then, will watch the body from a distance, until it dawns on them that their mate is dead. A Therizinosaurus who has lost their mate may never find another lifelong pairbond unless under very specific circumstances. Bonded individuals will sit together and watch over one another, maintaining a secure personal space bubble, and preening one another from time to time. If a male is not interested in the females within a group, or he is related to all of them, he will opt to leave the group to instead find another group of females to court. Albino individuals are shunned and pushed out of their groupings at Adolescent, as soon as they begin to show their white feathers. They will be chased out of the territory by the Dame, with no exceptions, and left to fend for themselves.
