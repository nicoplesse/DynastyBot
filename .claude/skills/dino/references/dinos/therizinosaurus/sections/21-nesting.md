# Therizinosaurus – Nesting

At this point, the female will lay her eggs in his nest and then move on. If she is not lifelong pairbonded and it is during the Rainy Season, she may opt to take more than one male. Eggs may be split between males at a player’s discretion - a female may nest with up to 3 males during the rainy season, and must provide a minimum of one egg per male. Males must make the nesting post. The female’s nesting timer begins when the final nesting post is made. The male’s nesting timer begins when their individual nest post is made. If a male and female court 5 times with successful nests over multiple nesting seasons, they may opt to form a lifelong pairbond, and will only nest with each other in all nesting seasons from that point onward. This is an optional behavior; pairs who have 5 successful nests are not mandated to form a lifelong pairbond. Male same-sex pairings are welcome, and follow the same protocols as opposite sex pairings. However, if they wish to form a lifelong pairbond, they must adopt a minimum of 5 chicks together, over time, and raise them to adulthood successfully. Male same-sex pairs may be in Tidings or Sects. Males build their nests long before courting, opting out of patrolling for a night in order to pick the prime location for his nest. Males have no qualms with being close to other males while nesting, and spend a great deal of time tidying and making their nests soft and warm for future hatchlings. Females will stop by the nests initially to lay their eggs. Aside from this, they are very rarely around the nest, and may not be present when the eggs hatch. They may at times approach the nest to observe the babies and to greet them. If a male is struggling with keeping a nest under control, she will step in to correct the chicks, typically by loudly calling and shooing them back in the correct direction. Males incubate the eggs until they hatch, and remain on the nest for as long as possible, unless hungry or thirsty, as leaving a constantly peeping chick unattended may result in it being picked off by a predator.
