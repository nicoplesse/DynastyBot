# Therizinosaurus – Cross-Species Adoption

Males may attempt to adopt any species with feathers, whether carnivorous or herbivorous. These adoptees may remain with a Tiding until sub-adult, and will be protected as if one of their own chicks. However, if a part of a Sect, the Dame will drive the adoptee out at Adolescent, and the father will not welcome it back into the group. There may only be one different species adoptee per Therizinosaurus grouping. Dames and other females will not protect different species adoptees. If Therizinosaurus was raised by a species different than their own, they may adopt orphaned individuals of that species. These adoptees will not be defended by any other individual than the one who adopted it. If Therizinosaurus runs into the same species as their adoptee, it will encourage the adoptee to follow their same species. These adoptees will disperse at sub-adult. Therizinosaurus do not attack first, preferring to call aggressively and flex their calls in the direction of the predator on sight. However, if the predator gets within 3 body-lengths of the Therizinosaurus, 5 body-lengths of a chick, or visibly uses/prepares an attack move, it may move forward to meet the predator, claws outstretched. This applies to any predator, no matter their size tier. Only 2 Sub/Adult Therizinosaurus may take part in an engagement. This is most often the Dame and one other female, but targets of a hunt may defend themselves. A male take the place of a second female if the dame is the only adult female in the sect.  Chicks will also be defended ferociously by their fathers, with the second defender being the Dame. Lifelong pairbonded Therizinosaurus will always defend each other. Therizinosaurus females will chase a predator down if necessary. They will not stop attacking a creature until it forfeits, moves out of LOS, or is killed. Females are particularly prone to this behavior. Males will not chase if defending chicks, and will instead move away if a predator begins to flee. When chasing a predator, Therizinosaurus will pursue for as long as it can, unless the predator or Therizinosaurus does not land an attack for 15 seconds. If a predator forfeits but is on heavy deathscars (covered in scarring/wounds completely, female Therizinosaurus may chase it past the forfeit, following the above rule. If the predator turns around at that point and attacks the Therizinosaurus or one of its groupmates, the Therizinosaurus is again allowed to chase, if necessary. Therizinosaurus do not form defensive formations beyond males ushering chicks beneath their tails, and backing away as the Dame rushes forward to take part in the engagement. If there is no adult that is able to safely protect an adolescent or below Therizinosaurus beneath its tail, a chick will flee to deep brush and shrubbery instead, remaining entirely silent until the threat has been chased away or killed.
