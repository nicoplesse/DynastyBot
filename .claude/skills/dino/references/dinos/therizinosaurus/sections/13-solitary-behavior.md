# Therizinosaurus – Solitary Behavior

Solitary Therizinosaurus females are those that prefer a life of solitude, claiming and fiercely defending medium sized territories in deeply forested areas. They are quiet in their patrols, but unstoppable when they come across other species whom intrude upon or feed from their resources, issuing loud screeching calls. Solitary Therizinosaurus males, on the other hand, are rarely solitary for long, constantly seeking the comfort of another male or a sect. They are more nervous and less aggressive than their female counterparts, and will opt to back or bolt away from danger when threatened. Males allow - and at times prefer - smaller creatures to help them tend to their feathers-- specifically Struthiomimus . These creatures are babied by males and are allowed to hide under their tails. Solitary Therizinosaurus spend a lot of time idly collecting items and adding them to their hoard - these items may be recycled as gifts when they become a part of a group, or fiercely defended from other members, dependent on which the Therizinosaurus prefers. Solitary Therizinosaurus may be seen standing in healing water at night for extended periods. If they see a carnivore, they will move out of the healing water and will stay out of it, not wanting to muddy the water with blood, as it plays a major role in them keeping their feathers clean.
