# Therizinosaurus – Adoption

Only male Therizinosaurus adopt orphaned Therizinosaurus chicks. Females, on the other hand, will fiercely ignore them or drive them away. Male same sex pairings who repeatedly adopt offspring together and raise the child to adult successfully on 5 separate occasions become bonded for the rest of their lives. Elderly Therizinosaurus begin to get slower moving, and stop chasing predators. Their colors become more desaturated, and their feathers start to become clumpy and matted (unless they have a partner to preen them). Females tend to become grumpier on a day to day basis, but are less likely to physically aggress another creature from its personal space, preferring to vocalize their annoyance instead. Elderly Dames may be pushed out of their dominant position, resulting in her being solo for the rest of her life, unless she has a lifelong pairbond. Males do not change much in behavior as they age, and still continue to try to court and nest.
