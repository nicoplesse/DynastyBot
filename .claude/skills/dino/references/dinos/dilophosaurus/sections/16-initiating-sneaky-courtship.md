# Dilophosaurus – Initiating Sneaky Courtship

Dilophosaurus that attempt to court outside the courtship grounds, initiate what is referred to as "sneaky courtship". These pairings never form bonds, but are used as fleeting moments in order to produce offspring. Individuals seeking to court outside their courtship grounds spend considerable time stalking other Dilophosaurus from afar, silently trailing behind individuals or groups to assess opportunities available. The ideal partner is one which is healthy, with fewer battle wounds and a louder call. When trailing behind a leash, interested individuals watch from a distance to identify which reynard is the dominant male and avoid approaching him. When a window of opportunity appears - with the chosen Dilophosaurus successfully distanced from the rest of their group members - the interested approaches with soft affectionate calls and head bobs, gently twisting their head to flash their crests. If the courted Dilophosaurus is not interested, rejection is portrayed with a responding:danger_call: a fear call and fleeing from the dispersal or rushing back to their group. If the courted Dilophosaurus  is interested, they accept the dispersal's advances with curiosity and interest, standing still and watching (❔ emote) for their next move. The dispersal continues to call affectionately, stepping back and bobbing their head to lure their suitor to follow them away. If the courted follows, the dispersal leads them to a resource, which can range from a pile of mushrooms, flowers or bones. If the resource is satisfactory, the courted accepts the dispersal with affectionate calls. Females within a leash that were successfully courted by a dispersal male immediately abandon him and return to her dominant male. Her first instinct is to attempt courtship with her dominant male to trick him into believing the clutch she lays is fathered by him. If the dominant male rejects her courtship display, the female is forced to destroy her eggs once laid, or raise them in secret as a dispersal. Solo or same-sex female pairs disperse into a sneaky nesting group to rear their young, and chase away the male. The dispersing male, once exiled by his new mate, quickly flees the POI in search of others. The male must share his genetics to the female via whispers or local chat before leaving the area.
