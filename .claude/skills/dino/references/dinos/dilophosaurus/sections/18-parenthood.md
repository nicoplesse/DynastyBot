# Dilophosaurus – Parenthood

Reynards do not take part in the raising of offspring, beyond providing food for the mother. They have no interest in their offspring until adulthood, and instead prioritize protecting the general nesting area, and taking care of their vixens and the rest of the leash. A reynard only tolerates his breeding group nesting within a leash. If he discovers that one of his daughters has nested, he will quickly exile her, and cull all offspring. All reynards in a leash spend their time away from the vixens during this period, with subordinate males sticking closely to the dominant male in the vixens' absence. Instead of socializing with the vixens, these reynards stalk the outside of the nest, keeping the territory safe from predators - or other Dilophosaurus. When ready to nest, vixens set off with other vixens or on their own to find a proper nesting site. This is dense foliage, a den (preferably with multiple chambers), or other enclosed spaces. Some more established groupings usually have a regular nesting ground in their preferred biomes – in this case, they work together to maintain and fill their nests with food, in order to make it as easy as possible for their offspring to thrive. Vixens work together to construct their own and each other's nests. This is a strong bonding activity among vixens and they show great affection to one another as a result. If a vixen has been sneakily courted, she will often go by herself entirely, in order to avoid detection. Other vixens do not tolerate offspring of males that are not the dominant male, and will quickly alert the dominant male towards any suspicions. Once their nesting place has been established, a vixen will lay her eggs and curl snugly around them until they hatch. Once her hatchlings emerge from their eggs, she fondly coos at them, nuzzling them often and staying very close by. She will stay in this position with them, hissing at anything else that comes close, until her offspring are Juvenile. Then, she introduces her offspring to the other vixens and their offspring, in order to allow them to begin to form strong bonds. Vixens parent their offspring together, often sending out females on short trips to successfully find more food. One or two of the other vixens stay with the babies to carefully watch over them while they play and explore things with their teeth. Once the babies reach adolescent, the vixens return with them to the dominant male and any other reynards. The father still does not express much interest in the babies beyond watching them curiously, and defending their mothers fiercely from predators - and any male that may attempt to court her. If a female is able to conceal that her pups are not related to the dominant male, they are permitted to join alongside the rest of the group. These individuals will not be culled in their adulthood. Once the group has successfully returned to one another, and the father has met and properly assessed his offspring, the breeding group will return with the adolescent pups to the rest of the leash. Adult siblings meet the newest of the bunch with excited curiosity. The dominant breeding group or adult siblings will assist with teaching offspring how to hunt and scavenge, taking them on traipses throughout the territory to encourage them to explore, dig, and sharpen their claws on trees and rocks. From then on, pups are raised communally and protected communally by the entire leash, until they reach adulthood.
